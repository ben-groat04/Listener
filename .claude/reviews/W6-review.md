## 3. Observed problems: verified or refuted
a. Slow prep / mid-run fetches. CONFIRMED, with a correction on the cause. Evidence: plan lead's estimate "about 4 requests/s about 20 min" for user tracks (research-lead-plan.md); prep-r1 report: user tracks 34 min, decoys 2174 of 5723 attempted (39%), P2 65 min; prep-r2: iTunes lookup "4-8 s each", stopped at the 90 min cap with 710 of 1074 TUNE tracks, folds 2023-07 and 2023-10 missing. Mid-run additions: prep-r1 restarted the MERT job and added a separate downloader (`w6_dl.py`) at 11:18, and the decoy fetch was restarted at 11:31 after a reshuffle; the catalogue-context builder ran its own `fetch_artist_top.py` and `fetch_related.py` for comparison data. I could not find a distinct "extra preview batch" apart from the w6_dl restart. Root causes: (1) plan lead had no timings (script has zero mentions of iTunes or rates); (2) the single global Deezer throttle caps throughput regardless of threads; (3) iTunes at 3.5 s/lookup was never budgeted for 1074 tracks (~63 min + CPU contention); (4) round 2 was decided late so the prep could not overlap anything.
b. Ensemble builder. CONFIRMED cause, RESULTS-W6 PARTLY WRONG. Transcript: at 14:29:26 it started a Monitor, wrote "I'll continue when a monitor reports that it has ended", then the harness forced a structured result at 14:29:28 and it returned at 14:29:44 (6.4 min). The Monitor reply even said "Keep working - do not poll or sleep. Events may arrive...". fast1.txt was last written 14:29:54, i.e. the run finished ~10 s AFTER the forced return (runtime 269 s); at the time the builder said "still running" it was true, and the works-DA reading fast1.txt at ~14:30 saw only the 1408-byte header. The dismissed DA re-ran it (157 s) and got the table. So section 7's "it thought its first test run was unfinished. In fact the run had finished" is inaccurate: it was unfinished when the builder stopped; the loss is the builder's turn, not a misreading. Also "a probably stronger candidate (+0.014 to +0.015) was lost" overstates: those are undeclared fast TUNE figures with 15 variants of selection, untuned in-sample weights, negative yearly trend vs incumbent, no gain on new artists (DAs).
c. Runs spent on things logic settles. CONFIRMED. Builder-r2 preview-source: 23 analysis models, 4 label-free "fixes" (mean shift, std, coral, ridge); its own report notes the recipe is linear (PCA + ridge, window mean), so a constant feature shift is a constant score shift and cannot change rankings (mu result identical to raw, -0.020). 94% / 86% of alternative clips are a different part of the song, so "loss" mixes excerpt and codec, and the same-part subsets (13 and 19 loved) cannot separate them. The decision "no fix" was predictable. Round-1 content builder also ran three scoring modes (dev, Deezer, mean) for the same reason.
d. Missing report files and held-out numbers. CONFIRMED (missing: DA r1 catalogue-context works, builder r2 ensemble; DA r2 ensemble works said the report file did not exist at its path). LATE numbers: the r1 content "dismissed" DA put the LATE gain over the incumbent and the LATE new-artist difference with its interval in its structured summary (and in its report); the review lead flagged it, the final lead re-confirmed. Grep of RESULTS-W6.md and prereg-w6/ for those figures, any 2024 numbers, and LATE interval text: none; RESULTS line 95 gives only a qualitative statement ("no LATE interval lay wholly below the incumbent"), which carries one bit of held-out information. The round-2 preview-source builder transcript matched my search strings only through TUNE numbers (-0.038 in coral rows), not LATE. No LATE numbers were in any round-2 builder prompt.
e. Round-2 clip-source builder. CONFIRMED. `final_avg21_fallback.py` imports `PREDICTORS["avg21_dev"]` from the round-1 file and renames it. The docstring and report claim "a track with no clip at all gets the neutral score 0" and "explicit fallback handling", but the file has no such code (fallback comes only from whatever `features.mert` already does). LATE was refused as a duplicate (Spearman 1.0). The builder itself says it is "NOT a new candidate". The docstring should be corrected or real handling added and tested.
f. User preferences as weak ideas. CONFIRMED. "Faster" and "settle by logic" were in "Unvetted ideas" with the standing "may well not be the best way" caveat (workflow6.js lines 105-111); the user's direct request to parallelise fetching and the past slow-source timings were not in the script at all (script saved 10:36, the notes entries about them were added after the run). Agents did not read notes (forbidden). Effect: no fetch budget, no parallelism, 2-thread cap for everyone.
