## 0. Short answer
- The frame is not arbitrary everywhere. Harmless at the 80% level: shifting fold boundaries by 1-2 months (tune, tune_q1, tune_q2, tune_m, tune_h give the same ranking, Kendall tau 0.76-0.91 for sauc over 12 rows), pairing by fold vs by calendar quarter (tau 0.88-1.0), the session gap 5-120 min and most exposure-bucket edges for wx, the artist_recent half-life 30-730 d, the GBM settings of the baselines (none of them becomes best_ref), 60/90-day label thresholds.
- Frame choices that change conclusions beyond noise: (1) the pair weighting. Two quarters (2023-Q1, 2023-Q4) carry 69.3% of pooled pair weight. Under pooled pairs the content/listening references and the stack beat artist_recent (lo80 > 0); under equal weight per quarter, half-year or year none does at 80% (point deltas -0.004 to -0.020 for the stack). Dropping 2023-Q1 raises the stack's lead (+0.084 sauc), dropping 2023-Q4 removes it (+0.004). (2) Training staleness of 6-12 months (tune_y): content and listening references lose 0.04-0.07 sauc (fold pairing, tune to tune_y) while artist_recent loses 0.007 (0.019 with quarter pairing); the stack-minus-artist_recent delta goes from +0.043 (1-month cutoffs) to +0.034 (3 months), +0.028 (6 months), -0.003 (12 months). (3) The label horizon: with the 365-day keep label or 180-day threshold the stack's lead over artist_recent is no longer lo80 > 0. (4) wa under calendar-quarter pairing with monthly cutoffs is a below-chance artefact (see 6).
- A time trend sits under (1): the per-quarter paired delta of content models over artist_recent rises by about +0.10 sauc per year (80% interval +0.03 to +0.16 for the stack), negative in 2021-2022, positive from 2022-Q4. Early quarters are small (31-81 songs).
- Best reference identity depends on the fold set (listening on tune, q1, m; music on q2, h; artist_recent on tune_y). The declared stack never beats its own best_ref at 80% on any fold set (lo80 -0.005 to -0.049), and is never worse than it by more than 0.014 point.

## 6. Below-chance results (investigated). results/a7_below_chance.txt
- wa for artist_recent / artist_plays under calendar-quarter pairing with monthly cutoffs (tune_m): 0.427 / 0.425 [80% .369-.469]. Cause: inside one (quarter, artist) group songs are tested at different cutoffs; a later first play has a higher artist-history score (more plays) and lower love rate (AUC of 'later month' alone vs love within the group 0.425, 3045 pairs, 51% of rows in groups with >=2 months). Artefact of the pairing, not a model property; wa must be paired inside the same cutoff when cutoffs are finer than the pairing group. stack3 wa under the same pairing is .647.
- popularity sauc 0.476 on tune (80% .451-.514, P(<0.5)=0.80), 0.466-0.494 on every other fold set, wa 0.445-0.472: consistently slightly below chance, P(<0.5) 0.59-0.86; weak evidence that more popular tracks are slightly less loved by this user; the 80% interval includes 0.5 on every fold set (tune .451-.514, q1 .437-.509, h .447-.503). Left as a weak finding.

## 9. Strongest case against my conclusions
1. All fold sets re-use the same 1074 songs and the same artist clusters. The 'spread across fold sets' therefore mixes real frame effects with idiosyncratic placement of a few hundred songs; I compared spread to the bootstrap half-width descriptively, not with a test.
2. The weighting result rests on 10 quarterly AUCs, two of which are 41% and 28% of the weight; the per-quarter 80% intervals are wide (0.1-0.2 for 2021-22 quarters, ~0.06 for the heavy ones) and the macro / half-year / year weights give small quarters the same influence as large ones, so 'content does not beat artist_recent under equal weights' is as noisy as the pooled result is dominated. Neither weighting is the correct one; I only show that the conclusion depends on it. The slope +0.10/year is a regression on 10 noisy points.
3. Over 100 paired comparisons were inspected at 80%; chance lo80 > 0 happens about 1 in 10 for a null delta, and the reference models were tuned on TUNE, so TUNE favours them.
4. The label-horizon finding (longer labels favour artist_recent) is confounded with the fact that long-horizon labels also shorten the effective period (songs near the end of TUNE have windows that run to the end of 2024) and with changes of listening habits; the 30/60-day proxies give a bigger lead partly because short windows reward quick-fire encounters.
5. The declared stack fails the independence gate as written (rank normalisation); my sensitivity check shows no effect on scores but the check ran on the dump, not through the harness.
6. wa/new-artist values for artist_* are structural (chance by construction), so new-artist deltas vs artist_recent overstate the lead (the baseline cannot score new artists).
7. Fast-mode dump scores (nboot 100 harness, own bootstrap at 300) rather than slow full scoring for most frame numbers; the full-run values agree to 3 decimals for sauc on the default folds.
