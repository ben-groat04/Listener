## 1. Short answer
- Pooled sauc is a defensible primary score but it is noisy: the 80% half-width of a paired delta is about 0.025 between references and about 0.04 against artist_recent, so deltas below roughly 0.02 are not resolvable on 1074 tried songs.
- Of 14 scores compared, pooled sauc and AUC against the neighbouring labels strong / love_10_4 were the most reproducible per unit of noise; top-of-ranking scores (top-decile lift, recall@20%, precision@10), macro, keep and graded scores were clearly noisier. Nothing in the data picks a better primary score than pooled sauc on love.
- Equal-weight-per-fold averages are dominated by the four earliest folds (31-45 songs each, 3.9% of pair weight); the difference EW minus pooled is within noise (|z| about 1-1.4) so they carry no demonstrable extra information; they are the noisiest estimator (80% width 0.065 vs 0.054 pooled, 0.049 IV).
- Per-fold heterogeneity of paired deltas is not detected, but the test has only about 27% power against tau = 0.03, so "homogeneous" is not shown.
- The upward trend of content/listening models over artist_recent is NOT explained by training-history length: with every model trained on only the last 12 months before each cutoff the trend is the same (+0.12/yr, 80% [+0.063,+0.159] vs +0.10 [+0.047,+0.148] full history), and it survives dropping the first 2 or 4 folds. It is a rise of the content/listening models' absolute AUC (+0.06 to +0.08/yr) with a mild fall of artist_recent (-0.03/yr, 80% [-0.055,+0.006]); even `duration` rises (+0.05/yr). Part of it looks like era / candidate-pool change (love rate 0.74 in the first fold to 0.23 in 2023).
- The open-pool score disagrees with tried-pool sauc about references vs artist_recent: overall undecided, known-artist subset and p20 favour artist_recent, new-artist subset favours the references by about +0.2.
- Label measurement noise is not what limits scores (see 5); the limit is predictive information.

## 4. Validity for the end goal
Key deltas (point, with z = delta/bootstrap SE in brackets), from a2_open_validity.py:
- Pooled sauc: pair_z-artist_recent +0.042 (+1.5), listening-artist_recent +0.032 (+1.2), music-artist_recent +0.028 (+0.9), audio-artist_recent +0.013 (+0.4); listening-music +0.005, listening-audio +0.020 (+1.6), music-audio +0.015 (+1.3). - Top-decile lift: positive for 7 of 9 key deltas (one 0.000, one -0.028) but every |z| <= 1.2 (listening-artist_recent +0.14 lift, z 0.8).
- gc plays90: references vs artist_recent are NEGATIVE (-0.004 to -0.026, |z| <= 1.2), i.e. the sign flips versus sauc; graded concordance cares about the ordering inside the 1-play group, where the artist signal helps. I did not separate the reasons further.
- Macro: signs flip for listening vs artist_recent (-0.014) and audio (-0.015).
- Open pool (decoys = never-played songs by pre-cutoff seed artists; Spotify-CDN subset; run_default_fast.txt, harness --fast, 100 resamples): open sauc, 80% interval of delta over artist_recent: pair_z (-0.049,+0.115), listening (-0.060,+0.095), music (-0.063,+0.111), audio (-0.089,+0.062): all undecided. Known-artist subset: all four references NEGATIVE at 80% (e.g. listening (-0.155,-0.078)). New-artist subset: all POSITIVE (+0.16 to +0.33) because artist-history models are blind to unfamiliar artists. Open p20: all references NEGATIVE vs artist_recent (listening (-0.142,-0.016)). Among references the open sauc agrees with tried sauc (listening-audio +, music-audio +, pair_z-listening POS80 (0.007,0.028)).
Why they disagree: a never-played decoy is "not tried", not "would dislike"; tried songs are strongly concentrated in familiar artists, so a model that knows artist familiarity separates tried from decoys; the decoy seeds are the user's top artists plus related ones, so the known/new mix (and therefore which model "wins") is set by the decoy design, which is itself arbitrary. The tried-pool sauc removes the tried-vs-untried selection and asks only about loved vs tried-not-loved. Judgement (belief): the tried-pool within-fold sauc is the cleaner measure of taste; the open pool answers a different question (what the user tries) and should only be a cross-check, read by new/known subset. I cannot say which better predicts success in a real unheard-song search.

## 5. Ceiling (a4_ceiling.py, results/a4_ceiling.txt)
- Label noise is small: love vs love_4_2 kappa 0.89, vs love_10_4 0.96, vs strong 0.94, vs love at 180 d 0.92, vs love at 365 d 0.81, vs keep (6 plays in days 90-365) 0.75 (agreement 0.89). plays90 predicts keep with AUC 0.92; AUC of love_4_2 / love_10_4 / plays_h30 / plays_h60 as predictors of love: 0.97 / 0.98 / 0.95 / 0.99.
- Near-threshold share: only 3.8% of loved songs have 6-7 plays or exactly 3 days; 20.8% of all tried songs have plays90 in 4-8 or days90 in 2-4 (4% of loved, 29% of not loved, i.e. the not-loved side is where borderline cases are). 46% of songs have exactly 1 play in 90 days, 30% have 12 or more.
- Gamma-Poisson ceiling (assume all variation beyond Poisson is stable taste): AUC of true rate vs realised plays>=6 label 0.985-0.993; observed plays as rate with Poisson replicate 0.995. This is a loose upper bound (assumed model, base rate not matched: simulated 0.49-0.64 vs real 0.32) and says only that Poisson label noise is not the limit.
- Information ceiling: leaky diagnostics: day-1 plays (oracle_day1) 0.759, first-session replays 0.703, 7-day oracle 0.886; the best leak-free model here 0.661. So even knowing how the first day went leaves large unexplained variance; I cannot derive a pre-listening ceiling from these data. Belief: realistic room is bounded by information, not label noise; another label or graded outcome would not leave more room (graded scores and horizon-365/keep were noisier and ranked the models similarly or worse).
- Resolution: 80% half-width of a paired delta 0.025 (reference vs reference) to 0.04 (vs artist_recent), so only improvements above about 0.02-0.04 are decidable at this N.

## 11. Strongest case against these conclusions
- All reliability numbers come from one 1074-song dataset: split-half agreement ranks the metrics on the same data and has no standard error; the gaps between sauc, strong and love_10_4 are within noise, and top-k metrics may look worse mainly because they use fewer pairs (they have lower information by construction, not necessarily lower validity for a top-of-list end goal).
- Reliability is not validity: the most reproducible score (pooled sauc) is dominated by two quarters and by within-quarter pairs; a model that wins there may still not find loved songs among unheard ones; the open pool, the nearest proxy, disagrees and I did not resolve which is right.
- The trend test: 10 folds, slope SE about 0.04; truncation controls only the reference side; "trend not explained by history length" does not show what does explain it; the fold-set variants share data so their agreement is not independent evidence.
- The Q test and EW analysis have low power; "not significant" is not "homogeneous".
- pair_z was chosen knowing the reference TUNE scores; its +0.009/+0.014 over the references is undecided at 80%.
- Ceiling: the Poisson bound is a loose model-based number and the information ceiling was not measured.
