## 1. The set as one work

Public Service Broadcasting's "Elfstedentocht Part 1" (3:09) and "Part 2" (5:04) were commissioned for the 50th anniversary of the race of 18 January 1963 (Explore the North festival, Leeuwarden; footage and voice-over from the Fries Film Archief; `context.md`) and released together as a two-sided single (MusicBrainz; the mapping video1 = Part 1, video2 = Part 2 is high confidence, see section 8). Together they tell one account twice. The words are one Dutch archive script (the date, the hour, the cold, "more than 200 km", 575 racers and about 9,500 tour skaters), the pictures draw on one pool of Frisian film, and the music is the part that changes: a loud, flat, band-led piece on a D pedal at about 78 BPM in Part 1, and a quiet-to-loud, loop-built electronic piece on a B pedal at 120 BPM in Part 2. Part 1 gives the event as a record (print, paperwork, signs, a banner); Part 2 gives it as a place and an effort (frost, pack, dusk, water). Neither film shows a winner, the finish or the finishers. The pair is about the run-up and the effort, and it ends twice without arriving. The best one-line description from the team is **continuous in material, restarting in narration, re-set in music** (story and music, round 3; INT, M). Its clearest finding about the work is that, with script and footage held roughly constant, **the sound decides what the same material means** (music, round 3; INT, M-H).

## 3. How the two parts talk to each other

**What they share (OBS unless marked).**
- **One script, in the same order.** Both give the date, then the marathon and cold lines, "twelfth", the title sentence, "16 degrees below zero", "more than 200 km" and the 575 / 9,500 line, in much the same sequence (Whisper medium on both mixes; Part 1 also has a second "twelfth" line at 0:40.6, and the town list). It is the same words, not shown to be the same take: cross-correlation of the title sentence between the parts is at control level, though stems are separated and processed (music, round 2; M that the words are shared, L on the take).
- **One footage pool.** Four shots match across the two films, grey in Part 1 and in colour in Part 2: bib 170 (video1 1:05, video2 3:34), bib 145 (1:08, 3:30), bib 157 (3:00, 3:51) and a striped-headband skater (3:02, 4:44). Picture confirmed them by eye; four is a floor, found at 1 fps with low recall (H on the shots, M on the order). The order is the same apart from one swap, and the spacings differ (the last two are 2 s apart in Part 1, 53 s apart in Part 2), so Part 2 is neither a stretched copy nor a shuffle: INT, M-L, "a recut from the same rolls". No narrated sentences sit under any of the four in either film, but Part 2's voice fragments (3:28.6 on; Whisper "Eerst" 3:30.6, "Slop" 3:34.5 to 3:35.9) overlap the 3:30 and 3:34 shots (story, round 3, corrected on check).
- **Same method of drama.** Both are pedal pieces with no tune and no cadence; the feeling is carried by layers, level and timbre. Both put the title sentence directly before a rhythm-section hole (video1 2:47.8, 0.4 s; video2 1:24.3 to 1:28.6, about 4 s). The team settled on "habit of the mix, the same stage direction used twice", not "signature" or "rehearsal" (SPEC); the hole is sound-led. Part 2's picture holds one static dyke-road shot (1:22.1 to about 1:28.5; round sign, footprints, a few walkers) through the hole and cuts on the drop itself (blurred whip to a bridge crowd, within about 0.1 s; frame-checked, OBS). Part 1 has no cut at its hole either.
- **Shared dry, band-limited narrator, centred against a wide band** (music, round 1).

**What they do not share.** No riff, melody or bass line (searched; none found, H on absence), no key (D against B), no tempo (78 against 120), no drum behaviour, and no narration after 2:36 in Part 2. The old "same pulse" idea (a 0.385 s hat in Part 1, a 0.375 s bass figure in Part 2) was withdrawn by its author as coincidence.

**Does one answer the other?** Not as an argument. Part 2 opens about 15 dB under Part 1's end, which is **relief in level**, but it restarts the script at the date and re-sets key, tempo and kit, which is **restart in content** (music, round 2; M). The only real hand-over is footage: Part 1's last race burst (video1 2:55 to 3:02) is the kind of material Part 2 spends about two minutes on (listener: "Part 2's plateau returns to the part of Part 1 that was over too soon", INT, M). Note that the shared shots are not held longer in Part 2 (two longer, two shorter); the time is gained by more shots (listener R3 check).

## 4. The arc across the set

There is no single arc, and the team dropped "the pair rises then falls". What can be said:

1. **Two loudness shapes.** Part 1 sits flat and loud; by K-weighted loudness its end (2:48 to 3:08, about -7.0 to -6.4 LUFS) is level with 1:07 to 1:15 (peak about -6.6 LUFS at 1:07), not louder. Part 2 builds from a quiet pad, peaks about 4:12 to 4:36 and is dismantled from 4:36.8. Only the order of release links them; the "staircase" across both is a description, not a design (music and listener, round 3; M).
2. **Two registers of picture.** Documents first, then place and pack: Part 1 leans on print, desks and signs and ends on a banner and black; Part 2 leans on pack, crowd and landscape and ends on water. This is a tendency, not a division of function, because Part 1 also has empty frosted fields and Part 2 also has road signs and lorry lettering (picture and story, round 3; M).
3. **Neither film arrives.** Part 1 lists the route (2:16 to 2:36) and ends mid-effort on a banner. Part 2 withdraws to water. Research context only (`elfstedentocht_1963.md`, not from either film): 127 official finishers, the winner Reinier Paping (10:59) and 9,862 starters; none of these appears in either film (OBS on the absence; INT, M on its meaning).
4. **Pace of the telling.** Part 1 is compact (facts, a list, a sprint). Part 2 states the same facts more slowly, then spends two minutes with no new facts and ends by subtraction. Read in order, the pair goes from telling to staying with it (INT, M-L).

## 5. Disagreements and open questions

These are real and unresolved, not smoothed over.

- **"Variation" or "second telling".** Music prefers "variation" (the sound is what differs); story prefers "second telling" because the narration restarts at the date, which is a narrative device. Both can hold (round 3).
- **Which part works better.** Listener: Part 1 alone (events spread through the run, ends level with its loudest K-weighted stretch), Part 2 as the one that stays after it (the long subtraction, the clean joint at 1:24.3 to 1:28.6, the probable bar-lock). It is a reasoned expectation, M to M-L (round 3).
- **Does Part 2's picture follow its music better?** Listener and picture said yes (bar-start cluster; round 3); on check the picture does cut on the drop (about 1:28.5), so the drop is marked as well as the cluster. The cluster is post hoc, the window was chosen after looking, the 0.1 s lead is within detector and mp3 error, and the earlier team line was "no lock" (corrected in round 3). I reproduced 18 of 66 (section 8). Treat as M-L. Part 1 is not shown to be less aware: it has a beat-rate lagged cluster in two flurries, and the tracker may be offset, so edit habit against tracker phase is unresolved (music and picture).
- **Are Part 2's voice fragments town names?** Story's hypothesis (an echo of Part 1's eleven names) was downgraded to L, leaning against. Re-weighed on check: Whisper medium (mix) does give nine short segments at 3:25.9 to 3:48.1 (Stief, Eerst, Slop, Stapen, In de lopen, In de banken, In de bonswacht, Vrijdiger, Vrijdiger) whose order loosely echoes Part 1's list (eerst, slotten/Slop, Staveren/Stapen, in de lopen), and nine equals the "nine main bursts" counted by music. That is mild evidence for the echo (SPEC). Against: the music reading finds the ASR passes mutually incompatible and calls these probable hallucinations; the bursts are longer (0.4 to 1.0 s against 0.03 to 0.58 s) and bar-quantised against free; no legible place-name sign was found in the picture (a road sign appears at 4:28). Now open, L-M, neither leaning; only a Dutch listener can settle it. Still excluded from the meaning of the pair.
- **Why was it split?** A two-sided release is the only documented reason; "one account given two jobs" is INT, M-L; a pure format effect is not excluded (SPEC, L). No source says how the two were commissioned.
- **The hole.** Whether the 0.4 s dip in Part 1 registers at all on a first hearing is open; the listener thinks it may not, with the banner covering it. The 4 s hole in Part 2 is hard to miss (INT).
- **Same image, effort or routine?** Bib 157 under Part 1 (loudest run, glimpsed 2 s) is expected to read as effort; under Part 2's plateau as routine. Faster or slower is genuinely mixed (Part 1 slower in tempo, denser; Part 2 twice the beat rate, calmer). The listener calls for a viewer test.
- **Agreement is weaker than it looks.** All colleagues read the same dossiers, and Whisper on this material has been wrong before (story, round 3; ERRORS). Part 1's Whisper line "Bedankt voor het kijken!" (3:07) is not used anywhere: it falls at the "Reportage" banner (3:07) in a stretch where no colleague reports speech, so I treat it as a likely ASR artefact (my note, SPEC).

## 6. What a human takes away

All of this is INT, expected rather than reported.

- **Same words, different weather.** The strongest impression is how much the score decides: the pair is a demonstration that identical facts and near-identical footage can feel like a bulletin in one film and a vigil in the other (music, listener, story; M-H on the facts, M on the meaning). It is best met in order, because only then does the constancy show.
- **Part 1 is the telling, Part 2 the room.** Part 1 is urgent, stern and eventful; Part 2 lets a viewer who already knows the facts stay with the cold, the pack and the ice.
- **What lingers is a subtraction.** The end of Part 2 (bright layer off, bass gated, groove cut, a short drums-and-bass tag from 5:02.34, then water) is the passage most likely to stay, and it rests on the order of removals, not on colour (listener; M). Next, for a viewer who notices it, a small private recognition of bib 170, grey then blue (SPEC, L-M), and Part 1's bass return at 1:40.0 on speakers that carry it.
- **No arrival.** One leaves with the run-up and the effort, not the result. A Dutch viewer would add hindsight (the last such winter for 22 years; `context.md`, round 2 limits); it is not in either film as an observed property.
- **The script is noticed before the shots.** A viewer is more likely to notice the date coming round again (video2 0:41.7) than four recurring shots (listener, picture, story; SPEC, M-L).
