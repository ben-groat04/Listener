# Elfstedentocht diptych: the listener's comparative reading (round 1)

Role: listener. Focus: what it is like to watch and hear video1 (Part 1, 3:09) and video2 (Part 2, 5:04) in sequence; what lingers; which works better and why. Timestamps are `<video> m:ss`. British English.

**Standing caveat.** Nobody on the team, me included, has heard the audio. Every "I feel" below is a reasoned expectation built from measurements (`music_reading.md`) and frames, not a report of hearing. Tags: **OBS** seen or measured, **INT** interpretation, **SPEC** speculation; H/M/L confidence as in the source files.

**Part order verified.** `research/part_mapping.md` holds. MusicBrainz lengths 3:09 and 5:04 match our files (video1 188.7 s; video2 305.9 s, about 1.9 s over, plausibly trailing black) (H). The genre description (Part 1 loud and rock-oriented, Part 2 calmer and more electronic) fits our own tags: video1 78 BPM on a D pedal; video2 120 BPM in B Aeolian (OBS, music readings). One caution: the part_mapping note leans on CLAP "distorted guitar 0.44" for video1, but the music reading treats guitar as an inference and calls the CLAP label unstable. ## 1. Watching them in sequence: what each feels like

**Part 1 (INT, from the music reading's emotional journey and interpretation.md).** Cold, effortful and pressed forward. A swell and a hit at video1 0:00.7, then drums that never change gear. The bass, a dense "wall" layer and the voice are switched in and out over a D pedal with the third missing, so it sounds stern, not sad or triumphant (M). The narrator supplies facts only (date, "half past five", the cold, the distance, 575 racers, the towns); the band supplies stakes. I would expect to be pushed across a landscape rather than told a story. The tension sits in a few felt events:
- the floor falling away at video1 1:15 and returning at 1:40 (bass about -53.8 to -19.1 dB in one second);
- the thin tea-table passage at 1:55 to 2:14 under six slow sub thumps;
- the town list at 2:16 to 2:36, where signs land within about a second of each name;
- the 0.4 s hole at 2:47.8, then a bright, wide sprint to a cut-off at 3:08.4.

**Part 2 (INT).** Quieter, stranger, more like being left alone with a place. 28 s of hushed pad and frost (video2 0:00 to 0:28); a stride begins at 0:28.1; a dated, funereal voice at 0:42; then a long, steady, determined 120 BPM walk. Nothing resolves. The emotional work is done by one layer slowly brightening (about 24 to 28 dB at 4 to 8 kHz between about 2:50 and 3:50), and then by subtraction: bright layer off at 4:36.8, bass gated into stabs at about 4:48.6, groove stopped at about 5:00.8, picture fading on open water from about 5:02.5. The music reading's phrase is "calm on the surface, pressure underneath".

**Sequence effect (INT).** Part 1 sets up an expectation that sound will "arrive" (the 2:48 sprint) while the picture never shows a finish (only a hand-lettered "FINISH" banner, then a "Reportage" banner and black). Part 2 then does not arrive either, and takes the pulse away on purpose. Watched in order, the second film reads as the answer to the first: Part 1 is the effort, Part 2 is what the effort turns into once the people are gone. I notice this as a viewer's pattern. Whether PSB planned it as a diptych is not established (SPEC; the commission and two-part release are documented in `context.md`, the arc is not).

## 2. The same gesture, done twice

Both pieces contain the same device, and the comparison shows it clearly (OBS):

| | Part 1 | Part 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Key sentence | "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" heard at video1 0:27 to 0:31.7 and again at 2:44 to 2:48.2 | Heard at video2 1:24.0 to 1:28.5 (the only time it is placed against a hole) |
| Hole | Drums out about 0.4 s, bass about 0.65 s, at 2:47.83 (H on timing) | Drums out about 4.3 s, bass about 3.6 s, 1:24.3 to 1:28.6 (H) |
| What follows | A bright, wide sprint, loudest and steadiest, 2:48 to 3:08 | Full four-on-the-floor groove on one downbeat, drums up about 50 dB, no fill (H) |
| Picture at the joint | Whip-pan to the "FINISH" banner (sub-second, 1 fps cannot say more) | A static road shot holds, then a hard cut to the first crowd at about 1:28.5, within about 0.1 s of the drop (dossier, re-checked on frames; I confirmed road at 1:28 and bridge crowd at 1:29 on the contact sheet) |

My reading (INT): in Part 1 the hole is a catch of breath inside a continuing engine. In Part 2 it is a real silence, so the jolt is the biggest event of the piece (every music specialist agrees, music reading section 8). Part 2 spends its drama early (1:28.6) and then has to sustain interest for three and a half minutes with almost no events, while Part 1 distributes its events across the whole running time.

## 3. What lingers

Things that, by the panels' own rankings and by my own weighing, would stay with a listener after both films are over:

1. **Part 2, the long subtraction at the end (video2 4:36.8 to 5:04).** Ducks, floes and open water, the groove dying, a last pad ring-out and a short dry tag (see the discrepancy below), black. It is the strongest "after-image" in either film because it removes people, pulse and picture in order (music reading, interpretation.md section 3). I expect it to linger longest (INT).
2. **Part 1, the bass returning at video1 1:40.** The music reading ranks this first for Part 1: a 24 s absence, then weight. On a laptop it is partly lost; on good speakers it is the moment the whole method becomes felt (M-H).
3. **Part 1, the tea tables at 1:55 to 2:14** and the town-sign run at 2:29 to 2:34. These are the warmest human moments in either film and the only places where voice, picture and place agree within about a second (OBS on timing; "warm" is INT).
4. **Part 2, the first voice at video2 0:42**, because it turns a mood into a dated day after 42 s of pad (interpretation.md moment 3; the top-band jump there is the narrator's own sibilants, not a new instrument, per the check log).
5. **Part 2's voice turning into bar-locked fragments from about 3:28.6** is audible as a change of kind, but I rate it lower: the source of the fragments is unresolved and the bar-lock phase is unresolved (L-M).

What does not linger: no named person in either film (interpretation files, both), no winner, no face held for long. This is a shared property, and I count it as one of the things that makes the pair a pair.

## 4. Which works better, and why

**As a stand-alone piece, Part 1 works better (INT).** Reasons I can tie to data:
- It has more felt events per minute: bass out and in at 1:15 and 1:40, voice exit at 0:31.7, thumps at 2:01 to 2:32, the hole, the cut-off. Part 2's long middle (2:48.8 to 4:36.8) has "no new element for 47 s", then a slow ramp that, by the music reading's own wording, "no single event announces".
- It is shorter, and the narration is interleaved with the band, so facts and feeling alternate (voice-free block 1:27 to 2:15 sits beside the loudest passages, the story is told in the sparse bars).
- Its picture and sound meet in places a viewer can notice (sign-to-name matches at 2:15 to 2:36).

**As a sequel and as half of a diptych, Part 2 works better (INT).** It is the only one that changes what the pair means: it takes the "arrival" Part 1 promised in sound and declines it, and its ending (floes, open water, a stop with nothing resolved) is more memorable than Part 1's cut-off, which the panel itself cannot attribute to the studio or to the video edit (M on feel, L on cause). Part 2's colour and the 28 s prelude also make the strongest first impression of the two.

**Where I hold back.** I cannot say that either film's cut pattern is designed against the music. The measured sync is weak in both. video1: 7.8% of cuts within 80 ms of a beat against 19.7% expected by chance, a beat-phase Rayleigh p of 0.0055 that disappears at the half-beat (p = 0.95) and the bar (p = 0.56), and the interpretation notes that the cut list is provisional. video2: 37.2% within 80 ms against 43% by chance, Rayleigh p 0.82 (beat), 0.89 (half-beat), 0.78 (bar). Both `sync.json` files agree with the panels' "joined at the voice, not co-composed". The best-documented joints are at narration or section edges (video2 0:28.08, 1:28.5, 2:36.6; video1 sign matches), which a viewer feels as coincidence of events, not as beat-locked editing.

**What I would tell a friend (INT).** Watch Part 1 loud and wait for 1:40 and 2:47. Then watch Part 2 straight after and let the first minute be slow; the reward is not a moment but the ending.

## 5. Discrepancies and cautions for the other readers

- **Part 2 ending.** `interpretation.md` (video2) says "one last full-level hit at 5:02.3". The music reading's re-check finds two drum onsets (5:02.341, 5:02.591) and three bass onsets (5:02.341, 5:02.602, 5:02.840) about a quaver apart, and says the earlier "one hit" is superseded. I follow the music reading (H on onsets). The interpretation should be corrected.
- **Part 1 voice exit "on a bar line" (video1 0:31.7).** The music reading states it; interpretation.md says L-M after round 3. I treat it as unconfirmed.
- **Narrator and source.** Part 1 and Part 2 both carry a flat Dutch voice. Whether it is the same narrator, the same film series or even the same archive is not established (`context.md` for video2: Fries Film Archief named, narrator not confirmed). I do not claim continuity of voice.
- **"Rock/guitar" in Part 1** is an inference (music reading section 5); `part_mapping.md` item 4 quotes the CLAP label as if firmer.
- **Playback dependence.** The video1 42.6 Hz thumps largely vanish on small speakers, and the video2 pad and bass dropouts depend on bass reproduction. My ranking of Part 1 first assumes decent speakers.
