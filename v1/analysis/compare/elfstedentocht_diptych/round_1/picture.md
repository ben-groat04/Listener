## 1. The one-paragraph comparison

Same event, same archive source family (Fries Film Archief per `context.md`; reels and narrator unconfirmed for both), same narrator's key figures, but two different kinds of picture. video1 is monochrome (OBS: saturation 0.0 in all 189 per-second samples), a 3-minute collage of print, paperwork, signs, crowds and tea tables, with a fast, short-shot edit (52 listed shots, median 1.6 s), cut around a driving 78 BPM track whose "feeling" is made by bass on/off. video2 is colour throughout (OBS: no per-second sample below 5 on that scale; minute means 59 to 81), longer-held (87 listed shots, median 2.4 s), mostly landscape and pack-from-behind, set to a 120 BPM groove that never changes pulse. video1's picture supplies a world of administration and place names; video2's supplies weather, light and anonymous bodies, and then withdraws them. Both refuse a finish line. INT (M): video1 is the briefing, video2 the walk.

## 3. Colour against black and white

OBS: the two parts differ in format, not just in grade. This is the biggest picture difference and I do not think it is only a restoration choice, but intent is SPEC (we have no source reel information). Facts first:
- video1: zero saturation throughout; brightness mean 103 (0 to 255 scale, per_second.json); contrast is carried by white-out snow against black coats and night crowds. The colour barcode is a greyscale strip: near-black first 40 s, a pale plateau about 44 to 56 s, bright bands at 60 to 76 and 90 to 106 and 129 to 135.
- video2: saturation 59 to 81 in every section; warmth (red minus blue) swings from cold (mean -30 at 0:00 to 1:00) to warm (+30 at 1:00 to 2:00, because of the pink sun at 0:48 and the sepia crowd at 1:30) and back to near neutral (+1, -4, -5) for the rest. The barcode shows blue-grey and lilac early, a peach and cream block about 80 to 125 s, pale lilac-blue about 125 to 150, and a nearly black run about 3:10 to 3:25 (193 to 205 s). That black run is the dark plateau start: per_second brightness falls from about 143 (3:06) to 37 (3:22). The shot list shows several 0.7 to 4 s shots there (OBS).
- Dominant palette words that survive: cold blue/cyan/lilac with warm bursts; red as a spot colour (lamps 2:44; hats 3:36 to 4:34; a flag or cloth about 4:45; dossier motif table, M).

INT (M): black and white makes video1 a document; colour makes video2 an environment. Black and white aligns with the dry newsreel narrator and a track that is all texture and weight; colour aligns with a slower, more electronic track that offers a place to sit in. A caution against over-reading: in video2 the tints change within one reel (mauve to orange at 1:22.1, "tint cycling" 1:40 to 1:50), so warm and cold are partly stock and fade, not necessarily grading (the dossier marks this unresolved, L-M). I treat colour in video2 as atmosphere, not as a signal.

## 4. Editing

OBS (`sync.json`, `shots.json`):
- Average cut rate is nearly identical: 51 cuts in 188.7 s is 2.7 per 10 s (video1); 86 cuts in 305.9 s is 2.8 per 10 s (video2). The difference is distribution and shot length. Listed median shot: 1.62 s (video1) against 2.36 s (video2). Longest listed shot: 30.3 s in video1 (a shot-detector artefact I suspect, but I did not open it; it is probably the 1:28 to 1:49 stretch that the dossier says holds at least six images, so the list is wrong there) against 15.6 s in video2.
- video1 shot list is flagged by the interpretation as missing cuts "in snow above all" (1:27 to 1:49). video2's list misses changes about 2:01, 2:04, 2:08, 4:03, 4:15 and the real cut at about 1:28.56. So every shot-length statistic here is a lower bound on cuts and an upper bound on median length. Do not rely on 1.6 against 2.4 as precise.
- Neither edit is locked to the beat. video1: 7.8% of cuts within 80 ms of a beat against 19.7% chance, though cut phase in the beat is non-random (Rayleigh p 0.0055, about 0.25 s after the beat) and flagged as tracker-dependent; no phase in half-beat or bar. video2: 37.2% within 80 ms against 42.9% chance; phases non-significant (p 0.82, 0.89, 0.78); on the true 120 BPM grid for 1:28.6 to 5:00.7, 34% on beat against 32% chance (the video2 dossier). So both are "cut by image and phrase".
- Both follow loudness weakly: cut rate vs loudness r 0.27 (video1) and 0.23 to 0.27 (video2); motion vs loudness 0.32 and 0.31 to 0.33. Roughly equal, weak.
- Shape of the edit over time. video1 is a rise in motion: 7.3, 10.6, 13.4 (mean motion by minute, arbitrary units, rising as the track proceeds), with cut rates of 7.58 per 10 s at 1:06 to 1:16, 5.43 at 1:49 to 1:59 and 4.34 at 2:39 to 3:08. video2 is a hold-and-flurry arc: 9.7, 6.4, 6.6, 13.4, 11.5 per minute: slow in the middle, fast from 3:00 on. My own count from shots.json (listed shots, caveated): video2 median shot 3.4 s in 1:28 to 2:36 (13 shots), 2.0 s in 2:36 to 4:36 (44 shots), 1.6 s in the last 30 s. I take this as: the voice section is the held part and the voiceless plateau the fast part.
- Tight joints of picture to music (video2): 0:28.08 cut to 0:28.13 bass and drum entry (about 0.05 s, H); cut onto the drop at about 1:28.5 against 1:28.6 (about 0.1 s, after the fact-check); 2:36.6 against the narrator's last word and the loop change at 2:36.1 to 2:36.8 (about 0.2 s, H on times). video1 joints are looser and ambiguous: cut 1:27.16 against the voice exit and wall jump at 1:27.0; cut 1:06.56 against a section boundary at 1:06.4; cuts at 2:01.08 and 2:13.36 against thumps 1 and 3; 2:38.24 against the bass return 2:38.4 (the interpretation says chance overlap is plausible for several). Notably video1 has no cut at the biggest music events, 1:15.3, 1:40.0, 1:55 and 2:47.8 (nearest 2:48.8).

INT (M): video2's editor treats the music's structural joints as places to cut on; video1's editor treats the music's broad energy as a pace and the narration as the anchor (signs within about a second of each spoken town at 2:16 to 2:36, the tightest word-to-picture match in either film). The Sceptic's framing in the video1 interpretation ("two independent constructions joined at a flat voice") fits video1 better than video2; for video2 it holds between 0:28 and 2:36 but fails at the three joints above.

## 6. How each video's visuals serve its track

**video1.** The music is built by subtraction on a fixed pulse (bass out at 1:15.3, in at 1:40.0, six sub thumps 2:01 to 2:32, a 0.4 s hole at 2:47.8). The picture does not mirror those events; it supplies a different strand, content and warmth. Where the music thins (1:55 to 2:14, width 0.2 to 0.3) the picture goes indoors to tea and faces; where it re-ignites with the narrator's town list the picture delivers signs. The match is thematic and at the level of broad shape (the 7.58 cuts per 10 s at 1:06 to 1:16 sits on the loudness peak at about 1:07). The ending is the clearest asymmetry: the music has a bright, wide release plateau at 2:48 to 3:08, the picture has a hand-lettered FINISH and a banner, never a crossing. INT (M): the sound promises an arrival that the picture is structurally unable to give, because a film made of signs "has signs" (Film critic's phrasing in the interpretation). Whether intended is not decidable.

**video2.** The music is a long fixed pulse, brightening, then dismantled (drop 1:28.6, bright layer off 4:36.8, bass gated 4:48.55, stop 5:00.8, one last hit 5:02.3). The picture follows with a similar subtraction, in order: people thin out from about 4:52 to ducks and floes, sound stops (5:00.8), picture fades (about 5:02.5). It is the most complete music-picture agreement in the pair, and the interpretation's list is the same: layer, low end, people, sound, picture. The picture before 2:36 is voice-led, after it brightness-led: the shot rate and image motion rise with the plateau (13.4 in minute 4 against 6.6 in minute 3, OBS). Where the music has a "trance" (2:48.8 to 3:24.8, "nothing new happens for about 47 s") the picture offers tractors, lights and a light leak, so the trance is a visual blur as well as an audio sameness (INT).

## 7. Moments I would flag as the "picture's own"

- video2 1:20.0 to 1:28.5: the long road with footprints, cut to the crowd on the drop. Open, as the interpretation notes, whether the shot placement is designed.
- video2 2:36.6: the stock change to a rime road seen from a vehicle, on the end of the narration. This is the only moment the picture is clearly "after the voice".

- video1 2:47 to 2:49: whip-pan, the closing sentence, the hole, FINISH within a second (not a mirrored event).

## 8. Disagreements I have with the existing analysis, and limits

1. video1's dossier says the shot at 1:28 to 1:49 is "the slowest cutting in the film" (0.46 cuts per 10 s). The interpretation withdraws it ("holds at least six images; the picture changes nearly every second from 1:34"). I agree. My own listed-shot counts for that window are useless (a single listed shot); I report that rather than a number.
2. video2's dossier text in section 4 still says "picture does not mark" the hole and a cut at 1:30.3 lands 1.7 s after the drop; the interpretation's fact-check refutes this (hard cut about 1:28.5). The dossier table has not been corrected. Use the interpretation.
3. video1 interpretation section 1 says "no one is seen crossing a visible line at 1 fps"; fine, but at 1 fps a missed instant is possible. Both films are 1 fps-sampled for most claims; claims about "nobody" are claims about the sampled frames.
4. The warm/cold readings for video2 rest on saturation and red-minus-blue per-second stats; a lilac light cast is read as cold by the metric. These are image-colour statistics, not a judgment of grading.
5. I opened two contact sheets and the two barcodes, not all 26. Everything else is carried from the analysis files; where I repeat it, it is tagged as theirs.

2. **To listener:** because video1 is black and white and the track is the loud, fast one, while video2 is in colour and the track is the "calmer, more electronic" one, would a first-time viewer who watched them in order feel the colour arrive as relief, or feel video2's ending (floes, no people) as heavier because of the colour?
