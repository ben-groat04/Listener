## 1. Answers to questions put to me

### 1.1 Music: after Part 1, is Part 2's 28 s of pad and thuds relief or a restart?

I cannot report hearing it, so this is a reasoned expectation (INT, M-L).

- **In level, relief.** Part 1 ends at its loudest and brightest (2:49 to 3:07; centroid about 2.9 kHz per the music colleague), and Part 2 opens about 15 dB lower (-25.5 dBFS) with no drums until 0:28.1 (OBS, `per_second_levels`, music reading). A drop of that size is the usual shape of an exhale.
- **In content, restart.** The date is spoken again at video2 0:41.7, and every later line re-runs in the same order (story table). The key and tempo change as well (D pedal at 78 BPM; B pedal at 120).
- **My answer:** the first 28 s are felt as relief (the ear gets quiet), and the 0:41.7 date is felt as a restart (the telling begins again). I expect the listener to move from the first to the second inside the first minute of Part 2. That is a sharper sequence experience than I described in round 1, where I read Part 2 as "what the effort turns into once the people are gone". That sentence is too strong now. It treats Part 2 as chronologically after Part 1, and the script shows it is a second telling of the same day. I keep the weaker form: Part 2 is the version in which the people are taken away at the end (4:36.8 to 5:04), not the version that comes after the race.

### 1.2 Picture: does colour arrive as relief, or does the end of video2 feel heavier because of colour? Does my reading depend on colour?

- **Does my reading depend on colour?** Partly, and I should say how. My round-1 phrase "left alone with a place" rests mostly on the music (28 s of pad, then a long pulse that does not resolve) and on the empty images (frost, the lane to the horizon, ducks and floes). Colour adds to it (rose-brown water, the cold-to-warm swing the picture colleague measured), but I would expect the music alone to carry "alone with a place" (INT, M). Colour is secondary, not the cause.
- **The new evidence makes colour more than relief.** Because the pack-from-behind material exists in both films, the viewer who watches in order meets bib 170 in grey at video1 1:05 and in blue at video2 3:34 (R2 check above). Expected effect (SPEC; no viewer test, and a viewer must notice a 1 fps-scale detail in a moving shot): a flicker of recognition, "I have seen this skater", and a sense that the colour is the thing as it was, and the grey a handling of it. This is the one place where I would say the colour is a meaningful design fact of the pair and not only a feature of the reel. The picture colleague adds the caution that desaturation is the economical reading but the direction of copying is undeterminable; I agree (M-L).
- **Heavier ending because of colour?** I do not think colour is what makes the ending heavy. What makes it heavy, I expect, is the order of subtractions: bright layer off (4:36.8), bass gated (4:48.6), groove cut (5:00.8), picture still going to about 5:02.5 (music reading; picture round 1). A colour image of open water with floes may be gentler than a monochrome one, if anything: the rose water at 4:52 to 5:02 is a warm tone, which sits oddly against the stopped pulse, and I think that mismatch (warm picture, cold silence) is part of why it lingers (SPEC; one reaction I cannot test).

### 1.3 Story: do the video2 fragments (3:28.6 to 4:37) sound like the town names, one per bar?

I cannot hear Dutch or audio, so I cannot settle it. Three things I can add:

- The music colleague's structure test is inconclusive (R2 `music.md` 1.2): 28 of 37 bars have the vocal onset within 0.05 s of the bar start, a faint repeat at an 11-bar lag (0.19) against continuity at lag 1 (0.45). The 34 bars against 33 (three passes of 11) is a coincidence I would not use.
- The picture colleague's check removes the visual echo: no town sign, route sign or newspaper in video2 3:20 to 4:39 (R2 `picture.md`, 80 frames). So any return of the names is in the audio only.
- **What I would need (INT):** one native Dutch listener, playing video1 2:16 to 2:36 and then video2 3:25 to 3:50 once each, and writing down the syllables they hear at each bar start before being told the hypothesis. I would count the hypothesis supported only if at least about half of the bar-start syllables match the route order (Sneek, IJlst, Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward). I would count it as failed if they hear nothing word-like. Until then, the Whisper strings in the story note ("eerst", "slop", "stapen", "in de lopen") are pattern-matches on noise-prone ASR, as the story reader's own caveat says (M-L at best).

I would also point out an inconsistency a listener would feel at once if the hypothesis were true: Part 1 gives the names at about 1.8 s per name and in a clear voice, and Part 2 would give them at 2.0 s with the voice chopped. A listener who recognised them would experience the second as a ghost of the first. If the hypothesis is false, the fragments are, as the music colleague says, texture. The two outcomes feel very different, so this is the most valuable open test for the sequence experience.

## 2. Building on and challenging colleagues

**Music: "same words, no evidence of the same take" (round 2, 1.3).** Accepted. The cross-correlations (0.063 to 0.091 against a 0.079 control) are at control level. For a listener the difference is small: a human hears the same flat voice saying the same lines, and the lines recur in the same order with longer gaps in Part 2. Whether it is one take or a re-recording is invisible to the ear unless the performance is audibly identical, which I cannot test. I do not rate this as a perceptual matter; it matters for provenance only.

**Music: withdraws "hole as signature" to "the same stage direction used twice" (round 2, 1.3).** I accept the withdrawal of "rehearsal", since I proposed it as a question and the evidence does not support it. But I would defend one thing on perception grounds. The refrain sentence is heard three times and a hole follows two of them. A listener does not need an authored arc to feel a habit: after the second time they would expect a break when they hear "dat is de Elfstedentocht". In Part 2 the first time they hear it (video2 1:24) it is a surprise; in the pair as played in order they have already met the 0.4 s hole at video1 2:47.8, so the 4 s hole is a bigger version of something half-learned. That is an experience claim about sequence (INT, M-L), not a claim about design. It is weakened by the 0.4 s length: a 0.4 s hole may not register as a hole on laptop speakers.

**Music: video1's cluster at a fixed offset from the beat (round 2, 1.1).** Good catch, and it changes how I read video1's edit. If the cuts sit about 0.245 s after the tracker beat (39 cuts, R 0.365, p 0.0055; no multiple-testing correction), a viewer might feel the edit as "behind the beat" in a steady way, or the tracker may be a quarter beat out. I have no way of telling these apart by eye. From the point of view of a first-time viewer it is irrelevant, because 0.25 s is within the window where cut and beat feel joined. I would not say video1's edit is more beat-aware (M-L), only that the "7.8 % on the beat" headline is misleading in the direction of "uncorrelated".

**Picture: "the sprint's picture is Part 2's plateau footage" (R2, 0.2).** This revises my round-1 statement that the picture never arrives. At video1 about 2:55 to 3:02 the picture is hand-held race footage at speed, under the loudest music, between the FINISH banner (2:48) and the "Reportage" banner (3:07). I accept it. I should correct my section-2 table in round 1: in Part 1 the hole is followed by a banner, then within seconds by actual skating. The sense that the film "withholds a finish" survives (nobody crosses a line, at 1 fps), but the picture and sound do meet briefly in effort. Part 1's last 20 s is therefore the one place where I would expect the listener to feel picture and music pulling the same way (INT, M), and Part 2 then spends two minutes on that same kind of footage (video2 3:09 to 4:36). From a viewer's chair this makes Part 2's plateau a **return to the part of Part 1 that was over too soon**. That is a stronger statement of the pair's relation than any I made in round 1, and it rests on picture evidence.

**Picture: "anonymity may come from camera position, not from the edit" (R2, section 2).** This is a good challenge to the story reader and I side with picture, with one addition. What a viewer feels is anonymity, whatever the cause. The same camera operator behind the skater explains both why no face is held and why bib numbers are the only identity. But the same bibs reappear in both films, so a viewer who notices 170 and 157 twice may start to treat them as characters. That would be an accidental protagonist: a numbered back, not a person (SPEC). It does not contradict the "anonymous effort" reading; it adds a mild pull against it.

**Story: "pair rises then falls: effort then withdrawal" (section 4.3).** With one script told twice, "rises then falls across the pair" is a loudness story only (Part 1 flat at about -12.5 dBFS and cut off loud; Part 2 climbing about 15 dB, then taken apart). I would write "the pair is two loudness shapes, not one arc". That fits music's round-2 conclusion and removes my own round-1 "sequence effect" paragraph as stated.

**Story: "the same list reads as a census in one and a last word in the other" (section 5).** I like this, and I can add a listener's sharpening. The 575/9,500 line arrives in Part 1 at video1 1:19.7 into a bass-less float (the bass is out from 1:15.3 to 1:40.0), so the numbers are heard in a thin, exposed space. In Part 2 they end the voice at video2 2:36.1 and the loop changes 0.7 s later. In both the numbers are the most exposed fact, but in Part 1 the exposure is the thinness of the mix and in Part 2 it is the change after the last word. These are two different felt effects from one sentence (INT, M).

## 3. Which works better? Updated

- **Stand-alone:** I still say Part 1, for the same reasons (events spread through the running time, narration and band alternating, sign-to-name matches at 2:16 to 2:36). New point against my own round 1: Part 1's strongest picture-and-music agreement is its last 20 s, which a viewer who tires of the thin middle might not reach with full attention. That is a risk for Part 1, not for Part 2 (SPEC).
- **As a pair:** I now say Part 2 works as the version that gives the weight to the footage and to the ending, and Part 1 works as the version that gives the facts. Neither is a finish. The pair is better than either alone because the two tellings differ in medium (grey and colour), tempo, voice density and ending, while using the same sentences and the same pool of shots. Viewing in order lets recognition do the work (the same words, the same back, a different weather). Viewing in reverse would lose the recognition of the already-heard script, but would gain the pleasure of the 28 s opening followed by the dense Part 1 (SPEC; I would suggest the team does not push this).
- **What lingers (revised).** Unchanged at the top: Part 2's long subtraction (video2 4:36.8 to 5:04). New second place: bib 170 in grey and then in blue, if noticed (a small, private recognition and the only moment where the pair visibly touches). The Part 1 bass return at 1:40 stays strong for good speakers only.
