## 0. Headline: the two films share footage (R2 check, H)

| 1:08 | 3:30 to 3:31 | Bib 145, bobble hat, group of bent skaters ahead (same framing). |

Method: I matched 1 fps frames by greyscale correlation (top matches r 0.95 for the 3:00 and 3:02 pairs, next-best 0.59 and 0.72), then opened every claimed pair by eye. Other high scores were dark or snowy frames and I rejected them (e.g. video1 2:23 against video2 0:28, which are merely pale). Bib 157 also appears at video1 1:21 (registration, standing, white hat) and video2 3:57 to 3:58 (skating): same rider, different shot (M). I did not check every second, so more overlaps are likely (the match is by crude 1 fps correlation, so recall is low).

Consequences:
1. **Colour is not a property of two different archive reels.** The pack-from-behind material exists in colour and was used in monochrome in video1 (INT, M-H: either a desaturated copy or a b/w print; I cannot tell which direction the copy runs, but the colour source obviously carries more information, so desaturation is the economical reading). Part 1's black and white is therefore at least partly a **treatment**, not a given. That answers the listener's Q2 and my own round-1 caution: colour against b/w is a design choice, or at least a delivery choice, for this material. It is not established for video1's other footage (signs, newspaper, registration table, the control hut), which I cannot show was ever in colour (L).
2. **video1's closing "sprint" picture is video2's plateau footage.** At 3:00 to 3:02 video1 shows the same hand-held pack-from-behind shots that dominate video2 from 3:09 to 4:36. I said in round 1 that video1's picture gives a sign, not a crossing, at the sprint. Revise: from about 2:55 (skaters with hands clasped behind, sheet_09) to 3:02 the picture is the race itself, at speed, hand-held, against the loudest music. The FINISH banner (2:48) and the "Reportage" banner (3:07) bracket it. So video1's last 20 s is the one place its picture and track both go to "effort" together (INT, M), and it is the footage video2 then spends two minutes on.
3. INT (M): the pair is not two films of two different days of material, but one pool of footage cut twice: Part 1 sequences it as document (print, desk, signs, then a short burst of race), Part 2 as experience (the burst, extended). Whether Part 1 reuses Part 2's shots or the reverse is undeterminable. This strengthens the story colleague's "one script, two films" (story section 2) into "one script, one footage pool, two films". I would put it first among the diptych's findings.
