## 1. Answers to questions put to me

### 1.1 Picture Q2: does the same image (bib 157, video1 3:00 to 3:08; video2 3:48 to 3:58) feel faster or slower under Part 1 than under Part 2, and which makes it effort, which routine?

I cannot report hearing; this is reasoned from the shots and the music measurements.

- **The picture is nearly identical.** Shot 46 in Part 1 (179.04 to 181.40, 2.36 s) and shot 62 in Part 2 (230.56 to 232.80, 2.24 s): same length to within 0.12 s (R3 check, OBS). So any difference in felt speed is not from the cut. It is the track.
- **Part 1 (78 BPM tracker grid, drums constant, narrator gone, loudest and brightest run of the film).** Expectation: **effort** (INT, M). The image arrives after the FINISH banner (2:48) and the hole (2:47.8) and carries the film's hardest sound; the music says "the point of it all" as the picture cuts to the race. But it is also two seconds, then a different skater (the headband at 3:02), then a banner at 3:07: the effort is glimpsed, not lived in.
- **Part 2 (120 BPM, brightening layer, 72 s plateau from 3:24.8 to 4:36.8).** Expectation: **routine, as a good thing** (INT, M-L). The same back, sat in a steady groove and a long state, would read as a regular pull, the kind of image that settles into a rhythm. Because the plateau is one groove (music round 3, 1.2), nothing in the sound marks 3:51 as special; the image is one of many.
- **Faster or slower?** Part 1's music is slower in tempo, but its image comes under the film's densest sound, so I would expect it to feel more urgent; Part 2's is twice the beat rate, and so should feel quicker in pulse but calmer in event (INT, L-M). Two answers which pull apart are an honest sign that the question needs a viewer test, not another reading. A cheap test: show ten people the two clips with sound, no context, and ask "which skater is working harder?" and "which is further into the race?".

### 1.2 Story Q: which is more noticeable to a viewer, the repeated shots or the repeated script?

I agree with picture and story: the script (INT, M-L). Reasons I can add from the sequence:
- The script returns at a **known point** (video2 0:41.7 "18 januari 1963") after a title board and 28 s of pad, so the viewer has had time to forget the start and is then told it again. The shots return in unmarked moving footage lasting 1.4 to 3.5 s each (R3 check above), under music.
- The shared shots all sit where the narration is silent (story R3, table), so they are never in the channel where the viewer is attending to meaning. Recognition happens as a flicker, if at all.
- One qualification for the in-order viewer: Part 2 holds bib 157 (3:51 to 3:58, same rider in a second shot) and bib 170 (3:34 to 3:35) in a long pack run, so the second encounter is better placed to be noticed than the first, as picture says (2.5). A viewer who notices the first bib only on the second film would have to go back to find it.

### 1.3 Music: does the 0.4 s Part 1 hole register on laptop speakers?

I stand by "may not". A 0.4 s drop, at the end of a long flat stretch, after which the title sentence ends and a banner appears (picture 2:48), will be covered in most listeners' attention by the banner. I would call a deliberate pause a **possible** reading, not the likely one for a first hearing (INT, L-M). The 4 s Part 2 hole is a different matter: it holds an empty road and then the first crowd lands on the downbeat, about 0.1 s apart (picture round 2), which is hard to miss.

## 3. Which works better? Final

- **Stand-alone, I choose Part 1** (INT, M). It has events spread through its running time: bass out at 1:15.3 and back at 1:40.0, the town list at 2:16 to 2:36, the banner and burst at 2:48 to 3:07. It ends at its loudest, which gives it a shape, and it is 3:09. Its risks are a long thin middle (bass absent 1:15.3 to 1:40.0, 28 % of the film at below -50 dB on music's count) and an abrupt end on a banner.
- **As a pair, I choose Part 2 as the one that stays** (INT, M-L). It has the long subtraction at the end (4:36.8 to 5:04), the cleanest audiovisual joint (the 4 s hole and the cut onto the drop, 1:24.3 to 1:28.6), and the probable bar-lock of its cuts. It would not make sense without Part 1's facts, so it is the second reading, not the first.
- **Why Part 1 wins alone and Part 2 wins after it:** Part 1 tells; Part 2 lets the viewer already know what is being told. A first-time viewer needs the telling. A second-time viewer needs the room. That is the one reason I would defend for the order (INT, M-L).
- **What lingers.** First, the long subtraction at the end of Part 2. Second, the small private recognition of bib 170, grey then blue, but only for a viewer who notices it (SPEC, L-M). Third, Part 1's bass return at 1:40.0, but only on speakers that carry it.

## 5. Settled view in five bullets

1. **One script, one footage pool, two scores.** The pair keeps the words (same order from the date, video2 0:41.7), the shots (bibs 170, 145, 157 and the headband skater, b/w in Part 1, colour in Part 2, nearly the same order but at very different spacings) and changes the music, tempo and treatment. For the viewer the pair is a demonstration that the sound decides what the same material means (INT, M-H on the facts, M on the meaning).
2. **Part 1 works better alone; Part 2 works better after it.** Part 1 is eventful, ends at its loudest and gives the facts; Part 2 gives the room (28 s pad, the 4 s hole at 1:24.3, the long plateau, the subtraction at 4:36.8 to 5:04). Neither shows an arrival, so the pair feels like a run-up and a dissolving, not a finish (INT, M).
3. **What lingers is a subtraction, not a picture.** The end of Part 2 (bright layer off, bass gated, groove cut, a hit at 5:02.3) is the one passage I would expect to stay in a viewer, and it rests on the order of removals, not on colour (INT, M; no one has heard it).
4. **The pictures are better tied to Part 2's track than to Part 1's.** Part 2's cuts cluster just before bar starts and it cuts onto the drop at 1:28.5; Part 1 has two beat-rate flurries with a lag and no cut at its three biggest sound events. Colour and the shared shots are real, but secondary. (M-L: post hoc, within detector error.)
5. **What cannot be settled without ears and a Dutch speaker.** Whether the Part 2 fragments are town names (structure leans against), whether the Part 1 hole registers, whether the relief or the restart is the first felt, and whether the same image feels like effort in Part 1 and routine in Part 2. A small viewer test, with ten people and no context, would answer the last two (SPEC).
