## 3. What the pair covers, the arc, and why it is split (final)

**What each part covers (OBS, from the dossiers and the script table).**
- Part 1 (3:09): the event as a record. Print (0:04 to 0:26), date and framing (0:10), "twelfth" and "the hell of 1963", organisation (registration, tea, control hut, 1:18 to 2:17), the route in order as signs (2:16 to 2:36, eleven names), a crowd and a FINISH banner (2:38 to 2:49), a burst of racing (2:55 to 3:02), the "Reportage" banner and black (3:07).
- Part 2 (5:04): the event as an experience. A title board and 28 s of pad with frost and snow before anyone is seen (0:00 to 0:41), the same script restated (0:41.7 to 2:36.1), then 2:00 of pack, crowd and landscape under a rising plateau with no new facts (2:36.8 to 4:36.8), then subtraction to open water (4:36.8 to 5:04).

**The arc from one to the next.** It is not an arc in the story sense. The script restarts (video2 0:41.7), the key and tempo reset, and the footage is shared. What continues is footage and level, in one place only: Part 1's last 20 s at video1 2:55 to 3:02 and Part 2's long middle (picture), and Part 1's ending at its loudest against Part 2's opening 15 dB lower (music). Those are real but small. I would say: **continuous in material, restarting in narration, re-set in music.**

**Why split this way (INT, SPEC where marked).**
1. A two-sided single: Part 1 as A side, Part 2 as B side (MusicBrainz; H on the release, SPEC on motive). The shorter, louder rock track as A and the longer electronic one as B is the usual single pattern, and the press said so (`part_mapping.md`). This is the only reason with a document behind it.
2. The same material for two jobs: tell the day compactly, then let the viewer stay in it (INT, M-L). The shared pool and shared script make this more plausible than I could say in round 1, because someone chose to say the same thing twice.
3. A format effect (lengths dictated by sides) with no story reason: not excluded (SPEC, L).
The files do not say which, and I would not choose between 1 and 2 without a source on how the two were commissioned.

**What the whole says that one part does not.**
- The meaning is carried by the treatment, not the content. With script and footage held constant, the register changes entirely with sound, pace and colour (b/w against colour for at least the pack material, picture sec 0). Neither alone shows that (INT, M-H).
- The event is shown from two sides and finished from neither: Part 1 lists the route and ends on a banner and black, Part 2 withdraws to water; no film shows the crossing or the 127 finishers (`elfstedentocht_1963.md`; round 1 sec 4) (OBS on the absence, INT, M on the meaning).
- The account is anonymous in both, by script and by camera position; the recurring bibs give the viewer a faint "character" without a name (SPEC).
- Hindsight (the viewer's knowledge that this was the last such winter for 22 years) is not in either film as an observed property; it is what a Dutch viewer would bring (`context.md`; round 2 limits).

## 6. Settled view (5 bullets)

- **One account, told twice.** Same script (the date at video1 0:10 and video2 0:41.7, then the same sentences in the same order, Part 1 with one extra "twelfth" line), same footage pool (four verified shared shots, shared bibs 170, 145, 157), different music (D at 78 BPM, B at 120). So the pair is a **second telling in a different register**, not Part 1 then Part 2 in time (H on the script and shots, M on the reading).
- **Document, then experience, as a tendency.** Part 1 leans on print, desks and signs and ends on a banner; Part 2 leans on pack, crowd and landscape and ends on water. The split is by emphasis, not by exclusive footage (M).
- **What continues is small: footage and loudness.** Part 1's last 20 s (video1 2:55 to 3:02) is the same kind of footage Part 2 spends two minutes on, and both reach their loudest near their ends; nothing else (key, tempo, riff, narration) links them (M-H).
- **Neither film arrives.** The route is listed and then dissolved; the finish and finishers are in the sources and in neither film. The pair is about the run-up and the effort, not the result (OBS on the absence, INT, M on its meaning). Anonymity of the account is an editorial choice; anonymity of the skaters is mostly camera position.
- **Why split: a two-sided release is the only documented reason; the pair also works as one account given two jobs (INT, M-L).** The town-list in Part 2, the hole as signature and the "rise then fall" arc are not supported and are out of the settled view; a Dutch listener and a viewer test are what would change it.
