# Style guide: writing about music like a person

Sources: the corpus in `corpus/` (text.md, forum.md, review.md) and our own pieces (`../video1/LISTENING.md`, `../video2/LISTENING.md`, `video1_human.md`, `video2_human.md`). The corpus is small and mostly fragments, so treat the numbers below as rough.

The user's verdict on our pieces: "such ai phrasing trying too hard". That is the problem to fix. The facts are mostly fine. The prose is performing.

## 1. How real people write, by register

### Short text or tweet (one line, a few words)
- Often under 15 words. Fragments are normal: "Unbelievable,", "sza is BACK you guys", "LANA DEL REY ATE".
- Slang and in-group words, no explanation: "gagged", "bop", "hit different", "served".
- Reaction, not description. Nobody says what the sound is like. They say what it did to them or what they want: "she literally could run me over and I'd apologise", "me applying to be the horse in the next sza music video".
- The music is named by a point, not a feature: "the transition in A&W", "it still hit the same". Never "the interplay of".
- Complaints are small and practical: "Why is the song so short?", "I almost forgot what songs over 4 minutes sounded like".
- Personal fact first, feeling second, and flat: "I'm 56 and cried like a baby." The comment about a dead father is four plain sentences with a date and a place. No adjectives doing work.
- Humour is a throwaway: self-deprecating, deliberately silly. No setup and punchline structure.
- Ends where it runs out. "Hope he can hear this song from where he is." "I actually like Levels".

### Forum post (a paragraph or three)
- Mixed lengths. Some sentences run on with commas and "and"; others are five words. Typical: a long rambling sentence, then "Doesn't matter at all if I like the song or the message."
- Starts from the poster's own situation ("A Spotify playlist has led me to...", "Saw them last night at Rock City (sixth time now)"), not from the music.
- Digressions are allowed and unpolished: route in via krautrock, a gig, the support act ("Palace are now my new favourites! Stunning."), the Fire in the Cockpit fact they did not know.
- Imprecise on purpose: "some kind of", "I guess", "I don't know why but", "It's really hard to explain", "pretty damn good", "ace", "cracking".
- When the music is named it is by title or band or a loose thing: "that male voice choir was ace", "the use of old audio newsreel being played over with fitting and awesome music", "that driving motorik beat". Almost no terms of craft. Nobody says "a bass that is gated for four seconds".
- One image per post at most, and it is usually a cheap one: "as if they turned a sunrise into music". It is said once and then dropped. The one post that stacks images (p7eaven's "steel-grey cloud of permanent knowing", "a mock and a hardened place") is a joke, and it is a sarcastic parody of that style.
- Feelings are stated in plain words ("I'll tear up at the stupidest things", "goosebumps every time") and then qualified or admitted as embarrassing. Not elaborated.
- Honest about boredom and limits: "I haven't got the patience", "I've still not really got into Every Valley yet".
- Typos and rough edges stay in. Trailing comma, lower case, "agessss".
- Endings: a question to the thread ("Any other fans on here?"), a recommendation, or just the last thing they thought of. Never a moral.

### Short review (blog, user review site)
- Opens with context or a position: "I wasn't entirely sure what to expect when this debut album ... popped into my inbox", "So what's the problem? If you ask me, it's the first two tracks."
- Judgement is explicit and small: "an absolute blinder", "a little bit naff", "confusingly stagnant", "doubles down on a surprisingly enduring formula". Evaluative words are everyday ones.
- Comparisons are to other bands, not to nature or furniture: Lemon Jelly, Ghost Box, Bowie's Berlin albums, Thomas Dolby.
- Specific named things: track titles, the sample's source film ("First of The Few", David Niven), line-up, where they saw it. Facts show the reviewer did their homework, and are delivered flatly.
- The review admits what it missed: "I missed them playing in Bolton by a week or so", "I'm certain that the live dimension would've enhanced my feelings".
- Gripes are concrete and a bit petty: the opener replays snippets of songs you are about to hear, a track was already on an earlier EP.
- Sentences are medium to long, with parentheses and dashes, and a bit of lazy padding ("if more intense", "in some ways"). They are not polished.
- Music is described in one clause when at all: "skittering between electro beats and indie guitar", "pulsating, nervous build-up". Then "let the silence fill 'the longest wait' and then return for an explosive, triumphant finale" is about the most poetic it gets.
- Endings vary. Usually a one-line hedged verdict ("one more solid entry", "who knows, maybe the next one will take place literally on the Moon") or a plain recommendation. Never a closing aphorism.

### Across all registers
- Specific on facts (dates, band names, places), vague on sound.
- Few metaphors, mostly dead ones, almost never two in a row.
- Personal context does most of the work (age, father, gig number, route in via another band).
- Nobody explains the structure of the piece. They name one thing they liked or disliked.
- Weak, offhand endings.
- Short is normal. A real listener's whole reaction is often under 100 words.

## 2. The tells in our pieces

Quotes are from `video1/LISTENING.md` (V1) and `video2/LISTENING.md` (V2), and the `_human.md` drafts (H1, H2). None of these is fact-wrong. They are all written, not said.

### Metaphor and simile in every paragraph
- V1: "like a long march, or of a factory heard from the pavement outside, something enormous going on whether or not you are paying attention." Two comparisons stacked for one sound.
- V1: "as if a door had been pushed back." H2 uses "like a door kicked open on a quiet house". The same door appears in three pieces.
- V1: "like a stair that is not there when your foot comes down." / "like a hand laid flat on your chest after you had got used to nothing there." / "like a heartbeat heard through a wall." Each paragraph gets its own.
- V2: "like being towed", "like someone had been in a room with you for a long time without speaking, and then leaves", "like something being taken away". A human uses one such line in a whole review, if that.

### Manufactured bodily sensations
- V1: "I came out of it braced, the way you are after standing in a wind for a while."
- V1: "like a hand laid flat on your chest".
- V2: "I actually sat back." "I was leaning towards the screen." "I came out of this one braced and a little hollow." The writer did not sit back; no one was watching. Every one of these is a gesture added for effect. Real people say "goosebumps" or "I teared up" once, flatly, or not at all.

### Tricolons and rhythmic lists
- V1: "no tune to hum and no chorus to wait for."
- V1: "a registration table, hands writing on forms, the voice giving figures". Lists of three are everywhere in the picture descriptions.
- V1: "relentless and a bit stubborn", "brave more than beautiful".
- V2: "Skaters on dark glossy ice, farmhouses, a telegraph post, low light".
- H1: "a fixed pulse, layers arriving one at a time" and "a cold, relentless pulse, a voice that sounds like history, and a grey crowd".

### "Not X, but Y" and "X more than Y"
- V1: "Not sad, not lifted. Tired for the people on screen."
- V1: "Not louder so much as heavier."
- V1: "I would call it brave more than beautiful."
- V1: "The music promises an ending and the film steps round it."
- V2: "the feeling has to come from you", "it did not change what I felt", "the music is doing that, not the pictures."
- H1: "the payoff is weight, not noise", "build by taking things away rather than adding them". Forum writers do not define a thing by what it is not.

### Aphoristic or neat closing lines
- V1: "I would call it brave more than beautiful." then "But when the bass came back after that thin wait, and in the hush of the bench with its cups and slow thuds, it got to me." A balanced verdict plus an emotional landing.
- V2: "But I would sit through the middle again for the room going quiet at the road, and for the knocks at the end."
- V2 headline: "Nothing happens, and I couldn't look away."
- V2 earlier: "It felt less like an ending than like something being taken away." / "The ice was not a floor any more."
- V1: "The ending itself is a shrug."
- H1: "A calm man reading the facts over an engine that will not ease off is the feeling of the whole piece." Real endings stop mid-thought.

### "I will admit" and performed candour
- V2: "I will admit that for a stretch in the middle I was bored." Bored is fine; announcing it as a confession is a tell.
- V2: "I did not like it all the way through; the middle asked a lot of me." The balanced concession before the positive verdict.
- V2: "I did not know that while watching, and it did not change what I felt." Tidy disclaimer posing as honesty.
- V1: "I found I liked that more than I expected." / "the thing I did not expect." A repeated move: expectation set, then reversed.
- V1: "It was the one place I felt tenderness, and it caught me off guard in something this hard-faced."

### Over-balanced paragraphs
- Nearly every paragraph has the same shape: scene description, then one metaphor, then an emotion, then a turn. V1 "The first moment that stayed with me is a small one..." and "Then the thing I did not expect..." both follow it.
- Each one is complete and can stand alone. Forum posts are lumpy; these are even.
- The piece goes in order from the start to the end, with a verdict at the close. People mention two or three things and stop.

### Literary or craft vocabulary
- "litany", "cadence", "hairline flicker", "ridge of windblown dunes", "rime on their clothes", "hunched figure", "the hush of the bench".
- H1/H2 and VERDICT already list: "on the one", "no fill", "held sound", "subtraction", "taught", "the wall". Also "payoff" and "landing" as structural terms.
- The words an ordinary person uses for the same things: "the bit where it goes quiet", "then the bass kicks back in", "the voice".

### Performed sincerity and precision
- Fine sensory detail that nobody would remember after one watch: "a hazy windmill, a wooden platform, a telegraph pole", "a figure in a long coat walked away across blue ice", "mouth open ... swigging from a bottle".
- Frame-by-frame sequences read as a log, not a memory.
- The timecode-style and number-heavy claims ("sixteen degrees below zero", "575 racers and about 9,500 tour skaters") are fine as facts, but are delivered with a hedge ("sounded like") that is itself a pattern.

### Too-neat structure (H1, H2)
- Headings ("The front page", "First listen, forward in time", "Who hears what", "What the picture does to the music", "Where it sits"), a bold "The line.", and personas. The VERDICT notes already condemn the personas, the review-blurb opener, the survey ending ("Two questions for you") and the committee scaffolding.
- Titles that are themselves lines: "Cold, and a banner that says FINISH", "Nothing happens, and I couldn't look away".

### Other patterns
- Even tone from start to finish. No irritation, no joke, no aside about the writer's own day, no mention of what they were doing when they watched. The human corpus is nearly all personal context.
- No comparison to other music at all, or only to generic things (march, factory). Real listeners say "reminds me of Neu!" or "like Lemon Jelly".
- The writer never gets anything wrong or forgets something. Humans say "I think it was a bridge" or "can't remember".

## 3. Checklist (a piece must pass all of it)

1. **Length.** Does it match the register? A text is one to three lines; a forum post is one to three short paragraphs; a short review is 150 to 300 words. If it is longer, cut moments until it fits. Two or three moments, not a walkthrough.
2. **Start.** Does it begin with where the writer was, a reaction, or a plain opinion, not a scene description or a headline?
3. **Similes.** At most one comparison in the whole piece, and it names a thing (another band, a film, a song) or a plain object. No stacked comparisons, no comparison that has appeared in an earlier piece (door, hand on chest, heartbeat, stair).
4. **Body.** No sitting back, leaning forward, braced, hollow, hand on the chest, breath held. A body word is allowed once, only if it is the true reaction (goosebumps, bored, smiled) and it is stated flat.
5. **Patterns.** Search the text for "not X, but Y", "X more than Y", "less like ... than like", "I did not expect", "I will admit", "I found I". Remove all of them. Also search for lists of three and break them to two or four, or one.
6. **Ending.** Does it stop on a plain fact, a small gripe, a recommendation or a trailing thought? There must be no moral, no summary verdict that balances the good against the bad, and no line that sounds good read aloud.
7. **Balance.** Is it allowed to be lopsided? One paragraph can be long and the next two words. At least one thing is not explained, one thing is vague ("some bit near the end"), and one admission is blunt without being framed.
8. **Words.** No craft terms (cadence, subtraction, held, payoff, wall, litany). No fine sensory inventory. Use the words a friend would: "bit", "kicks in", "goes quiet", "voice".
9. **Specific facts, vague sound.** Names, dates, the 127 finishers, the Dutch voice reading a date: stated flat. Sound described by what happened ("it goes quiet, then everything comes back at once"), not by texture.
10. **A person.** Is there at least one piece of personal context or limit that is not decorative (what they were doing, what they know, what they missed)? And is it said once, not repeated as a pose?
11. **Honesty.** No claim of hearing or seeing beyond the evidence. State the limit once, plainly, and stop apologising.
12. **Read it aloud.** If a sentence sounds like a good sentence, rewrite it as a worse one.
