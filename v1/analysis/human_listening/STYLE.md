# Style guide: writing about music like a person

Sources: `corpus/text.md` (13 short forum/thread comments), `corpus/review.md` (11 blog/zine excerpts, 4 of them on PSB), `corpus/forum.md` (NOT collected: blocked, so no long forum posts exist on disk), and our pieces `../video1/LISTENING.md` (V1), `../video2/LISTENING.md` (V2), `video1_human.md` (H1), `video2_human.md` (H2).

Caveats. The corpus is thin: comments are under 30 words, reviews are small-zine writing, and there are no genuine forum paragraphs. An earlier version of this guide quoted other items (a 56-year-old crying, "p7eaven", "Palace are now my new favourites") that are no longer in `corpus/`; those are used below only for the forum register and are marked "(earlier corpus, unverified on disk)". Re-collect before leaning on them.

**Music first.** The user's reminder: it is more music than video. Real listeners write about the sound, the bass, the drop, the voice, the song as a thing they keep. Our pieces spend most of their words on picture inventory (windmills, kettles, bibs). A piece should be at least two thirds about what was heard and what it did, with one picture detail at most, only where it changed how the music landed.

The user's verdict on our pieces: "such ai phrasing trying too hard". The facts are mostly fine. The prose is performing.

## 1. How real people write, by register

### Text or one-line comment (corpus/text.md)
- 10 to 25 words, one sentence, sometimes two. Fragments and exclamations are normal: "Fire For You is AWESOME! I still remember where I was when I first heard it."
- Name the song and artist, then react. Reaction beats description: "I've been really into Guest House by Daughters." "Gotta be Old Town Road by Lil Nas X".
- Sound gets one cheap, everyday phrase, if any: "super warm, super smooth bass that just hits different"; "a great kind of unrelenting with a much softer ending"; "holy **** when it swells..!!!!"; "there's a huge rumbling machine outside my house". The music is a bass, a swell, a low end. Never a texture essay.
- Personal context is most of the content: where they were ("I still remember where I was"), how they found it ("playing beat saber"), how long ("looping it"), being sick of it ("I kinda of got sick of it after looping it, but I still love the song").
- Humour is incidental and deflating: "started as a meme originally but is actually a damn catchy song regardless"; "Not the Seinfeld theme, because I just found out they don't use a bass".
- Typos and rough grammar stay ("kinda of"). Emoji and "!!!" are fine.
- Ends where it runs out. No sign-off, no moral.

### Forum post (a paragraph or three; corpus gap)
No real long posts survive in the corpus. From the earlier corpus (unverified on disk) and the short comments: starts from the poster's situation, mixed sentence lengths, digressions (how they found the band), vague on purpose ("some kind of", "I don't know why but"), one cheap image at most and then dropped, feelings stated flat and then qualified, honest about boredom, ends on a question to the thread or the last thing they thought of. Treat this register as guidance, not evidence.

### Short review (corpus/review.md)
- Medium to long sentences (25 to 60 words), stuffed with parentheses, dashes and a trailing clause. Unpolished: "the kind shown in cinemas in the early to mid 20th century"; "so it's more than a little odd".
- Opens on a position or a plain label, not a scene: "Their musical 'formula' is one that I find enthralling and deliciously simple". "'Lichtspiel III' is my favourite track on the album and I use it daily for meditation."
- Personal use is the evidence: "I use it daily for meditation"; "became a go-to instrumental album for me".
- Comparison is to other music and films, constantly: Vangelis, Ennio Morricone, Hans Zimmer, Blade Runner, Kraftwerk, A Clockwork Orange, Every Valley. Rarely to nature or furniture.
- Craft words are used loosely and flatly: "pounding synth rhythms", "gentle bells", "off-kilter drums", "neck-snapping drums", "dense and bleak layering". Stock adjectives ("superb", "intriguing, engaging and vastly enjoyable", "lush") come in runs and nobody minds.
- Hedged, specific complaints: "It's not without odd bumps however"; "so the ending falls a little flat". Reviewers do dislike things, in plain words.
- Facts are delivered flatly and are the proof of homework: release year, track titles, sample source, label.
- Some clumsy sentences survive: "sci-fi 'warps'", "news footage surrounding Welsh Miners". Grammar slips, repeated words ("surrounding ... surrounding").
- Endings: a stock recommendation ("For fans of electronic music... intriguing, engaging and vastly enjoyable"), a mild gripe ("the ending falls a little flat"), or a wish ("I'd like to see it get the attention it so very much deserves"). Never an aphorism.

### Across registers
- Specific on names, titles, dates and where they were; vague on sound.
- Metaphors are rare, dead or borrowed from other music. Never two in a row.
- Evaluation is plain: awesome, catchy, odd, superb, a little flat.
- Personal context does the work. Structure is not explained.
- Short is normal.
- Endings are abrupt, weak, or a recommendation.

## 2. The AI tells in our pieces

Quotes are from V1, V2, H1, H2. None is wrong on fact. All of it is written, not said.

### Metaphor and simile in every paragraph
- V1: "It put me in mind of a long march, or of a factory heard from the pavement outside, something enormous going on whether or not you are paying attention." Two comparisons for one sound.
- V1: "as if a door had been pushed back"; H1: "like a door being kicked open"; V2: "like a door kicked open on a quiet house". The same door in three pieces.
- V1: "like a stair that is not there when your foot comes down", "like a hand laid flat on your chest", "like a heartbeat heard through a wall". One fresh figure per paragraph.
- V2: "like being towed"; "someone had been in a room with you for a long time without speaking, and then leaves".
- Corpus comparisons point at other music (Vangelis, Kraftwerk). Ours point at furniture and weather.

### Manufactured bodily sensations
- V1: "I came out of it braced, the way you are after standing in a wind for a while."
- V2: "I came out of this one braced and a little hollow." "I actually sat back." "I was leaning towards the screen."
- V1: "like a hand laid flat on your chest".
- The corpus body words are "hits different", "holy ****", "rumbling machine outside my house": about the sound's size, not about the writer's posture. A real listener does not stage their own posture.

### Tricolons and rhythmic lists
- V1: "no tune to hum and no chorus to wait for"; "relentless and a bit stubborn"; "a registration table, hands writing on forms, the voice giving figures".
- V2: "Skaters on dark glossy ice, farmhouses, a telegraph post, low light".
- H1: "a fixed pulse, layers arriving one at a time"; "a cold, relentless pulse, a voice that sounds like history, and a grey crowd".
- Corpus lists are stock-adjective runs from reviewers ("intriguing, engaging and vastly enjoyable"), which are flat and unrhythmic, or name lists of tracks.

### "Not X, but Y" and "X more than Y"
- V1: "Not sad, not lifted. Tired for the people on screen." "Not louder so much as heavier." "I would call it brave more than beautiful." "The music promises an ending and the film steps round it."
- V2: "so the feeling has to come from you"; "the music is doing that, not the pictures"; "It felt less like an ending than like something being taken away."
- H1: "the payoff is weight, not noise"; "build by taking things away rather than adding them".
- Nobody in the corpus defines a thing by what it is not, apart from the one throwaway "not the Seinfeld theme".

### Aphoristic closing lines
- V1: "I would call it brave more than beautiful... it got to me."
- V2: "But I would sit through the middle again for the room going quiet at the road, and for the knocks at the end." / "The ice was not a floor any more." / title "Nothing happens, and I couldn't look away".
- H1: "A calm man reading the facts over an engine that will not ease off is the feeling of the whole piece."
- Corpus endings are a recommendation, a gripe or a trailing wish.

### "I will admit" and performed candour
- V2: "I will admit that for a stretch in the middle I was bored." (Bored is fine. Announcing it as a confession is the tell.) "I did not like it all the way through; the middle asked a lot of me." "I did not know that while watching, and it did not change what I felt."
- V1: "I found I liked that more than I expected." "Then the thing I did not expect." "it caught me off guard in something this hard-faced."
- Corpus admissions are blunt and unframed: "I kinda of got sick of it after looping it".

### Over-balanced paragraphs and too-neat structure
- Each paragraph: scene, one figure, an emotion, a turn. All complete and even. V1 "The first moment that stayed with me..." and "Then the thing I did not expect..." have the same shape.
- A chronological walk from first second to last, with a verdict. A reviewer says two or three things and stops.
- H1/H2: headings ("The front page", "First listen, forward in time", "Who hears what"), a bold "The line.", personas. Titles that are themselves lines: "Cold, and a banner that says FINISH".

### Picture inventory instead of music (against the music-first rule)
- V1: "a hazy windmill, a wooden platform, a telegraph pole, then a line of skaters"; "a kettle pouring, cups and saucers on a patterned cloth"; "a face in a snow-crusted knitted cap, mouth open".
- V2: "A young man seemed to glance towards the camera in a flurry of other faces and was gone." Whole paragraphs of faces. A music listener would not carry these.
- The corpus never describes frames; even in the PSB reviews, the film is only the sample source ("newsreel commentaries").

### Literary and craft vocabulary
- "litany", "cadence", "hairline flicker", "rime", "ridge of windblown dunes", "hush", "payoff", "landing", "subtraction", "the wall", "held sound", "on the one".
- The corpus: "bass", "low end", "swells", "drums", "bells", "synth rhythms", "build-up", "sounds like X".

### Performed precision, hedging and evenness
- "sounded like half past five", "sounded like 575 racers" is a hedge repeated as a style.
- Even tone from top to bottom. No gripe about the speakers, no aside about the day, no musical comparison, no forgetting.
- Humans: "can't remember", "I think", "I just found out".

## 3. Checklist (a piece must pass all of it)

1. **Music first.** At least two thirds is about sound: bass, drums, voice, the moment it goes quiet, the ending. Count picture sentences; at most one or two, and only where they changed the music. No frame inventory.
2. **Length.** Text: one to three lines. Forum post: one to three short paragraphs. Review: 150 to 300 words. Two or three moments, no walkthrough.
3. **Start.** Opens with where the writer was, a reaction, a plain opinion or the track name. Not a scene, not a headline, not a thesis line.
4. **Similes.** At most one comparison, and it names other music, a film or a plain object (Vangelis, Kraftwerk, a washing machine). No stacking. No door, stair, hand on chest, heartbeat, wind, march, factory.
5. **Body.** No braced, hollow, sat back, leaned in, held breath. One flat body word at most (goosebumps, bored), only if true.
6. **Search and delete:** "not X, but Y", "X more than Y", "less like... than like", "I did not expect", "I will admit", "I found I", "the way you...". Break every list of three.
7. **Ending.** Stops on a plain fact, small gripe, recommendation or a trailing thought. No moral, no balancing verdict, no line that sounds good aloud.
8. **Lopsided.** One long paragraph and one very short. Something left unexplained or vague ("some bit near the end"). One blunt admission with no frame. A flat gripe.
9. **Words.** A friend's words: bit, kicks in, goes quiet, bass, drums, voice. No cadence, litany, subtraction, payoff, wall, held, hush. Stock adjectives (great, odd, a bit flat) are fine.
10. **Facts flat, sound vague.** Dates, names, 127 finishers, the Dutch voice reading a date: plain. Sound: what happened, not texture. Drop the "sounded like" hedge.
11. **A person.** One real piece of context or limit (what they know, what they missed, how they listened, what they compared it to), said once.
12. **Honesty.** Nothing claimed beyond the evidence; state a limit once, plainly; do not perform candour.
13. **Read it aloud.** If a sentence sounds like a good sentence, make it a worse one.
