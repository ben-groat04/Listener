# Elfstedentocht Part 2, as a listener would put it

**A cold walk that stops before the end.**

*Public Service Broadcasting, 2013, about five minutes, with the film. Written as a prediction for named kinds of listener, not as testimony. The evidence behind each paragraph is in the sidecar at the bottom; you do not need it to read the page.*

---

## The front page

Worth five minutes if you like music that builds by staying exactly where it is: a steady beat, one layer slowly opening, an old voice that talks and then goes. Best with the picture, and best on headphones. Not for you if you want a tune, a chorus or a payoff. There isn't one.<!-- p1 -->

The moment you will remember is probably not the loud one. It is the few seconds just before it, when the band falls away and the narrator says the name of the race over a held note and nothing else, and then the kick comes back on the one. That is my bet, and I am prepared to be wrong about it.<!-- p2 -->

The ending does not resolve. The brightness goes first, then the bass breaks up, then the beat stops, and a short, dark little figure of two drum hits and three bass notes closes it before silence. The picture fades out after the sound has gone.<!-- p3 -->

What it is like: a long, determined walk. Calm on top, pressing underneath, and with nobody told to cheer at the end of it.<!-- p4 -->

## First listen, going forward

**The wait.** For nearly half a minute you get one dark, hushed, held sound, a few low thuds and nothing else: no beat, no voice. Over it, a title board and frosted branches. What you have been taught is that this is slow and cold and in no hurry.<!-- p5 -->

Then bass and a sparse drum pulse walk in underneath the same held sound, and the picture cuts to cold purple light at that same instant. A stride has started, still muffled.<!-- p6 -->

**The first voice.** At about 0:42 a dry, narrow, old-sounding Dutch voice says "18 januari 1963" over a snowed-in churchyard. It is the first human sound, and it turns a mood into a dated day. If you have no Dutch you still get a date, and then a run of figures: degrees below, kilometres, and numbers of skaters.<!-- p7 -->

From here the voice leads and the band steps back around it. Notice what you are being taught: when the narrator speaks, the drums thin out, and at moments (just after one minute, again around 1:10) they nearly vanish. Hold on to that.<!-- p8 -->

**The turn.** At 1:24 the bass and drums go altogether, for about four seconds, and what is left is the held sound and the narrator finishing the sentence that ends on the name of the race. You have been taught that the beat steps back for the voice, so you half-expect something. It is denied: it goes all the way to nothing. Then it is paid: the full kick comes back on the one, with no fill, no rise, nothing to announce it. The picture cuts from an empty road to a crowd on the same instant. From here to the last bar the beat never changes.<!-- p9 -->

The groove is a steady foot-tap with a slight limp. The bass never lands on the one, so it rolls rather than marches.<!-- p10 -->

**The last sentence.** Around 2:36 the narrator finishes a head-count of racers and tourists, and that is the last time anyone speaks in sentences. The loop changes, a layer pulls back, and the picture cuts to a car on a frosted road. From here nobody explains.<!-- p11 -->

**The middle.** This is the long stretch where the beat stops being something you listen to and becomes something you walk on. Some people will love it, some will check the time, and most will only notice afterwards that something up top had been opening, a little at a time, and that it was louder than it started. The voice comes back near the end of this stretch as short fragments, probably wordless, falling roughly with the bars, and it is the least like a person of anything in the piece.<!-- p12 -->

**The ending.** At about 4:37 the brightness falls away, but the beat carries on, so it sounds like the energy has gone out while the machine goes on. About ten seconds later the bass breaks into stabs, and the floor goes. At about five minutes it all stops. The held sound rings out for a couple of seconds, then comes the short figure of two drum hits and three bass notes, then nothing. It sounds like a machine clearing its throat, not like a full stop that means something.<!-- p13 -->

## Who hears what

**A cold first-timer on a phone.** Probably hears a date, a voice, a steady modern beat and a long brightening, and an ending that stops before they are ready. A phone speaker may give them little of the low end, so the drop may land more as a return of the beat than as a thump in the chest.<!-- p14 -->

**A fan of this kind of record** (archive voice over electronics, the band's usual method). Probably hears the voice as the lead and the band as the frame, enjoys the hole before the drop as a well-made one, and notes with some surprise that after 2:36 the voice never explains again.<!-- p15 -->

**Someone who knows the race, or speaks Dutch.** If you know that 127 people finished out of nearly ten thousand, it is a wake. The "18 januari 1963" lands as a date you already own, and the stop at the end is a leaving. If you do not, it is a cold march that stops before you are ready.<!-- p16 -->

## Second listen, and afterwards

Second time through you hear the warning you missed: the drums thinning under the voice before 1:24. You hear that the bass never plays on the one. You hear the brightening for what it is.<!-- p17 -->

What survives in memory after the music has stopped is usually short. The nothing before the kick. The date over the churchyard. The ice that is no longer a floor at the end. And "it kept going, steady, got harder, then came apart".<!-- p18 -->

## What the picture does to it

Over the pictures the held sound is cold air, and the stride is somebody setting out. After the voice goes, the picture turns to backs, bibs and hands behind backs, bodies from behind in a line, and the beat starts to mean effort rather than celebration. The pictures do not hit the beats; they follow the voice at first and then loosely follow the loudness, with a few cuts landing on the music very closely: the cut to purple light at 0:28, the cut to the crowd on the drop. The last pictures are ducks, then broken ice on open water, then fade. No finish, no winner, no one named.<!-- p19 -->

## Where it sits

It is in the band's usual manner: a voice from an archive carries the story and the band frames it. A drum machine walking steadily under a documentary voice is exactly that. It has no tune and no chorus, and no ending that arrives.<!-- p20 -->

## What nobody here could hear

None of us heard this. We worked from separated stems, levels and pitch tracks, and from frames. We could not hear the weight of the kick, the grain of the voice, the room, or whether any of it moved us. Where we write "you", it is a prediction about a named kind of listener, and we would like your ear to overrule it.<!-- p21 -->

**Two questions for you:** What stayed with you? Which sentence above rang falsest?
