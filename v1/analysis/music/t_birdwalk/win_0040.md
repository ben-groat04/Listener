## 8. How this window moves the song on
- 0:40-0:47 is more of the sub-led groove of 0:25-0:40 (same bass level -14 dBFS, same voice near D4); it speeds up at the end (bass run at 45.2-46.7, 0.13 to 0.27 s hits) into the cut.
- 0:47 strips the bass and thins the drums to one hit per bar, drops the voice by about two octaves and widens it, and takes the mix down about 7 to 8 dB. This is a contrast/breakdown, not a build: the hook "do that bird walk" is now delivered in a low, wide voice over a light pulse.
- 0:59.4 is the release: bass and drum return together, mix +11 to +14 dB within 0.2 s. `window_0060s.png` shows the dense drum bed returning at 1:00 and the voice moving to a higher register around 1:11 (x = 10.8 s there), so the song resumes the loud groove.

## 9. What a human listener likely feels
- 0:40-0:47, weight and drive: a deep, steady thud (56% of energy below 60 Hz) with a repeated chant, which feels like momentum. The fast bass hits at 45-46.7 add a lean-forward push (measured onset spacing 0.13 to 0.27 s).
- 0:47, a drop-out: the floor of low frequency goes (bass -60 dB, mix -7 to -8 dB), so the music suddenly feels thin, light and open, with air around the voice. The voice is lower and wider, so it feels closer or more confiding, like the track is whispering the hook (the lowness is measured, the "confiding" is a feeling).
- 0:47-0:59, slow and teasing: hits only every 1.56 s on a flat A, the same four words over and over; this feels like waiting or showing off a move in slow motion, with anticipation built by the stripped bass (cause: low density and repetition). Whether it reads as comic or menacing depends on the video, not the data.
- 0:59.4, the re-entry: sub-bass returns (+53 dB in the bass stem, +11 to +14 dB in the mix), which feels like a sudden physical release or "hit".
