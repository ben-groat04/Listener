## 8. How the window moves the song on

The previous window (2:00 to 2:20) builds: from 2:11.3 the bass enters and drums thicken to a steady pulse, with the vocal sweep at about 2:09 to 2:12. This window opens with the groove thinned (sparser hits) and then drops the rhythm section completely for 12 s, using a contrast device (a stripped breakdown) centred on the chopped hook voice, with a downward sweep as a lead-in; the full groove slams back at 2:35.7 and continues into the next window (2:40 to 3:00), where drums and bass stay and the voice then drops out in turn (vocal stem near floor from about 2:48 to 2:56). So the sequence is: build (2:11), tease (2:20), breakdown (2:23.5), re-entry (2:35.7), then a different strip-down. Release comes from the re-entry, not from any new harmony.

- 2:20 to 2:23: lurching, loose, bouncy. Cause: sparse kicks at uneven gaps (0.46 to 1.1 s) over a sub bass at about 54 Hz; the groove is more like a stumbling walk than a metronome. Interpretation (not measured): playful. CLAP mood "playful" 0.999 agrees but is a weak model.
- 2:23.5 to 2:35.6: suddenly exposed and intimate, a bit comic. Cause: an 8 dB loudness drop, bass and drums removed in under 0.1 s, stereo width widening (-10.8 to -5.1 dB side/mid), the voice (low speaking pitch, about 113 Hz median, gated chops about every 0.26 s) now the only rhythmic element. Expectation builds because the harmony stays on A with no movement.
- 2:32.5 to 2:35.7: rising anticipation. Cause: the falling sweep and the vocal chopping up to the downbeat, with no bass for the ear to hold on to.
- 2:35.7: relief and a bump of energy. Cause: a rise of about 62 dB in the bass stem and about 8 to 10 dB in the mix back to -11 to -14 dB. Interpretation: the "drop" of a dance or hip-hop track (genre labels hip hop 0.999 from CLAP are weak evidence).
- Overall: repetitive, hypnotic, tongue-in-cheek. Cause: one chord centre (A) for the whole window and one repeating phrase. The comic feel is interpretation; the measurements show only the repetition and the low speech register.
