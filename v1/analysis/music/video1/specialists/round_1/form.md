## 0. One-paragraph verdict

This is not a verse/chorus song. It is a **single continuous drive in D at one fixed density, in which form is made almost entirely by subtraction and re-addition of three things: the bass, the "other" (guitar/synth) wall, and the spoken sample.** The drums never stop for 3:08 (only a roughly 0.4 s all-stem hole at 2:48). Loudness is very flat (per-second mix between about -9.5 and -16.5 dBFS across the track [lv]), so the big moments are *not* loudness peaks. They are changes of floor (bass out/in), width (0.28 to 0.83) and top-end (centroid about 1.7 to 2.9 kHz). The arc is: **launch and layer-up (0:00-0:41), narrated plateau and build (0:41-1:15), a release-by-subtraction and a bass-less "wall without a floor" (1:15-1:40), a bass slam-in and peak (1:40-1:55), a deep breakdown with bass reduced to six-second stabs (1:56-2:38 in two stages), a stop-and-reveal (2:48) and a bright, wide final run to a gated cut (2:48-3:08).** Confidence in the map: high for the event times, medium for the labels, because the song has no lyrics and no melodic hook to confirm "sections" by ear.

## 4. Tension and release: how the big moments are engineered

1. **Opening.** A rising swell into a downbeat (0:00.1-0:00.7), then four independent entrances over eight seconds: drums (0:00.8), wall (0:01.3), bass (0:03-0:08), voice (0:09). Ear-catching, "starter pistol and engine warming". [lv, mine]
2. **The first release (0:32).** Not a loud event: the voice leaves, width jumps 0.38 to 0.55 and the drums brighten. The release is *air* (space, high frequencies) and instrumental autonomy, and it comes on a phrase endpoint (the last word of the narration lands on a chord change [win 0020]). Medium-high.
3. **Slow build 0:41-1:15.** "Other" +11 dB (0:40-0:58), the drums and bass hold, width 0.45 to 0.7, brightness up at 1:06-1:15, harmony frozen on D. A build made only from texture and width, with no new instrument and no change in tempo; the listener gets "pressure without progress". [lv, win 0040/0060]
4. **Release by subtraction (1:15).** The bass vanishes, the wall thins to bright lines; the effect is lift (like an inhale) because the floor goes. The ear then waits for the bass. The narrator re-enters at 1:18.8 into the space. [mine form_events; win 0060]
5. **Suspension and second build without a floor (1:15-1:40).** Voice leaves at 1:27, "other" doubles in apparent power (-27 to -16 dB) and width goes to its maximum (0.78-0.83), yet there is only a faint D2 blip for bass. By 1:40 the bass has been absent 25 s; that is the longest delay of resolution in the song. [lv, win 0080]
6. **Peak by return of the floor (1:40).** +34 dB in the bass stem within a second and a width collapse (0.78 to 0.47) [lv]. The "peak" is the *landing* of a long suspension, not a louder climax. The loudest sustained 15 s of the song is the next stretch (1:40-1:55).
7. **Engineered drop (1:55-2:01).** Bass out at about 1:55.5; the top of the wall is rolled off from about 1:49.5; a swell/crash-like smear of the "other" stem at about 1:58 [img 0100]; then collapse to a thin layer. Pre-drop darkening and a final swell followed by a gap is a standard "pull-up" gesture, but the evidence for a cymbal or riser is a spectral smear; instrument identity unknown.
8. **Breakdown with a heartbeat (2:01-2:38).** Drums and a quiet wall keep going; the only low end is a held note about every 6 s. The slow bass pulse over a 5-per-second grid is the clearest example of two time scales in the track. Mix is 4 to 5 dB lower and mono-ish (width 0.25-0.35) at 2:00-2:13 [lv]. A return at 2:14 (other +7 dB; chroma to A minor, F, C) starts the second narration block.
9. **The title-card and the stop (2:44-2:48).** The two final phrases land loud on the grid; the band then cuts out for 0.4 s (all stems black in [img 0160]). It is aligned with the end of the vocal line, so the stop acts as a full stop after the sentence. It then returns with a different drum sound (cymbal-led, bright) and no voice. The stop is the "big moment" of the track by pure engineering: the one place where the constant drum grid breaks. [img 0160, mine]
10. **Reveal and run-out (2:48-3:08).** No new section label can be justified; the novelty is brightness (drum energy above 5 kHz from about 1% to 9-12%), width (0.6-0.8), and the narrator's absence, which together read as "the film has ended, the band continues". A 0.5 s taper ends the file: a sudden stop rather than a resolution; the final chord is a D pedal as for the whole track.

## 5. Form analysis: summary shape

Two proposed readings (they overlap; I think both are true at different scales):
- **Arch with a hole in the middle:** launch (0:00-0:41), build (0:41-1:15), floor-less middle (1:15-1:40), peak (1:40-1:55), trough (1:56-2:14), re-ignition and block (2:14-2:38), stop and final run (2:48-3:08).
- **Narration-framed:** voice-led first third (0:09-1:27 with gaps), instrumental pushes (0:32-0:41; 1:27-1:46), narration punctuation in the latter half (1:46, 1:57, 2:15-2:36), then the one-sentence "title" at 2:44 and silence after. The voice returns to speak the name of the race at the end of its arc, so for form, the narrator's last line is the climax of the *text*, and the stop is its exclamation mark.

Where it is conventional: loud-intro, breakdown, return, final run, with the long suspension (1:15-1:40) as a lead-in. Where it is unconventional for rock: (a) the drum density never changes, (b) energy changes through bass, width and brightness rather than level, (c) no key change and one drone chord for most of the track (D is 94 s of chord time, Dm 33 s [summary.json]), so harmonic form is almost absent and does not carry the build.

## 6. Questions about what I can and cannot tell

- The mix has almost no loudness contrast, so I am sure I am describing *arrangement* changes rather than classic dynamics. A listener may nonetheless feel strong swings at 1:15, 1:40 and 1:56, because bass removal reads as loss of weight; I cannot measure perception directly (loudness-weighted, K-weighted LUFS per second would be a cheap next check).
- I cannot say whether the 2:48 hole is a mixing "edit" (muting all stems) or a real rest played by the band; the 10-12 dB drop with a faint remainder (-21 to -23 dB) points to decay and bleed, not an edit, but that is inference.
- I cannot say whether the 1:15 and 1:27 events are one guitar/bass performance or editing; section lengths of about 4 bars suggest planned arrangement but bar one is unverified.
- The windows' use of "sixteenth note at 78" vs "eighth at 156" matters for how I count bars (above I counted four tracked beats as a bar). If the true bar is eight eighth-notes at 156, my "bars" are really two-bar units.
