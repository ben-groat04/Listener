# Lineage specialist, round 1: "Elfstedentocht Part 1" (video1)

British English; times m:ss. Evidence keys: AF = audio_features/summary.json; LV = music_views/per_second_levels.json; CH = chords.json; CL = clap_tags.json; LY = lyrics/whisper_medium_mix_nl_*; IMG = music_views/window_NNNNs.png (as reported in win_*.md notes, not all re-opened by me); W = win_*.md notes; CTX = data/video1/context.md and research/*.md; LC = my script `v1/scripts/agents/lineage_check.py`.

**Honesty frame.** Measurements cannot name a genre or an influence. What follows has three tiers: (a) sourced facts about the artist and commission (from research files, which cite pages); (b) what the audio measurements support about the construction; (c) my own genre placement, from general musical knowledge, which no source in this project confirms. Tier (c) is marked "own placement" and is hypothesis, not finding. I found no interview in which PSB name Part 1's models.

## 1. Sourced lineage facts (tier a)
- Artist: Public Service Broadcasting, London duo (Willgoose; Wrigglesworth), formed 2010. Method: archive film and public-information sound set to new music; samples carry the narrative (psb_method.md, from Wikipedia and the Outline interview; high for the general practice, medium-high for the interview).
- Place in the catalogue: a two-part commission for the Explore the North festival, Leeuwarden, for the 50th anniversary of the 1963 race; footage and voice-over from the Fries Film Archief; released autumn 2013 (digital 11 Nov 2013), after the debut album *Inform - Educate - Entertain* (6 May 2013) and linked to its deluxe edition (CTX; Written In Music 4 Nov 2013; high on commission, medium on format). So this is a **second-phase, "commissioned documentary" piece, made between the debut and the next concept album (*The Race for Space*, 2015, not covered by our sources, so I only mention it as outside-knowledge context)**. It continues the debut's "sample as narrator" method but swaps British public-information film for Dutch regional film.
- Intended contrast: Part 1 "loud, rock-oriented", Part 2 "calmer, more electronic" (Written In Music, high); Part 1 has a "simple blood-pumping guitar riff" (Record Rewind Play, medium-high). I verify the "simple, driving" part below; the "riff" I cannot verify (see 3.2).
- What the artist was trying to do: not stated in any source I can cite for this track. The brief was commemoration for a festival. Everything about intent below is inference.

## 2. What the measurements say about construction (tier b)
1. **A pedal-based, one-chord-world track.** Whole-song key D major 0.76 vs D minor 0.756, a tie (AF). Chroma of the "other" stem over the whole track: D 1.00, A 0.71, C 0.58, then E, G, C# near 0.4 (LC). So the harmonic stack is D + A (open fifth) + C natural (flat seventh), with the third (F or F#) not established; F# at 0.39 is not prominent. The chord detector's "D / Dm / Am / C / G" shuffling (CH; W win_0080, win_0120, win_0160) is best read as noise around a power-chord/pedal. Confidence: high for D pedal, medium-high for "third ambiguous", low for any mode name (Mixolydian/Dorian are both consistent). The 2:00-2:30 key-block flip to A minor (AF) comes from Am-D-G fragments, a brief tonal wobble, not a modulation (W win_0120).
2. **A fixed, machine-like grid.** Beat tracker 78.3 bpm; drum-stem autocorrelation peaks at 0.766 s (78.3 bpm, strength 0.73), 0.395 s (152 bpm, 0.47) and 0.186 s (0.28) (LC). Drum onsets about 4.2 per second, spacing 0.19/0.39 s, in every window from 0:00 to 3:08 (W all windows). So the felt pulse is either 78 with a semiquaver hat grid or about 156 with quavers; the 78 reading has the strongest periodicity, so a listener sensing "about 156 driving rock" or "about 78 heavy" are both defensible. Whether the kit is played or programmed is NOT determinable here; CLAP's "drum machine" (0.88-1.0 across 1:35-2:15) is weak evidence. The sameness of the grid for 3 minutes, with no fills until the very end (3:06-3:08, W win_0180), leans towards programmed or heavily quantised.
3. **Arrangement by subtraction and addition, not by chord change.** Layer logic from the window notes, cross-checked against LV: bass creeps in 0:03-0:20; bass cut dead at 1:15 and absent to 1:40 (a ~25 s "wall without a floor"); bass back 1:40-1:55; bass and "other" drop together about 1:55-2:00; from 2:01 the bass appears only as sub-bass "thumps" every ~6.1 s (8 beats at 78 bpm) to 2:38; full band returns 2:38; everything cuts 2:47.8-2:48.2; a brighter, cymbal-led full band ends the track (drum energy above 5 kHz 0.8-1.2 % before 2:48 vs 9-12 % after, W win_0160) with no fade, ending on a held D from 2:48 (CH). Form is therefore a long plateau with timbral and bass-presence moves: this is the central fact for lineage.
4. **Voice is archival speech, not singing.** Dutch announcer in short bursts: 0:09-0:32 (date, time, "only the strongest selected"), 0:41, 0:51-0:56 (-16 degrees, 200 km), 1:03.5-1:05.5 (unrecognised), 1:19-1:27 (575 racers, ~9,500 tourists, nationalities), 2:15-2:36 (a list of Frisian towns: Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward(?) ..., Dokkum, Leeuwarden), 2:44-2:48 ("Where everyone ... that is the Elfstedentocht") (LY translate pass; W). Whisper is garbled on several lines; town names are my reading of garbles (W win_0120, win_0140). Nothing after 2:48. Vocals-stem contour is not a tune (W win_0000).
5. **Guitar vs synth is unresolved.** "Other" is a dense, harmonically rich wall to 8-10 kHz (W all). CLAP says "distorted guitar" in most windows (0.85-1.0) but also "brass" 0.98-0.999 at 0:50 and 2:45, "synthesizer", "organ" (W win_0040, win_0080, win_0160). The "guitar" label is therefore supported by the cited review (Record Rewind Play) and by CLAP consistency, not by my own isolating of a guitar. Banjo: no evidence (CTX).

## 3. Genre placement (tier c, own placement, low-medium confidence)
The audio features line up with several overlapping traditions. I give each with what in the data supports it and what argues against.

**3.1 Post-rock / "cinematic rock" (best fit).** CLAP top genre post-rock 0.84 (CTX; weak model). Supporting structure: instrumental; sustained distorted wall over a pulse; arrangement by layer entry and exit rather than verse/chorus; long static harmony; dynamics managed by bass and density (3 above). The "climb" reading of 0:00-0:20 (bass creeping up, "other" brightening, W win_0000) is the stock post-rock build gesture. Against: post-rock builds usually crescendo to a release and then decay; here the level plateau is almost flat (section RMS 0.17-0.28, AF) and the end is a hard cut, not a catharsis. So it borrows the texture but not the long-arc narrative; the spoken narration supplies the narrative instead.

**3.2 Motorik / krautrock-adjacent drive.** Evidence: unbroken, metronomic, hat-heavy drums with no fills (3.2 above), single-chord harmony, and a steady pulse, which together give the "propulsive but static" quality that the motorik family has. Against: motorik has a recognisable kick-snare-hat pattern at 4/4 around 120-160 bpm; I have NOT transcribed the pattern, only counted onsets, so I cannot say it is that beat. Treat as a family resemblance, not an influence claim.

**3.3 Indie/electronic crossover, with a programmed-feel rhythm section.** Evidence: grid regularity; sub-bass "thump and hold" every 8 beats (W win_0120, win_0140) which is an electronic-production gesture (a held 808/synth-bass-like low note), not a guitar-band bassist's behaviour; CLAP "drum machine". This matches PSB's usual position as a guitar-and-electronics duo (psb_method.md roles) and the cited contrast that Part 2 is "more electronic" (so Part 1 is *relatively* rock, not purely so).

**3.4 Sample-led / "found voice" tradition.** This is the strongest lineage claim I can support from sources: archival speech used as the lead voice, music built round it. Within this the track uses the voice as *punctuation* (silences of 8 s, 11 s, 17 s between bursts: 0:42.5-0:50.5, 1:27-2:15 with only one 1:03 burst earlier) while the band stays constant. That is a conscious design: the voice does not shape the music; the music runs on and the voice is cut into it. Own wider placement (not sourced here): this follows the documentary-sampling line (spoken-word-over-rock, e.g. in the manner of Steve Reich's speech works or the Eno/Byrne collage records); do not cite as a PSB statement.

**3.5 Not enough evidence for:** Britpop/indie-dance, math-rock (grid is straight, no odd metre seen), folk (no acoustic or banjo evidence), or any claimed link to Dutch/Frisian music. The track uses no identifiable Dutch musical idiom; the Dutch is only in the speech. High confidence on that negative (no pitched Frisian/Dutch tune is detectable: lead notes are scatter, W win_0000).

## 4. Where it sits within the two-part commission
Part 1 (this file) is the "event" half: steady, loud, drive, one pedal; Part 2 (5:04) is described by sources as calmer and electronic (high for the description; I have not analysed video2 audio here). The tonal difference sets Part 1 up as the effort/race and Part 2 as the aftermath/thaw, which is my interpretation, consistent with the footage observations in artist_and_tracks.md (video1 night start and Dokkum sign; video2 colour, thaw floes). Do not state this as PSB's intent.

## 5. Interpretation: what the artist may have been doing (own, medium-low)
A commemoration needs two things: the facts (date, temperature, distance, field, route) and the physical feel (cold, endurance, crowd). The track delivers the facts through the sample (all the factual lines come in 0:09-0:56, 1:19-1:27, 2:15-2:48, i.e. facts early, field size in the middle, the route near the end) and the feel through a relentless D pedal and unvarying grid, so an English-speaking listener who cannot parse the Dutch still gets "something big, cold and relentless" from the music (Record Rewind Play's "blood-pumping"). The late town list (Sloten to Dokkum to Leeuwarden, 2:21-2:39) is a route-recital that plays like a checklist of stages; the track then ends on a held D with a cymbal-bright, louder final minute, which fits a finish rather than a resolution. These are readings; the measurements support the structure but not the intent.

## 6. What I corrected or doubted in the window notes
- Several windows read the 0.19 s drum spacing as definitely "semiquavers at 78 bpm". My autocorrelation shows 152 bpm (0.395 s) has equal claim as the felt pulse (0.47 vs 0.73 for 78). I would not fix one reading (LC).
- "Mixolydian/Dorian/major vs minor" in the notes are all plausible; the data does not choose. The "other" chroma has C (0.58) clearly above F# (0.39) so a flat seventh is better supported than a major seventh, but the third remains open.
- The Whisper town list (Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Dokkum, Leeuwarden) is my gloss on garbles; the translate pass itself shows Staveren, Hindelopen, Warken, Dokken, Leeuwarden (LY). Confidence: medium-high on the route-list nature, medium on individual names.
- CLAP "drum machine" 1.0 and "brass" 0.999 are not independent evidence; CLAP is the weakest instrument in the box.
- One earlier note's "chord changes" (Am/D alternation, W win_0080) are a plausible thin moving line on a pedal, not a progression to name.

## 8. Three questions for fellow specialists
1. **Rhythm:** Can you transcribe the repeating drum pattern from drums.wav over a few bars (kick vs snare vs hat positions) and say whether it is a motorik-style 4/4 backbeat, a straight rock beat or a programmed pattern? That decides whether my "motorik" resemblance in 3.2 stands or falls.
2. **Harmony:** Looking at the "other" and bass stems separately, does anything establish F or F# (the third) anywhere in the track, and what does the Am-D-G run at 2:00-2:30 do tonally (modulation, borrowed colour, or detector noise)? That decides whether "modal pedal, post-rock style" is right or whether this is simply D major/minor rock.
3. **Form:** Is there a rule behind the layer entries and exits (bass cut at 1:15, back at 1:40, 8-beat bass thumps from 2:01, full-band return 2:38, cut at 2:47.8), such as multiples of 8 or 16 bars at 78 bpm? A grid-locked structure would support the programmed/loop-based, electronic-production lineage over a band-in-a-room one.
