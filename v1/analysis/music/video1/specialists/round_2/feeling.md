## 1. What changed in my view (short)
1. **The "suspended, yearning" 1:27 to 1:40 passage is now better supported, and for a second reason.** R2-harmony shows the tonic D is nearly absent from the `other` stem from 1:15 to about 1:23 (D 0.08 to 0.26) and the layer plays a slow stepwise line (A, C, B, G, then D). So the floor is missing twice: no bass note and no D in the upper layer. I had only the first cause. Confidence medium-high.
2. **The "dark rim" at 2:49 to 3:08 is grit, not minor mode.** R2-harmony: the third (F or F sharp) is at most 0.16 in power everywhere, and the extra darkness is chromatic neighbours (C, C sharp, D sharp, B). I keep the feeling ("grinding, no turning back") but I now attribute it to chromatic friction plus a much brighter drum voicing (R2-sound: share of drum energy above 5 kHz rises from 3.3% to 12.4% after 2:48), not to mode. Medium-high.
3. **I withdraw "the 1:46 to 1:49 voice intrudes" from my journey.** R2-voice finds that burst has voiced fraction 0.00 and no pitch, so it is probably leakage (noise from cymbal or guitar), not an announcer. I wrongly gave it an emotional role ("a voice from the past"). Also the 1:57 "burst" is a held D3 with a crash leaking into the vocal stem, not speech (R2-voice, high). The voice map therefore has no voice between 1:19 to about 1:27 and 2:15; the 1:40 to 2:00 stretch is wholly instrumental. Confidence in the withdrawal: medium-high.
4. **The 2:00 to 2:34 "coiled spring" is a different sound, not the same wall turned down.** R2-sound: `other` centroid is 1480 Hz at 2:01 to 2:13 (high open fifths and octaves: A4, D5, A5, D6) against 650 Hz at 1:30 to 1:49 and 333 Hz at 1:50 to 1:59. I had said only "quieter". This explains why it feels hollow and exposed rather than merely softer. Medium.
5. **My "mile markers" reading of the 6.15 s sub hits needs a caution.** R2-harmony and R2-sound show the sub is a triggered synth-like thump at a fixed 42.6 Hz (starts 3 to 6 Hz sharp, falls within about 0.3 s, soft 20 to 110 ms rise, almost nothing above 300 Hz). A listener on small speakers (laptop, phone, earbuds) hears little or nothing at 42 Hz, so the "heavy footstep" feeling depends on the playback system. On a weak system the thing felt is the drop in the `other` layer and the width collapse. I cannot measure the playback system (limit).
6. **The 2:48 "gasp" stays**, but I now know it is also a timbre reset (R2-sound): the drums come back brighter. So the felt gasp is a gap plus a change of voice, which is stronger than a plain gap. High on cause, medium on size.

## 2. Answers to questions put to me

### 2.1 To Sound: which shifts does a human actually feel as a build or release? (medium; my ranking is an interpretation, the numbers are measured)
Sound observed that loudness spans only about 2 to 3 dB (P10 to P90). I checked: mix P10 -14.96, P90 -10.9 dBFS (4 dB), width P10 0.32, P90 0.73 [F2]. Change at each event, 4 s after versus 4 s before [F2]:

| Event | mix dB | width | bass dB | `other` dB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:15 bass out | -4.5 | -0.14 | -36 | -5.4 |
| 1:27 widen | +2.4 | +0.30 | (bass off; the +9 is noise near the floor, ignore) | +7.6 |
| 1:40 bass slam | +1.4 | -0.27 | +42.6 | -0.4 |
| 1:55 collapse | -3.0 | +0.10 | -35 | -2.2 |
| 2:00 thin block | -0.7 | -0.23 | +22 | -5.0 |
| 2:38 bass locks on | -0.7 | 0.00 | +42.8 | -4.8 |
| 2:48 stop, bright return | -0.8 | +0.26 | +3 | +3.7 |

My reading, in order of how strongly I think a listener registers them:
1. **Bass entries and exits (1:15, 1:40, 1:55, 2:38).** These are the only events with 35 to 43 dB swings in one stem, and they land on low-frequency energy that people feel as much as hear. At 1:40 the mix moves only 1.4 dB, yet I expect it to feel like the biggest event, because the change is concentrated in one register after 24 s of absence. This is the feeling "arrival". Medium-high that it is felt as such on decent speakers; lower on small ones.
2. **Stereo width (1:27: +0.30; 2:48: +0.26; 1:40: -0.27).** A 0.3 change in a track whose width usually sits in 0.32 to 0.73 is large relative to its range. Width is felt as "opening out" (1:27) or "closing in" (1:40, 2:00), and the sign matters: the 1:27 widening reads as lift, the 1:40 narrowing at the same time as the bass returns reads as "everything pulled into the middle and the chest". Medium; this is my interpretation of a recognised width-to-spaciousness association, not tested.
3. **Voice entering and leaving.** The 0:33 exit is mix-neutral but, I think, felt strongly because speech is a foreground "someone is telling you" signal. Medium.
4. **Overall loudness changes (1 to 5 dB).** Smallest effect; the track is level-flat, so feeling is by texture, as I said in round 1.

**Controlled/mechanical or urgent?** The dry, gated, 110 ms-decay drums and the 0.3 s cut-off ending (R1-sound) read to me as both: "controlled" in the sense of nothing sloppy or washing over, "urgent" because the 16th comb never rests. A fair compromise: **disciplined urgency**, like a metronome in a hurry. Low-medium (pure interpretation; R2-sound itself cannot say machine or human).

### 2.2 To Sound (my own round-1 question, now answered): guitar-heroic or machine-like?
R2-sound says `other` has four timbral states and that 1:30 to 1:50 has dense noise-like partials (leaning guitar-rock) while 2:00 to 2:13 has clean, widely spaced octave-and-fifth peaks (leaning synth or clean picked octaves). Feeling consequence, interpretation only: the big sections (0:40 to 1:15, 1:40 to 1:55, 2:49 to 3:08) feel like a band; the 2:00 block feels like a machine ticking over, which suits its "held breath" role. I do not know instrument identity (R2-sound agrees, and R2-lineage notes nobody has isolated a guitar). Low on labels, medium on the contrast.

### 2.3 To Harmony: the third (answered above; high that it is weak, medium that this means "no mode-based sadness")
Accepted. The consequence for feeling is stronger than before: nothing in the 0:00 to 3:08 harmony pulls to sadness or to triumph by a third, so "stoic" is a texture and rhythm judgement, not a mode judgement. I tone down the R1 word "wistful" to "wistful at most through the 1:15 to 1:40 line and the spoken-archive timbre". Medium.

### 2.4 To Rhythm (felt tempo; I side with a duality, not one number)
R1-rhythm gives about 60/40 for felt 156 with a backbeat every 0.77 s (hierarchy: strong every 0.77 s, medium at 0.38, weak at 0.19). R2-harmony adds that chord-walks in 2:12 to 2:33 change every 0.77 s, which is a normal rate at 156 (one chord per two beats). For feeling: I describe the pulse as **"heavy step with a fast heartbeat"**: the 0.77 s accent is the weight, the 0.19 s comb is the nerves. I would tell a human listener "it tempts you to nod slowly while your chest runs fast". The slow-heavy reading governs the sub-bass section (one hit per 8 pulses feels like a very slow gesture); the fast-driving reading governs the 2:49 sprint (bright cymbal-led drums). That the same song invites both is part of why it feels "determined, not frantic". Medium (interpretation of an unresolved tempo octave).

### 2.5 To Form: where are the emotional peaks, and do 1:15, 1:40, 2:00, 2:38, 2:48 divide the piece? (medium)
Form asked whether the long bass-less passage, the pulsing breakdown and the 0.4 s stop read as lift, dread or anticipation, and which is the "emotional peak" rather than the louder 1:40 to 1:55.
- **1:15 to 1:40 (bass-less):** lift and yearning, with a suspended quality. Not dread: it is bright, wide from 1:27, and the line is stepwise (R2-harmony).
- **2:00 to 2:38 (6 s thumps):** anticipation tipping to mild dread. It is the quietest, narrowest, thinnest stretch, and the only stretch where silence in the low end is regular. I read it as "waiting for something large", not menace. The place names (2:15 onward) turn it from dread into itinerary. Medium.
- **2:48 stop:** a gasp, then release, not a peak in itself.
- **Which is the emotional peak?** If "peak" means most strongly felt, I name **1:40** (arrival after absence), with the **2:48 gasp into the bright sprint** a close second. If it means highest intensity, it is **1:52 to 1:54** (loudest, -9.7 dBFS; R1-feeling) but that is mid-way through a bass-led plateau, not a surprise. This answers Form: the louder stretch is not the emotional peak; the moment of return is.
- **Sections:** I accept R2-harmony's boundaries (1:15, about 1:24, 1:40, 1:56, 2:12, 2:36 to 2:38, 2:48) as a refinement of mine. The 1:24 return of D and the 2:12 A-centred shift are real harmonic events that I called only "texture"; they fit "emerging from the float" (1:24) and "setting off" (2:12 to 2:15, which I had at 2:13 to 2:15). The auto-sections (AF 14.8, 66.4, 75.6, 87.9, 109.4) do not match what I find felt, with one exception: 1:15.6 (75.6) coincides with the bass exit.

### 2.6 To Voice: calm against drive, the litany (accept, with one qualification)
I agree with R2-voice that the absence of a performed vocal is the key and that the listener "does the singing". I accept that town-name onsets are not locked to the grid (beat phase range 0.07 to 0.76, R2-voice): this makes the litany feel **free against a machine**, human and slightly breathless over an unflinching pulse. I withdraw my "the sub hits are mile markers" as anything but a loose analogy: there is no measured link between the names and the 6.15 s cycle, only a coincidence in the period I noticed. Low.

### 2.7 To Lineage ("post-rock texture without post-rock arc")
I agree on feeling grounds: a post-rock listener expects a long crescendo to a big climax; this piece has plateaus, subtractions and a hard cut. The emotional shape is "stop-start endurance", not "build and bloom". That is a design observation (R2-lineage), and I add that the missing arc is exactly what makes the cut at 3:08.4 feel like "continuing offscreen". Medium. I do not claim intent.
