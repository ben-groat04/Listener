## 1. My three round-1 questions, answered from colleagues' evidence

**Q1 (rhythm): is the drum pattern motorik-like? Answer: my motorik resemblance mostly falls.**
R1-rhythm gives the kick profile over 16 slots as strong on 0, 2, 4, 8, 12, weaker on 5, 9, 14, silent on 1, 3, 6, 15, i.e. "syncopated, not four-on-the-floor", with a constant 16th hat/snare comb accented every 0.766 s (R1-rhythm sections 2-3; its measurement, medium-high). Classic motorik is a plain, even pulse (kick on the beat, snare on 2 and 4, open eighth hats) with no kick syncopation. A clustered kick over a gated 16th comb is closer to a programmed or heavily quantised alternative-rock/electronic groove. So I withdraw 3.2 as a lineage claim. What survives is a **functional** parallel that rhythm itself names ("same logic as minimalist or motorik music": a fixed ground with the other layers breathing against it). Revised wording: "motorik-like in function (unchanging pulse as a platform), not in pattern". Confidence: medium that the pattern is not motorik; low that any krautrock influence exists at all.

**Q2 (harmony): is a third ever established, and what do the 2:00-2:30 chords do?**
R1-harmony: D5 modal pedal, third unspecified (F 0.52 at 0:40-1:00 vs F# 0.28-0.40 early; F# 0.52 at 1:56-2:15), C natural consistently above C#, so a flat seventh (medium-high). The 2:00-2:30 A-minor flip is the Am-D-G walk with A dominant in the guitar at 2:15-2:40, not a modulation, and the bass goes to a held F sub-tone (R1-harmony section 5; R1-sound measures it as a sine at about 42.7 Hz, roughly F1, 40 cents flat). This agrees with my round-1 reading (pedal, third open, wobble not modulation) and **sharpens it**: the F sub-bass (a minor-third or bVI colour under the D wall) is the one place harmony does something, and it is a sub-sine hit rather than a played bass line. So "modal pedal" stands; "post-rock" is supported only as texture (see 2 below).

**Q3 (form): is there a rule behind the layer entries?**
R1-form converts events to bars of 3.06 s: 1:15.3 to 1:27.4 about 4 bars, 1:27.4 to 1:40.0 about 4, 1:40.0 to 1:55.5 about 5, the 2:01 bass stabs every 6.1 s (2 bars), 2:14 to 2:38.4 about 8, and the first voice-off at 0:31.7 about 10 bars after the first beat. It reports 2-, 4- and 8-bar blocks "with events landing near block boundaries", medium confidence (bar one unverified, tracker +/- 0.3 s). R1-harmony independently finds a two-bar (6.1 s) loop as the main harmonic unit in 0:00-0:20 and 2:15-2:40. The one "5-bar" block (1:40-1:55.5) and the 10-bar opening are not powers of two, so the structure is **loop-and-block based but not rigidly square**. For lineage this supports a construction from loops/stems switched on and off (sequencer or DAW logic, with the sub-sine gate fitting it) more than a band playing a through-composed arrangement in a room. It does not prove it; a quantised band could also be arranged in 4-bar blocks. Confidence: medium-low.

## 3. Connecting lineage to the other specialists

- **To rhythm:** your "felt 156, backbeat every 0.77 s, 78 as half-time" (about 60/40) matters for genre: 156 bpm with eighth hats and a backbeat is the ordinary tempo of driving guitar rock, and 78 with sixteenth hats is ordinary for slow, heavy electronic or hip-hop-derived grooves. I cannot choose, but the *syncopated kick over a rigid hat comb* suits a rock band playing to a click or a programmed loop. A skating-race subject fits a rock-speed reading (cadence of strides), which is your own analogy and mine only as interpretation.
- **To sound:** you place the production in the 2010s alt/post-rock language with a clean sine low end and saturated wall. That is the strongest sound-based lineage evidence we have. It also means I should not treat the "loud, rock-oriented" review phrase as proof of a live band: it describes the effect, not the means. Your finding that there is no steady archival noise bed under the speech gaps (vocals stem about -66 dBFS when silent) corrects a hunch of mine that the track might reuse the film's own atmosphere; the voice is lifted cleanly, and PSB's "grain as material" prior (CTX) applies to the picture, not to the audio.
- **To voice:** the film narration is the lead, as in PSB's stated method (samples drive the story, Outline interview via psb_method.md, medium-high). Your 2:44 reprise of "waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" is the lineage-relevant device: a sampled phrase returned to as a closing statement. Whether it is the same recording re-cut, or a second occurrence in the film's script, is not measurable by me; I only note that a returning sampled line is a normal PSB device, so it was most likely a deliberate edit (low-medium). I make no claim about narrator or film (CTX: unconfirmed).
- **To form and feeling:** form's reading of the piece as a plateau with texture and presence moves, and feeling's "pressure without progress" at 0:41-1:15 and the long bass-less passage, are exactly why I call this "post-rock texture without post-rock arc". The commemorative function (facts early, field in the middle, route near the end, per round 1) gives the narrative that the music declines to supply. That is a design observation, not a sourced intent.
- **To harmony:** your cold, modal, power-chord colour is the sonic link to the 1963 subject (race at about -16 degrees per the narration) and to the commissioned "loud, rock-oriented" brief; the F sub-tone drop section is the only moment that sounds engineered rather than played.

## 4. Corrections

1. **Mine:** round-1 section 3.2 (motorik resemblance by pattern) is withdrawn, see above.
2. **Mine:** I wrote the track "ends on a held D from 2:48" as a cadence-like finish. R1-harmony shows the last 20 s are a drone with one-beat stabs (C, A, B, E) and a last-2-s drift towards G/E/F# that [CH] does not label, so "held D" is the chord detector's label, and the end is better described as "a drone with a one-beat riff, then a cut". I also said "finish rather than resolution"; that stands as a feeling-level reading, but with no V-I pull anywhere (R1-harmony: no A-to-D cadence) it is not a harmonic resolution either way.
3. **Mine:** I called the 0.19 s onsets "4.2 per second" and "no fills until 3:06". R1-form counts 4.0-4.8 per second and R1-rhythm agrees on the stop at 2:47.8-2:48.2 and a ragged cluster at about 3:06-3:08; consistent.
4. **Theirs (small):** R1-form and R1-harmony date the F-drop section from 1:56, whereas my round-1 table starts "bass thumps" at 2:01 (the first thump per R1-sound/R1-rhythm is 2:01.2; 1:56 is the guitar cut, per R1-harmony section 3). They are two events, not a disagreement, but notes should keep them separate: guitar out at about 1:56, first sub hit 2:01.2.
5. **Theirs:** the window notes' CLAP-based "brass", "organ", "vinyl crackle", "bells" and "drum machine" should not appear as instrumentation in the synthesis. R1-sound and I agree; I add that CLAP's "post-rock 0.84" is in the same weak class, so my own genre placement must rest on structure, not on that score.
6. **Open and not corrected:** R1-feeling, R1-form and I all lean on "guitar" for the wall. Only R1-sound's indirect evidence (energy shape, roll-off, probability about 85% heavy overdrive, medium) and the cited review support this; no one has isolated a guitar. Banjo: no evidence.

## 5. What lineage can say now (tier summary)

- **Sourced (high):** PSB's two-part commission for the 50th anniversary of the 1963 Elfstedentocht, released autumn 2013 after the debut album; archive film and voice-over via the Fries archive; Part 1 loud and rock-oriented, Part 2 calmer and more electronic (CTX).

- **Not known:** what PSB said about this track's models; whether the drums are played; whether the wall is guitar.
