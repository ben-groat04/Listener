That is a held D3 guitar/bass note plus a crash leaking into the vocal stem as the bass and guitars fall away (form's 1:55.5-2:01 pull-down). Confidence: high that it is not an announcer. Remove it from the voice map; harmony's "D3 repeats" warnings in round 1 apply to it.
2. **The 1:46.6-1:48.4 burst is doubtful too.** [V4]: voiced fraction 0.00, 81 % at 150 Hz-3 kHz, 19 % above 3 kHz: noise-like, with no pitch at all. That is either a fricative-heavy word (unvoiced speech does exist, but 1.7 s of it with zero voicing would be unusual) or a cymbal/guitar noise burst leaking. I now rate it low confidence as speech, not the "wordless-to-me interruption" I implied. This matters because form lists "voice bursts 1:46-1:49" as a section marker and harmony reads "Cm-D-Bm under the voice" there; both should treat the voice as unconfirmed at that spot. 3. **Retraction on beat-lock.** Round 1 said the town-name onsets sit "consistently 0.2-0.4 beat after the beat". ## 3. How the voice supports the form (connection to form, rhythm, harmony)

- **Voice as the piece's clock for narrative, band as clock for time.** The band keeps a rock-steady pulse (rhythm: 78 bpm, no drift). The speech is the only element that carries *information over time*; the form specialist's three subtractions (bass, wall, speech) are therefore really two musical layers and one narrative layer. High on the distribution, medium on reading.
- **Entries follow openings, exits follow chord edges.** Starts: 1:18.9 right after bass-out at 1:15 (form event 6), 2:15.0 (the Am-D-G walk and +7 dB "other" at 2:14). The longest voice-free stretch inside the track is 1:27-2:15 (48 s, apart from the two doubtful events), which coincides with the wall at full power (1:27-1:55) and the six-second sub stabs (2:01-2:14). Those are the heaviest, least "told" passages. High on timing.

## 4. How the sound creates the feeling (voice side; interpretation labelled)

- **Distance and time.** (Observation) The speech is narrow-band (about 150 Hz-5 kHz), dry (no tail), centred, 4-5 dB under the instruments; (interpretation) a listener would hear a different era and a different place sitting calmly inside a loud modern band. The band never ducks for it (round 1: mix -12.5 vs -12.3 dBFS with and without), so the feeling is of a story *running alongside* the music, not a presenter introducing it. Confidence medium; measured parts high.
- **Calm against drive.** A flat falling-cadence male voice (median f0 about 160-196 Hz, voiced 17-49 %, no sung pitch [V4, round 1]) on top of an unbroken drum comb. If a person taps 156 bpm, the speech seems slow; if 78, it seems about equal. Either way the voice reads as objective, the music as emotional. I think the *absence* of any performed vocal is the key to the feeling: the listener does the singing. Medium (interpretive).
- **The litany.** The 2:15-2:36 town list is the nearest the voice comes to a melody or chant: short falling phrases (B3-A3-G3 contours in [LN] 2:16-2:17.5, speech-intonation drops), uneven spacing, over a harmony that finally moves (Am-D-G). I read it as the track's "journey" moment: the names are meaningful as places, but to a non-Dutch listener they work as rhythm and colour, a counted, travelling feeling. Medium.
- **The sparse start of the whole piece.** The first 9 s have no voice; the date stamp at 0:09.9 arrives after drum, wall and bass are all in (form: 0:00.8, 0:01.3, 0:03-0:08). So the first words are read as a caption for an already moving machine, not the first thing heard. High on timing.
