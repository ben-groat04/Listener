# Lineage specialist, round 3 (final): "Elfstedentocht Part 1" (video1)

British English; times m:ss. Keys: R2-x / R3-x = another specialist's round-2 / round-3 file; CTX = context.md and v1/analysis/research/*.md (psb_method.md, reception.md, artist_and_tracks.md). I ran no new measurements; I rely on colleagues' numbers and say so. Tiers: (a) sourced, (b) constrained by audio, (c) my own placement, hypothesis only. R2-rhythm was empty when I wrote round 2, so rhythm's answers come from R3-rhythm.

## 1. My round-2 questions, answered

**Q1 (to sound): is the kit sampled or played, and do the edits look like stems muted in a mix?** R3-sound: kick amplitude CV 0.18 and hat CV 0.27. This is "moderately consistent, not identical", so it does not support copy-pasted machine hits, nor does it prove a played kit (R3-rhythm agrees: fits either). The bass stem drops to about -65 dB with a flat floor, no ring-out and no room tone at 1:15 and 1:53. R3-sound reads this as suggestive of muted stems or a programmed bass, but demucs gates quiet stems, so it is not proof (medium-low). The drums stem has a real hole only at 2:48 (-59 dB). **My lineage claim is therefore this: the arrangement behaves like layers switched on a grid over a continuous drum and wall bed, which is production language; whether a band in a room made the raw layers is unresolved.** This matches CTX: Willgoose (guitar, samples, electronics) and Wrigglesworth (drums) are a live-instrument duo working with a laptop (psb_method.md, Wikipedia, medium-high for roles; no source says who played what on this track).

**Q2 (to form): is the structure exactly 2/4/8 bars once phase is fixed?** Form's file was not among the round-3 files I could use, so I rely on R3-rhythm, which re-counted from drum phase. Block lengths between structural events are whole numbers of 2-beat units (1:15 to 1:40 = 16 units, 1:40 to 1:54 = 9, 1:54 to 2:38 = 29), and the six sub drops sit at slot 21 mod 32, exactly 6.15 s apart. So the 6.15 s drop cycle is exact, but the 9 and 29 unit blocks are odd. I cannot upgrade to "rigid 2/4/8 construction". The result is **loop-built where it repeats (the sub cycle, the chord walk phase-locked to the 8-beat cycle), but not square at the section scale** (medium). This is a mild argument for sequenced or DAW-assembled layers and does not exclude a quantised band.

## 2. Debates settled with others

- **Motorik: closed.** R3-rhythm agrees with my withdrawal. Hat on every 0.385 s with the accent each 0.769 s, kick on sixteenth offbeats: an interlocking, syncopated groove, "nearer funk, hip-hop or post-rock programming", not motorik. Only the function (a fixed ground) survives. Confidence in the non-motorik pattern: medium-high.
- **Tempo and genre.** R3-rhythm now leans about 55/45 to 78 as the written quarter note (hat eighths, kick on sixteenth positions), reversing my earlier "156 rock speed" gloss. I accept this: it makes the groove more hip-hop/electronic-derived in structure while the sound (dense wall, limiting) stays rock. A slow heavy groove with a fast subdivision fits the band's "blood-pumping guitar riff" description (reception.md, Record Rewind Play) as effect, not as a pattern claim. Low-medium on genre inference from tempo.
- **Voice repeat (2:44 against 0:27).** R3-voice: same words in the transcript, same take not provable (waveform correlation 0.09, MFCC distance above speech controls), so "a frame, not a proven loop". I had called a deliberate edit "most likely"; I downgrade to **"a returned sentence, either re-cut or said twice in the source film" (low-medium)**. The device fits PSB's sample-as-narrator practice (psb_method.md) either way. Rhythm adds that the two narration exits (0:31.7 and 2:47.8) are both beat-aligned but on different beats, so the voice is edited to the grid at its exits but free across it in the town list.
- **Harmony.** The held 150 Hz tone at 1:57 is a D-pedal note in all stems, not a vocal (R3-harmony). No effect on lineage except that it confirms my round-2 correction that the end is drone plus riff, not a cadence. Mode is unresolved (third weak, at most 0.16 in power chroma per R2-harmony), so I stop using "modal" as a lineage trait and say **"pedal-based, mode-neutral"**.
- **Sound.** R3-sound: "contemporary alt/post-rock production, heavily limited" (high on the style, medium on instrument labels). That is still our best lineage evidence, and it is production, not authorship.
- **Feeling.** R2-feeling's "stop-start endurance, not build and bloom" is consistent with my "post-rock texture without post-rock arc". I note that the plateau form also suits a film-scored piece: the music holds a level while the narration and picture supply the progression (interpretation, tier c).

## 3. Placement, final

The most defensible one-line placement is **a sample-led instrumental rock piece with electronic arrangement habits, made for a film**: archive narration as lead voice, a loud guitar-like wall (identity unproven), drums that run continuously, a gated sub-sine as the one designed gesture. "Post-rock" fits the texture and instrumental, layered approach. It is not a typical post-rock crescendo piece, and "motorik/krautrock" and any Dutch/folk idiom have no support. The CLAP "post-rock 0.84" tag is weak evidence and I do not rely on it.

## 4. Settled conclusions (5 bullets)

- **Sourced (high):** PSB commissioned for the 50th anniversary of the 1963 Elfstedentocht, released autumn 2013 after the debut album *Inform - Educate - Entertain*; Part 1 is the loud, rock-oriented part ("simple blood-pumping guitar riff"), Part 2 calmer and more electronic; footage and voice via the Fries archive (CTX). The narrator and source film are unknown.
- **Genre (medium):** alternative/post-rock texture with electronic production habits and a sample-narration frame, in the 2010s production language R3-sound describes. Not motorik by pattern (kick syncopated, hat/kick interlock; R3-rhythm), no folk, banjo or Dutch idiom found.
- **Construction (medium-low):** layers behave as stems switched on a grid (flat bass floors, exact 6.15 s sub cycle at slot 21 mod 32), over continuous drums; section blocks are not strictly 2/4/8 bars (9 and 29 unit blocks). Band in a room against loop-built cannot be settled from stems (kick CV 0.18 fits both).
- **Method (medium-high, interpretation of sourced practice):** the PSB device is voice as lead, band as frame: the narration opens and closes (0:27, 2:44, same words, same take unproven) with a free-time town litany between, over an unflinching pulse; the plateau music leaves progression to the voice and picture.
- **What lineage cannot say:** instrument identity (guitar or synth in any state of `other`), whether the drums are played, what PSB cited as models, and whether the 3:08 cut is the studio ending or the video's edit.
