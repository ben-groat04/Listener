High confidence on the slot profiles (3 bands, consistent in 3 sections), medium on kick-band purity (bass bleed into the drums stem is possible, see section 4).
   - In the sub-drop block 2:01-2:38 the hat thins further: slots 0, 4, 5, 8, 10, 12, 14 only (0.4-0.66); slots 1, 3, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 are below 0.1 [R3]. That is the "hat rests" of round 1, now seen as a fixed per-cycle pattern, not random gaps.
2. **Retract "the grid shifts by 1/8 beat after the 2:48 stop".** Refit per block [R2 g]: phase 0.148 (0:05-0:35), 0.100 (1:40-2:10), 0.084 (2:15-2:45), 0.060 (2:50-3:06); period 0.1924 s before and 0.1922 s after. ## 2. Tempo octave: where I now stand

## 5. How the rhythm serves form and feeling (connections)
- **Constant ground, moving layers.** Drum rate 5.3 per second, hat pattern fixed for minutes; every form event is a subtraction or addition in bass, wall or voice, never a drum change [R1, R3]. The stop at 2:47.8 is the single exception, which is why it reads so strongly (Form, Feeling).
- **The cycle that carries form.** The 8-beat 6.15 s cycle appears twice: as sub drops (slot 21) and as chord-walk starts (slot 0 of the next cycle), with a drum burst at the second. The 2:00-2:38 block is the only part where harmony, low end and drums share one clock, which fits Feeling's "waiting for something large": a regular slow gesture over an unchanged fast pulse.
- **Sound serves the feel.** Hat at 0.385 s, kick on the sixteenths and a dry gated kit (round 1 gating; Sound: 110 ms median decay) give the stiff, mechanical, determined character; the sub's fall-to-pitch gives the leaning "drop" at slot 21.
- **Voice is the loose clock; band is the tight one.** Speech chunks (0.4-2.4 s) do not fall on the grid except at the endpoints near the stop; the contrast is part of the newsreel-over-machine character.

High on the number, low-medium on which octave (now about 55/45 in favour of 78 as the quarter note).
