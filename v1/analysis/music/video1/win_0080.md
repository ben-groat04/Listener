## Summary
A two-part window. For the first 7 s (1:20 to 1:27) the band is a loose, bass-less groove with the narrator's list of competitors speaking over it in several bursts. At about 1:27 the voice stops for good, the guitar/synth layer ("other") becomes a much louder, wider, sustained wall locked on a D pedal, and the drums carry on unchanged. There is still no bass for almost the whole window; the low end only comes back at 1:40, which is the start of the next window [lvl, img100]. So this is a "wall without a floor": big and wide, but airy underneath, waiting for the bass to return.

Reverb/space: no long tails visible on drum hits; the wall's long sustained harmonic lines suggest sustain/echo on the lead tone, but I cannot separate sustain from reverb. CLAP sound tags ("wind" 0.84 to 0.95, "vinyl crackle" 0.99 at 1:30) are weak; "wind" probably just reflects a broadband noisy wash, and I do not claim any real vinyl or wind sound.

## Voice and words
- **Speech, not singing, in Dutch archive narration.** Whisper transcribes 1:19.74 to 1:26.90 "575 wedstrijdrijders, rond 9500 toerrijders, een aantal Zweden, Canadezen, Zwitsers en ..." and translates "575 race drivers. Around 9500 tour riders, a number of Dutchmen (machine translation of 'Zweden', which means Swedes), Canadians, Swiss and Belgians" [lyr] (the last words are garbled; "Dalmatians" in the Dutch pass is an error, and the English "Dutchmen"/"Belgians" are also uncertain; I would trust the numbers more than the nationalities). Meaning: 575 competition skaters, about 9,500 tour skaters, from several countries. This agrees with the research file (9,862 starters) [context.md].
- **How it sits:** the vocals stem has about five bursts in the first 7 s [img80]: x about 0 to 1, 1.3 to 3.7, 4.2 to 5.2 (short), 5.4 to 7.1 (two bursts with a tiny gap at about 3.9), with level -20 to -30 dBFS (peaks -20.8 at 1:19, -22 at 1:22, 1:24 to 1:26) [lvl]. That is 6 to 10 dB below the drums and about the same as the "other" layer's loudest stab, so the voice is audible on top. Harmonic ladder (formant bands up to 4 kHz) in the vocal stem makes it a real human voice, a flat, old-newsreel type delivery with no singing [img80; clap "radio announcer speech" 1.0 at 1:20 to 1:30, "no vocals" 1.0 from 1:30]. It is treated like a lead instrument, a sample laid over the beat; the band moves out of its way: the voice ends at 1:27.1 and the guitars immediately surge into the space it leaves (see below). Confidence high.
- **Silence of the voice from 1:27 to 1:40 (about 13 s):** no speech or singing at all; the vocal stem is at the floor [lvl]. The only sound that is "voice-like" is leakage.

## How this window moves the song on
- **From 1:00 to 1:20 [img60]:** the previous window was a D-pedal build (wide, bright, bass heavy), then a release by subtraction at 1:15 (bass out, thin bright "other", spoken voice returning at 1:18.8). This window continues that bass-less, voice-led state through 1:27, then escalates: the voice leaves, "other" re-enters as a loud wide wall with a locked D/A pedal, and the drums keep pace. It is a second build that happens without bass.
- **To 1:40 and beyond [img100]:** at 1:40 the bass stem returns at -19 dBFS and the mix is louder (-10 to -11 dBFS) and narrower, the chroma bright D with C/G movement, and a short voice burst at 1:46.5 to 1:49 [img100]. So the long bass-less passage resolves (the "floor" arrives) at the end of this window. The whole 1:15 to 1:40 stretch can be heard as one long suspension that finally lands.
- Contrast within the window: sparse and speech-led (1:20 to 1:27) versus dense, loud, wide, voiceless (1:27 to 1:40).

## What a human listener would feel, and why
- **1:20 to 1:27, informational, buoyant, a little restless.** Causes: the narrator's speech is clear (-21 to -25 dBFS vs mix -15) and rhythmic, delivered as a list of numbers, so it feels like a news report spoken over a cheerful, lilting groove; no bass, a light "other" (-20 to -25 dBFS), steady brisk drums at 4 hits/s keep forward motion; chords step C-F-G-D, which sounds hopeful and open rather than dark. CLAP gives "playful" 0.998 at 1:20 (weak evidence, but it agrees).
- **1:27: a sudden lift and a rush of energy.** Causes: the loudness jumps 2.5 to 3 dB and stereo width jumps from 0.36 to 0.78 in two seconds; the voice stops, giving a feeling of the band taking over; the guitar/synth centroid rises, so it sounds brighter and bigger. CLAP "triumphant" 0.91 for 1:25 to 1:30 (weak).
- **1:27 to 1:40, driving, wide, soaring, a bit suspended or yearning.** Causes: static D pedal (little harmonic change) gives a trance-like, hypnotic momentum; the sustained high lines against pulsing drums feel like gliding over a steady beat; very wide image feels immersive; but the missing bass leaves the mix without a foundation, and the occasional G/C/F colour (1:34 to 1:38) makes it lean forward unresolved. The expectation of the bass is met at 1:40, which would feel like a release and a thump. "Nostalgic" 0.95 to 1.0 in [clap] for 1:30 to 1:40 is plausible but it is a noisy label; I note it as weak support for a warm, wistful tone (consistent with the old film narration, not with anything I measured in the music itself).
- I would not call this aggressive: although the wall is distorted, the harmony is open (a bare fifth), tempo moderate and CLAP "aggressive" is near 0 (0.02 at 1:25).

## Confidence and gaps
High: bass absent almost all window and returning at 1:40; drums constant at about 4 onsets per second; voice only in the first 7 s, then none; loudness and width step at 1:27; beat spacing 0.77 s. Medium: D pedal with a bare fifth; early C-F-G-D movement; cause of width change; subdivision of drums. Low: instrument identity of "other" (guitar vs synth/organ), pitch of the sustained high lines, exact words after "toerrijders", any of the Am/Dm chord labels, the lead-tracker notes after 1:27 (they are leakage).
