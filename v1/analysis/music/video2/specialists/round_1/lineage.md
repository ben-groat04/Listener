## 1. Verified facts (artist, commission, place in the catalogue)
- PSB are a London duo (Willgoose, Wrigglesworth) who set archive film and speech to new music. Sample-led storytelling: Willgoose says the speech samples drive the story (psb_method.md, Outline interview). H for the method, M-H for the quote.
- Commissioned by the Explore the North festival, Leeuwarden, for the 50th anniversary of the 1963 race; footage and voice-over from the Fries Film Archief (context.md; writteninmusic.com, 4 Nov 2013). H.
- Released autumn 2013 (digital 11 Nov 2013; RSD 7" in 2014). It sits next to, not inside, the debut album *Inform - Educate - Entertain* (May 2013), as a bonus/commissioned single. H on commission, M on format.
- Contemporary description: Part 1 "stevig", rock-oriented; Part 2 "calmer, more electronic" (Written In Music). Track of the Day (May 2014): "an explosion of driving rhythm and gutsy guitars followed by a spell of glowing ambience". H as quotes.
- No Willgoose statement about these two tracks was found (reception.md). So **what the artist was trying to do is inferred from the commission, the method and the sound, not from his words.** Do not present it as his stated aim.

## 2. What the measurements say, in terms of lineage
These are the facts that any genre claim has to rest on.
1. **Key and harmony:** B minor (whole song 0.88, audio_features/summary.json); Bm about 136 s of 305 s, then D 44 s, Em 22, G 20 (music_views/summary.json). Looping minor-mode progressions on i / VI-III-VII-type moves (Bm, G, D, A, Em), changing every 4 s early (0:40-1:00) and every 8 s later (2:00-2:20, win_0120). Win 0200/0240: a held B-D-F# triad with bass roots D-B-A (3:20-3:36, pyin in win_0200). H on the key, M on the loop labels (chord detector on dense mixes).
2. **Tempo:** the beat tracker gives 161.5 bpm (beat gaps 0.372 s, beat_times.json). My own autocorrelation of the onset envelope (1:40-2:40, song.wav) peaks at 0.998 s and 1.997 s, with 0.372 and 0.743 s lower. 0.372 s is 3 sixteenths at 121 bpm (0.5 s beat, 0.125 s sixteenth) and the drum-onset gaps of 0.12-0.14 s (win_0260/0280) are sixteenths of that grid. My reading: **a 4/4 feel near 120-121 bpm with 16th-note hat/drum activity, which the tracker locks on as a dotted-eighth pulse (161 bpm).** Medium; the win_0180 note's "161 bpm four-on-the-floor" is a tracker artefact I cannot fully rule out. This matters for lineage: ~120 bpm, straight, steady and unaccelerating is dance/electronic and krautrock-adjacent tempo territory, not rock-band feel (rock drummers drift; here the beat grid is machine-regular, win_0300 "last hit exactly on the beat grid").
3. **Sound palette:** dense, bright "other" stem to 8 kHz with fine horizontal striations (win_0240, 0260, 0280), a low, sub-200 Hz bass that pulses or is gated, tight dry drums with 4.4 kHz centroid (win_0220). CLAP song average: drum machine 0.63, organ 0.18; synth-pop 0.35, electronic 0.23, **krautrock 0.16**, post-rock 0.06 (clap_tags.json). Weak evidence; but it agrees with the stems and with the 2013 press ("more electronic"). I cannot tell guitar from synth in "other" (every window note says so); a distorted guitar layer is possible (CLAP "electric guitar" 0.90 at 4:30-4:35, win_0260) but it is a noisy model.
4. **Form (from the notes):** drumless pre-groove 0:00-0:28; hard groove entry 0:28; narrator over the groove 0:42-1:19; breakdown to bare bed 1:20-1:28; full re-entry 1:28.5; heaviest block from 1:41 (mix +3 dB, win_0100) to 2:37; second narration 2:25-2:36; instrumental B-pedal plateau 2:40-3:20; chopped "vocal" bursts from 3:24 and a call-and-response 3:40-4:20 (win_0200/0220); the bass drops out at 4:36-4:48 leaving "other" and drums (win_0260/0280); stop at 5:00.8, ringing tail, one last full-band hit at 5:02.3, digital silence from about 5:04.3 (win_0300). This is **additive, loop-based, plateau-driven form with gradual layer changes and a hard-stop ending.** It is dance-music/krautrock architecture (long steady grooves, slow evolution), not verse-chorus song form. H on the shape, M on interpretation.
5. **Voice:** no sung lead. Dutch archive commentary (0:42-1:19, 1:49-1:56, 2:25-2:36) plus later vocal-stem bursts (3:24-4:59) that win_0220/0240 call processed, chopped, call-and-response material, not sustained singing. CLAP "no vocals" 0.97 and "radio announcer speech" only 0.008 show the model does not hear speech at all, so it is useless for this layer. The ASR words "Stuck/End of the road/Climbing up" (3:32-3:58, context.md) are low confidence; I did not re-test them.

## 3. Lineage: where this sits (interpretation, graded)
I separate what the evidence supports from what is my reading of genre history.

**A. Within PSB's own work (M, evidence = research files + stems).**
- The parent album's mode is sample-plus-live-band indie rock with narration. Part 1 matches that (guitar riff, per Record Rewind Play). Part 2 is where PSB lean into the electronic side: a machine-regular groove, long plateaus, a bass that is gated, and narration used as a texture. That is consistent with the band's own "calmer, more electronic" label, with one caveat: **the measurements do not show "calm".** Mix level is -9 to -13 dBFS for the last two minutes (win_0240, win_0280), drums run at 5.5 onsets per second (win_0260), and bright broadband energy never lets up. "Calm" is true of the harmonic stillness (B-minor pedal, slow loops) and of the dynamic flatness, not of the energy. A listener would more likely feel "relentless, steady, hypnotic" than "calm". This is a real mismatch between the press wording and the stems that other specialists should weigh.
- It runs against the usual PSB habit of putting the sample over a rock band's changes: here the Dutch voice is *interleaved* with the groove (0:42-1:19, a gap 1:11-1:19, then 1:20 voice with the band falling away) and later is cut into rhythmic fragments. This second use, voice as rhythmic instrument, is the more electronic idea. M.

**B. Wider genre (M-L; all genre naming is my inference from structure, not from a source).**
- **Krautrock / motorik-descended electronic rock.** Features that fit: steady, unaccelerating 4/4 at about 120 bpm; long static harmony (a B-minor pedal for minutes: B-D-F# held in every frame of win_0200); additive layering; hypnotic repetition; a hard stop. CLAP's third-ranked genre is krautrock (0.16; it names it at 3:40 and elsewhere, win_0220), which is weak but independent evidence. What does *not* fit: no motorik "ride-and-snare" figure can be confirmed from my measurements, and the drums are 16th-busy and machine-bright, which is closer to electronic dance drumming. So "krautrock-adjacent" at best (L-M).
- **Synth-pop / electronica / post-rock crossover.** CLAP top genre is synth-pop (0.35), electronic 0.23; the "other" stem is a dense saw-like wash. The gradual-build, plateau and sudden-stop construction, plus the guitar-or-synth ambiguity, is common to **post-rock and to mid-2000s electronic rock** (the kind of loop-built, drum-machine-driven indie of the period). I would name those as comparison families, not influences. L-M.
- **Hauntology / archive-sample electronica** (Boards of Canada, Ghost Box and similar, cited in cultural_context.md). The shared device is old public-information film used as material, and CLAP's "nostalgic" 0.58 and "vinyl crackle" 0.46 are consistent with a nostalgic archive texture. But PSB's *music* here is bright, clean and propulsive rather than faded and woozy, and critics describe drive, not mourning (reception.md says no source speaks of nostalgia or grief). So I rate hauntology as a **lineage of method (archive as raw material), not of sound**. M for method, L for sound.
- **Dotted-eighth echo-guitar / U2-Edge-type sound.** The 3-sixteenth pulse (0.372 s) against the 120 bpm grid would be the signature of a delay locked to dotted eighths. That is tempting but I can only see the periodicity in the drum-and-mix onsets, not a guitar with delay. **Speculation (L); flagged as a question.**
- **Sport/documentary scoring tradition.** Endurance-race footage with a pulse that does not let up and a hard stop is a familiar documentary-sport device; the steady grid is the music's way of "being" 200 km. Interpretation, not evidence (L).

## 4. What was the artist trying to do? (inference)
Brief from the commission: to make a new piece for the 50th anniversary that carries the "hel van '63". PSB's known method says the samples lead and the band frames them (psb_method.md). Measured here: the voice enters on top of a ready-made groove (0:42, 0:28 groove start earlier), disappears for long stretches (no speech 2:00-2:25 per win_0140; 2:37-3:24), and returns fragmented, so the music carries the endurance (steady pulse, ever-fuller texture, bass gating at 4:36 to give the final 20 s a stripped, nervous edge) while the voice gives the facts (date, -16 degrees, 200 km, 575 racers). The final stop, with a ring-out and one hit on the grid (5:02.3), is a designed full stop, not a fade; it can be heard as a finishing line or as the thaw (the video ends on ice floes, artist_and_tracks.md). That reading (race-as-pulse, thaw-as-silence) is **mine, M-L**, not a stated intention.
