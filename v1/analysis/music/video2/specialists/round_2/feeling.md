## 2. Questions put to me, answered

**R-lineage Q3: are the long B-minor plateaus experienced as hypnotic calm, rising pressure, or exhaustion?** My answer is that they are three different feelings in sequence, and the measurements separate them:
- **2:37-3:24, hypnotic (medium-high).** Mix flat at -12 to -13, "other" *down* to -26/-29 at 2:37-2:48 then slowly back, no voice, width opening 0.33 to 0.5 [LVL]. No new element for 47 s; the brain stops tracking the loop. Calm is fair here only in the sense of "not attention-seeking"; the 120 BPM machine kick is not calm in itself.
- **3:24-4:36, rising pressure (medium).** The mix is at its loudest (-9.7 to -10.0 at 4:30-4:36 [LVL]), "other" is up 10 dB from its trough, hats go to sixteenths everywhere (R-rhythm), 2-10 kHz band +14 dB (R-form), and the voice becomes rapid bursts. Each is a pressure cue: more energy, higher pitch, denser subdivision, with no harmonic arrival. Press language of "calm" (R-lineage) is not what the numbers suggest for this stretch; it fits the 0:00-0:28 prelude and the 2:37-3:20 trance.
- **4:37-5:01, exhaustion (medium).** The mix falls 2-3 dB at 4:37 and never recovers, the voice becomes a 2 s metronome (R-voice), the bass goes to on/off stabs, width opens. The musical mass is draining while the clock keeps going. A runner who has slowed but not stopped is the best image, but note it is an image: the tempo does not change [R-rhythm].
- **Moments that change the reading:** 4:37 (pressure to exhaustion) and 5:01 (exhaustion to stop). The 4:37 step is the single most important emotional pivot in the second half and no earlier specialist gave it its own line except R-form's "withdrawal".

**R-harmony: is the B pedal plus rising-third lift (Em to G, A to D) hopeful or elegiac?** Elegiac with a lift, never fully hopeful (medium). The lifts are real (G and D are major, bass rises a minor third each time: E to G, A to D) and R-harmony's "sunlit minor" is fair for the 0:28-2:37 loop. But the lift is always followed by a fall back to the home B: loop A ends on the B bar (the "odd one", 0:52, 1:20, 1:53, 2:21, 2:37 [R-harmony]), loop B is B - A - D - B. A rise that is cancelled every 12-16 s feels like effort that does not accumulate. That is why the track reads as endurance rather than triumph. The B bar after D carries a pull-back (dominant-like per R-form Q2), which I take as the "held breath" feel at 0:52 and 1:20; at 1:20 it comes right before the break.
Is a modal drone-over-arpeggio known elsewhere? That is R-lineage's job; I only note it explains my "rolling wheel": a fixed reference note under a moving loop removes the sense of leaving anywhere.

**R-voice Q on loud 4:38.5 burst.** [LVL] vocals stem 4:38 is not abnormal (-33.7 at 4:38, -57 at 4:39); the dark gap I saw in the window note at 4:38.3-4:38.8 coincides with the 4:37 mix step. I cannot call it voice or drum leak. I read it as the audible "hinge" of the sag; low confidence on its source.

**R-form Q (confirm arrangement-contrast ranking against AF boundaries 1:41 and 3:37).** [AF] 3:37 matches my plateau ramp but it is only an +1 to +2 dB step [LVL], not a 5-18 dB event; it does not enter my top-ranked causes. The boundaries that deliver felt contrast are 0:28, 1:24-1:28.5, 1:41, 4:37 and 5:01 (all >=3 dB in a second or two). 1:16 in [AF] matches the first D-chord hit I noted; low.

## 4. Revised arc, short

Held breath (0:00-0:28), stride begins (0:28), testimony over a restrained band (0:42-1:20), stillness and jolt (1:24-1:41, with the grid arguably re-set), locked machine and the sentences that give scale (1:41-2:37), trance (2:37-3:24), voice becomes percussion and pressure rises (3:24-4:37), sag (4:37-4:48), stutter (4:49-5:00), blunt stop (5:01).
