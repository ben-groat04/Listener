## 1. What I change from round 1

1. **Tempo: I withdraw the "dotted-eighth pulse is a guitar delay" lean and accept 120 bpm in 4/4 (H).** Rhythm [R1-rhythm] and harmony [R1-harmony] both found a 0.5 s grid, 2.0 s bar, and my own round-1 autocorrelation agreed. New, from the drum stem: autocorrelation at 0.5 s is 0.65 (1:00-1:40), 0.45 (2:40-3:20), 0.39 (4:00-4:40); at 1.0 s 0.64/0.58/0.56; at 2.0 s 0.66/0.60/0.56. The 0.375 s lag is weak in the drums (0.19-0.27). So the tracker's 161 bpm is a bass/other-stem artefact, as rhythm says.
2. **Where the 0.375 s (dotted-quaver) pulse lives.** New: bass stem autocorrelation at 0.375 s is 0.16 (1:00-1:40), 0.20 (2:40-3:20), 0.27 (4:00-4:40), while 0.5 s is about 0 in the bass after 2:40. The "other" stem has 0.375 s at 0.69/0.71/0.59 but also 0.5 s at 0.68/0.73/0.53 and 2.0 s at 0.77/0.78/0.71, so "other" carries both grids at once. This supports rhythm's claim that the dotted figure is a played bass ostinato, and shows "other" repeats on the bar and on the 0.375 s cell. It does not separate "delay" from "arpeggiator" or "played"; I still cannot say (L). I therefore drop "U2/Edge-style dotted-eighth delay guitar" as a lineage claim: it rested on a tracker artefact, and the bass, not a guitar, is the clearest carrier.
3. **"Motorik" is not supported; "four-on-the-floor dance" is (M-H).** I asked rhythm for kick/snare placement. I measured it myself (drum stem 4:00-4:40, onset phase within the 2 s bar, 16 steps of 0.125 s, latency 0.10 s removed). Kick band (30-120 Hz) onsets, counts per step: steps 0, 4, 8, 12 = 20 each (one on every beat), plus 17 at step 11 and small counts elsewhere. The snare band (180-400 Hz) is 20 on the same four steps, which is kick bleed as much as snare, so I cannot isolate a backbeat in this band (rhythm's "backbeat every 1.0 s", win_0200, is not contradicted but not confirmed by me). Hat band (6-10 kHz) fills steps 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 11, 13, 14 broadly: busy sixteenths. The motorik figure (Neu!/Can: kick on 1 and 3 with snare/backbeat on 2 and 4, steady 8th-note ride, no kick on every beat) is therefore the wrong template. A kick on every beat with sixteenth hats and an added off-beat kick at step 11 is house/techno/electro-rock drumming. I downgrade my round-1 "krautrock-adjacent" to **long-form, steady-grid, additive structure shared with krautrock, but drum language from dance music** (L-M). The CLAP krautrock 0.16 [CLAP] is not evidence for the drums.
4. **"Calm" (press) vs the stems: stands, now with form and feeling agreeing.** Feeling's measured rise from -26 to about -10 dBFS and form's "single long crescendo" [R1-form, R1-feeling, LVL] back my round-1 point. The press word "calmer" fits only the first 42 s, the harmonic stillness and the absence of rock-band attack. I would write "calmer than Part 1 in gesture, not in energy" (M).

## 2. Questions put to me

Static modal harmony with a pedal and a broken-triad figure (B-F#-D at 3:00-3:08, 4:00-4:08 per [R1-harmony]) is common to post-rock (slow-build, loop-layered, e.g. the Mogwai/Explosions in the Sky family), minimal electronic music and krautrock-descended electronica. I can name the families, not a documented influence; no source in our research says PSB drew on any of them for these tracks (L-M).
- Within PSB's work, research only supports: sample-led narration over a live-band-and-electronics bed (psb_method.md) and Part 1 as the "stevig" rock half (Written In Music, in context.md). I have no evidence about how other PSB tracks harmonise; I will not claim a "house style".
- Hopeful or elegiac: the harmonic facts are B Aeolian with a flat sixth (G) and relative-major lifts to D [R1-harmony]. Rising thirds into the relative major are a conventional "lift" gesture. Combined with feeling's reading of "striding, jogging pulse" and a bass that gates at 4:36-4:48 [R1-feeling, R1-form], I read it as **determined/elegiac-heroic, not triumphant** (M-L). The documented reception speaks of drive and physical effort, not grief (reception.md), which supports "determined" over "mournful".

## 3. Connecting lineage to the others

- **Rhythm to form to lineage.** A kick on every beat, machine-locked for 200+ s with no drift (rhythm: phase constant 1:40-5:00), plus additive layers [R1-form], makes the piece function like a dance-music track: arrival of layers, not changes of chord, is the narrative. That is exactly why a documentary-sport device works: a grid that never lets up is the music's version of 200 km. My round-1 "race-as-pulse, thaw-as-silence" reading stands as interpretation (M-L). The hard stop at 5:00.8 with one grid-locked hit at 5:02.3 [R1-rhythm, win_0300] is typical of the dance-edit/film-cue ending, not of a rock outro that would ring or fade.
- **Voice design to PSB method.** Voice's finding that narration (0:42-2:36) turns into chopped percussive bursts (3:24-4:59) [R1-voice] is the strongest piece of PSB-specific lineage I have: PSB normally use samples as story, and here in the second half they become rhythm. That is a departure from the method in psb_method.md, not the norm. It is also where "more electronic" is most literally true. The 48 s voice-free stretch 2:36-3:24 [R1-voice, R1-feeling] therefore reads as the hinge between "words" and "texture". I leave the intent as inference: no Willgoose statement exists for these tracks (reception.md).
- **Sound to lineage.** Sound's finding that the drums are the flat loudness anchor (-18 to -19 dBFS from 1:28) and that "other" and bass supply the rise [R1-sound] fits an electronic-production approach (constant drum bed, build through the synth layers) more than a live rock approach where drums swell. M.
- **Harmony to lineage.** The 8-bar Em-G-D-B and 6-bar B-A-D loops [R1-harmony] are loop lengths typical of DAW-built music (6-bar is unusual for rock song form). Supports electronic construction (M-L; no knowledge of PSB's workflow).
