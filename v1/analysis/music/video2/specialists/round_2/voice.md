## 1. What changed in my view
1. **The 120 bpm / 2.000 s bar is now settled** for the voice question. Rhythm's kick fit (period 0.49995 s, constant phase, [RH-R2]) agrees with my round-1 reading of the 4:40-4:59 bursts. High.
2. **The 4:40-4:59 bursts are mostly not pitched. This corrects my round 1.** In round 1 I described the late material as "voiced (harmonic ladders, 200-1000 Hz stripes)". pyin on [S3] finds *no* voiced frames in 7 of the 10 pulse bursts (4:40.7, 4:46.8, 4:48.7, 4:50.8, 4:52.7, 4:54.7, 4:56.8, 4:58.8: 0 voiced of 28-56 frames each; only 4:42.6, a 3.1 s block, has f0, G#4 median, 51/132 frames). The earlier run 3:32-4:31 does show voiced frames (see section 3). So the late pulse is better described as gated, breathy or consonant-like or noisy-harmonic stabs, not sung or spoken vowels. Pitch tracker failure on short gated bursts is possible, so medium on "unpitched", high on "no stable pitch found".
3. **The bursts sit at one constant phase.** On the kick grid, burst starts fall at 1.19-1.29 beats (mod 4) for all ten of 4:40.7 ... 4:58.8, spread 0.10 beat (about 50 ms). That is rock-steady locking to the grid (high). Whether this is "beat 1" depends on where bar one is, which I cannot see: Rhythm counts from a chord-lattice downbeat and reports 3.8-4.0 for the same events, mine is counted from a kick-grid zero, so the two origins differ by roughly 1.4 beats and I will not claim the absolute beat. What both agree on: one burst per bar, same position every bar, about 50-100 ms of scatter. Medium-high.

## 2. Answers to the questions put to me

**Harmony Q1 (pitch of each burst's strongest voiced partial; fixed note or scoop?; any relation to bass root B/A/D?).** [S3] f0 medians are: for the ten pulse bursts, nothing stable (above). For the run before it (3:32-4:31): G4 in 3:50.1, 3:54.3, 3:56.2 (5.7 s, 160/244 frames voiced), 4:12.7 and 4:23.9 (7.4 s, 199/319); G#4 in 4:16.3, 4:42.6; A4 at 3:32.5 and 3:38.7; E4 at 3:36.5; F5/G#5 at 4:02-4:05; C5 at the 4:38.6 burst. So where a pitch exists it is most often **G4/G#4**, not B, F# or A. Against the B pedal that is a minor sixth (G4) or a clash by a semitone (G#4), and G is a chord tone of the earlier Em-G-D slot of loop A. I read this as speech intonation of a quasi-sustained vowel (a "wah"-like contour, consistent with the lead tracker's G4-A#4 chatter in [R1] and the harmony specialist's note that the lead line is speech contour) rather than a deliberately tuned drone stab. No relation to the bass root can be shown because there is no systematic change of f0 between loop-B slots (G4 recurs regardless). Confidence: medium on "most common f0 is G4", low on any harmonic intent.

**Rhythm Q (is the narrator's timing placed to the grid?; round 1 rhythm file asked whether the human narrator's rhythm is placed to the grid).** Measured now [S3]: narration phrase starts at 0:42.6, 0:51.3, 0:59.2, 1:04.5, 1:20.1, 1:24.5, 1:49.3, 1:53.5, 2:25.5, 2:29.1, 2:32.6 sit at 0.94, 2.34, 2.05, 0.85, 3.85, 0.75, 2.36, 2.66, 2.66, 1.96, 0.86 beats on the same scale. That is **not** a single phase: the speech is free against the grid, which fits a recorded narration laid over a machine (high). A weaker pattern: four of the eleven starts (0:42.6, 1:04.5, 1:24.5, 2:32.6) fall within 0.75-0.94, and these are the entries after a gap or after the band stepped back; the late bursts sit at 1.2. Only 0.2-0.4 beat from each other, i.e. possibly the same bar-position. That is suggestive, not proof (low-medium; eleven points, grid-origin uncertainty, onset error 50 ms plus separation lag). I do **not** support a claim that the narration was beat-quantised; the 2:25-2:36 list fits chord slots (Harmony's finding, still standing: starts 0.2-0.6 s after the change, ends 0.6 s before the next), not beats.

**Sound Q (do the 3:24+ bursts share the narrator's band-limited character, and is the 4:38.5 burst a voice or a drum leak?).** [S3] 95 % roll-off *within detected speech only* is 4.4 kHz (1:04-1:10), 4.9 kHz (2:25-2:28) and 4.6 kHz (2:29-2:32); the late bursts are 3.0-5.4 kHz (typically 3.9-5.0). Centroid is 1.6-2.2 kHz for narration and 0.9-2.9 kHz for bursts. **The ranges overlap**, so the late material is compatible with the same band-limited source (an archive recording); it is not proof, since stem separation limits the top end for both. This also qualifies Sound's window-level figure (roll-off 2.5 kHz at 1:05-1:20 against 4.1 kHz at 4:10-4:25): it was taken over whole windows including silence and bleed, and when I restrict to detected phrases the narration is not duller than the bursts. So "different recording or different processing" is not needed to explain the difference (medium). The 4:38.6 burst: 0.7 s, f0 median C5 with 24 of 31 frames voiced, centroid 2.56 kHz, vocal-stem level about 18 dB under the drum stem there. Voiced frames argue for a vocal-like sound rather than a snare/noise hit leaking in (medium-low; a bright pitched synth stab in "other" could also leak). It coincides with the bass gap Form and Feeling call out (about 4:38.3-4:38.8), so it works as an exclamation or marker of the change (medium).

**Form Q (is 2:36-3:24 a verse-to-chorus gap? does it mark where words become rhythm? do 1:28.7 and 2:25 voice-gated re-entries match your boundaries?).** Voice absences are 0:00-0:42, 1:28.7-1:49.2 (20.5 s), 1:56.4-2:25.4 (29 s), 2:36-3:24 (48 s, Feeling counts 2:35-3:28), then no absence to the end except a sparse 4:31-4:38. Harmony places the loop switch (Em-G-D-B to B-A-D) at 2:36.8, exactly where the voice stops. That gives a **two-part measured boundary at 2:36.8**: last narration ends 2:36.2 and the new loop starts 0.6 s later (high on times, medium on the interpretation that this is a deliberate "last word, new part"). The change of voice *kind* happens 48 s later, at 3:24-3:28, and nothing else in the other specialists' boundaries is there (Form has 3:20 "peak plateau" begins, Sound has 3:36 "other" brightening). I propose **3:24-3:28 as the words-to-rhythm boundary**, with 3:36-3:40 as the point where the voice becomes dense (Form's 3:36 marker fits, because the 3:40.5-4:22 run starts there). The two voice-gated re-entries (1:28.5 band returns as voice leaves; 2:25 voice enters over an already-running band) are different gestures; only the first is a "gate" (band thin while voice speaks). So the voice does **not** consistently predict form: it predicts it only at 1:04, 1:24-1:28.7 and 2:36.8 (medium-high).

**Feeling Q3 (are the 3:40-4:40 bursts looped or different phrases? is the voice a hook?).** Same answer as round 1 with one new number: ten pulse bursts at one bar spacing and constant phase look like a *retriggered element*, but pyin shows them with different durations (0.7-1.3 s) and roll-offs (3.7-5.4 kHz), and unpitched, so they are not a single identical sample played ten times (medium). A fair wording: "a repeating vocal-derived stab or breath, re-cut each bar, with some variation". In 3:40-4:22 the bursts are different from each other (varied f0, G4/F3/G#5, durations 0.8-5.7 s), so that stretch is not a loop; it is a *different-phrase* run that the ear reads as babble or chant (medium). As a hook: the voice only becomes a recognisable repeating motif at 4:40, i.e. in the last 20 seconds, after the bass leaves. Before that it is texture 8-25 dB under the drums (R1 levels), so it is a late, quiet hook rather than a main one (medium).

## 3. Cross-links to other specialists
- **Rhythm and voice serve form together (high):** the voice's two modes line up with the two grooves Rhythm describes: free-timed sentences over the sparser, 4 s-accent groove (0:42-1:28, drums 11 dB lower per Form), then bar-locked stabs over the dense groove (4:40 on). So the grid is firm while speech is free; when the speech disappears into texture, the texture *snaps to the grid*. That is the track's central voice idea: human timing replaced by machine timing.
- **Harmony:** the narration segment 2:25-2:36 occupies exactly one 4-slot cycle (E, G, D then B: 2:24.8, 2:28.8, 2:32.8, 2:36.8 from [HA-R2]); with three phrases of about 3 s each and a pause between, a chord slot per phrase. Together with the 2:36.8 loop switch this is the strongest evidence in the track that voice and harmony were edited together (medium-high; I still cannot say which was cut to which).
- **Sound/Feeling:** the bandwidth opening at 0:42 (Sound) coincides with the first speech; the feeling is "documentary gravity" (Feeling). The measured dry, centred, band-limited voice is the cause of "archive grain" more than any noise layer (Sound found no hiss). Dry and centred in stem width (width 0.55-0.65 when speaking) reads as authority and distance at once (interpretation, medium). The later bursts are wider (Sound: vocals S/M -1.6 dB at 3:40-4:20 against -9.9 at 1:40-2:20), so the voice moves from centre (a statement) to surround (a crowd or breath), which matches the "told about it, then in it" arc (interpretation, medium-low).
- **Lineage:** the PSB method (speech leads, band frames) holds for 0:42-2:36; after 3:24 it reverses, the voice being framed or consumed by the groove. Lineage's "voice as rhythmic instrument is the electronic idea" is supported by the bar-locked pulse (medium-high); but "chopped sample" is unproven, since the burst audio could be any vocal-like source.
