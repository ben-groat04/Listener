## 2. Answers put to me

The pulse (drum stem about -19 dBFS, constant) never changes until the last bar [Rhythm], so by the numbers the track remains a groove, with less in it, until 5:00.8. What changes at 4:36.8 and 4:48.8 is what fills the groove. "Exhaustion" is an interpretation I can defend only as medium: the bright layer leaves first and the bass last-but-stuttering, which is the reverse of a build.
- **Sound (via Voice): chopped voice at 3:24.75 on a cell start.** Unchanged (H on arithmetic: 24.0 bars after 2:36.8). - **Harmony: is the break part of the cycle?** Harmony settled that E at 1:28.5 begins a fresh E-G-D cycle. I agree and use it in the map below: the break is a seam between two harmonic cycles, not a cell of either.

## 3. Connections

- **Rhythm serves the form by not moving.** After 1:28.6 no fill, stop or gap longer than 0.32 s marks 2:36.8, 3:24.8, 3:36.8 or 4:36.8 [Rhythm]. The pulse is the constant; every section change is timbre, level, loop and voice. The two exceptions are the 1:24-1:28.6 silence and the last bar, i.e. the only moments where the pulse itself is the event. The song therefore has exactly one rhythmic "event" at the front (drop) and one at the back (stop), which frame an arc of pure timbre. (H.)
- **Sound creates the form.** "Other" is the carrier of every post-1:28 boundary: mute 2:36.8, body 2:48.8, ramp 3:00-3:50, plateau to 4:36, loss at 4:36.8. All on 6-bar cell starts. The designer automates one layer on the loop grid. (H on times, M on mechanism.)
- **Harmony.** Two loops, two halves; B Aeolian throughout (Harmony). The loop switch at 2:36.8 is the one harmonic boundary and it coincides with the narrator's last word (2:36.2) and the partial mute. Three things stacked on one cell start (voice out, loop change, layer mute) make 2:36.8 the best-engineered transition after the drop. (H.)
- **Voice.** Narration fills the first half, chopped fragments the second; the hand-offs are 1:28.6 (voice stops 0.1-0.2 s before the kick), 2:36.2 and 3:24.75. The voice's role (witness then texture) follows the form's two arcs.
- **Feeling.** Calm surface (fixed pulse, no cadence) with timbral pressure underneath (top band up about 28 dB, onset rate 4 to 5.3 per s) is consistent across every specialist's numbers. The pivot at 4:36.8 is where that pressure is withdrawn.
- **Lineage.** A fixed four-on-the-floor skeleton with one-layer automation on a loop grid is DAW electronic form, not rock song form: no verse/chorus, no cadence, no modulation.

## 4. Final song map (m:ss, bars of 2.000 s)

1. 0:00-0:28 pad alone, thuds every 4 s (H).
2. 0:28-1:24 groove (loop A, E-G-D with B tag); narrator from 0:42.7; drums stripped to the pad and voice by 1:04-1:24 (H).
3. 1:24-1:28.6 hole: bass and drums silent about 4 s; narrator ends; hard return on beat 1 (H).
4. 1:28.6-2:36.8 body 1: full groove, bass drops lower at 1:40.6 (6 bars after), narration interjections to 2:36.2 (H).
5. 2:36.8-3:24.8 loop B, 4 cells: "other" partly muted then rebuilt, voice-free (H).
6. 3:24.8-4:36.8 plateau, 52 bars: chopped voice one per bar, sixteenth hats, top band at +25 to +29 dB from 3:48.8 (H; secondary mark 3:36.8, M).
7. 4:36.8-4:48.8 sag: bright layer gone, bass down 4 dB (H).
8. 4:48.8-5:00.6 stutter: bass gated, stabs; 5:00.8 cut, ring-out and two buttons to 5:03 (H).

## 5. Settled conclusions

- **The song is two 12 s-cell arcs bolted to a prelude and a hole.** Prelude 0:00-0:28, loop A 0:28-2:36.8 (with the 4 s total silence at 1:24-1:28.6 and the drop), loop B 2:36.8-4:48.8 in exactly 11 six-bar cells (60 bars to 4:36.8 as 10 cells), then the stutter. High on timings and counts.
- **The only pulse-level events are the break (1:24-1:28.6, one-beat-late lattice) and the final stop (5:00.8).** Everything between is timbre and level over a constant 120 BPM grid. High.
- **The build is timbral and slow:** top band of "other" about +28 dB from 2:48.8 to 3:48.8 (ramp, fastest 3:26-3:50), mix only about +2 dB; the mix peaks at -9.9 dB at 4:24-4:36. Mechanism (filter, drive or layer) is not recoverable. High on numbers, medium on mechanism.
- **The ending is graded subtraction in two stages (sag 4:36.8, bass gate 4:48.8) and a hard stop,** not a drop or a resolution; no cadence exists to resolve it. High on events, medium on "exhaustion" as the felt reading.
- **Voice marks the joints:** it leaves before 1:28.6 and 2:36.8 and returns as bar-rate rhythm at 3:24.75, exactly four cells after 2:36.8; this edit alignment is the strongest evidence the form was designed, not looped. High on times, medium on intent.
