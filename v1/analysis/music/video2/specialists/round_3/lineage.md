## 1. Answers to the questions put to me in round 2

Two questions were put to me directly, both indirectly through "Lineage Q" labels.

**Rhythm Q1 / Sound Q1: is the late groove motorik or dance?** Settled by Rhythm [R3-rhythm]: kick on every beat, an extra kick on the "a" of beat 2, sixteenth hats from about 3:40, a strong backbeat only after 3:20 (M), and a bass cross-rhythm of dotted quavers (steps 2, 5, 8, 11, 14). That is four-on-the-floor dance language (house/disco/synth-pop), not the Neu!/Can motorik template. I stand on my round-2 correction. Caveat I keep: my own kick/snare split was bleed-affected, so "backbeat" rests on Rhythm's analysis, not mine.

**Sound Q2: guitar-like and synth-like layers, and is there a delay at 0.375 s?** Rhythm and I agree the 0.375 s pulse is in the bass, not provably a delay on "other" [R3-rhythm, my R2 autocorrelations: bass 0.16 to 0.27 at 0.375 s]. So I make no "delay guitar" lineage claim. Sound [R3-sound] says instrument identity is not recoverable (L). I therefore also drop any "guitar-led" or "banjo" lineage claim for Part 2; the only guitar evidence in the research is for Part 1 (psb_method.md, part_mapping.md).

**Harmony Q (is a pedal-plus-lift loop a PSB device?)** Answered in R2: I can name families only. Harmony now says one key (B Aeolian), two loops (E-G-D then B-A-D), no cadence [R3-harmony]. Its remark that the 6-bar cell is unusual against 4-bar rock and 16-bar dance phrases is a lineage fact I accept: it suggests DAW-built loops (L-M), nothing more.

**Voice-to-me (are bursts on the grid?).** I had asked this to decide "rhythm" versus "texture". Answered [R2-voice, R3-rhythm]: from 4:40 the bursts are constant-phase, one per bar, locked to the kick grid (H); narration earlier is free against the grid (phrase starts at 0.75 to 3.85 beats, no single phase, [R2-voice]). So my "voice becomes rhythm" claim stands for 3:24.75 onward, with Voice's caveat that "chopped sample" is unproven; the bursts are band-limited like the narrator but stereo-wide [R3-sound], and their source could be processed narration or another layer (L).

## 2. Corrections to my own earlier claims

1. **"Press says calm" versus the stems.** Settled with Feeling [R2-feeling]: the calm is true of surface (fixed pulse, no cadence, no rock-band attack), not energy. The mix climbs from about -26 to about -10 dBFS, peaking -9.9 dB at 4:24-4:36 [R3-form]. I keep "calmer than Part 1 in gesture, not in energy" (M).
2. **Sound lineage is not hauntological.** Sound [R3-sound] finds no vinyl or tape noise floor (4 kHz+ at about -70 dB early), a clean, bright late mix; the only band-limited thing is the archive speech (hard edge near 4-5 kHz). So the archive-hiss, lo-fi, Boards of Canada/Ghost Box lineage applies to method (old public-information material as source), not to timbre (M for method, L for sound). Note that the intro darkness (rolloff about 1.5 kHz, 0:00-0:42) is the pad, not a global filter, because the drums at 0:28 share that rolloff [R2-lin].
3. **I overstated "voice as chopped sample = electronic idea" earlier.** Reduced to: the voice is used at bar rate as a rhythmic element in the second half; the mechanism is not proven.
4. **"Rock song form" is refuted.** Form [R3-form] shows no verse/chorus, no cadence, no modulation; sections are made by one layer automated on a 6-bar loop grid, with a hole at 1:24-1:28.6 and a hard stop at 5:00.8. That is DAW electronic form, so I withdraw any suggestion of post-rock structure as a primary lineage: post-rock builds (Mogwai, Explosions in the Sky) usually add live-band dynamics and change chords, and this piece does neither. The family resemblance is only "slow build over a loop" (L).

## 3. Connecting lineage to the others

- **Rhythm and Form.** The grid that never moves after 1:28.6, with every section change carried by timbre, level, loop and voice [R3-form, R3-rhythm], is the dance-track method of arrival-of-layers. Rhythm's two pulse-level events (the 1:24-1:28.6 hole and the final stop) frame the arc. A gap-then-drop at 1:28.6 is a dance/film-cue device; a hard stop followed by ring-out is an edit-style ending, not a rock outro (M).
- **Harmony and Voice.** Narration's last sentence fits one E-G-D-B cycle and ends 0.6 s before the switch to loop B; the first chopped fragment is exactly four cells later at 3:24.75 [R3-harmony, R3-form]. That alignment is the strongest evidence the piece was designed with the voice, which is consistent with PSB's stated method of samples driving the story (psb_method.md, M-H) and a track built around the edit. Causation (music cut to speech or speech cut to music) is not measurable (L).
- **Sound and Feeling.** Drums flat at -18/-19 dBFS from 1:28 with the rise in "other" (+24 dB at 4-8 kHz, 3:00-4:24) [R3-sound]: a production approach, not a live-band swell. The felt result (calm surface, mounting pressure, then withdrawal at 4:36.8, then bass gate at 4:48.8) matches the documented reception of "drive, physical effort" (reception.md) and supports a reading as a long effort rather than a triumphant arrival (M interpretation).
- **Voice.** PSB's norm (speech leads, band frames) holds 0:42-2:36; after 3:24 the voice is framed by, or absorbed into, the groove [R2-voice]. This is the clearest departure from the PSB method I can measure, and it fits the commission's brief that Part 2 should be the electronic half.

## 4. Lineage picture, as settled

- **Artist and catalogue.** Commissioned by the Explore the North festival for the 50th anniversary of the 1963 race; second part of a two-part piece released as autumn 2013 digital single, linked to the debut album *Inform - Educate - Entertain* (context.md; H on commission, M on release formats). Part 1 is the rock half; Part 2 the calmer electronic half (writteninmusic.com, via context.md).
- **Genre.** Loop-based electronic rock/dance hybrid: house/disco-style kick, syncopated bass, sixteenth hats, a modal one-key harmony, a long timbral build. Closest named neighbours are families, not influences: krautrock-descended electronica and minimal build music for structure, synth-pop/house for drums. No source says PSB cited any of them for these tracks.
- **Method lineage.** Archive-as-material (public-information film, Dutch regional archive FFAA) with new music composed to it, shared with the hauntology/archive-sample tradition; but the sound is modern and clean.
- **Intent.** Inferred only: to answer a commemorative film with machine-steady, accumulating music, so that a fixed pulse stands for the length of the race. No Willgoose statement about these tracks exists in our research (reception.md); this is interpretation (L-M).

## 6. Settled conclusions (5 bullets)

- **Where it sits:** Part 2 of a commissioned two-part piece (Explore the North, 50th anniversary of the 1963 race), released autumn 2013 beside PSB's debut album era; Part 1 is the rock half and Part 2 the electronic half (H on commission and mapping, M on release details).
- **Genre:** a loop-built, steady-grid electronic piece; four-on-the-floor kick with extra step-7 kick, syncopated dotted-quaver bass and sixteenth hats are house/disco/synth-pop drum language, not motorik or rock (H-M, from Rhythm). Form is DAW-style (one layer automated on a 6-bar loop grid, no cadence), not rock-song or classic post-rock form (H on the facts, M on the label).
- **The PSB signature, and its departure:** the archive-narration-led method holds until 2:36 (free-timed speech over the band), then after 3:24 the voice becomes a bar-locked, one-per-bar rhythmic element (H on timing, L on how the bursts were made). Voice and loop changes align (last sentence ends 0.6 s before the 2:36.8 switch; first burst four cells later), the best evidence of a designed edit (M-H).
- **Sound versus lineage:** the method is hauntological (old public-information film as source), the sound is not (clean, bright, no tape or vinyl floor, instrument identity unrecoverable); the "calm" of the press describes surface and gesture, while the mix rises about 16 dB to a loud, bright plateau before a graded subtraction and hard stop (H on numbers, M on reading).
- **Intent and feeling:** the best-supported reading is a long, determined effort conveyed by an unmoving pulse and a slow timbral build, ending in withdrawal rather than resolution (no V-I, final bass B and a stop); this matches the documented reception of drive and physical effort but rests on measurement plus interpretation, with no artist statement to confirm it (L-M).
