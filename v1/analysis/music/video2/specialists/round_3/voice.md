## 3. Cross-links
- **Voice with rhythm and form (high):** narration is free-timed over a groove that does not change (Rhythm's constant kick phase after 1:28.6); the handovers are all at the end of a word group: 1:28.3 ("dat is de Elfstedentocht") then drop; 2:36.1 (the figures) then loop B at 2:36.8; 3:24.75 first chop exactly 24 bars later. **Three joints, each with the voice stopping or starting 0.1-0.6 s from a music change.** That is more than coincidence (three of three joints I can check) and means voice and music were edited, not merely laid together; the *direction* (music cut to voice or voice cut to music) remains unprovable; for 1:28 the voice leads (the drop follows it by 0.2-0.3 s, with the voice tail fading as the kick lands), so there the music is cut to the voice; for 2:36.8 and 3:24.75 the music leads (loop starts) and the voice is placed relative to the lattice.
- **Voice with harmony:** voice never moves the harmony (loop changes only at 2:36.8). At 4:40+ the bursts sit on a static B-F#-A pad (Harmony), so a bar-locked unpitched stab over a drone is rhythm, not harmony.
- **Voice with sound and feeling:** the dry, centred, band-limited narrator (S/M -9 to -10 dB) reads as testimony; the later bursts wider (-2.3 to +1.1 dB S/M) and bar-locked read as crowd or breath inside the machine. Feeling's arc "witness, then part of the machine" is supported by: width, bar-locking, lengthening then collapse, loss of words. It is interpretation (medium-low) with measured causes.
- **Voice with lineage:** the PSB method (speech carries the story) holds to 2:36; from 3:24 the speech loses words and becomes rhythm, a departure Lineage already flagged. I note that the narration itself is as the context says (Dutch archive voice-over, [W-nl] nl p=1.00), but the narrator and film remain unidentified.

## 5. Settled conclusions (five points)
1. **No singing anywhere: the voice is Dutch archive narration (0:42-2:36, free-timed, band-limited, dry and centred) that, after 3:24.75, becomes chopped, wordless fragments; the date, "dat is de Elfstedentocht", "16 graden" and the 575/9500 rider figures are the only words I trust.** High on no singing and the Dutch narration; medium-high on the words.
2. **The voice and the music were edited together at three joints: the title-sentence ends 1:28.3 as the drop arrives; the last figures end 2:36.1 as loop B starts at 2:36.8; the first chop is 24 bars after that at 3:24.75.** High on timings, medium on intent, direction unprovable.
3. **From 3:24 the voice is bar-locked (17 of 21 onsets in a one-beat window, 0.1-0.2 s before the bar line) but not beat- or cell-quantised; fragments lengthen (1.5, 2.2, 3.6 s mean) and thicken, then collapse at 4:40 into a one-per-bar unpitched stab (static B-F#-A pad beneath).** High on bar lock and the 4:40 pulse; medium on the lengthening arc being intended.
4. **The chops are compatible with the narrator's band-limited source (roll-off 3-5 kHz, same hard edge) but wider in stereo; whether archive voice or a vocal-like synth is not decidable; the 4:38.5 event is a noise impact, not voice.** Medium on source, medium-high on 4:38.5.
5. **The central voice idea is human timing replaced by machine timing: sentences as testimony and scale, then fragments as crowd, then a metronomic breath; the voice is a late, quiet hook, 8-25 dB under the drums, never a tune.** Interpretation, medium; the measurements behind it are listed above.
