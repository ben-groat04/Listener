## 4. What a viewer feels here, and why

OBSERVATION-led interpretation: first, awe and loneliness at 1:40-1:42, since tiny figures are nearly swallowed by white. The aerial village shot then gives a sense of scale and of a whole community around the event. At 1:46-1:48 we get ordinary, readable human detail (crowd, ramp, bib) so we can finally orient. The fast handheld run from 1:49 gives breathlessness and jostling, as if we are physically among the spectators and skaters; it feels urgent and slightly dangerous (the scrum at the bank). Then the kettle pour is a release: a small, tender domestic act that suggests warmth and survival. The final held gaze of the young person at 1:59-2:00 gives a quiet, unsettling intimacy: they are not performing, they look cold and wary, and the music drops away so we are left with that face. The mood is exhilaration turning to tenderness and unease. Why: loud music and quick cuts raise adrenaline; the sudden drop in level plus a held, silent-ish close-up reads as a breath after strain.
