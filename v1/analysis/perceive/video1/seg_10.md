## 4. What a viewer feels, and why (INTERPRETATION)

- Rush and immersion at 3:00-3:03: the bib close-up and pack-level camera are visceral, and the blur makes it feel fast, which matches a loud, even guitar-driven track.
- Anticipation turning to a sense of closure: the accelerating cuts (4.2 s down to 0.5 s) feel like a montage of the day in miniature (skater, crowd, officials, a town sign, a banner). The static DOKKUM sign gives a moment of orientation, and the banner naming "Elfstedentocht" is a kind of title card at the end.
- Abruptness at 3:08: the cut to black arrives after a crowd shot, with no narration or resolution, so it feels like the footage has simply run out. Because Part 2 follows (per context), the black is probably an end-of-part gap, not a final ending (speculative).
- Distance and anonymity: most of the people are seen from behind or blurred, so the viewer is placed among the spectators and not facing individual heroes. There is a faint melancholy in the cold, faceless crowd and the empty snowy landscape.
