## 4. What a viewer feels here, and why

- **Observation to feeling.** The sudden saturation of the tinted colour after the sepia windmill is a jolt of warmth in a cold scene. The tint swings (red, lilac, yellow, blue) feel hallucinatory and unstable, like a memory that will not hold its colour. Combined with the louder, driving music, I would expect a viewer to feel anticipation or a gathering of momentum, with an overlay of nostalgia and slight eeriness (interpretation).
- The man in the dark coat walking at the camera (1:44-1:48) is the emotional hinge: the only person who really "looks back". Since he approaches the lens, the viewer feels watched, or feels that history is approaching. His impassive, ordinary face reads as dignified and human, not heroic.
- The matter-of-fact voice-over (cold, distance) gives stakes: a very cold day and a very long ride. Then the long file of tiny skaters (1:56-1:58) is the first image that conveys the scale of the event: the individuals become a line, a crowd, an endurance march. The sheer flatness and low pink sky give a sense of vast, empty, beautiful cold.
- Also note the ghostly violet figure-shape at 1:41: it adds a faintly uncanny, haunted feeling, though it is probably a film flaw.
