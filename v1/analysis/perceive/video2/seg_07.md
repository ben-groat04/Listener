## 4. What a viewer feels, and why (interpretation)

- Cold, exposed, and slightly dreamlike. The washed-out pink/cream/lilac tints and overexposed flash make the snow feel like a blank, overwhelming white; people are tiny and silent in it. This is the visual equivalent of the numbers just spoken ("-16, 200 km"): the huge distance and the cold are shown, not told.
- A rhythm of determination without celebration: the skaters trudge, heads down, hardly gliding. The music stays steady and urgent underneath, so the picture is slow and effortful while the sound drives on. The contrast (slow human effort, mechanical groove) gives a sense of endurance as routine, not as spectacle.
- No narration for twenty seconds lets the viewer sit in the images; a quiet-human moment inside a loud section.
- The colour shifts (blue, red, cream) feel like film decaying or memory unstable; that adds nostalgia and distance.
