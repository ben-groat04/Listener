## What a viewer feels, and why (INTERPRETATION)
- Immersion and strain: the over-the-shoulder, close, wobbling shots put the viewer in the pack, behind someone's back, with the ice rushing past. Because faces are hidden, the skaters become anonymous numbers: bibs 238, 529, 157 turn people into a mass. This gives both the "one of thousands" feeling and sympathy for the ordinary effort.
- Relief and exposure at 3:46-3:50: the static wide shots of the canal and the quiet farmhouses are pale, silent-looking and open. After the crowded close-ups they feel like a deep breath, with the lone sidecar a faintly comic or lonely note, and the skaters small against the huge white landscape.
- Intensity again at 3:50-3:58: tilted, blurred close-ups of backs, snow flecks, hands behind backs. This is bodily and relentless, which with the loud, minor-key music and (possibly) repeated "end of the road / climbing up" phrases reads as endurance and fatigue. The ending shot (3:59) steps back to a long blue lane stretching ahead, a "road ahead" image.
- Colour: blues and purples of ice and shadow, with sparks of red (238 jacket) and orange-brown (529): warm accents against cold fields. The archival film stock gives nostalgia and a sense of looking at a lost world.
