## What a viewer feels here, and why

SPECULATION/INTERPRETATION. Up to 4:44 the viewer sees the race itself as human effort: strained faces and fast legs, which raises tension (and, with the louder music before it, an adrenalin peak). The sudden cut to the flag and cold blue sky at 4:45 feels like a breath, an open, proud, civic moment, which gives a sense of place and occasion. The close shots of legs on streaked ice put you physically on the surface, with vertigo and speed. Then the film turns away from people altogether: ducks crowded into the last bit of open water, then broken ice, then empty pink water. The pink-brown cast and the slow pace feel like melancholy, nostalgia and the end of winter. A viewer who knows the story (the 1963 race was a once-in-a-generation freeze, as in context.md) may read the thawing ice as the window of the event closing, a quiet elegy after the effort. The ducks bunched in one hole in the ice also read as survival under hard cold, a counterpoint to the skaters.
