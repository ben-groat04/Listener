## 1. Who and what (H)
- Public Service Broadcasting (PSB): London, formed 2010 by J. Willgoose, Esq., first as a solo project; Wrigglesworth joined on drums soon after. JFAbraham joined officially in 2016 (after Elfstedentocht, so irrelevant to these tracks). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Service_Broadcasting_(band) (fetched). H.
- Roles per Wikipedia: Willgoose = guitar, banjo, strings, samples, electronics; Wrigglesworth = drums, piano, electronics; Mr B = visuals and set design for live shows (non-musical). Same source. H. A live-review search summary agrees (Mr B runs visuals, sometimes live camera; Willgoose on laptop, theremin, guitar, banjo; Wrigglesworth drums), e.g. https://www.thestar.co.uk/whats-on/arts-and-entertainment/live-review-history-today-as-public-service-broadcasting-deliver-stunning-show-8942452 (search snippet only, not fetched). M.
- Gear listed on Wikipedia (Rickenbacker 330, Telecaster, Juno-106 etc.) is a current-era list, not shown to apply to 2013. Do not assume it for these tracks. L.
- Parent album: *Inform - Educate - Entertain*, released 6 May 2013 on Test Card Recordings, UK No. 21, BPI Silver. Elfstedentocht was a Record Store Day single released 11 November 2013. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Service_Broadcasting_(band). H. (Other research files say it is on the deluxe edition; consistent with Written In Music, see artist_and_tracks.md.)

## 2. The method: sample-led songs from archive film (H for the general practice)
- PSB sample old public information films, archive footage and propaganda material and set them to new music. Wikipedia (fetched) and search summaries. H.
- Archive sourcing varies by project. For *The War Room* they had exclusive access to the BFI archives (Wikipedia). One 2013 video's credits cite the Prelinger Archives (US road-safety films) (search summary of imcdb.org, https://www.imcdb.org/m1004514926.html; M). Willgoose said clearing BBC material is expensive ("it's BBC and it's very hard to clear at the moment without paying") and the next project would move forward in time. https://www.outlineonline.co.uk/reviews/interview-with-public-service-broadcasting (fetched, undated, c. 2013 by content). M-H.
- Origin story via a Medium profile (search summary; page 403 on fetch): Willgoose, inspired by archive material on BBC Radio 4, began sampling public information films and setting them to indie/electronic music. https://medium.com/songful/public-service-broadcasting-153411fde57c. L-M (secondary, unverified).
- The tagline "teach the lessons of the past through the music of the future": I could NOT verify the wording or its source. It is absent from the Wikipedia article. A search matched only the Medium profile's tagline pairing, not confirmed word for word. Treat as a commonly quoted band slogan, confidence L-M; do not quote it as a verified Willgoose statement. Name "Inform - Educate - Entertain" echoes the Reithian BBC mission, which is a reasonable reading but my inference (L).
- Samples carry the narrative. Willgoose (Outline interview): speech samples drive the story; remixers' versions have not "quite captured the narrative"; he avoids predictable sample structure and likes to surprise (his example: a shifted section in "Spitfire"). He starts "with a very small idea and then things grow." https://www.outlineonline.co.uk/reviews/interview-with-public-service-broadcasting. M-H (one interview, fetched summary).

## 3. Live and visual aesthetic (M)
- Willgoose says he made most of the videos shown live himself, including black-and-white footage on a large screen; stage uses an eight-foot TV and stacks of old televisions with differing content; the official videos for "Signal 30" were made by Alex Kemp and Tony Powell. Same Outline interview. M-H.
- Reviews describe vintage TV sets, a radio-mast prop and large projection screens (search summary of the Nottingham review, https://themicmagazine.uk/post/public-service-broadcasting-rescue-rooms-13-11-13). M (snippet only).
- Caveat: "visuals by Mr B" is established for live shows (Wikipedia). Whether Mr B made the Elfstedentocht videos is NOT established. The Elfstedentocht videos are archive-led (Fries Film Archief footage per archive_sources.md); no source I found credits their edit. Do not attribute it.

## 4. What this implies for listening to the two tracks (own inference unless cited)
1. **Narration is the lead voice.** In PSB songs there is normally no sung lead; the sample does the storytelling (Outline interview above, M-H). In video2 the Dutch commentary ("18 januari 1963", "monsterrit van ruim 200 kilometer", per artist_and_tracks.md, own audio) plays that role. Listen to what the voice does rhythmically and emotionally, and what the band does around it, before looking for "lyrics". Our ASR hits on the 3:32-3:58 section ("Stuck", "End of the road") are low confidence and may be sample or noise.
2. **The band answers the voice.** Expect the music to frame, underline or contradict commentary: guitar and drums push where the story speeds up, electronics where it goes cold or still. This is a method claim from interview plus our reading of the two parts as described by Written In Music (Part 1 loud, rock; Part 2 calmer, electronic; https://writteninmusic.com/nieuws/public-service-broadcasting-doet-hel-van-63-herleven/, H). It is a hypothesis to test per section, not a result.
3. **A Dutch voice, English band.** The listener outside the Netherlands hears the commentary largely as timbre and cadence, not meaning; a Dutch listener gets content and period tone. Both readings are valid; keep them separate (see cultural_context.md).
4. **The footage is part of the composition.** PSB's output is film-plus-music; the visuals are chosen archive, so cuts, titles and crowd shots are structural cues. The 2013 "live projector show" context (TVs, screens) means these tracks were conceived for being seen. Weight the music first (project rule), but read the picture as the second voice.
5. **Archive texture is intentional.** Hiss, mono, period commentary and film grain are material, not defects. Do not treat low fidelity as a quality fault.
6. **Instrumentation to listen for (2013-era, from role lists, not from our audio):** electric guitar riff (Part 1 "simple blood-pumping guitar riff", https://www.recordrewindplay.co.uk/tracks/elfstedentocht-part-1/, M-H), live-feel drums (Wrigglesworth), electronics/samples; banjo is a Willgoose instrument but I found no evidence it appears on these tracks. Verify from stems before claiming it. Our CLAP tags (post-rock 0.84, distorted guitar for video1; synth-pop/drum machine for video2, per part_mapping.md) are consistent but are model tags, not confirmation.
