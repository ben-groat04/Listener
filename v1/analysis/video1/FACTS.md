# video1 FACTS: the verified sheet for the writer

Public Service Broadcasting, "Elfstedentocht Part 1", 3:09. Black-and-white archive film of the Frisian ice race of 18 January 1963 under a loud instrumental rock track.

**How to use this sheet.** Draw only from it. Everything below was re-checked against the raw material: the 1 fps frames and contact sheets (frame `f_NNNN` shows second NNNN minus 1; picture times are good to about one second), the per-second level data, the separated stems, the Dutch transcripts and the shot and cut lists. Private notes were used only as a map and were not trusted where the raw data disagreed. Times are m:ss and approximate. "Sure" means seen or measured directly. "Probable" means the evidence points one way but a step is inferred or the source is rough. The honesty disclosure (nobody on this project has heard it with ears; the sound is known through measurement and the picture through stills) belongs once at page level, never inside the writer's voice. Do not use process words (frames, transcript, stems, measurements) in the piece.

## 2. The voice: what can and cannot be said

**Can say**
- It speaks Dutch. The language is certain; the words are partly recovered.
- It reads facts in a bulletin manner (an inference from the wording, not a verified property of the sound): the date, the hour, the temperature, the distance, the entry figures, the towns. It tells nothing about a person, a winner or a feeling.
- What it says in English, with confidence: "18 January 1963" (0:09); "half past five in the morning" (0:16; one pass misheard it as six-thirty, so say half past five only as probable); "What everyone is preparing for, that is the Elfstedentocht" (0:27 and again 2:44); "the twelfth Elfstedentocht" (0:41); "Temperature sixteen degrees below zero" (0:51); "a monster ride of more than two hundred kilometres" (0:54); "575 racers, about 9,500 tour skaters" (1:19, probable); the towns of the route ending on "Leeuwarden" (2:15 to 2:36).
- Not understood at all: the sentence at 0:12 to 0:16, and the noun and ending after "two hundred" at 0:19 (a gist of "two hundred ... through Friesland, which only the strongest ..." is a guess).
- Delivery: an unhurried speaking pace, about four syllables a second, slower and slightly higher while listing towns. Its pitch moves like ordinary speech; it is not sung and does not follow the beat. It is dry and band-limited, like an old recording; it sits in the middle of the sound, well inside the music, not on top.
- The band does not duck for it. It enters into spaces the band clears (0:09, 1:19, 2:15) and leaves at changes (0:32, 1:27, 2:36, 2:48).
- Where it speaks: 0:09 to 0:32, 0:41 to 0:43, 0:50 to 0:56, 1:19 to 1:27, 2:15 to 2:36, 2:44 to 2:48. It is absent from 1:27 to 2:15 and from 2:48 to the end.

**Cannot say**
- Age, sex, accent, mood, or who the narrator is. Do not write "he", "his", "old", "male-sounding", "calm" as a fact about the person, or any name.
- Whether it was recorded in 1963 or later, whether it comes from a newsreel, a club film or a regional film, or whether the film and voice belong together originally.
- Whether the voice at 0:27 and at 2:44 is the same recording; the words are the same, the take is unproven.
- Whether there is more than one speaker.

## 3. The overall shape of the music, in plain words

- It is a driving, heavy, grinding instrumental, loud nearly all the way. Drums run without a break from the first second to the last, apart from one tiny gap at 2:48. A distorted, wall-like layer (probably guitars; it may be synthesisers) fills the middle. There is no verse, no chorus, no tune to hum. It sounds stern and effortful rather than sad or triumphant.
- The drama is made by taking things away and putting them back: the deep bass, the wall and the voice come and go.
- Shape: it snaps in with a hit; the bass swells in over about 17 seconds; the voice sets out the date and hour and then the band opens up and gets louder, with a first bright peak at about 1:05 to 1:15. At 1:15 the bass vanishes and the music gets lighter and uneasy for about 25 seconds, ending with a floating, stepping line over no bass at 1:28 to 1:39. At 1:40 the bass slams back: the track's main release, a feeling of weight and relief, not of more volume. A busy, loud stretch follows, then at about 1:55 the bass leaves again and the music shrinks to a thin, high, quiet block with six slow, deep thuds; this is the quietest, most exposed passage. From about 2:13 the sound thickens; chords begin to walk under the town list; at about 2:38 the bass returns and the opening sentence comes round again; after a half-second gap at 2:48 the track runs a steady, bright, wide sprint, with cymbals and a gritty low riff, to the end, where it stops in half a second with no resolution.
- There are two peaks: the arrival at 1:40 (weight) and the sprint from 2:48 (steady, bright, relentless). Do not say the track climbs steadily; it holds a level and the switches make the drama.

- "Fur" collars or hats: say "pale, fuzzy collar" or "woollen hat".
- "Hand-lettered" banner or board: describe the letters (black capitals on white cloth); leave the manner open.

- "Motorik", "krautrock", or "post-rock" as a fact: the groove is syncopated and stiff, nearer programmed funk or hip-hop; "post-rock" is a model tag and a loose fit. Do not claim banjo. Do not claim whether the wall is guitar or synthesiser, or whether the drums are played or programmed.
- "Major" or "minor": undecided. Say stern.
- "Drums stop" or "all sound stops" at 2:48: only drums and bass, for about 0.4 s, while the guitar holds and the voice finishes.
- "The bass return is louder": the volume barely changes; it is a feeling of weight.
- "The tea table passage is all indoors": 2:08 to 2:10 is a skater kneeling in open snow, and 2:04 shows a van.
- "Sneek and Sloten signs": no sign for them in the pictures; Hindeloopen and Harlingen likewise. Seven showings in all (IJlst, the Stavoren inn, Workum, Bolsward, Franeker, Dokkum, the Dokkum/Leeuwarden signpost, plus Dokkum again at 3:06).
- "Part 1 has a winner and a finish line crossed": no.
- "The whole film is 1963 footage" or "one reel": not established. The aerial view (1:43), helicopter (2:26), aeroplane (2:19), skaters filmed from beside them, and the double exposures at 0:04 and 0:10 may come from other cameras or editions.
- "Amateur home movie", "newsreel", "Polygoon": not established. The archive credited is the Fries Film Archief; the narrator and the films are not identified.
- Facts that come from outside the video must be tagged as from outside: the artist, the commission for the 2013 anniversary festival in Leeuwarden, Part 1 of a two-part release, 9,862 started and 127 finished, the winner. None of it appears in the picture or sound.

**Tempting errors**
- **Cuts do not land on the big sound events.** There is no cut at the bass drop at 1:15, none at the bass return at 1:40, none at the bass leaving at about 1:55, and none at the 2:48 gap (the nearest cut is about a second away at best). Cuts do land close to a few smaller things (1:27 voice exit and wall surge; 2:01 and 2:13 thuds; 2:38 bass return) but chance cannot be excluded, so say "around the same moment", never "cut to the drop".
- Cuts are not cut to the beat. The picture follows the narration (signs near the spoken towns, the date at 0:09 and the registration scene at 1:19); do not claim a designed beat sync. The overall cut rate rises and falls only weakly with the loudness.
- The first 42 seconds are not "three shots": the shot detector misses most soft cuts; the picture changes far more often than its list shows.
- Do not say the picture "mirrors" the music's mood. At 2:48 the music says arrival and the picture shows a banner, officials and a crowd.
- Do not say the film is "about" a victory. It is a crowd, paperwork, cold and effort; no winner is named or shown.
- Do not turn the exact figures (575 and about 9,500; sixteen degrees below zero) into truths about the race. They are what the voice says, and later sources give slightly different figures. Say "the voice gives ...".
- Do not say the sound "ends at the picture's end by design": whether the half-second ending and the black are the track's own ending or an edit is unknown.
