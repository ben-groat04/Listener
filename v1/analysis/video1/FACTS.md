## 2. The voice: what can and cannot be said

**Can say**
- It speaks Dutch. The language is certain; the words are partly recovered.
- It reads facts in a bulletin manner (an inference from the wording, not a verified property of the sound): the date, the hour, the temperature, the distance, the entry figures, the towns. It tells nothing about a person, a winner or a feeling.
- What it says in English, with confidence: "18 January 1963" (0:09); "half past five in the morning" (0:16; one pass misheard it as six-thirty, so say half past five only as probable); "What everyone is preparing for, that is the Elfstedentocht" (0:27 and again 2:44); "the twelfth Elfstedentocht" (0:41); "Temperature sixteen degrees below zero" (0:51); "a monster ride of more than two hundred kilometres" (0:54); "575 racers, about 9,500 tour skaters" (1:19, probable); the towns of the route ending on "Leeuwarden" (2:15 to 2:36).
- Not understood at all: the sentence at 0:12 to 0:16, and the noun and ending after "two hundred" at 0:19 (a gist of "two hundred ... through Friesland, which only the strongest ..." is a guess).
- Delivery: an unhurried speaking pace, about four syllables a second, slower and slightly higher while listing towns. Its pitch moves like ordinary speech; it is not sung and does not follow the beat. It is dry and band-limited, like an old recording; it sits in the middle of the sound, well inside the music, not on top.
- The band does not duck for it. It enters into spaces the band clears (0:09, 1:19, 2:15) and leaves at changes (0:32, 1:27, 2:36, 2:48).
- Where it speaks: 0:09 to 0:32, 0:41 to 0:43, 0:50 to 0:56, 1:19 to 1:27, 2:15 to 2:36, 2:44 to 2:48. It is absent from 1:27 to 2:15 and from 2:48 to the end.

**Cannot say**

## 3. The overall shape of the music, in plain words

- It is a driving, heavy, grinding instrumental, loud nearly all the way. Drums run without a break from the first second to the last, apart from one tiny gap at 2:48. A distorted, wall-like layer (probably guitars; it may be synthesisers) fills the middle. There is no verse, no chorus, no tune to hum. It sounds stern and effortful rather than sad or triumphant.
- The drama is made by taking things away and putting them back: the deep bass, the wall and the voice come and go.
- Shape: it snaps in with a hit; the bass swells in over about 17 seconds; the voice sets out the date and hour and then the band opens up and gets louder, with a first bright peak at about 1:05 to 1:15. At 1:15 the bass vanishes and the music gets lighter and uneasy for about 25 seconds, ending with a floating, stepping line over no bass at 1:28 to 1:39. At 1:40 the bass slams back: the track's main release, a feeling of weight and relief, not of more volume. A busy, loud stretch follows, then at about 1:55 the bass leaves again and, by about 1:58, the music shrinks to a thin, high, quiet block with six slow, deep thuds; this is the quietest, most exposed passage. From about 2:13 the sound thickens; chords begin to walk under the town list; at about 2:38 the bass returns and the opening sentence comes round again; after a gap of about a third to half a second at 2:48 the track runs a steady, bright, wide sprint, with cymbals and a gritty low riff, to the end, where it stops in half a second with no resolution.
- There are two peaks: the arrival at 1:40 (weight) and the sprint from 2:48 (steady, bright, relentless). Do not say the track climbs steadily; it holds a level and the switches make the drama.
