## 1. Overview

A 3:09 loud, instrumental rock track (bass mostly D with short pickup notes and a D tonic, major or minor unresolved; about 78 BPM; drums that never change gear) carries a montage of black-and-white archive film of the Elfstedentocht of 18 January 1963 (the 12th race, "De Hel van '63"), read by a flat Dutch narrator whose only job is to supply facts: the date, "half past five in the morning", "16 degrees below zero", "a monster ride of well over 200 kilometres", 575 racers and about 9,500 tour skaters, the eleven towns. The band supplies all the feeling, and it does so by subtraction: bass, a dense "wall" layer and the voice are switched on and off on a bar grid. The picture is a thematic collage, not a day's chronology: pre-dawn crowds and newspapers, the first skaters, the cameramen, bibs and paperwork, white-out landscapes, a tea table and shivering faces, a roll-call of town signs, a crowd at a "FINISH" banner, and a final scramble of bibs, signs and a "Reportage ELFSTEDENTOCHT" banner that ends on black. We never see a winner or a finish line crossed. The two big emotional events in the music (the bass returning after 24 s at 1:40, and the half-second hole at 2:47.8 followed by the bright sprint) are not simply mirrored by the picture: at 1:40 the picture is at its emptiest and slowest, and at 2:47.8 the hole falls between a whip-pan and a "FINISH" banner. Documented reception speaks of drive, physical effort and image and sound fused; the memory and ghost readings below are ours.

Context (sourced in `context.md` and research): PSB commissioned by the Explore the North festival, Leeuwarden, for the 50th anniversary; footage and voice-over from the Fries Film Archief (FFAA); source films and narrator unconfirmed; mapping to Part 1 is high confidence (MusicBrainz 3:09).

## 3. Recurring motifs and where they occur

**Picture motifs**
- **Newspapers and printed text as frames.** Leeuwarder Courant "Elfstedentocht op vrijdag" (0:04), Friese Koerier "bovenmenselijk zwaar" and "Chaos aan de finish" (0:09), Leeuwarder Courant "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar" (0:22; again tighter at 2:57), then signs (POSTHUMA 0:07; IJLST 2:18; Welkom in Workum 2:27; Bolsward 2:29; Franeker 2:32; Dokkum 2:34 and 3:06.6; the Leeuwarden signpost 2:35), the "FINISH" banner (2:48) and the "Reportage ELFSTEDENTOCHT" banner (3:07). The film opens with print and ends with print. The two headline pages are post-race reports shown before and during the race: the outcome is spoiled by the text frame (INT).
- **Reused footage.** The Dokkum sign shot (2:34 and 3:06.6) and the Leeuwarder Courant page (0:22 and 2:57) are repeats. These are the only two verified reuses; the viewers' "possible repeats" of the tea table or the whip-pans were not checked.
- **Bibs and numbers.** Seen: 12 (0:18), 170 (1:05), 245 (1:08), 460 (1:11), 296 or 295 (1:12), about 242 (1:13), 255 (1:15), 157 (1:21), about 24 (1:27), 342 (2:52), 57 (3:00). Spoken: 1963, 12th, 16 below, 200 km, 575, 9,500. People as numbers, set against statistics. The legibility is poor on most; none is traced to a named skater.
- **The camera looking back.** Large film camera and a gooseneck microphone (0:57-0:59), a tripod cameraman (0:59 to 1:04), a camera-like object (2:49), the "Reportage" banner (3:07). The medium is the subject, a PSB trait of foregrounding provenance (research, M).
- **The skating posture.** Bent torso, hands clasped behind the back: lines of skaters at 0:38, 0:44, 0:49, 1:30, 1:35, 2:55; tangled or collapsing versions at 0:42, 1:15, 1:53, 2:08.
- **Frost and the cold made visible.** Rime on caps and clothes (0:27, 0:31, 2:01, 2:13 to 2:14), frosted reeds (1:34), hoar on branches (2:27), blown snow (0:51 to 0:56).
- **White-out and tiny figures versus crowd and faces.** Empty fields: 0:56, 1:30, 1:40, 2:19, 2:22, 2:54. Crowds: 0:01, 0:14, 1:02, 1:46, 2:21, 2:40 to 2:46, 3:01. The film swings between a lone figure in blankness and a packed bank of faces.
- **Hot drinks and warmth.** The tea table (1:55), cups on the bench (1:58 to 2:00), a bottle and cups (2:05 to 2:12), the row of cups on a table (2:11). Warmth is shown only in the quiet, thin section of the music.
- **Direct gaze.** The curly-haired young person (1:58 to 2:00) and, less certainly, a few others (2:13, 1:49). Most faces look off-frame (2:44, 2:46) or away. None of the viewers noted a smile.
- **Checkpoints and hands.** Writing and stamping hands at the table (1:18 to 1:26); control hut, counter and signing (2:15 to 2:17). Hands behind backs on the ice, hands on paper in the towns.
- **Bridges and villages as place-markers.** Bascule bridge (1:12), arched bridge (1:21), drawbridge and spire (1:27), canal ramp (1:46), stone arch (2:03), church and town (2:30).
- **Whip-pans and blur as a speed device.** 0:00, 1:09, 2:47, 2:51, 3:03. The film's first and last seconds are blur.
- **Aircraft.** A small plane (2:19) and a helicopter (2:26): an eye above the course, "modern machinery over an old landscape".
- **Light in darkness.** POSTHUMA (0:07), the lamp (0:12), the flare (0:14), bibs (0:18), the lamp-post (2:40). Appears only in the first minute apart from the lamp-post.
- **The peaked-cap official.** 1:07, 1:51, 2:44, 2:45, 2:49, 3:05. Authority as a repeated figure (SPEC on meaning).
- **Ground-level ice.** 1:09, 1:50, 2:58: the surface the race depends on.
- **The ambulance.** 2:04, the only explicit medical image.

**Audio and structural motifs**
- **The refrain sentence** "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" (0:27 to 0:31.7; 2:44 to 2:48.2). The only repeated line: the nearest the piece has to a chorus. Both times it ends at a state change (0:31.7 on a bar line; 2:47.8 into the hole).
- **The D pedal and the bass switch.** Bass in at 0:03, out 1:15.3, in 1:40.0, out about 1:55, six thumps 2:01 to 2:32 (bass otherwise silent), back about 2:38.4, then to the end. Drums never change gear; the only drum event is the 0.38 s hole at 2:47.83.
- **The sub thump** every 6.15 s (2:01.09, 2:07.24, 2:13.39, 2:19.55, 2:25.70, 2:31.85): inaudible on small speakers.
- **The voice enters into space the band has cleared** (1:18.9 after the bass leaves; 2:16 on the A, D, G walk) and leaves at changes of state.

**Picture-music relations (measured, `sync/sync.json`)**
- Cuts are not snapped to the beat: only 7.8% of 51 cuts fall within 80 ms of a beat, against 19.7% expected by chance. But the cut phase within the beat is not random (Rayleigh p = 0.0055, mean phase about 0.32 of a beat, roughly 0.25 s after the beat): cuts land consistently a little late. The cut phase against a 4-beat bar is random (p = 0.56). So the editor follows footage and narration, with a mild lag against the beat. A viewer's impression that the film "cuts to the music" fits the climax cuts at 1:48 to 1:54 but not the film as a whole.
- Cut rate and motion follow the music's broad energy: r about 0.27 (cut rate vs loudness, 5 s smoothing, picture leading by about 4 s) and r about 0.32 (motion vs loudness). Weak. Cuts per 10 s by auto-section: 1.35 (0:00 to 0:15), 1.55 (0:15 to 1:06), **7.58** (1:06 to 1:16), 1.62 (1:16 to 1:28), **0.46** (1:28 to 1:49), **5.43** (1:49 to 1:59), 3.24 and 4.06 (2:00 to 2:14), 2.84 (2:14 to 2:39), 4.34 (2:39 to 3:08). - Several picture events sit very close to music events: cut 1:06.56 vs section boundary 1:06.4; cut 1:27.16 vs voice exit and wall jump 1:27.0; cut 1:48.68 vs a beat; cuts at 2:01.08 and 2:13.36 vs thumps 1 and 3; cut 2:38.24 vs bass return 2:38.4. - Picture activity is highest in the loud, voice-free stretches (1:06 to 1:15, 1:49 to 1:58, 2:48 to 3:08) and lowest at 1:28 to 1:49, which includes the bass return itself.
