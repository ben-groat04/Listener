## 1. The piece in one paragraph

A loud, relentless instrumental rock track on a D-centred drone (chord detector: D about 94 s and Dm about 33 s of 189 s; an A, D, G walk from about 2:15; chromatic friction in the closing figure; mode unsettled), at about 78 BPM, with a drum pattern, tempo and grid that never change (only the timbre does), carries a three-minute collage of black-and-white archive film of the Elfstedentocht of 18 January 1963 ("De Hel van '63"), read by a flat Dutch narrator who supplies only facts: the date, "half past five in the morning", "16 degrees below zero", "a monster ride of over 200 km", 575 racers and about 9,500 tour skaters, the towns of the route. The band does the feeling and does it by subtraction: the bass, a dense distorted "wall" layer and the voice are switched off and on, and the only break in the drum pattern is a 0.4 s drop-out at 2:47.8. The picture does something different: it supplies a world, made largely of print, paperwork, tea, signs and crowds, in which skaters are mostly anonymous bent figures with hands behind their backs. What it does to you is push you steadily across a cold landscape: tense, stern and driven rather than sad or triumphant (music reading, M; Music critic, Sceptic and First-Timer in round 3). In the frames sampled, no one is seen crossing a line (1 fps, plus dense 12 fps frames at 2:45 to 2:50 and 2:58 to 3:08; at about 2:49 a bibbed skater is held among officials and a photographer, which could be an arrival but is not shown as a crossing). At full size, f_0168 (2:47, the frame numbered second+1) literally shows: a blurred, motion-smeared crowd, and at lower right a hooded person in a white knitted helmet and dark glasses with one hand raised beside the head. READING (separate, INT, L): the raised hand could be a wave, a salute or a mid-gesture blur; I do not call it a fist or a celebration. The sound has an arrival (the bright sprint from 2:48), the picture only a banner with the word FINISH (lettering style not judged), then a repeated town sign, a banner reading "Reportage ELFSTEDENTOCHT", and black. The panel's settled description of the whole is **two well-made layers joined at the narrator**, not a proven counterpoint (Sceptic, Film critic, Insider, Music critic, round 3).

## 3. Meaning and themes (consensus)

**3.1 The band supplies stakes and register; the picture supplies content and warmth.** Agreed by all six in round 3. The band states withholding, return, waiting and release but no valence: with the third almost never played the mode is unsettled (D major 0.76 against D minor 0.756), so it sounds stern, not sad or triumphant (music reading, H on D, L on mode). The picture supplies faces, cups, cold and crowds. Evidence: no clear smile in the sampled frames (First-Timer, Symbolist, with the caveat that only about 50 of 189 frames were checked at full size; faces in the dense frames are small or blurred); the same tea scene "under a bright major-key bed would be cosier" (Sceptic). Strongest evidence: a flat voice whose rate varies by only about 25% (slowest at the start, fastest around 0:20 to 0:56, easing by 2:44, with no sustained acceleration; the town list is slower), and a band whose drum pattern and grid never change (the timbre does, section 5).

**3.2 Collective, civic effort rather than heroics.** The film's units are places and documents (print at 0:04, 0:09, 0:23, 2:57; a registration table; tea; a control hut; six town and route signs (seven showings, since Dokkum is repeated) and an inn; FINISH; the Reportage banner); skaters are connective tissue, mostly seen from behind or at distance, in bibs (Film critic, round 3). The Insider, Symbolist and Film critic converge on "administration" and endurance. Evidence: bib and counter shots (1:18 to 1:26), the registration count at 1:19 to 1:27, no named skater, and the only print that could name a person is the small type at 0:23, which is unread (illegible at 1 fps, unreliable at full size); "Paping" belongs to research context (the 1963 winner, per context.md), not to anything legibly read in the frame. The narration reads "575 racers, about 9,500 tour skaters", not a winner.

**3.3 The subject is the effort, not the result.** No one is seen crossing a line in the frames sampled (First-Timer, Symbolist, Film critic, Sceptic; the dense frames at 2:49 show a bibbed skater among officials, not a crossing). The panel disagrees about whether this is meaningful (section 5), but all describe it as what a viewer experiences.

**3.4 Names are given to the route, not to the people.** The Symbolist's round 3 thread, with Insider support: the towns are the only things said aloud one by one, each gets a sign within about a second, and no skater gets that treatment. "Language says where, the band says how hard, and the faces are left unlabelled." Labelled INT by its author.

**3.5 Roll-call or litany as grammar.** Fixed pulse, a drone with no third, a flat narrator and the town list make a litany-like form (Insider, Film critic; the Music critic finds real marks in the sound: fixed drum pattern, no tonic movement, flat delivery). Limits agreed in round 3: it is apt for the 2:15 to 2:36 passage and for the general grammar, not a proven design for the thumps; the thumps run 2:01 to 2:32 and only three of the six sit under the voice.

**3.6 Print and the camera looking back.** The film opens and ends on print (newspaper, signs, banners) and shows its own recording (large film camera and microphone 0:57 to 0:59, tripod cameraman to 1:04, a camera-like object at 2:49, the Reportage banner at 3:07). Held as a motif ("print as record"), not as a structure.

**3.7 Cold made visible.** Rime on caps and sleeves (0:27, 2:01, 2:13 to 2:14), frosted reeds (1:34), hoar on branches (2:27), a ground blizzard (0:51 to 0:56). This is a picture-side feeling that survives with no Dutch and on any speaker.

## 4. Live disagreements

Where a disagreement was resolved in round 3 I say so; where it is still live I give both sides.

**4.1 Is the picture's relationship to the music designed?**
- *Designed / fitted (earlier rounds; Film critic's partial position):* cut-density follows the music (7.58 cuts per 10 s at 1:06 to 1:16 against 0.46 at 1:28 to 1:49), cuts lag the beat consistently (Rayleigh p = 0.0055), and picture events sit near music events (cut 1:27.16 against voice exit and wall jump; 2:01.08 and 2:13.36 against thumps 1 and 3; 2:38.24 against bass return).
- *Not demonstrated (Sceptic; the Music critic and Film critic have since conceded):* the cut list misses cuts in snow (shot 20, 1:27 to 1:49, holds at least six images; the picture changes nearly every second from 1:34), so the cut-rate, phase and boundary figures are provisional. A shuffle test found only 3 of 9 boundaries within 0.5 s, which is chance (39% of shuffles). The phase result vanishes at the half-beat (p = 0.95) and the bar (p = 0.56) and is tracker-dependent.
- *Status:* unresolved and, with the current cut list, not decidable. The Music critic withdrew "fitted rather than co-composed" as a finding (round 3) and the Film critic dropped "lag" as a property of the edit. Where all agree: the picture follows the narration (signs within about a second of each spoken town; the date at 0:09 to 0:12). The Sceptic's framing "two independent constructions joined at the seam of a flat voice" and the Insider/Film critic's "fitted, not co-made" are the closest to consensus, held as INT.

**4.2 Do the thumps and the cuts line up?**
- *First-Timer:* a new image appears in the second of every one of the six thumps, and the first three land on tea-table people, the last three on the world outside.
- *Film critic:* only two of six are within 0.03 s of a detected cut (thumps 1 and 3, both cuts to a frost-caked face); thump 4 has no event; thump 6's sign is timed by the narration.
- *Sceptic/Music critic:* the baseline cut rate in this stretch (a change every 1 to 2 s) makes this expected by chance.
- *Status:* "consistent with, not shown to be"; the First-Timer's own wording. No case of an event held back for a thump was found.

**4.3 What does the 1:58 to 2:00 glance mean?**
- *Early rounds (First-Timer, Symbolist, Insider, Film critic):* the centre of the picture, a stunned, wary look into the lens, "the survivor".
- *Sceptic:* a neutral, overexposed look, one glance picked from the archive; the reason the passage moves people is not the gaze.
- *Resolved in round 3:* all dropped "stunned", "survivor" and "emotional centre". What stays: one of several direct looks to the lens (the others are brief: 0:15, 0:27, 0:31, 2:43 to 2:44); at 1:58 to 2:00 two of the five on the bench look at it, the longest steady look being the fair-haired person at far right (Film critic), under the thinnest mix; the Symbolist's observation that it is the one face looking while its neighbours drink; and the tea scene as the one place people sit down indoors. The Music critic adds that the music has "made room" by subtracting everything.

**4.4 Is the absence of a finish meaningful?**
- *Symbolist, First-Timer:* a viewer experiences a track that says "arrive" and pictures that never do; the Insider's early round-1 line "a kind of truth" and "refusal" is the strongest form.
- *Insider (retracted), Sceptic:* a 3:09 Part 1 of a two-part release; archive cameras usually stood at control points; do not treat an absence in an excerpt as deliberate.
- *Status:* the panel agrees on the effect and splits on intent. Viewer-side: no arrival is seen in the frames sampled. Maker-side: undecidable. The Film critic's refinement: a film made of signs and rooms could not deliver a finish line, "because a sign is what it has".

**4.5 Are the post-race newspapers a deliberate "outcome told first"?**
- *Symbolist, Film critic, First-Timer, Insider (earlier):* prolepsis, the afterwards inside the before.
- *Sceptic, Insider (later), Film critic:* ordinary title-card practice ("print as record"); newsprint was how the nation received the race.
- *Status:* observation only. OBS: the page at 0:23 is post-race (verified), the page at 0:04 is pre-race, and the page that appears at 0:23 returns at 2:57 (same crest, headline, photograph). Why is unknown.

**4.6 Is the "memory", "ghost", "1997 absence" layer in the film?**
- *Insider:* no tour since 1997, so the 2013 audience hears a refrain about waiting after 16 winters; the strongest "beyond the frame" reading, with an external cause.
- *Sceptic, Symbolist, First-Timer:* these are a viewer's context, not a property of the film; the vocabulary of ghosts and memory was the panel's own.
- *Status:* legitimate as reception, not as a finding about the film (Insider, round 3). The 16-winters count is arithmetic from 1997 to 2013, not a sourced statement.

**4.7 Elegy, comedy or affectionate recital? (the town list)**
- *First-Timer and Film critic:* pleasant, almost comic; a smile at the signs.
- *Symbolist:* a possible elegy.
- *Insider:* "affectionate recital", fond recognition first, loss second if at all.
- *Status:* all INT; "affectionate" is the one defended. No face smiles in the sampled frames, so any lightness lives in the signs and the inn front.

**4.8 Do the bilingual signs make a statement?**
- *Symbolist:* BOLSWARD / BOALSERT and FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER may be marked for a Frisian eye.
- *Insider:* possibly, if 1963 signage was Dutch-only, but this is unsourced background; not to be used.
- *Film critic:* a tight crop on a sign is also just how a sign is read on a small frame; "visible to a Frisian eye", not "emphasised by the edit".
- *Status:* unproven.

**4.9 Which single moment best captures the piece?** Four answers (section 7).

**4.10 Band alone: neutral drive, or moving?** First-Timer: "good but emotionally neutral". Music critic and Sceptic corrected this: not neutral, but tense not tender; moving as shapes of tension and release (floor lost and returned, thinning then re-thickening, an ending with no cadence), not as content.

## 5. Craft notes

**Music**
- **The mixer is the composer** (Music critic, M-H): pitch barely moves (D-centred drone; the third never above about 0.16 in power chroma; chords.json holds 143 changes, many of them probably detector reactions to distortion around a static D, doubt tagged; the A, D, G walk from about 2:15 is the one clear harmonic movement), so the form is stem on/off events: bass in at about 0:03, out 1:15.3, in 1:40.0, out about 1:55, six thumps 2:01 to 2:32, back about 2:38.4. Events are equally spaced in multiples of about 3.08 s (24, 28, 32 bars in the first half; odd blocks in the second). "Bar one is here" is tracker-derived and weaker (M, L-M for 0:31.7 "on a bar line").
- **Drum pattern and grid never change; timbre does**: dry, tight, a hat on 0.385 s slots against a kick on odd sixteenth slots; the only break is the 0.38 s hole. Brightness moves (share of drum-stem energy above 5 kHz: 1.4% at 0:20 to 0:31, 13.9% at 1:06 to 1:14, about 3% at 1:20 to 1:55, 14.1% at 2:50 to 3:07); hats open from about 1:05 and the kit is cymbal-led from 2:48. This is both the piece's identity and, per the Music critic and First-Timer, its main risk for a listener who does not lock into it (the middle can sag).
- **The voice is placed by subtraction**: it enters into cleared space (1:18.9 after the bass leaves; about 2:15 on the A, D, G walk) and exits at state changes (0:31.7, 2:47.8). The long voice-free block (1:27 to 2:15) coincides with both the loudest music by raw per-second dBFS (1:40 to 1:55; K-weighted about 1 LU lower) and the thinnest (1:55 to 2:13, about as quiet as the bass-less 1:15 to 1:27).
- **The sub thumps** are "the piece's one truly designed sound" (Music critic): a fixed 42.6 Hz tone, slot 21 mod 32, a second clock at 6.15 s over the 0.19 to 0.77 s grid. They are the part most likely to vanish on a phone.
- **Loudness and weight are different axes**: K-weighted loudness peaks equally (about -6.5 to -7 LUFS) at 1:07 to 1:15 and 2:48 to 3:08; weight peaks at 1:40 to 1:55 where the bass adds only about 0.2 to 0.5 LU. Contrast is made by width, bass and bandwidth, not dynamics (a heavily limited mix, about 8 LU short-term range).
- **Instruments**: "the wall" is distorted-sounding; guitar versus synth, played versus sequenced drums, and bass guitar versus synth bass are not settled (stems cannot separate them; CLAP is weak support).
- **Weaknesses** (Music critic's round 1 list, not retracted): no harmonic argument; repeated devices (the 1:15 drop and the 2:01 pull-down are structurally similar); a steady, non-densifying plateau at 2:48; Dutch speech over distorted rock is not always intelligible.

**Voice**
- One flat, band-limited, centred voice; no chopping or pitch shifting measurable; mostly facts. The refrain "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" appears at 0:27 to 0:31.7 and 2:44 to 2:48.2. Same words (H); same recording unproven (correlation 0.09, DTW 0.278). The Insider and Sceptic rank "a stock announcer's opening and closing line" above "deliberate refrain", untested.
- The town list is in route order (Sneek, IJlst, Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward, Harlingen, Franeker, Dokkum, Leeuwarden, with the start town moved to the end), which the panel reads as an ordinary list, not an editorial signal. Sneek, Sloten, Hindeloopen and Harlingen have no sign in the frames seen; the picture covers seven of eleven (six signs and the Vrouwe van Stavoren inn).

**Picture and archive**
- The footage is Frisian regional archive material (Fries Film Archief, per a MetaFilter post and a Written In Music article; no primary credit opened); which films and who the narrator is are unconfirmed. The shape (cameras at control points) may be partly inherited; "whoever assembled it kept it" (Film critic).
- Two verified reuses: the newspaper page (0:23 and 2:57) and the Dokkum sign shot (2:34 and 3:06.6, the same shot with a slight framing offset).
- Whip-pans and blur open (0:00) and recur (1:09, 2:47, 2:51, 3:03). A recurring peaked-cap official (1:07, 1:51, 2:44, 2:45, 2:49, 3:05) is SPEC and not used as a conclusion.
- **Structure by scale** (Film critic, round 2, adopted): close faces and paper at 1:15 to 1:27; an abrupt wide street at 1:27; progressively smaller figures in paler fields from 1:30; an aerial view of a snowy town about 1:44; back to a skater on a wooden ramp or steps at the ice edge with a crowd behind (1:46 to 1:48; no bridge visible). This is a shape the picture can be credited with, noting every element is inherited archive.
- **Sync measurements**: treat every cut-based figure (cut rate, phase, boundary counts, 7.8% versus 19.7%) as provisional, "in snow above all" (Film critic, Sceptic). The dossier's "slowest cutting in the film" at 1:28 to 1:49 is withdrawn. The one overall link kept is weak: cut rate against loudness r about 0.27 (0.262 at lag 0; sync.json, picture leading the music by about 4 s).

## 6. Context that changes the reading

- **The event.** 18 January 1963, the twelfth Elfstedentocht, "De Hel van '63": per the official site elfstedentocht.frl, 9,862 starters (568 racers, 9,294 tour skaters) and 127 finishers (sources differ: Wikipedia gives about 9,292 contestants and 69 finishers), Reinier Paping winning in 10 h 59, reported as about -18 C at the start (research notes; context.md). The film's figures (575, about 9,500, "16 below") are approximate or from a different time and place; they do not conflict (dossier).
- **The commission.** PSB were commissioned by the Explore the North festival, Leeuwarden, for the 50th anniversary; released autumn 2013; part of a two-part release (Part 2 is calmer, more electronic, 5:04, per research). Part 1 is therefore a half: the missing finish may belong to Part 2 (Sceptic, Insider).
- **Regional film, generic music.** From the sound alone no regional marker survives; the Frisian identity lives in the speech, the signs and the place names (Music critic, Insider). The music works for anyone; the film is regional.
- **What survives without Dutch and on a phone** (First-Timer, Music critic, Sceptic, Symbolist, INT):
  - Survives: the cold, the crowd, the tea scene, the sense of a relentless pulse, the abruptness of the ending, the bass-in and bass-out contrasts on a decent speaker, the voice exit at 0:31.7, the 2:48 release (width and brightness), the hole, the readable FINISH, and the pleasure of the town signs.
  - Needs Dutch: the content of "16 below" and "200 km", the refrain's meaning, the count over the paperwork, the town list as litany, the post-race headlines.
  - Needs a subwoofer or good speakers: the 42.6 Hz thumps and the full 1:40 weight. A laptop may reduce it to "the music gets a bit thicker".
  - Needs the history: the 1997 absence reading, "Hel van '63" as shorthand, the Frisian reading of the signs.
- **Reception**: documented reception speaks of drive, physical effort and image and sound fused (context.md; thin). Memory, ghost and hauntology readings are the panel's own vocabulary.
- **Time of day.** "Half zes" is 5:30 in the morning, the narrator's claim (the actual start time was not found in the research) (M-H); the first minute shows night crowds. The Friese Koerier page at 0:09 and the Leeuwarder Courant at 0:23 are post-race reports.

## 7. The moments a human would remember

The panel's four candidates for "the single moment that best captures the piece" in round 3, and the other moments that recur across the panel.

1. **1:40.0, the bass returns after about 25 s** (Music critic's choice; ranked first by the music reading). Bass about 35 dB higher in its own stem, width pulled from 0.78 to 0.40 over a pale picture of tiny skaters. It shows the whole method (subtract, wait, return), is measurable, and does not depend on Dutch or on any picture claim. Caveat: on a laptop it is partly lost.
2. **2:47 to 2:49, the whip-pan, the narrator's closing sentence, the 0.4 s hole, the FINISH banner** (Film critic, Sceptic, Symbolist). The word where an event should be, a crowd where a skater should be; then the sprint, with no crossing seen in the frames sampled. Sceptic's version: what is measured (the hole), what is seen (the sign) and what is absent (the finish) are all there, and nothing needs inventing. Symbolist: "a promise made in print, and the band keeps it for the picture."
3. **1:55 to 2:14, the tea tables** (First-Timer; Insider holds the passage as the film's humanest). The thinnest music, the first thump at 2:01.09, a row of cold people sipping from white cups, one looking toward us, a frost-caked face.
4. **2:29 to 2:34, the sign run** BOLSWARD / BOALSERT, FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER, DOKKUM (Insider). The one place voice, picture and place agree, within about a second each.

Also remembered: **1:15, the floor falling away** (all agree on the cause, H); **the six slow thumps** (largely lost on a phone); **0:31.7, the voice finishing and the band opening up**; **the sprint and the cut-off at 3:08.4**; **the repeats** (the newspaper and the Dokkum sign) and the **"Reportage" banner into black**.

The First-Timer's advice to a friend: watch it loud, on good speakers or headphones, and wait for 1:40 and 2:47; if you only watch one minute, watch 1:55 to 2:48.

## 8. What remains uncertain

**Retractions the panel made, so they do not return (all from rounds 2 and 3)**
- 1:40 is not a "white-out" or "the emptiest, slowest picture"; the frame is a pale, legible landscape with skaters in neighbouring frames and the picture changes nearly every second from 1:34. "Counterpoint by stillness", "erasure", "withheld cuts" and "weight against emptiness" are withdrawn; "weight against smallness in a large pale field" is the surviving form.
- "Cuts lag the beat by a quarter of a second" is a tracker-dependent hint, not an editorial signature.
- "Stunned", "survivor", "ghost", "funeral bell" and "seismic" are withdrawn, as is "the individual appears only where the music thins" (failed in principle: one footage cluster against one arrangement block, and individuals also appear under full sound).
- "The hole lands on FINISH" is "within a second of".
- "The one melody" is "the one moving line"; whether listeners hear it as melody is unknown.
- Voice bursts at 1:46.7 and 1:57, and "Bedankt voor het kijken!" at 3:07 and "MUZIEK" at 1:42, are not speech (H).
- "Dokkum leaves the longest leg to come": wrong. Franeker to Dokkum is about 45 km; Dokkum to Leeuwarden about 25 km, the last leg. "The film stops one leg short of closing its own loop" (Insider) is a modest INT.
- The narrator shows no sustained acceleration (rate varies within about 25%). The grid shift after the hole was retracted by Rhythm. Motorik was withdrawn as a pattern claim.

**Open on the picture side**
- Source films and narrator; whether footage from other editions or cameramen is mixed in (the on-skater shots at 1:05 to 1:11, the aerial at 1:44, the helicopter and plane); whether the double exposures at 0:04 and 0:10 are 1963 optics or 2013 edits.
- The 0:12 to 0:16 line (do not quote); the noun in "tweehonderd [...]" at about 0:19.
- Whether the 2:00 and 2:13 faces return; what the tangled pair (0:42), the 1:38 figure, the 1:53 scrum and the 2:08 kneeling skater are doing; where the table, village, ramp, control hut and FINISH crowd are; whether the rope at 2:40 is finish tape.
- Whether the banner enters inside the hole or just after it (1 fps, somewhere in 2:47.5 to 2:48.5).
- The small second line of the closing banner ("op de pa...": unreadable).
- Bib readings other than the few clear ones; none leads to a named skater.
- A rebuilt cut list (histogram or optical-flow check) is the first thing to do; all cut-based statistics should be re-run.

**Open on the sound side**
- Instrument identity (guitar versus synth; played versus programmed drums; bass type); where beat one is; the tempo octave (78 or 156 BPM, 55/45 for 78); major or minor (D major 0.76 against D minor 0.756); the notes of the final riff and of the 1:15 to 1:36 line.
- Whether 0:27 and 2:44 are the same recording; the exact timing of the town list (Whisper's two passes differ by about 3.6 s at "Leeuwarden", 2:34.7 vs 2:38.3; the vocal stem supports about 2:35); whether "Sneek" and "Sloten" are really spoken.
- Whether 3:08.4 and the black are the studio ending or the video edit, and how they relate to Part 2.
- The perceptual effect of the 42.6 Hz thump on any given playback system.

**Open on feeling and meaning**
- Every feeling word is an interpretation of measurements and pictures, not tested on listeners.
- Whether the picture-music pairings (1:27, 2:01 and 2:13, 2:38, 2:47.8) are designed or chance is not decidable on the current evidence; the narration is the one demonstrated meeting point.
- Whether the "outcome told first" opening, the "reportage as medium" ending, and the bookends are PSB's choices or the archive's.
- How much of the reading (litany, 1997, memory) a given viewer brings; Frisian, Dutch and non-Dutch viewers will hear different things.

**Measurement caveats carried over from the music reading**: K-weighted figures are the specialist's own implementation; demucs stems leak; stereo width is a side-to-mid ratio; window images were read by eye (about 0.3 s timing error); Whisper segment times are loose by about 0.5 to 0.8 s.
