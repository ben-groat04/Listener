# video1 (PSB, "Elfstedentocht Part 1", 3:08.7): the interpretation

Moderator's synthesis of `dossier.md` and all three panel rounds (Film critic, First-Timer, Insider, Music critic, Sceptic, Symbolist), with `music_reading.md` as the audio authority. Nothing here is new evidence: every claim traces to the panel, the dossier, the music reading or the data they cite. Times are m:ss. Tags: **OBS** seen or measured, **INT** interpretation, **SPEC** speculation; **H/M/L** confidence as in the music reading. Nobody on the panel, and not I, can hear audio: every "feels like" is a reasoned inference about a listener, not listener data. Where later rounds retracted earlier claims, the retraction wins (section 8 lists them).

---

## 1. The piece in one paragraph

A loud, relentless instrumental rock track on one pedal note (D), at about 78 BPM, with drums that never change gear, carries a three-minute collage of black-and-white archive film of the Elfstedentocht of 18 January 1963 ("De Hel van '63"), read by a flat Dutch narrator who supplies only facts: the date, "half past five in the morning", "16 degrees below zero", "a monster ride of over 200 km", 575 racers and about 9,500 tour skaters, the towns of the route. The band does the feeling and does it by subtraction: the bass, a dense distorted "wall" layer and the voice are switched off and on, and the one drum event in the whole track is a 0.4 s drop-out at 2:47.8. The picture does something different: it supplies a world, made largely of print, paperwork, tea, signs and crowds, in which skaters are mostly anonymous bent figures with hands behind their backs. What it does to you is push you steadily across a cold landscape: tense, stern and driven rather than sad or triumphant (music reading, M; Music critic, Sceptic and First-Timer in round 3). Nobody is shown arriving. The sound has an arrival (the bright sprint from 2:48), the picture only a hand-lettered word, FINISH, then a repeated town sign, a banner reading "Reportage ELFSTEDENTOCHT", and black. The panel's settled description of the whole is **two well-made layers joined at the narrator**, not a proven counterpoint (Sceptic, Film critic, Insider, Music critic, round 3).

## 3. Meaning and themes (consensus)

**3.1 The band supplies stakes and register; the picture supplies content and warmth.** Agreed by all six in round 3. The band states withholding, return, waiting and release but no valence: D with the third missing is neither major nor minor, so it sounds stern, not sad or triumphant (music reading, H on D, L on mode). The picture supplies faces, cups, cold and crowds. Evidence: nobody in the sampled frames smiles (First-Timer, Symbolist, with the caveat that only some stretches were checked); the same tea scene "under a bright major-key bed would be cosier" (Sceptic). Strongest evidence: the flat voice at a steady 4 to 5 syllable peaks per second with no quickening, and a band that never changes its drums.

**3.2 Collective, civic effort rather than heroics.** The film's units are places and documents (print at 0:04, 0:09, 0:22, 2:57; a registration table; tea; a control hut; eight town signs and an inn; FINISH; the Reportage banner); skaters are connective tissue, mostly seen from behind or at distance, in bibs (Film critic, round 3). The Insider, Symbolist and Film critic converge on "administration" and endurance. Evidence: bib and counter shots (1:18 to 1:26), the registration count at 1:19 to 1:27, no named skater, and the only named person a headline fragment ("R. Paping op kop", 0:23, small print). The narration reads "575 racers, about 9,500 tour skaters", not a winner.

**3.3 The subject is the effort, not the result.** Nobody is shown crossing a line (First-Timer, Symbolist, Film critic, Sceptic). The panel disagrees about whether this is meaningful (section 5), but all describe it as what a viewer experiences.

**3.4 Names are given to the route, not to the people.** The Symbolist's round 3 thread, with Insider support: the towns are the only things said aloud one by one, each gets a sign within about a second, and no skater gets that treatment. "Language says where, the band says how hard, and the faces are left unlabelled." Labelled INT by its author.

**3.5 Roll-call or litany as grammar.** Fixed pulse, a drone with no third, a flat narrator and the town list make a litany-like form (Insider, Film critic; the Music critic finds real marks in the sound: no gear changes, no tonic movement, flat delivery). Limits agreed in round 3: it is apt for the 2:16 to 2:36 passage and for the general grammar, not a proven design for the thumps; the thumps run 2:01 to 2:32 and only three of the six sit under the voice.

**3.6 Print and the camera looking back.** The film opens and ends on print (newspaper, signs, banners) and shows its own recording (large film camera and microphone 0:57 to 0:59, tripod cameraman to 1:04, a camera-like object at 2:49, the Reportage banner at 3:07). Held as a motif ("print as record"), not as a structure.

**3.7 Cold made visible.** Rime on caps and sleeves (0:27, 2:01, 2:13 to 2:14), frosted reeds (1:34), hoar on branches (2:27), a ground blizzard (0:51 to 0:56). This is a picture-side feeling that survives with no Dutch and on any speaker.

## 4. Live disagreements

Where a disagreement was resolved in round 3 I say so; where it is still live I give both sides.

**4.1 Is the picture's relationship to the music designed?**
- *Designed / fitted (earlier rounds; Film critic's partial position):* cut-density follows the music (7.58 cuts per 10 s at 1:06 to 1:16 against 0.46 at 1:28 to 1:49), cuts lag the beat consistently (Rayleigh p = 0.0055), and picture events sit near music events (cut 1:27.16 against voice exit and wall jump; 2:01.08 and 2:13.36 against thumps 1 and 3; 2:38.24 against bass return).
- *Not demonstrated (Sceptic; the Music critic and Film critic have since conceded):* the cut list misses cuts in snow (shot 20, 1:27 to 1:49, holds at least six images; the picture changes nearly every second from 1:34), so the cut-rate, phase and boundary figures are provisional. A shuffle test found only 3 of 9 boundaries within 0.5 s, which is chance (39% of shuffles). The phase result vanishes at the half-beat (p = 0.95) and the bar (p = 0.56) and is tracker-dependent.
- *Status:* unresolved and, with the current cut list, not decidable. The Music critic withdrew "fitted rather than co-composed" as a finding (round 3) and the Film critic dropped "lag" as a property of the edit. Where all agree: the picture follows the narration (signs within about a second of each spoken town; the date at 0:09 to 0:12). The Sceptic's framing "two independent constructions joined at the seam of a flat voice" and the Insider/Film critic's "fitted, not co-made" are the closest to consensus, held as INT.

**4.2 Do the thumps and the cuts line up?**
- *First-Timer:* a new image appears in the second of every one of the six thumps, and the first three land on tea-table people, the last three on the world outside.
- *Film critic:* only two of six are within 0.03 s of a detected cut (thumps 1 and 3, both cuts to a frost-caked face); thump 4 has no event; thump 6's sign is timed by the narration.
- *Sceptic/Music critic:* the baseline cut rate in this stretch (a change every 1 to 2 s) makes this expected by chance.
- *Status:* "consistent with, not shown to be"; the First-Timer's own wording. No case of an event held back for a thump was found.

**4.3 What does the 1:58 to 2:00 glance mean?**
- *Early rounds (First-Timer, Symbolist, Insider, Film critic):* the centre of the picture, a stunned, wary look into the lens, "the survivor".
- *Sceptic:* a neutral, overexposed look, one glance picked from the archive; the reason the passage moves people is not the gaze.
- *Resolved in round 3:* all dropped "stunned", "survivor" and "emotional centre". What stays: the only direct look to the lens in the film (the Film critic), under the thinnest mix; the Symbolist's observation that it is the one face looking while its neighbours drink; and the tea scene as the one place people sit down indoors. The Music critic adds that the music has "made room" by subtracting everything.

**4.4 Is the absence of a finish meaningful?**
- *Symbolist, First-Timer:* a viewer experiences a track that says "arrive" and pictures that never do; the Insider's early round-1 line "a kind of truth" and "refusal" is the strongest form.
- *Insider (retracted), Sceptic:* a 3:09 Part 1 of a two-part release; archive cameras usually stood at control points; do not treat an absence in an excerpt as deliberate.
- *Status:* the panel agrees on the effect and splits on intent. Viewer-side: nobody arrives. Maker-side: undecidable. The Film critic's refinement: a film made of signs and rooms could not deliver a finish line, "because a sign is what it has".

**4.5 Are the post-race newspapers a deliberate "outcome told first"?**
- *Symbolist, Film critic, First-Timer, Insider (earlier):* prolepsis, the afterwards inside the before.
- *Sceptic, Insider (later), Film critic:* ordinary title-card practice ("print as record"); newsprint was how the nation received the race.
- *Status:* observation only. OBS: the page at 0:22 is post-race (verified), the page at 0:04 is pre-race, and the 0:22 page returns at 2:57 (same crest, headline, photograph). Why is unknown.

**4.6 Is the "memory", "ghost", "1997 absence" layer in the film?**
- *Insider:* no tour since 1997, so the 2013 audience hears a refrain about waiting after 16 winters; the strongest "beyond the frame" reading, with an external cause.
- *Sceptic, Symbolist, First-Timer:* these are a viewer's context, not a property of the film; the vocabulary of ghosts and memory was the panel's own.
- *Status:* legitimate as reception, not as a finding about the film (Insider, round 3). The 16-winters count is arithmetic from 1997 to 2013, not a sourced statement.

**4.7 Elegy, comedy or affectionate recital? (the town list)**
- *First-Timer and Film critic:* pleasant, almost comic; a smile at the signs.
- *Symbolist:* a possible elegy.
- *Insider:* "affectionate recital", fond recognition first, loss second if at all.
- *Status:* all INT; "affectionate" is the one defended. No face smiles in the sampled frames, so any lightness lives in the signs and the inn front.

**4.8 Do the bilingual signs make a statement?**
- *Symbolist:* BOLSWARD / BOALSERT and FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER may be marked for a Frisian eye.
- *Insider:* possibly, if 1963 signage was Dutch-only, but this is unsourced background; not to be used.
- *Film critic:* a tight crop on a sign is also just how a sign is read on a small frame; "visible to a Frisian eye", not "emphasised by the edit".
- *Status:* unproven.

**4.9 Which single moment best captures the piece?** Four answers (section 7).

**4.10 Band alone: neutral drive, or moving?** First-Timer: "good but emotionally neutral". Music critic and Sceptic corrected this: not neutral, but tense not tender; moving as shapes of tension and release (floor lost and returned, thinning then re-thickening, an ending with no cadence), not as content.

## 5. Craft notes

**Music**
- **The mixer is the composer** (Music critic, M-H): pitch barely moves (D pedal; the third never above about 0.16 in power chroma; 125 chord labels mostly detector reactions to a static D), so the form is stem on/off events: bass in at about 0:03, out 1:15.3, in 1:40.0, out about 1:55, six thumps 2:01 to 2:32, back about 2:38.4. Events are equally spaced in multiples of about 3.08 s (24, 28, 32 bars in the first half; odd blocks in the second). "Bar one is here" is tracker-derived and weaker (M, L-M for 0:31.7 "on a bar line").
- **Drums never change gear**: dry, tight, a hat on 0.385 s slots against a kick on odd sixteenth slots; the only drum event is the 0.38 s hole. This is both the piece's identity and, per the Music critic and First-Timer, its main risk for a listener who does not lock into it (the middle can sag).
- **The voice is placed by subtraction**: it enters into cleared space (1:18.9 after the bass leaves; 2:16 on the A, D, G walk) and exits at state changes (0:31.7, 2:47.8). The long voice-free block (1:27 to 2:15) coincides with the loudest and the thinnest music.
- **The sub thumps** are "the piece's one truly designed sound" (Music critic): a fixed 42.6 Hz tone, slot 21 mod 32, a second clock at 6.15 s over the 0.19 to 0.77 s grid. They are the part most likely to vanish on a phone.
- **Loudness and weight are different axes**: K-weighted loudness peaks at 1:07 to 1:15 and 2:48 to 3:08; weight peaks at 1:40 to 1:55 where the bass adds about 0.1 LU. Contrast is made by width, bass and bandwidth, not dynamics (a heavily limited mix, about 8 LU short-term range).
- **Instruments**: "the wall" is distorted-sounding; guitar versus synth, played versus sequenced drums, and bass guitar versus synth bass are not settled (stems cannot separate them; CLAP is weak support).
- **Weaknesses** (Music critic's round 1 list, not retracted): no harmonic argument; repeated devices (the 1:15 drop and the 2:01 pull-down are structurally similar); a steady, non-densifying plateau at 2:48; Dutch speech over distorted rock is not always intelligible.

**Voice**
- One flat, band-limited, centred voice; no chopping or pitch shifting measurable; mostly facts. The refrain "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" appears at 0:27 to 0:31.7 and 2:44 to 2:48.2. Same words (H); same recording unproven (correlation 0.09, DTW 0.278). The Insider and Sceptic rank "a stock announcer's opening and closing line" above "deliberate refrain", untested.
- The town list is in route order (Sneek, IJlst, Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward, Harlingen, Franeker, Dokkum, Leeuwarden, with the start town moved to the end), which the panel reads as an ordinary list, not an editorial signal. Sneek, Sloten and Hindeloopen have no sign in the frames seen; the picture covers six or seven of eleven.

**Picture and archive**
- The footage is Frisian regional archive material (Fries Film Archief); which films and who the narrator is are unconfirmed. The shape (cameras at control points) may be partly inherited; "whoever assembled it kept it" (Film critic).
- Two verified reuses: the newspaper page (0:22 and 2:57) and the Dokkum sign shot (2:34 and 3:06.6, the same frame).
- Whip-pans and blur open (0:00) and recur (1:09, 2:47, 2:51, 3:03). A recurring peaked-cap official (1:07, 1:51, 2:44, 2:45, 2:49, 3:05) is SPEC and not used as a conclusion.
- **Structure by scale** (Film critic, round 2, adopted): close faces and paper at 1:15 to 1:27; an abrupt wide street at 1:27; progressively smaller figures in paler fields from 1:30; an aerial view of a snowy town about 1:44; back to faces at a bridge about 1:47. This is a shape the picture can be credited with, noting every element is inherited archive.
- **Sync measurements**: treat every cut-based figure (cut rate, phase, boundary counts, 7.8% versus 19.7%) as provisional, "in snow above all" (Film critic, Sceptic). The dossier's "slowest cutting in the film" at 1:28 to 1:49 is withdrawn.

## 6. Context that changes the reading

- **The event.** 18 January 1963, the twelfth Elfstedentocht, "De Hel van '63": about 9,862 starters (568 racers, 9,294 tour skaters), 127 finishers, Reinier Paping winning in 10 h 59, about -18 C at the start (research notes; context.md). The film's figures (575, about 9,500, "16 below") are approximate or from a different time and place; they do not conflict (dossier).
- **The commission.** PSB were commissioned by the Explore the North festival, Leeuwarden, for the 50th anniversary; released autumn 2013; part of a two-part release (Part 2 is calmer, more electronic, 5:04, per research). Part 1 is therefore a half: the missing finish may belong to Part 2 (Sceptic, Insider).
- **Regional film, generic music.** From the sound alone no regional marker survives; the Frisian identity lives in the speech, the signs and the place names (Music critic, Insider). The music works for anyone; the film is regional.
- **What survives without Dutch and on a phone** (First-Timer, Music critic, Sceptic, Symbolist, INT):
  - Survives: the cold, the crowd, the tea scene, the sense of a relentless pulse, the abruptness of the ending, the bass-in and bass-out contrasts on a decent speaker, the voice exit at 0:31.7, the 2:48 release (width and brightness), the hole, the readable FINISH, and the pleasure of the town signs.
  - Needs Dutch: the content of "16 below" and "200 km", the refrain's meaning, the count over the paperwork, the town list as litany, the post-race headlines.
  - Needs a subwoofer or good speakers: the 42.6 Hz thumps and the full 1:40 weight. A laptop may reduce it to "the music gets a bit thicker".
  - Needs the history: the 1997 absence reading, "Hel van '63" as shorthand, the Frisian reading of the signs.
- **Reception**: documented reception speaks of drive, physical effort and image and sound fused (context.md; thin). Memory, ghost and hauntology readings are the panel's own vocabulary.
- **Time of day.** "Half zes" is 5:30 in the morning (M-H); the first minute shows night crowds. The Friese Koerier page at 0:09 and the Leeuwarder Courant at 0:22 are post-race reports.

## 7. The moments a human would remember

The panel's four candidates for "the single moment that best captures the piece" in round 3, and the other moments that recur across the panel.

1. **1:40.0, the bass returns after 24 s** (Music critic's choice; ranked first by the music reading). Bass about 35 dB higher in its own stem, width pulled from 0.78 to 0.40 over a pale picture of tiny skaters. It shows the whole method (subtract, wait, return), is measurable, and does not depend on Dutch or on any picture claim. Caveat: on a laptop it is partly lost.
2. **2:47 to 2:49, the whip-pan, the narrator's closing sentence, the 0.4 s hole, the FINISH banner** (Film critic, Sceptic, Symbolist). The word where an event should be, a crowd where a skater should be; then the sprint, with nobody reaching anything. Sceptic's version: what is measured (the hole), what is seen (the sign) and what is absent (the finish) are all there, and nothing needs inventing. Symbolist: "a promise made in print, and the band keeps it for the picture."
3. **1:55 to 2:14, the tea tables** (First-Timer; Insider holds the passage as the film's humanest). The thinnest music, the first thump at 2:01.09, a row of cold people sipping from white cups, one looking toward us, a frost-caked face.
4. **2:29 to 2:34, the sign run** BOLSWARD / BOALSERT, FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER, DOKKUM (Insider). The one place voice, picture and place agree, within about a second each.

Also remembered: **1:15, the floor falling away** (all agree on the cause, H); **the six slow thumps** (largely lost on a phone); **0:31.7, the voice finishing and the band opening up**; **the sprint and the cut-off at 3:08.4**; **the repeats** (the newspaper and the Dokkum sign) and the **"Reportage" banner into black**.

The First-Timer's advice to a friend: watch it loud, on good speakers or headphones, and wait for 1:40 and 2:47; if you only watch one minute, watch 1:55 to 2:48.

## 8. What remains uncertain

**Retractions the panel made, so they do not return (all from rounds 2 and 3)**
- 1:40 is not a "white-out" or "the emptiest, slowest picture"; the frame is a pale, legible landscape with skaters in neighbouring frames and the picture changes nearly every second from 1:34. "Counterpoint by stillness", "erasure", "withheld cuts" and "weight against emptiness" are withdrawn; "weight against smallness in a large pale field" is the surviving form.
- "Cuts lag the beat by a quarter of a second" is a tracker-dependent hint, not an editorial signature.
- "Stunned", "survivor", "ghost", "funeral bell" and "seismic" are withdrawn, as is "the individual appears only where the music thins" (failed in principle: one footage cluster against one arrangement block, and individuals also appear under full sound).
- "The hole lands on FINISH" is "within a second of".
- "The one melody" is "the one moving line"; whether listeners hear it as melody is unknown.
- Voice bursts at 1:46.7 and 1:57, and "Bedankt voor het kijken!" at 3:07 and "MUZIEK" at 1:42, are not speech (H).
- "Dokkum leaves the longest leg to come": wrong. Franeker to Dokkum is about 45 km; Dokkum to Leeuwarden about 25 km, the last leg. "The film stops one leg short of closing its own loop" (Insider) is a modest INT.
- The narrator does not speed up. The grid shift after the hole was retracted by Rhythm. Motorik was withdrawn as a pattern claim.

**Open on the picture side**
- Source films and narrator; whether footage from other editions or cameramen is mixed in (the on-skater shots at 1:05 to 1:11, the aerial at 1:44, the helicopter and plane); whether the double exposures at 0:04 and 0:10 are 1963 optics or 2013 edits.
- The 0:12 to 0:16 line (do not quote); the noun in "tweehonderd [...]" at about 0:19.
- Whether the 2:00 and 2:13 faces return; what the tangled pair (0:42), the 1:38 figure, the 1:53 scrum and the 2:08 kneeling skater are doing; where the table, village, ramp, control hut and FINISH crowd are; whether the rope at 2:40 is finish tape.
- Whether the banner enters inside the hole or just after it (1 fps, somewhere in 2:47.5 to 2:48.5).
- The small second line of the closing banner ("op de pa...": unreadable).
- Bib readings other than the few clear ones; none leads to a named skater.
- A rebuilt cut list (histogram or optical-flow check) is the first thing to do; all cut-based statistics should be re-run.
