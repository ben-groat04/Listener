# The Symbolist (panel, round 1): video1, PSB "Elfstedentocht Part 1"

Basis: `music_reading.md`, `dossier.md`, and frames I opened myself (f_0101, f_0121, f_0169). Tags: OBS = seen or measured; INT = my reading; SPEC = speculation. Times m:ss. I cannot hear the audio; "feel" below is interpretation of measured sound and picture, not listener data.

## 1. What it is about

On the surface, a record of a race. Beneath it, I think the piece is about **how a community remembers an ordeal it has already survived**, and about the gap between the *count* and the *person*.

Three tensions carry this (INT, each tied to evidence).

**a) Facts versus feeling, split between two channels.** The narrator supplies only statistics: date, hour, "16 below", "200 km", 575, 9,500, eleven towns. The band supplies all the feeling, by subtraction, never by decoration (music reading sections 6 and 7; the voice does not quicken, and the band does not duck for it). The piece is therefore a study of what a number cannot hold. The sharpest place is 1:18 to 1:26: a voice reads "575 racers, about 9,500 tour skaters" while the picture shows hands writing and stamping paper at a table. The same minute contains bib 157 held tight on a gesturing man (1:21). A count becomes a person for one second and then is a count again.

**b) The outcome is told first.** Both newspapers shown before the start are post-race reports (0:09 "bovenmenselijk zwaar", "Chaos aan de finish"; 0:22 "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar", "Tal van uitvallers en vele gewonden"). So the "race" we watch is already history: everyone on screen is, in a sense, already a memory. I read the whole film as *prolepsis*: we are looking at a hard day through the headline that will outlast it (INT; whether this was PSB's device or the source film's is open, dossier Q11).

**c) The refrain that explains nothing.** "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" ("what everyone is preparing for: that is the Elfstedentocht") is the only repeated line (0:27 to 0:31.7; 2:44 to 2:48.2). It defines the event by anticipation, never by arrival. Hence the film never shows a finish crossed or a winner named (dossier overview). The subject is **the waiting and the preparing**, the "everyone", not the victory. I think that is the real subject: collective expectation as a landscape.

## 2. Motifs and visual rhymes I would defend

- **White versus the figure.** Emptiness against tiny bodies (1:30 extreme long shot; 1:40 white-out; 2:19; 2:54) set against packed banks of faces (0:14; 1:46; 2:40 to 2:46). I opened f_0101 (1:40): the frame is near-blank snow scrub, a vertical scratch, almost nothing. OBS. The film oscillates between *erasure* and *crowd*; the human being is either lost in the white or dissolved into a mass. Almost never an individual with a name.
- **Print as the frame of memory.** The film opens with newsprint (0:04, 0:09, 0:22), moves through signs (IJlst, Workum, Bolsward, Franeker, Dokkum), the "FINISH" banner, and ends on a hand-lettered "Reportage ELFSTEDENTOCHT" banner (3:07) and black. The 0:22 front page returns at 2:57, and the Dokkum sign at 3:06.6 repeats 2:34 (both verified in the dossier by frame comparison). So the piece is bracketed by *text about the event*, not the event. INT: the event survives only as writing and sign-painting.
- **Warmth only where the music empties.** Tea, cups, bench (1:54 to 2:14) arrive as the bass leaves and the mix drops to its thinnest. Warmth is offered only in the quiet. The loud passages hold no hot drink.
- **The looking-back camera.** Film camera and gooseneck microphone (0:57 to 1:04), a camera-like object (2:49), the Reportage banner (3:07). The medium watches itself being a medium: memory knows it is constructed.
- **Hands.** Hands clasped behind the back on the ice (0:38, 0:49, 1:30, 2:55); hands on paper in the towns (1:18 to 1:26, 2:15 to 2:17); hands around cups (1:58 to 2:12). Three kinds of hands: those that skate, those that record, those that hold on to warmth. A neat, readable triad (INT).

## 3. How it makes me feel, and exactly when

I feel *disciplined dread resolving into stoic release*, not triumph. The measured cause is a D pedal with the third missing (music reading section 3), so the sound is stern rather than sad or glad.

- **0:00 to 0:10, a snap and a gather (M).** Blur, then a dark crowd at a doorway, with the pulse already running. Furtive. The band is on before the story starts, which tells me the ordeal is underway before anyone has explained it.
- **0:31.7 to 0:41, band alone, picture cold and still.** Hot sound against a grey dawn: the first place where music and picture disagree on purpose, and where I feel the *distance* between inside and outside the body (OBS contrast).
- **1:15 to 1:27, the floor falls away.** The bass cuts, the mix drops 4 to 6 dB, and a spoken count enters into the cleared space over registration hands. Unease through absence (H on cause, M on feeling).
- **1:40, the bass returns after 24 s, over a white-out.** This is the moment I would put at the centre of the piece. The heaviest sonic arrival meets the emptiest picture (f_0101) and the slowest cutting in the film (0.46 cuts per 10 s). OBS. I read it as *weight meeting erasure*: the body's effort, the music insists, goes on in a place that has no features. The picture does not illustrate the effort; the music is the effort and the picture is the world that does not care. That is, to me, the thesis moment. It is also deliberately counter-sync: no cut at 1:40.
- **1:58 to 2:00, the gaze.** I opened f_0121: a row of young people drinking from white cups; at the right, a curly-haired young person looks straight into the lens, face washed pale, a faintly stunned, unsmiling stare. OBS. Under the thinnest, most exposed music. This is the human centre of the piece (INT): the only one of the few direct gazes that holds. The film's whole logic of crowds and numbers pauses for a face that looks back at *us*. I feel that gaze as "you who watch this sixty years on": the survivor looking at the future.
- **2:01 to 2:31, six slow sub thumps over a fast flutter.** Waiting; a slow heartbeat under a fast one. If you hear them at all (inaudible on small speakers, so on a laptop my feeling here would be the thinning, not the thump).
- **2:44 to 2:48, the refrain returns.** "Everyone" now literally on screen: faces turned off-frame, watching something we never see.
- **2:47.8, the hole.** I opened f_0169 (2:48): the hand-painted white banner "FINISH" held in front of a packed crowd. OBS. The band stops for about 0.4 s, the narrator's last syllable decays inside it, and the picture offers the one word that means ending. This is the cleverest single joint in the work (see below) and it moves me because it is *so small*: no cheer, no tape, a half-second of breath.
- **2:48 to 3:08, the sprint.** Loudest, steadiest, brightest. I do not feel victory, because there is no finish image to receive it. It is grinding persistence; the picture quickens to 0.5 s shots while the sound stays level. Then black and a half-second taper: the effort continues offscreen (M).

## 4. What is clever

1. **The FINISH banner in the hole (2:47.8).** Word and silence meet. Caveat I must keep: at 1 fps I cannot say whether the banner is cut in inside the hole or just after (the dossier says the cut falls within 2:47.5 to 2:48.5), and the nearest detected cut is 2:48.8. So the "sync" is plausible, not proven. But the *placement* (the only word of ending, at the only drum event in the track) is not nothing even if by luck.
2. **Counter-sync at 1:40.** Not cutting to the bass is a stronger choice than cutting. Cuts follow neither the beat nor the bar (sync: 7.8% within 80 ms against 19.7% by chance; cut phase consistently about 0.25 s late). I read that lag as a gentle *dragging* of the picture behind the pulse: the image is always slightly late, as memory is. That is a stretch (SPEC); the measurement itself is H.
3. **A band that never speaks for the narrator.** The voice is flat, unperformed, never speeding up (4 to 5 syllable peaks per second throughout), so the film refuses to editorialise; the emotion is placed wholly in the band and the viewer. A hard discipline.
4. **The bookends: 0:22 newspaper at 2:57, Dokkum at 2:34 and 3:06.6.** Quietly structural. The roll-call of towns is an actual walk around the route, and returning to its one fixed sign gives the closing sprint a spine.

## 5. What is weak

- **No emotional destination.** The piece withholds a finish image altogether. I admire that as a position, but as drama it leaves the final 20 s of full-volume sprint with nothing for the eye to arrive at. The music is loud and steady for 20 s (-7.0 to -6.4 LUFS), the picture merely accelerates. I feel more *held in place* than *carried*.
- **The town list is a catalogue.** 2:16 to 2:36 is the tightest word-to-image match, but also the most literal. Sign appears, word is said, repeat. After the white-out and the gaze it is a step down in poetry (INT). The dossier notes some names (Sneek, Sloten) have no matching sign at all, so even the literalism is patchy.
- **The refrain, delivered twice, is flat both times.** It is the nearest thing to a chorus, but a flat, unperformed voice cannot make a return feel earned. Whether it is the same take is unresolved (waveform correlation 0.09; DTW 0.278).
- **Statistics as a repeated emotional device.** "16 below" and "200 km" are the engine of the cold and distance; they carry less than the frost on a cap (2:01 to 2:03, f_0122 per dossier), which shows the temperature with no text at all. The best images out-argue the narration.
- **A caveat on my own reading.** The "outcome told first" and "memory" readings depend on the newspapers being post-race pages. The dossier says they are; I have not read the headlines myself beyond the dossier's frame-by-frame opening (f_0024), so I treat them as H on the facts, M on the prolepsis device.

## 6. What I am deliberately NOT claiming

- No symbolism for "POSTHUMA" (an ordinary surname; any "posthumous" pun is accidental).
- No meaning for "bib 12 and the 12th race" (probably coincidence).
- No ghost-eyes at 0:12 as a deliberate motif; they are an unidentified lamp or reflectors.
- No claim that the 6.15 s thumps are a funeral bell or a heartbeat. They are a measured 42.6 Hz tone (M-H on measurement, L on perception); "heartbeat" is my metaphor only.
- The ghost, memory and mortality readings go beyond documented reception (which speaks of drive, physical effort, and image-and-sound fused); they are mine.

## 7. Three questions for the other panellists

1. **To the music and sync panellists:** the one-measure cause of my "thesis moment" is the contrast of 1:40 (bass back, picture empty). Is there any reading of the sync data (cut phase about 0.25 s late; no cut at 1:15.3, 1:40.0, 1:55 or 2:47.8) that would let us say the editor deliberately *withheld* cuts at the big musical events, as opposed to simply following footage and narration? What would distinguish the two?
2. **To the research and context panellists:** the two pre-race newspapers are post-race reports and the film shows no finish crossed. Is there evidence (Fries Film Archief material, 2013 commission notes, PSB's other archive pieces) that withholding the finish and opening on print are PSB's characteristic devices, or are they the source films' own, so that I am crediting the editor with the archive's habit?
3. **To the human-experience panellists:** at 1:58 to 2:00 the curly-haired young person looks into the lens under the thinnest music. Does that glance read to a first-time viewer as the emotional centre, or do the loud peaks (1:40, 2:48) overwhelm it? And, separately: does the 20 s final sprint feel like arrival or like treading water, given that no image of arrival ever comes?
