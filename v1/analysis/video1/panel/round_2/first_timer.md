# First-Timer (panel, round 2): video1, PSB "Elfstedentocht Part 1"

Still the plain viewer. I read all six round 1 statements. I re-opened f_0121 (1:58 to 2:00) and contact sheets 09 and 10 (2:40 to 3:08) to check what I was asked. I still cannot hear the audio, so every sound claim below is borrowed from the music reading and the other panellists. Times are m:ss.

## 1. Answers to the questions put to me

**Symbolist: does the 1:58 glance read as the emotional centre, or do the loud peaks overwhelm it? Does the sprint feel like arrival or treading water?**

I have to correct myself first. In round 1 I called the person at the right of f_0121 "pale, wary, slightly stunned", and the Symbolist went further ("the survivor looking at the future"). Looking again, the Sceptic is closer: it is an unsmiling, rather blank look, with the forehead and cheeks blown out white by the exposure. Whatever is moving about the frame is mostly the row of people beside her or him, all sipping from white cups, one with glasses and a dark hood, one in a striped hat, and the steam-less ordinariness of it. The glance is a bonus, not the centre. I concede that "stunned" was my projection.

Does it survive the loud peaks? For me, yes, but for a reason the data supports and our "feelings" do not: it is the only place in three minutes where people are sitting down, indoors, doing something small. The loud moments (1:40, 2:48) are things I read about; the tea is something I can see without any help. That matters for the next point.

On the sprint, going by the sheets: 2:48 to 3:08 does not feel like arrival to me. Looking at sheet 09 and 10, no one crosses anything. After the FINISH banner (2:48) the sprint is a rear view of a bibbed skater (2:52, 342), one skater stooping on the ice (2:53), an empty white field (2:54), four skaters from behind (2:55 to 2:56), the newspaper again (2:57), ice with a crack (2:58), another skater bent over (3:02 and, from the sheet, 2:59), a blur, a crowd, Dokkum, and the "Reportage" banner. My own reading, not measured: the two bent-over figures look like people adjusting or resting rather than racing, though I cannot tell at one frame a second. So it is treading water in the picture while the sound pushes on. I would call that the film's honest answer: it is a day of effort with no ending we are given, and the sprint is the band doing the finishing the pictures will not do.

**Sceptic: which feelings survive phone speakers and no Dutch?**

A fair challenge, and my round 1 list was loose. Sorting it, by what I think each depends on:
- Survives with no Dutch and on a phone: the cold (frost on faces, whiteness, breath), the crowd pressing (0:14, 2:40 to 2:46), the tea scene, the sense of a relentless pulse, the abruptness of the ending, the smile at the town signs (the written names are readable by anyone).
- Depends on Dutch: nothing in "16 degrees below zero" or "200 kilometres" reaches a non-speaker as meaning. The refrain at 0:27 and 2:44 becomes "a repeated sentence", which still works as a bookend by sound alone.
- Depends on good speakers: the 1:40 "landing" and the 42.6 Hz thumps (2:01 to 2:32). Here I side with the Sceptic: the loudness change at 1:40 is only about 0.1 LU, so on a laptop my "weight of the country" feeling would, I suspect, shrink to "the music gets a bit thicker". I should have said so. I placed the biggest claim of my round 1 on the weakest foundation.
- Depends on knowing the history: the post-race newspapers as a "spoiler" (my own uncertainty about it stands), and anything about Friesland, 1997 or the tour being unrepeatable.

**Music critic: where does the sadness, pride or awe actually come from, and what is in the film that is not in the music?**

Not sadness for me: nobody in the film smiles or cries, and the band never goes major or minor. What I feel is endurance and a sort of tenderness towards strangers. That comes from the picture, not the music: the tea (1:55 to 2:00), the frost-caked face (2:01), people in the crowd looking tired and cold rather than excited (2:43 to 2:46: I opened sheet 09, nobody is cheering). The music holds the pulse; it never says "be sad". What the picture adds that the band withholds is simply human faces, and a place to rest. If the audio were muted I would still feel cold and respect; if the picture were removed I would hear a tough, tidy, slightly emotionless drive. I think that answers "what is not in the music": almost all the warmth.

**Insider: how much survives without Dutch and without knowing no tour has run since 1997?**

Most of the mood, little of the specifics. I did not know about 1997 until I read the notes, and I do not think the film pushes it. The town list works for me as a rhythm of names on signs, which is what makes it the one sequence I enjoyed on first look. But I agree with the Insider that Frisian viewers will get something I cannot, and with the Insider's honest caveat that the "memory and ghost" reading is ours. As a non-Dutch viewer I would call it a film about weather and effort that happens to have place names.

## 2. Where I agree, and where I push back

- **Agree with the Sceptic** that 1:40 is a guess about a listener, and that "the heaviest sound on the emptiest picture" is only meaningful if someone hears the bass. I now put my 1:40 feeling at low confidence.
- **Agree with the Film critic's own caveat** that the editor may simply have had long shots there. A white-out held for a while sits under the bass return; I cannot tell contrast from luck, and my round 1 "counterpoint" language overreached. What I can say is that to a viewer it looks like an empty gap, whoever intended it.
- **Push back on the Sceptic** over the null test only a little: I cannot run it, but the dossier's 7.8% of cuts on a beat against 19.7% by chance is itself evidence that cuts are not snapped, which cuts against "design" for individual alignments. So the Sceptic's point is already supported by the numbers, and nobody should describe 1:06.4 and friends as design.
- **Push back on the Symbolist** over "the survivor looking at the future": the frame does not show someone survived anything; we know nothing about this person. It is a nice thought, but it comes from us, not the frame.
- **Agree with the Insider and Symbolist** that the town list and the tea scene are where the film feels most like people.

## 3. The thread I most want to develop: nobody is shown arriving

Several of us noticed that no finish is ever crossed. Having looked at 2:40 to 3:08 frame by frame, I think this is the most interesting fact about the film from a viewer's seat. The FINISH banner at 2:48 is hand-lettered, held up by people in a packed crowd, with a policeman turning away at 2:49. It is the nearest the film gets to an ending, and it is a sign, not an event. Then the sprint offers more skaters going away from us (backs at 2:52 and 2:55), a headline that says "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar" (2:57), a town sign and a banner that says "Reportage". The music says "arrive"; the picture says "this is all about the effort and the telling of it". I think a first-time viewer ends up feeling half satisfied, half cheated (my round 1 phrase), and I now think that is not a flaw to fix but the whole shape: we are given the crowd that waits, the sign that says finish, the paper that says it was hard. Whether it is designed or inherited from the archive (the Film critic's and Symbolist's open question), a viewer experiences it as designed.
