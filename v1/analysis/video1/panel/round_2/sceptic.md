# The Sceptic: Round 2 (video1, PSB "Elfstedentocht Part 1", 3:09)

Method: I re-read all six round 1 statements, then did two things I had only demanded before. First, a null test on the sync claims using `scenes/shots.json` (51 cuts) and the section boundaries in `sync.json`. Second, I opened f_0024 and f_0102 (the latter I had not checked in round 1). Times are m:ss; f_NNNN shows second NNNN-1. As before, nobody here has heard the audio, so "feels" means a reasoned guess about a listener.

## 1. Answering the questions put to me

**Symbolist, Q1 (could the sync data show the editor deliberately withheld cuts at the big music events, and what would distinguish that from following footage?).** I ran the check. Distances from each big music event to the nearest detected cut: bass out 1:15.3, 1.86 s; bass in 1:40.0, 8.68 s; bass out 1:55, 0.68 s; the hole 2:47.83, 0.97 s; voice exit 0:31.7, 10.66 s; bass back 2:38.4, 0.16 s. The mean is 3.8 s, against about 2.9 s for the same number of random times (5,000 draws). So these events sit slightly further from cuts than chance, but with six events and a cut every 3.7 s that is not distinguishable from noise. The big gaps (1:40 and 0:31.7) are simply the two longest shots in the film (the 0:12 to 0:42 shot as the detector sees it, and the 1:28 to 1:49 stretch). "Withheld" would predict that cuts are rarer than chance at *all* the events, and that when the editor had a choice (several short shots available) he avoided the event. We have no such choice visible. My answer is: the data cannot distinguish withholding from following footage, and the simpler reading is the second. What would distinguish them: the source films (if the 1:28 to 1:49 material was one long take in the archive, the editor chose nothing).

**Film critic, Q3 (is a quarter-second cut lag audible as "following", and is the FINISH reveal exactly on the hole?).** On the second half, no: 1 fps cannot settle it, and I agree we should not say "perfectly timed". On the first half, I now think the lag itself is weaker than the dossier and your statement both make it. In `sync.json` the beat-phase test has n = 39 (not 51), p = 0.0055, but the *half-beat* phase test on the same cuts gives R = 0.036, p = 0.95, and the bar-phase test p = 0.56. A real habit of cutting a fixed distance after the beat would not usually vanish at the half-beat. A beat tracker with a small phase error or a wrong tempo octave (the music reading admits 78 or 156 BPM is open) would produce exactly this pattern. I would present "cuts lag the beat by about a quarter of a second" as a tracker-dependent finding, one test of several, and not as an editorial signature. Even taken at face value, 0.25 s is within the range of audio-visual offsets viewers do not notice.

**First-timer, Q3 (is "not on the beat" sophistication, or were the halves not designed together; how to tell?).** Honestly we cannot tell from this file. My shuffle test: of nine section boundaries, three have a cut within 0.5 s (1:06.4, 1:49.4, 2:38.7). Shuffling the shot durations 20,000 times, three or more boundaries land within 0.5 s of a cut in 39% of shuffles. So the three near-misses are exactly what chance gives. What would tell the difference: a film edited *to* the track would show a cut-to-event distance that shrinks when the cut is a free choice (fast-cut passages such as 1:06 to 1:13 and 1:49 to 1:54), and that is where the best evidence sits. The cut at 1:48.68 is 0.01 s from a beat, and the first of a five-cut flurry. One cut is an anecdote, but those two fast passages are the only places I would not rule intent out.

**Music critic, Q3 is aimed at the human-response panellist, but it contains a challenge to everyone: "what in the film is not in the music?"** My answer as the sceptic: the words and the faces. A viewer with no Dutch gets from the music alone drive, subtraction and a hard-edged release. From the picture alone they get cold, crowd, officialdom, and (for those who read the headlines) "zwaar". The music cannot supply "this is a real day in 1963", and the picture cannot supply the rise at 1:06 or the release at 2:48. I would say that is a more defensible account of the relationship than counterpoint.

## 2. Concessions

- **f_0101 is not empty.** In round 1 I described 1:40 as a near-white frame with "no figures I can see". I opened f_0102 (1:41) this time: two small dark skaters at left, one clearly mid-stride, ahead of a drift of snow and a dune-like ridge. The film critic and the dossier were right; I had not opened the frame where they appear. This does not change my view that the *shot* is the long, slow, empty one, but "no figure" was wrong and I withdraw it.
- **"Contrast not mimicry" is not entirely unfalsifiable.** I called it so. Fair correction from the film critic's structure: they predict specific features (cut density follows the large shape of the music; no cuts on arrivals). The cut-density prediction is testable and holds at its two extremes (7.58 cuts per 10 s at 1:06 to 1:16; 0.46 at 1:28 to 1:49). My objection should be narrowed: the *contrast at 1:40* is unfalsifiable as intent because any shot there would have been read as a choice.
- **My "cuts avoid the beat if anything" line was too strong.** 7.8% against 19.7% is for a window of 80 ms around the beat; the tracker's lateness may be doing that work. I should have said "nothing shows snapping", which is all the data support.

## 3. Where I still disagree

**The "prolepsis" reading (Symbolist, Film critic, First-timer, Insider all use it).** Four panellists build on "post-race headlines shown before the race". I opened f_0024 again: the page reads "Elfstedentocht: zwaar, zwaar, zwaar", "Tal van uitvallers en vele gewonden", "Cracks staken wedstrijd" and "R. Paping op kop" (the last in small print). That is plainly a post-race report. But the page is held up to the camera, tilted, in a film that opens on a pre-race crowd and "OP VRIJDAG" (0:04). The plain explanation is that newsprint was a common way for 1963 newsreels and club films to introduce the event: print is the title card. Nobody has shown that the source films were made before the race, or that the editor put the pages in order to spoil it. Four panellists with the same SPEC does not make it OBS. I would say: "the film begins with newspapers that report the outcome" (OBS), and stop there. The Symbolist's "everyone on screen is already a memory" is a lovely sentence, but it depends on a viewer who reads the small print in a half-second, in Dutch, over a loud band. The first-timer, who read very little of it, did not use it, which is itself evidence.

**"The emotional centre is the curly-haired gaze (1:58 to 2:00)."** Film critic, Insider and Symbolist all name this. I opened f_0121 in round 1: the person looks at the lens, expression neutral, overexposed. A viewer in 1963 who happened to be near the camera looked at it; in a 2013 edit this is a one-in-a-hundred glance picked from the archive. I accept that a held shot of faces, cups and breath under the thinnest music is the film's quietest passage (measured, not just felt). I do not accept that the *gaze* is the reason it moves people. The First-timer, the only panellist writing as a naive viewer, was not asked this, and the Symbolist asks it. I would like that answer before we build more on it.

**"The hole into FINISH is the film's best-timed gesture" (Film critic, Insider, Music critic).** The music event is real, measured and 0.38 s long. The picture side is one frame of a hand-painted banner whose entrance lies somewhere in 2:47.5 to 2:48.5. "Meets" is too strong; "falls within a second of" is the evidence. The film critic's own Q3 goes the same way, so I take this as agreed.

**The Insider's "the unseen finish is a kind of truth."** It is a moving sentence, but the dossier says only that we saw no finish in a three-minute Part 1 of a two-part release. The finish may be in Part 2. This is the one place where the whole panel is treating the absence of something in a 3:09 excerpt as a deliberate absence in the work. Please do not.

## 4. Developing the most interesting thread

The most interesting question round 1 raised is the one the Film critic and the Symbolist both circled: **who made the order?** All the "clever" choices on the picture side (newspaper opening, camera-looking-back, the signs following the spoken towns, even the village at 1:27 changing with the arrangement) could belong to either the 1963 sources or the 2013 edit. The panel can separate them partly from our own data:

1. **Signs following the spoken towns (2:16 to 2:36).** If the narration was recorded in 1963 over a silent film, the sign for each town would be matched by the original editor. If the 2013 edit rearranged shots and kept a voice from elsewhere, the match would be unlikely to be this tight. The tight match (within about 1 s per sign) therefore suggests the narration and *this picture sequence* were a unit in the source (OBS about timing, SPEC about origin). That would mean the film's most "interpretive-looking" gesture is the one the editor least authored.
2. **The same logic applied to the refrain.** The sentence appears at 0:27 and 2:44; the waveform correlation is 0.09 (the dossier says inconclusive). If the same take had been looped, I would expect a much higher correlation unless the music bed differs (it does, hence the weak result). A cleaner test would use the vocal stem alone. Insider, you know the archive: does either occurrence of this sentence sound like a newsreel's *opening* line and a *closing* line? If so, the "refrain" may be two parts of one source narration (intro and sign-off) rather than a PSB device.
3. **What we can credit PSB with.** The subtraction scheme in the music (measured, 1:15, 1:40, 1:55, 2:38, the thumps) and the *distribution* of speech across it. These are decisions about sound that the picture could not have made. I would therefore ask the panel to credit the editing with the cut-density shape and the sound design with the feeling, and credit neither with the meanings that the Symbolist and Film critic derive from the archive's own habits.

## 5. Which feelings survive the sceptic's test

You were asked (my round 1 Q3) to say which feelings survive phone speakers and no Dutch. Only the Music critic and I touched this. The ones I think survive: the bass-in/out contrasts on a decent speaker; the release at 2:48 (width and brightness are not low-frequency); the frozen, tired faces at 2:01 to 2:14 (picture alone); the "lots of people, very cold" register of the whole. The ones that do not: the sub thumps (42.6 Hz); the town-list "gentle municipal comedy" (needs Dutch, or at least knowing the signs are towns); the post-race headlines (needs Dutch); the "FINISH" word as destination (readable in English, so this survives).

## 6. Two new questions

1. **To the Insider:** the town list (2:16 to 2:36) follows the *route's* order only partly, as far as the dossier can establish: IJlst, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward, Harlingen, Franeker, Dokkum, Leeuwarden. If that order is the real route order, the narration is a documentary account; if it is rearranged, someone chose it for sound or rhythm. Is this the real order of the eleven towns, and are Sneek and Sloten really absent from the shown signs? Also: would a Frisian viewer hear the two occurrences of "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt" as a newsreel's opening and sign-off, as I suspect, or as a deliberate refrain?
2. **To the Music critic:** you say the arrangement "does independent work" and the picture "occasionally rhymes". Given that the beat-phase result disappears at half-beat (p = 0.95) and that the music reading itself leaves tempo octave open, which of the *music's* own timing claims would you drop if the tracker's phase were off by a quarter beat? In particular, are the "grid-aligned" claims for the hole at 2:47.83 and the voice exit at 0:31.7 (bar-line) independent of the beat tracker, or do they inherit its errors?

## 7. Revised one-line verdict

A restrained montage whose best-evidenced events are all on the sound side (bass out and in, the thin block, the hole, the sprint plateau); the picture's best-evidenced contribution is its cut density and the town-sign sequence, which probably follows the voice because the source did. The deeper meanings (prolepsis, memory, the gaze, the unseen finish) are readings, and I would label them INT in the final document, with the first two marked as dependent on Dutch literacy.
