## 2. A concession I owe the Sceptic, and the one I do not

The Sceptic's round 2 insists the 0.46 cuts per 10 s at 1:28 to 1:49 "holds at its extreme". It does not: in round 2 I showed shot 20 (1:27.16 to 1:48.68) contains at least six different images by f_0093 to f_0108. The Sceptic and I agree on the *concession* (cut-density claims are weakest in white-on-white snow) and still need an independent cut list. I re-state the practical consequence as plainly as I can: **until the cut list is rebuilt, treat every cut-based number in the dossier as provisional, in snow passages above all.** That covers the density shape (7.58 / 5.43 / 0.46), my own null test (3 of 9 boundaries within 0.5 s, which is chance), and the Rayleigh phase result. I accept the Sceptic's separate point (round 2, section 1) that the beat-phase result vanishing at the half-beat (R = 0.036, p = 0.95) makes "cuts lag the beat by a quarter of a second" a tracker-dependent finding. I drop "lag" as a property of the edit. My "cut on the consequence of the action" explanation therefore also drops: it explained a thing we cannot show exists.

Where I still disagree with the Sceptic: "credit the editing with cut-density shape" is itself unsafe now (the shape is what the detector gave us). What I can credit the editing with, from frames, is *ordering by scale and by room*, section 3 below.

## 3. The thread I develop: the film is built from rooms and signs, not from a race

Round 2 left two things standing from the picture side: the 1:30 to 1:47 zoom-out and zoom-in (small figures in pale fields, then the aerial town, then faces at a bridge), and the Symbolist's observation that names appear only as writing. Putting them with the Insider's warning (an absence in one half of a two-part work is not a thesis) and the Sceptic's "who made the order?", I now think the one structural claim the picture supports is this:

**The film's units are places and documents, and the skater is almost always the connective tissue between them.** Walk the frames. Printed matter (0:04, 0:09, 0:22, 2:57), a lit shop sign (0:07), a registration table with stamping hands (1:18 to 1:26), a tea table and a bench (1:54 to 2:00), cups and a bottle (2:05 to 2:12), a control hut (2:15), eight town signs and an inn (2:18 to 2:37), a hand-lettered FINISH (2:48), a hand-lettered "Reportage ELFSTEDENTOCHT" banner (3:07). Against these, there are scrums, lines and packs of skaters: always anonymous, always from behind or at a distance (bibs 170, 245, 460, 255, 342, 57). The two close faces that *are* held (the frost-caked man at 2:01; the profile at 2:14) are spectators or helpers or racers at rest, not racers in motion.

Why this matters for feeling, INT: it gives the picture its own kind of emotion that does not need the music's. A viewer gets the effort as a *civic* event, made of paperwork, tea and signs, with the athletic act reduced to a hands-behind-back silhouette. The music supplies drive (bass on and off, the wall, the hole); the picture supplies the world the drive is for. That is a division of labour I can defend from frames, and it matches the Insider's and the Symbolist's finding that "administration" fills the film. It also answers the First-timer's "half satisfied, half cheated": nothing in a film built from signs and rooms can deliver a finish line, because a sign is what it has. The FINISH banner is the film's own grammar (a hand-lettered word, held up) reaching its logical end.

Caveat I owe: I cannot say that PSB chose this. Insider and Sceptic are right that the archive is regional and that cameras of the day sat at control points, so the shape is partly inherited. What can be said is that whoever assembled it kept it. A 3-minute montage of 1963 footage that *could* have been all skating chose, or was given, rooms, signs and print for about half its running time.

## 4. Where I agree and where I still push back

- **Agree, Sceptic on prolepsis.** Opening a film on newsprint is an ordinary title-card device. I hold "print as record" as a motif and drop "told in advance" for good. The page at 0:22 and 2:57 is the same front page (I opened f_0024 in round 2 and the dossier opened f_0178), so the film uses one page twice. A repeated item is an editorial fact; why is not.
- **Agree, Music critic, "fitted, not co-composed".** I came into the panel believing in a close picture-to-music design. The measured picture-music evidence is: nothing statistically solid, bar the narration, which the picture follows (signs within about a second of each town name). The music critic's prediction (the 0:31.7 exit and the 2:47.8 hole are the fitted points) is consistent with what I see: at 0:31.7 the picture changes hard to a flat grey dawn on the same bar line, and at 2:47.8 a whip-pan ends on the banner. Both follow a narrator's sentence ending, so I cannot separate narration from band.
- **Agree, First-timer, Insider, Symbolist: 1:58 to 2:00 is the film's warmest passage.** I keep the eye-line as the *only* direct look to camera, and drop any claim that it carries feeling by itself (the Sceptic's point). What carries it is the bench: people sitting down indoors, drinking tea, in the thinnest music.
- **Push back, Insider on "an absence in one half of a two-part work".** Fair for a *thesis*, but this is an observation about this piece as it stands: Part 1 contains no arrival, whoever decided that. I keep it as a description and as the First-timer's viewing experience.
- **Push back, Symbolist on names as writing, never faces.** I largely agree, with one check: the frost-caked man at 2:01 and the profile at 2:14 are unnamed and so are all faces. That supports it. But the one place a name is in print and a person is on the page, "R. Paping op kop", is tiny print on a tilted page: the film does not draw attention to it. So the film names nobody. I would not read that as erasure; it is consistent with a collective subject.
- **Push back, Symbolist on the Frisian bilingual signs as statement.** I have no evidence either way and the Insider is the right person. In the frames, BOLSWARD / BOALSERT and FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER are filmed close and full-frame, but a tight crop on a sign is also just how you read a sign on a small film frame. I would call this "visible to a Frisian eye", not "emphasised by the edit".

## 6. Settled reading, in five sentences

1. This is a band track with a calm flat narrator laid over regional 1963 archive, and the evidence I trust says the two were fitted at the narration (the town signs, the refrain) and not locked to each other by the cutting. 2. What the picture adds that the band cannot is a world: print, paperwork, tea, signs and spectators in about half the running time, with the skaters as anonymous, bent, hands-behind-the-back bodies moving between them. 3. The feeling of the piece is therefore double: the band pushes (bass off at 1:15, back at 1:40, the pull-down, the thumps, the hole) while the picture shows ordinary civic effort, so the effort feels collective, cold and unglamorous, not heroic. 4. Meaning claims such as prolepsis, memory and the gaze are readings that I mark INT, and I credit no intention to PSB where the archive's habits can explain the shot. 5. The film never shows anyone arrive, and I now think that is the shape of it: it ends on a repeated sign, a banner that advertises a report, and black.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 2:47 to 2:48.** A whip-pan smears a packed crowd, the narrator's sentence "waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" finishes, the band drops out for about 0.4 s, and a hand-painted sheet reading FINISH is held up in front of the crowd. It carries all the film's features at once: a word where an event should be, a crowd where a skater should be, a gesture of the hands, a sound-side event the picture cannot be proved to match (the banner entrance lies somewhere in 2:47.5 to 2:48.5, 1 fps cannot say more), and then the plateau of the sprint without anyone reaching anything.
