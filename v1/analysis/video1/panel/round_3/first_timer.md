The Music critic asked me to check the six thumps (2:01.1, 2:07.2, 2:13.4, 2:19.5, 2:25.7, 2:31.9; these match the times in `music_reading.md`, so his extrapolation holds) against the cutting. What the sheets show (OBS):

So, on my reading of the sheets, a new image appears in the second of every one of the six thumps, which is better than the dossier's "two of six within 0.03 s". But I must not oversell it. Cuts in this stretch are frequent: I count about 23 changes of image in 2:00 to 2:34, roughly two in every three seconds, so a cut within a second of any chosen moment is likely by chance. This is exactly the Sceptic's shuffle-test territory, and I cannot run it. My honest claim is: "consistent with the edit leaning on the thumps, not shown to." I also did not see a head turn, a fall or a sign that looks saved for a thump. Two of the arrivals do look like events, though: the helicopter (2:26) and the big FRANEKER sign (2:32) are the most "announcing" images in the stretch, and they sit on thumps 5 and 6. That is a viewer's impression (INT, L), not a measurement. The Film critic's reading (cut on the consequence, a few frames late) is equally compatible.

One small thing the sheets give that nobody wrote down: the first three thumps (2:01, 2:07, 2:13) land on images of people at the tea tables, and the last three (2:19, 2:25, 2:32) on the world outside (shore, helicopter, signs). So the picture changes subject exactly where the music "re-ignites" (the wall thickens at 2:13, the town list starts 2:16). That is a clean division, but again it is how a montage would normally be arranged; I only note it.

## 2. Where I agree, concede and push back

- **Concede to the Music critic and the Film critic on 1:40.** I had called it "a white-out", like most of us. The Film critic and Music critic both opened it: it is a pale, legible dune landscape with skaters in the frames either side, and the "slowest cutting" claim was a detector artefact. I withdraw both. My round 2 line, "to a viewer it looks like an empty gap", also needs shrinking to "a quiet, bright, wide shot".
- **Concede to the Sceptic** that the individual-face and "survivor" readings come from us. I already dropped "stunned"; I now also drop "the emotional centre is the tea table glance". What stays is the plain fact that this is the only place where people sit down and drink, and that I can feel it without any help.
- **Agree with the Music critic** that "seismic" was rhetoric for the 0.38 s hole at 2:47.8, and with the Sceptic that "falls within a second of the FINISH banner" is all the evidence supports.
- **Push back on the Sceptic's "the unseen finish is not a deliberate absence".** He is right that this is a 3:09 Part 1 and that the finish may be in Part 2. But my claim was always about what a viewer experiences, not what the maker intended. A viewer of this file sees a track that says "arrive" and pictures that never do. I will not call that design.
- **Push back, gently, on the Symbolist's "the picture adds what the music withholds".** I agree with the sentence as a description of my own experience (faces, a place to rest). I do not agree it is a clever counterpoint: the Sceptic's shuffle test (three of nine boundaries within 0.5 s of a cut, 39% by chance) says we cannot tell design from coincidence, and the 7.8% of cuts on a beat against 19.7% by chance says cuts are not snapped to the pulse.
- **Answer to the Symbolist's pattern question** (individuals appear where the music thins): from my sheets it fits the tea stretch (2:00 to 2:14 is sparse music and the most identifiable individuals) but the stretch also has the most close-ups simply because the film is at the tea tables. I cannot separate cause from subject. I would not keep it as a finding without the Sceptic's null test.
- **Insider and the roll-call.** I accept that Frisian viewers will take the town list as a litany and the bilingual signs as ordinary. For me the names and signs are what I would show a friend first, and I accept my "smile" is mine: no face smiles in the frames I opened (2:00 to 2:39 sheets), so the lightness lives in the signs, the inn front with its beer sign, and the quickening cuts.

## 3. The thread I develop: what does a viewer get from each half, and does it matter that they were not tightly married?

Last round I asked whether the picture alone is "warm" and the band alone "a good emotionally neutral drive". Nobody answered directly, but the Sceptic and Music critic between them did: the music gives drive, subtraction and a hard-edged release; the picture gives the cold, crowds, officials and the faces. I think that is fair. The thump check adds a nuance: even if the cuts do not lock to the pulse overall, a loose fit is enough to make a viewer feel "this was assembled with the sound in mind". The panel has spent three rounds trying to prove or disprove that feeling and the data say "cannot tell". What I can report as a viewer is that the unsynced-yet-congruent quality is part of the pleasure: nothing fights, nothing is rigidly on the beat, and it feels like a weight being pulled steadily across a flat landscape. That is a feeling, not a finding.

## 4. Settled reading (five sentences)

1. This is a three-minute, loud, relentless instrumental drive carrying black-and-white archive of a brutally cold 1963 race, with a flat Dutch voice giving only facts, so the band supplies the urgency and the film supplies the human faces. 2. What I can stand behind is cold, effort, crowds and small acts of ordinary care (the tea tables, the signs), not sadness, victory or ghosts, which are ours rather than the film's. 3. The music's biggest events (the bass returning at 1:40, the half-second hole at 2:47.8) are real, but I cannot tell whether the picture was designed around them, and at 1:40 the picture is bright and quiet rather than blank. 4. Nobody crosses a finish line; the track says "arrive" and the pictures keep going, which I experience as endurance rather than triumph, though Part 2 may change that. 5. The middle, with its steady pulse and similar shots, can sag for a listener who does not lock in, but the tea tables and the roll-call of town signs are where I would hook a friend.

**The single moment that best captures the piece:** 2:01 to 2:14, the tea tables: the music at its thinnest, the six slow thumps starting, and a row of cold, ordinary people sipping from white cups, one looking at us, before the signs begin and the film moves again.
