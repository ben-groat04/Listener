# The Sceptic: Round 3 (final), video1, PSB "Elfstedentocht Part 1", 3:09

Method: I read all six round 2 statements. Then I checked what I could. I computed frame-to-frame differences for f_0088 to f_0111 (the disputed 1:27 to 1:49 stretch), opened f_0097 (1:36), f_0174 (2:53) and f_0180 (2:59), re-read `sync.json` and `music_reading.md`, and ran a crude null test on the Symbolist's "individual appears where the music thins" pattern. Times are m:ss; f_NNNN shows second NNNN-1. Nobody here has heard the audio, so "feels" means a reasoned guess about a listener.

## 1. The big correction of the round: the Film critic is right, and it damages my own tests

The Film critic said the detector's single "shot" from 1:27.16 to 1:48.68 is really several shots. I checked with mean absolute grey differences on 160x90 downscales (0 to 255 scale):

- f_0091 to f_0094 (1:30 to 1:33): 2.6, 4.8, 2.3. That is a held shot (the far skaters on white).
- f_0094 to f_0100 (1:33 to 1:39): 22, 22, 30, 42, 42, 51, 25. That is change on nearly every frame.
- f_0102 to f_0103: 83. f_0105 to f_0110: 42, 72, 43, 42, 91, 93.

Large differences at 1 fps are not by themselves proof of a cut (a fast pan would do it), but I opened f_0097 and it is plainly a different image from the reeds and the dune (three skaters in a full racing crouch against a reed bank), and the Film critic's list of six distinct images matches. So the detector missed several cuts in pale material, and my round 2 claim that the 1:28 to 1:49 stretch was "simply the longest shot in the film" is wrong. I withdraw it, along with the 8.68 s "distance from bass-in to nearest cut" I computed, which was an artefact of the undercount. I also said in round 1 that "slow cutting under the bass return" was the film's plainest fact. It is not a fact. It is a detector failure.

Consequences, which I want in the final document:

1. **Every cut-based number in `sync.json` is suspect in snow.** That includes the 0.46 cuts per 10 s, the cut-rate versus loudness r = 0.27, the cut phase Rayleigh test and my own shuffle tests (3 of 9 boundaries within 0.5 s, 39% by chance). More cuts raise the chance rate, so the null gets harder to beat, not easier. The honest status of "cut density follows loudness" is **untested**, not "weak but real". The Film critic's request to re-derive cut times is correct and is the first thing I would do if more work were allowed.
2. **The 1:40 contrast story loses its strongest number but not its frame.** What survives is the Symbolist's and Music critic's reduced version: a wide, pale, small-scale passage of picture (f_0093 five dots, f_0096 and f_0102 two skaters, f_0097 three) while the bass returns; no cut is known at 1:40.0; and the arrival is an audio event whose size does not depend on the picture (Music critic, which I accept). "Counterpoint", "stillness" and "erasure" are all gone. I also retire "no figure" (I conceded f_0102 last round) and agree with the Music critic that "white-out" should leave our shared vocabulary.

## 2. Answers to the questions put to me

**Symbolist: is there a null test for "the individual appears only where the music thins or the voice is counting"?** I did a crude one, and the result is awkward for both of us. From the dossier timeline I listed 19 shots where a single person is in focus and prominent (faces at 0:15, 0:27, 0:29, 0:31; bib 170 and the striped jumper at 1:05 to 1:06; bib 157 at 1:21; the fur-collared spectator at 1:49; the tea-bench gaze at 2:00; the frosted man at 2:01 and 2:03; the kneeler at 2:08; 2:13, 2:14, 2:17; the woman at 2:44; 2:53, 3:00, 3:02). Thin or voiced spans (voice 0:10 to 0:32, 1:19 to 1:27, 2:16 to 2:36, 2:44 to 2:48, plus the bass-less 1:15 to 1:19 and the thin block 1:55 to 2:15) cover about 78 of 188 s, 41% of running time. Fourteen of the 19 shots fall inside them (74%). A binomial test gives p of about 0.004. So it beats chance on its face.

I do not think it should be trusted, for three reasons. (a) I picked the shots and the span boundaries after reading the pattern, and I may have tilted the list. (b) The shots are not independent: the 14 sit in about four clusters (the opening faces, the table, the tea sequence, the town hut). Treated as four events, the result is nothing special. (c) The most important confound is mundane. The tea and frost-faces sequence is the only place the archive gives us people sitting and close up, and it is also where the arrangement thins. One footage cluster meeting one arrangement block is a single coincidence. So: the pattern is **observed (OBS)** and it passes a crude test, but it is not evidence of design. If the Symbolist keeps it, keep it as "where the picture offers individuals, the sound happens to be at its thinnest", a description. It also fails in the loud sections: 1:05 to 1:06, 2:53, 3:00 and 3:02 are individuals under full sound. I would not say "only".

**Film critic, via First-Timer: are the bent-over skaters at 2:53 and 2:59 resting or racing, and is the sprint an anti-climax in the picture?** I opened both.
- f_0174 (2:53): a skater in a striped jumper and hooded cap, mid-turn or mid-crouch, one hand reaching towards the ice, other arm trailing. This is a lean, not a rest. It could be a push-off, a fall, a stumble or a stoop to fix something; I cannot say.
- f_0180 (2:59): a blurred figure bent right over in the foreground, hands low, with other people standing or walking on the ice behind and a house and trees beyond. The people behind look like pedestrians or walking skaters, not a pack at speed.
My reading, down to earth: the sprint's pictures are scenes from the field of a mass event, not the front of a race. I accept the First-Timer's point that nothing in 2:48 to 3:08 looks like a finish, and that the pictures do not "sprint". But whether that is anti-climax by design is not decidable, for the Insider's reason: archive coverage of this day does not usually include the finish, and Part 2 exists.

**Music critic, to me: which of the music's timing claims are independent of the tracker?** I read `music_reading.md` to answer rather than guess.
- *Independent (measured on the stems against the drum's own grid):* the six thumps all fall at slot 21 mod 32, within 15 ms, with 6.15 s spacing; the hole is 167.8 to 168.2 s in the drum stem (absolute times); the bass events at 1:15.3, 1:40.0, 1:55 and 2:38.4 are measured on the bass stem. These do not depend on where beat one is or on the 78 or 156 BPM octave.
- *Inherited from the tracker:* "24, 28, 32 bars" (bar one is tracker-derived, M), "0:31.7 is on a bar line", and "the hole is a catch inside the bar". The music reading itself says the hole is "grid-aligned and not clearly bar-aligned" and that beat one cannot be confirmed from the kick band.
- *Mine to concede:* the picture-side beat-phase result (cuts about 0.32 of a beat late) comes from the *film* tracker's beat times and an undercounted cut list. The half-beat Rayleigh p = 0.95 I raised last round is not by itself a refutation (a broad cluster at one phase need not show at the second harmonic), so I over-argued that. The proper conclusion is smaller: the lag result is a single, post hoc, tracker-dependent test on a list I now know is incomplete. It should be reported as "a hint, unconfirmed".
Net: the music's *grid* is robust (the drum slot spacing is exact), the music's *bar* labelling is not, and no picture-to-beat claim survives.

**Insider: is the town-sign order a design signal?** The film's order (IJlst 2:18, Stavoren 2:24, Workum 2:27, Bolsward 2:29, Harlingen word cue at 2:30.7, Franeker 2:32, Dokkum 2:34, Leeuwarden) is the route order in `elfstedentocht_1963.md` (Leeuwarden, Sneek, IJlst, Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward, Harlingen, Franeker, Dokkum, Leeuwarden). I do not read that as an editorial signal. A narrator reciting eleven towns recites them in route order, and the editor then pulls a sign for each. The informative feature is the gaps: the dossier has no sign for Sneek, Sloten or Hindeloopen, and the 2:30 townscape is unnamed. If the editor were free to choose, I would expect each name to be illustrated. Missing signs suggest the picture is constrained by what the archive holds. So what changes my mind either way: a shot in non-route order, or a named town shown with no word, would indicate the picture leads. We have neither. The town list is picture-follows-voice, and I credit that to the source or to ordinary craft, in agreement with the Insider's own caution.

**Film critic's and Music critic's shared challenge, "credit the editing or the sound?"** I stay where round 2 left me, narrowed by section 1: credit the arrangement (stem switches, the hole, the thumps) with the feeling; credit the picture with scale and faces; credit nobody with the rest.

**First-Timer, Q2: is it fair that the picture carries the warmth and the band is "good but emotionally neutral"?** Mostly yes, with one correction I owe the Music critic. The band is not neutral. It is stern and withholding, third-less and unsmiling, and that colours the pictures: the same tea scene under a bright major-key bed would be cosier. So the band supplies *register* (hard, steady, unsentimental) and the picture supplies *content* (faces, cups, cold). The band alone, I would defend only as drive and relief: the bass-in at 1:40 after 24 s and the release at 2:48. I would not call it "moving" without images, and the thumps do not count on a phone.

## 3. Concessions, in one place

- The 1:28 to 1:49 stretch is not one long shot; the detector missed cuts. My "longest shot" argument and my distance-to-nearest-cut figures are withdrawn.
- My 5,000-draw shuffle tests (3 of 9 boundaries, withheld-cut distances) used the undercounted list, so they are unreliable in snow, not just "chance-level".
- "Cuts avoid the beat" is gone (already conceded); the half-beat objection to the lag result was weaker than I made it.
- I over-weighted Dutch literacy: the First-Timer says the signs are readable by anyone, and the Symbolist points out that the word FINISH is legible. Both survive without Dutch.
- On the 2:00 gaze: I still say the look is neutral, but the Symbolist's pixel-level point (the overexposed face and the white cups are the same tone as the snow) is observation, not decoration. I will not call it empty.

## 4. Where I still hold the line

1. **Prolepsis, unseen finish, litany, memory.** None has been shown to be the film's. The Insider has now downgraded prolepsis and the unseen finish, and the Symbolist, Film critic and First-Timer have agreed in effect. The "litany" reading is attractive and fits flat voice, fixed drone and the town list, but I cannot find a litany mark in the *sound* (the Music critic can answer the Insider on that). It is a fair INT for the 2:16 to 2:36 passage only. Do not extend it to the whole film.
2. **"The hole lands on FINISH."** Falls within a second of it; no more. Agreed by the Film critic, Music critic and Symbolist.
3. **The "emotional centre" of the picture track.** The Insider's weaker claim is the one I accept: from 1:55 to 2:14 the *subject* changes from racers to people keeping them going (kettle, cups, frost-caked man, ambulance, kneeler). That is visible. The gaze is not the reason.
4. **The Insider's 1997 absence.** It has a real external cause and I rate it the most credible of the "beyond the frame" layers, but it is a viewer's context, not a property of the film. Tag M and keep it out of the description.

## 5. The thread I would develop: how little of this is "composed"

The panel started with a story of counterpoint between two artists. After three rounds, the supported picture is plainer and, to me, more interesting. The *sound* is made on an exact grid by someone who knew what subtraction does: thumps within 15 ms of a 32-slot cycle, the bass out and in at 24, 28 and 32 bar spacings, a voice that enters into cleared space and exits at state changes. The *picture* is a sequence of archive scenes, ordered by the narration where it can be (towns, date) and otherwise unmeasured. They meet in three places that I think are real: the narrator's words (date, towns, refrain), the broad rise in activity around 1:06 to 1:16 and 1:49 to 1:54 (the one place where cut-to-music proximity could be deliberate, now needing a recount), and the fact that both halves are cold, repetitive and unsentimental. That makes the film not a counterpoint but a *joint*: two independent constructions with a flat voice at the seam. What a viewer feels at the seam depends on a lot we cannot test (speaker, language, knowledge), and I would rather the final document say so than invent a cause.

## 6. Settled reading (five sentences)

1. This is a restrained, loud, hypnotic montage in which the best-evidenced events are all on the sound side: the bass out at 1:15 and in at 1:40, the thin block with its thumps from 2:01, the half-second hole at 2:47.83 and the bright plateau from 2:48.
2. The picture's best-evidenced contributions are scale (small figures in pale fields), faces and cups in the 1:55 to 2:14 passage, and a town-sign sequence that follows the narrator within about a second per sign, and the sync numbers built on the shot detector, including the cut-rate and beat-lag figures, cannot be trusted until the cut list is rebuilt for snowy passages.
3. The warmth comes from the pictures and the sternness from the band, and no timing alignment between them has survived a null test, so we should call it an assembly of two well-made layers joined by a flat narrator rather than a designed counterpoint.
4. Deeper meanings (outcome told first, the unseen finish as refusal, memory, the 1997 absence, the litany) are readings, tagged INT, and several depend on Dutch or on history that the film does not itself supply.
5. What a first-time viewer can safely take away is cold, effort, crowds, paperwork and tea, driven by a relentless pulse, with an ending that gives us a banner saying FINISH and then a banner saying "Reportage" and then black.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 2:47 to 2:49.** A whip-pan smears a crowd, the narrator finishes "dat is de Elfstedentocht", the drums drop out for 0.38 s, and the next frame is a hand-painted sheet reading FINISH held up by hooded people, after which the music goes on at full strength and the film shows no one finishing. Whether the banner lands inside the hole or just after cannot be told at 1 fps, and I do not need it to: what is measured (the hole), what is seen (the sign) and what is absent (the finish) are all there, and nothing needs inventing.
