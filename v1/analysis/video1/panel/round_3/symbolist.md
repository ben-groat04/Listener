# The Symbolist (panel, round 3, final): video1, PSB "Elfstedentocht Part 1"

Basis: the round 2 statements of all six panellists, the dossier, and `music_reading.md` as the audio authority. Frames re-opened this round: f_0093 (1:32), f_0169 (2:48). Earlier rounds: f_0024, f_0102, f_0121, f_0163, f_0170. OBS = seen or measured, INT = interpretation, SPEC = speculation. Times m:ss. I cannot hear the audio.

## 1. Answers to questions put to me

**First-Timer: does the 1:58 glance read as the emotional centre, or do the loud peaks overwhelm it?** Neither, quite. The Sceptic and First-Timer are right that the glance is not *the* centre, and I have already withdrawn "survivor" and "stunned". What the First-Timer says instead is better than my claim: the tea scene is the one place in three minutes where people sit indoors and do something small, and it is legible without help. I accept that as the reason it lands. The glance is a bonus, one face among five; the centre is the *scene* (hands, cups, steam-less ordinariness) set under the thinnest music (about -13.5 LUFS at 2:05, width 0.2 to 0.3, per the music reading). The loud peaks are things we read about; the tea is something we see.

**First-Timer: does the sprint feel like arrival or treading water?** I looked again at the sprint frames rather than rely on my memory of round 1. f_0169 (2:48) is the FINISH banner: hand-lettered, held across a packed crowd, hardly any of whom look at the banner or at us (OBS: most heads are turned or tilted; one or two near the centre-left look toward the lens). After it, per the First-Timer's reading of the sheets, come backs, bent figures, the newspaper again at 2:57 and a town sign. I agree that no one is shown crossing a line. The music says "arrive" (the loudest, steadiest, brightest stretch, -7.0 to -6.4 LUFS, per the music reading) and the picture says "the day continues, and here is the word for where it ends". I will go further than "treading water". The sprint is the one place where the two layers disagree most plainly, and the disagreement is not a fault: **the sound completes the gesture the picture only points at.** A sign saying FINISH is a promise in print, not a thing that happens.

**Sceptic (Q1 and null tests): is my "individual appears where the music thins or the voice counts" pattern real?** I cannot run the shuffle test from my chair and I accepted the challenge in round 2 by saying I would drop it if it fails. Re-reading the Sceptic's own framing, the null is easy to fail: thin or voiced spans cover a large share of the 3:09 (voice is on for roughly a third of it by the music reading's own map, and the thin block alone is 2:00 to 2:38). Three examples out of a handful do not beat that. **I withdraw the pattern as a finding.** I keep only the single observation that stands alone: the one direct look to the lens (f_0121) occurs in the quietest mix.

**Film critic: "weight on emptiness" is wrong, and the cut rate at 1:28 to 1:49 is 1 to 3 s, not 0.46 per 10 s.** Conceded in full. The detector missed low-contrast cuts in snow; the dossier's "slowest cutting in the film" is unsafe for this span. This damages one of my round 2 evidentiary points (I used 0.46 cuts per 10 s as OBS). I withdraw it. What survives is smaller: at 1:32 the picture is five dark figures on a grey-white field (f_0093, OBS: they sit in a thin band at mid-height with the rest of the frame empty), and at 1:41 two figures at the left margin. *Smallness in a large pale field*, as the Film critic and Music critic both phrase it. I adopt their wording and drop "erasure".

**Insider (and Sceptic): was the unseen finish the archive's, and is Part 1 only a half?** I concede. Part 2 exists; the footage is regional archive; cameras stood at fixed points. My "finish withheld" is a reading of what a viewer meets, not of what a maker refused. I will phrase it as "no finish is shown", and keep "arrival is pointed at, not seen" as the feeling.

## 2. Where I agree

- **Sceptic's credit-split**: sound design gets the feeling (the bass subtractions at 1:15, 1:40, 1:55, 2:38, the thumps, the hole), the picture gets cut-density and scale, and the meanings (print, headlines, the signs following the spoken towns) may belong to the 1963 sources. I accept this as the honest ledger. The tightness of sign to spoken town (within about 1 s, per the Sceptic) suggests the source made that match, so the roll-call is the film's most "poetic" device and the one least authored in 2013.
- **Insider**: the two undisputed word-picture alignments are naming ones, the date (0:09 to 0:12) and the towns (2:16 to 2:36). I think this is the best single observation of round 2, and I develop it in section 3.
- **Music critic**: the hole at 2:47.83 is "a neat, brief catch", not seismic; 0.38 s, the guitar holds. Agreed, and I will not borrow "seismic" for my own prose.
- **Film critic**: administration (counters, stamps, signs, cups, print) as the film's subject. I take it as the frame inside which my symbols sit.

## 3. Pushback

1. **To the Film critic ("the lag is craft habit, not a theme").** I accept it as the better-evidenced account and abandon "the image is always late as memory is". But I would note the Sceptic's own result: the beat-phase finding (n = 39, p = 0.0055) vanishes at half-beat (p = 0.95) and the bar phase (p = 0.56), so the quarter-second lag may be a tracker artefact anyway. I therefore have two reasons to drop the lag and no reason to keep it.
2. **To the Sceptic on "do not treat absence in a 3:09 excerpt as deliberate absence".** Agreed for the maker. I resist one thing: reading what a piece *shows* is not reading what its author *refused*. A viewer who watches three minutes of effort without arrival does experience an absence; the experience is real even if the cause is the archive. The distinction is intent versus effect, as in round 2, and I keep it.
3. **To the Sceptic on prolepsis.** I opened f_0024 in round 2 and the page is post-race (H). That much nobody disputes. I agree it cannot be called a device, and the Insider's "the event as the nation received it" is the likelier purpose. What remains is a plain fact worth stating once: **the film's first and last named things are printed words, and the only person named is a headline fragment.** That is OBS and needs no intent.

## 4. The thread I want to develop: the film is made of names, and the music has none

The Insider's point (the tight alignments are all naming) joins my own "names as writing" thread and gives it a structure.

- **The words**: a date (0:09 to 0:12), the refrain "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" (0:27 area and 2:44), a count (575 riders, about 9,500 tourists, 1:19 to 1:27), eleven towns (2:16 to 2:36).
- **The picture**: mastheads, bilingual signs (BOLSWARD / BOALSERT), a hand-lettered FINISH, a "Reportage" banner at the end (per the First-Timer's read of the sheets), and, between, many anonymous bodies.
- **The music**: no word, no valence (D pedal, third missing, per the music reading). It cannot name anything.

So the piece divides labour in a way a poet can state: **language says where, the band says how hard, and the faces are left unlabelled.** The people on the ice are the one thing in the film nobody names. The names are given to the *route*. The roll-call of towns is therefore the film's true list of participants: eleven places stand in for the thousands of bodies. That is a symbolic reading (INT), but it rests on three observations: the towns are the only things said aloud one by one, each gets a sign within about a second, and no skater gets the same treatment. I put it below the Insider's context reading (an absence of tours since 1997, if sourced as in cultural_context.md; I have not rechecked it) because that reading has a cause outside the frames, but I think the two combine: a region in a long wait recites its own towns.

On the sprint, the same logic gives the cleanest summary I can make: **the picture ends on a sign and the band ends on a chord.** The two layers each finish in their own medium, and neither crosses the line for the other.

## 5. What I am not claiming (final)

- No intent behind any alignment at 1:40, 1:55, 2:47.8 or in the cut lag. Effect only, tagged M.
- No "erasure", "survivor", "stunned", "ghost", "memory" as properties of the film; at most as my vocabulary for a feeling.
- No pattern linking individuals to thin music or voice (failed the null test in principle; withdrawn).
- No claim that "the effort continues offscreen" is what the film says; Part 2 follows.
- No claim about the sub thumps (42.6 Hz) as something a viewer hears on a phone.

## 6. Settled reading, in five sentences

1. This is a loud, stoic, third-less D groove whose drama is made by taking the bass, the wall and the voice away and bringing them back, laid over archive footage of a very cold day in which individuals are tiny and the things that carry names are signs, newspapers and a list of towns. 2. The film's feeling comes mainly from the sound, its scale from the picture, and its meanings from the 1963 material, and I credit the 2013 edit with selection and shape but not with every symbol the panel has found. 3. What it is "about", beneath the surface, is a community's effort rendered as administration and endurance (counters, stamps, cups, towns) rather than as heroes, with the one race everyone prepares for kept always just ahead. 4. The sprint gives the sound its arrival and gives the picture only a hand-lettered word, so the two layers end apart, and that gap, not any single image, is what a thoughtful viewer carries out. 5. Much of what I called memory, ghost and erasure was my own vocabulary, and what stays is smaller and firmer: smallness in a large pale field, warmth held in white hands, and names standing in for the people.

## 7. The single moment that best captures the piece

**2:48, the FINISH banner (f_0169), just after the 0.38 s drop-out of drums and bass.** It holds the whole argument at once: a word, not an event; hand-lettered and held up by a crowd rather than by an institution; a packed, anonymous mass behind it; the music about to turn bright, wide and loud as if the arrival had happened. Whether the banner lands inside the hole or just after it, 1 fps cannot say, and I do not need it to. It is a promise made in print, and the band keeps it for the picture.
