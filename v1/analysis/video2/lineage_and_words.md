# Elfstedentocht Part 2: lineage, cultural context and the words

Public Service Broadcasting (PSB), "Elfstedentocht Part 2" (video2). British spelling. Tags: **FACT** means a sourced statement, with the research file it comes from in square brackets (research files are in `v1/analysis/research/`; transcript files are in `v1/data/video2/lyrics/` and summarised in `v1/analysis/perceive/video2/words.md`). **INTERPRETATION** means a reading of the evidence, ours, not established. Nobody on this project has heard the audio with ears; sound claims come from measurement and transcripts. This note adds to `REPORT.md` and does not replace it.

## A. Lineage and cultural context

**What the piece is**
- FACT: PSB, a London group, formed in 2010 by J. Willgoose, Esq. [psb_method.md]
- FACT: it was commissioned by the Explore the North festival in Leeuwarden for the 50th anniversary of the 1963 race, using Fries Film Archief material. [reception.md]
- FACT: video2 is Part 2: official length 5:04, a colour film opening on a title board, and press calls Part 2 the calmer, more electronic half. [part_mapping.md, reception.md]
- FACT: the film source, the narrator and the maker of the footage are not identified. A club film and a regional sponsor film are both possible. [archive_sources.md]

**Post-rock**
- INTERPRETATION: weak. Post-rock usually builds with live-band dynamics and changing chords. This piece does neither: one key, a loop that does not move, and sections made by one layer being opened and closed. The only resemblance is "a slow build over a loop". [lineage.md, round 3, video2, resting on colleagues' measurements]

**Motorik, krautrock, dance**
- INTERPRETATION: not motorik. The late groove has a kick on every beat and sixteenth hi-hats, which is house, disco and synth-pop drum language rather than the straight Neu!/Can pulse. "Krautrock-descended electronica" is at most a family resemblance, and no source says PSB cited it here. [lineage.md, round 3, video2]
- INTERPRETATION: the form is built like a dance track: layers arrive and leave on a fixed grid, with a gap and then a drop (about 1:28). [lineage.md, round 3, video2; REPORT.md]
- FACT: automatic tags for this part were synth-pop and drum machine, which is a model label, not proof. [part_mapping.md]

**Documentary tradition**
- FACT: PSB set archive speech and film to new music, and the narration usually leads as the "voice". [psb_method.md]
- INTERPRETATION: this holds until about 2:36, when the last sentence ends; after about 3:25 the voice is replaced by short bar-locked fragments whose source is not known. So the track begins in PSB's usual way and then lets the machine take over. [lineage.md, round 3, video2; REPORT.md]
- INTERPRETATION: music that answers an old commemorative film with a steady, accumulating pulse can be read as standing for the length of the race. No band statement exists to confirm it. [lineage.md, round 3, video2; reception.md]
- FACT: Polygoon newsreel is not shown to be the source. [archive_sources.md]

**"De Hel van '63" and the ice**
- FACT: the race was the twelfth Elfstedentocht, on 18 January 1963, about 200 km on natural ice through eleven Frisian towns; it is remembered as "De Hel van '63". 9,862 started and 127 finished; Reinier Paping won. [elfstedentocht_1963.md]
- FACT: the last official tour was in 1997, and scientists call the race an icon of climate change. [cultural_context.md]
- INTERPRETATION: this is the layer a Dutch listener adds to the closing images of broken floes and open water. The pictures themselves show only ice that is no longer a floor; the loss is carried by what the viewer knows. [cultural_context.md, section 3; REPORT.md]

**Archive-voice nostalgia**
- FACT: hauntology is a real critical idea about music haunted by lost futures, and PSB are usually described as adjacent to it, not inside it. [cultural_context.md, low-confidence placement]
- INTERPRETATION: the method is archive-led, but the sound is not: it is clean and bright, with no tape or vinyl hiss, so the lineage applies to how it was made and not to how it sounds. Only the voice is band-limited. [lineage.md, round 3, video2]
- FACT: reception we found speaks of drive and physical effort, not grief. [reception.md] "Mourning" is our reading, and REPORT.md itself rates it weaker than "withdrawal".

## B. Two audiences

**What a Dutch-understanding listener gets**
- INTERPRETATION: the first human sound after about 40 seconds of pad is a date said like a title, which they hear as the start of the story of the hard winter. The sentences then carry meaning: the early hour, the distance, the number and nationalities of starters.
- INTERPRETATION: they also notice what is missing. The voice never names a winner, and after about 2:36 it speaks no more sentences, so the voice they understand is gone for the whole second half. What follows is music alone, with the weight of what they know.
- FACT: the same opening sentences also run through Part 1, so a Dutch listener hears Part 2 as the second telling of a story already begun. [words.md for both videos]

**What a listener without Dutch gets**
- INTERPRETATION: the voice is a dry, band-limited sound (as the measurements show) that appears, speaks in spaced blocks, and goes. Its meaning is mostly hidden; the digits and the repeated words "Elfstedentocht" and "Friesland" may stand out. The picture (a title board "De winter van 1963 in Friesland", colour ice and crowds) supplies much of what the words would.
- INTERPRETATION: the late fragments of voice-like sound are the same to both audiences, because they carry no words we can read; they are heard as rhythm. [REPORT.md]
- FACT: automatic transcription fails on this voice and in the second half invents words over music. Whisper produced "without rain" for "without weather" and unrelated words after about 3:20; those are not in the video. [words.md]

**Narration as a short reading list**

Translations are machine or inferred from Whisper output (the vocals-stem runs and the mix runs), then corrected by sense. I cannot hear the voice. Where the text is garbled I say so; nothing here should be quoted as heard.

| Time (approx.) | Dutch (best reconstruction) | English (machine/inferred) | How sure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:42 | "18 januari 1963." | "18 January 1963." | Sure. |
| 0:51 | "Een schaatsmarathon zonder weer(ga)." | "A skating marathon without ... (weather? or 'without equal')." | Words clear, ending ambiguous. |
| 0:59 | "Half zes in de ochtend, 200 kilometer door een Friesland waar tegen alleen de allersterkste ... opgewassen." | "Half past five in the morning, 200 km through a Friesland that only the very strongest could match." | "Half zes", "200 kilometer", "Friesland" fairly sure; the last verb is corrected by sense, and other passes misread the hour. |
| 1:20 | "[Steden] zijn [klaar?] voor de twaalfde Elfstedentocht." | "[The towns are ready?] for the twelfth Elfstedentocht." | Only "for the twelfth Elfstedentocht" is reasonably clear. |
| 1:24 | "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht." | "What everyone is preparing for, that is the Elfstedentocht." | Fairly sure; the last word is rebuilt from garbled passes. |
| 1:49 | "Temperatuur zestien graden beneden nul." | "Temperature 16 degrees below zero." | Fairly sure ("nul" appears only in an uncommitted run). This is what the film says; later sources give about -18. [cultural_context.md] |
| 1:52 | "Een monsterrit van ruim 200 kilometer." | "A monster ride of more than 200 kilometres." | Fairly sure ("monstertocht" is an alternative). |
| 2:26 to 2:36 | "575 wedstrijdrijders, rond 9500 toerrijders, een aantal Zweden, Canadezen, Zwitsers en Belgen." | "575 racing skaters, about 9,500 touring skaters, some Swedes, Canadians, Swiss and Belgians." | Numbers sure; "Canadezen" less so. |

- FACT: after about 2:36 nothing intelligible is spoken; the later "words" in the transcripts are artefacts. [words.md; REPORT.md]
- FACT: the narrator's 575 and about 9,500 fit the official split of 568 racers and 9,294 tour skaters; the voice's figures are rounder. [elfstedentocht_1963.md]
- INTERPRETATION on delivery: it reads as a dry, formal commentary, spaced around the music. This is inferred from the text register and from measurement of the voice track, not heard by us; sex and age of the speaker are not established. It is a performed commentary in its own time, and we should not call the voice "unperformed". Our doubt is about its sound, not its intent.

## What this note does not claim
- No named narrator, source film, instrument or influence is claimed. Comparisons are to families of music, not to what the band heard.
- Reception evidence is thin. Readings of memory, loss and thaw are ours.
