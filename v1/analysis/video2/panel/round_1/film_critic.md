# Film critic: Round 1 reading of video2 ("Elfstedentocht Part 2")

Basis: the music reading, the dossier, and frames I opened myself (f_0089, f_0251, f_0302; the others I cite are from the dossier's own checks and are marked as such). Nobody has heard the audio; sound claims come from the music reading. Timestamps are m:ss on our file (about 1.9 s possibly off the release). Obs = seen or measured; Int = my interpretation.

## 1. What it is about

It is a requiem for an effort, not a report of a race. The archive is amateur or regional colour cine film, and the director (PSB, with whoever cut it) does something quietly radical: after one narrated block of facts (0:41 to 2:36: date, 200 km, 16 below, 575 racers, 9,500 tourists), they stop explaining and let the pack carry the feeling. The subject becomes anonymity under endurance: bibs without faces (3:09 to 4:36), small dark figures in white (1:22 to 1:29), and an ending that leaves the people altogether for broken ice and open water (4:52 to 5:05). Nobody wins. There is no finish line, no winner, no crowd roar. The 1963 "race of the century" is treated as a collective ordeal, and the film's final image is the surface that carried them giving way (Int, but the frames bear it: f_0302 is pink-sepia floes sliding across open water, no person in shot).

## 2. How it makes me feel, and exactly where

- **0:02 to 0:28, dread-tinged stillness.** Forty seconds of frost and branches before a human appears. Obs: long holds (shot 4 runs about 12 s), no people. This is patient and confident editing; it asks the viewer to sit in the weather first.
- **0:28, the stride begins.** The cut to purple dusk at 28.08 against bass and drums entering at 0:28.13 (about 50 ms apart, dossier H). This is the only true cut-to-music lock in the piece and it is placed exactly where a viewer needs it: the picture turns from admiring to uneasy at the moment the music acquires a body.
- **0:41 to 0:46, the date over snow-buried graves.** Obs: headstone and drifts under "Achttien januari negentien-drieenzestig". Whether PSB chose this pairing is unknown, but it works as commemoration: a dated record spoken over a churchyard. Funereal (Int).
- **1:22 to 1:30, the hole and the drop: the best moment.** Obs (f_0089): a flat, overexposed, sepia road, a round road sign, trampled footprints, three or four tiny figures far off. Over it the band drops out for about 4 s, the narrator says "dat is de Elfstedentocht", and the full groove slams in at 1:28.6. The edit's best decision is a non-decision: the picture does not cut on the drop (the next cut is 1:30.3, 1.7 s later). Cutting on the hit would have been the cliche; letting an empty road ride through it makes the loudest event in the piece feel like something happening to the viewer rather than to the screen. The event is named over the emptiest picture, which is the right irony.
- **1:30 to 1:34, the first crowd.** A hard cut after the empty road to a warm sepia bridge, spectators, a lorry: a human jolt. Good contrast, motivated by the drop even though it is not synchronised with it.
- **2:36.6, the clearing.** Voice ends 2:36.1, loop changes 2:36.8, layer partly mutes, picture cuts to the only vehicle-shot, rime-covered road within about 0.2 s (dossier H). Three things stack on one point. This is the cleanest structural joint after the drop and it reads as an exhale.
- **2:44 to 3:00, the airfield lamps and the bloom.** Surreal red globe lamps, then a flare that swamps the frame while a single skater is picked from the mass (f_0179 per the dossier). The picture overexposes as the music holds; "transcendent" is a risk word but the effect is real (Int).
- **3:09 to 4:36, the pack from inside.** Obs: hand-held, blurred, bibs, backs, hands behind backs, faces turned away. The camera is behind or among them, which makes the viewer a member of the pack. The loudness arc and the brightness ramp do the pressure; the pictures supply bodies. I think this is the heart of the film, and also where its weakness lies (below).
- **4:10, the stare.** Obs (f_0251): a young man in a fur-collared coat, mouth open, looks straight into the lens, other skaters behind and a yellow-vested man behind him. After minutes of backs, one face looks out. It is the single most direct human contact in the film, and it arrives in the loudest block (about -9.9 dBFS 12 s mean at 4:24 to 4:36 per the music reading). Chilling because unplanned: he is probably looking at the camera operator, not at us (Int).
- **4:28, the control point.** An exhausted red-hatted skater held up by an official (dossier, not re-checked by me). The only literal image of what the music has been saying.
- **4:36 to 5:05, the unmaking.** The picture turns calm 0.8 s before the music sags (4:36.0 vs 4:36.8), then legs and skates, then ducks and gulls on open water (4:52), then floes and fade. The fade starts about 5:02.5, just after the last full-level hit at 5:02.3. Sound ends before picture; the picture outlasts the sound. I find the last 15 s moving precisely because they decline to resolve: the music gets a stop, the image gets a thaw.

## 3. What is clever

1. **Restraint on the drop** (above): a hard cut stays away from the biggest event.
2. **Narration as an engine that is switched off.** The only intelligible speech finishes at 2:36 and then the voice turns to bar-locked wordless fragments (music reading, L-M on "wordless"). Editorially that means the film stops telling and starts being. Picture and music make the same move together.
3. **Material that insists on being old.** Light leaks, burnt frames (2:08), colour cycling (1:40 to 1:50), scratches: the director keeps damage in, so the image is always a physical object. This is archive handling as texture, not as illustration. Whether the tint swings are in the source or added is unresolved (dossier Q8); I say "kept", not "added".
4. **Contrast through scale.** Wide with tiny dark figures (1:13 to 1:29, 3:46 to 3:50) against crushing close-ups (3:50 to 4:20) gives the film a breathing pattern without any change in the music's pulse.
5. **Withholding colour.** Mostly cold blue-cyan-lilac, so the warm bursts (0:48 pink sun, 1:30 sepia, 3:36.7 flare, final pink-sepia water) mean something. The last 15 s are the warmest sustained stretch of the film, and they show no one.

## 4. What is weak or open to challenge

1. **The cuts do not talk to the music.** The dossier's re-test on the true 120 BPM grid: 34 % within 80 ms of a beat against 32 % chance; no significant phase locking. Between the structural joints (0:28, 1:28, 2:36, 4:36) the editing is by image and phrase, not by rhythm. That can be a virtue (it avoids the music-video reflex), but for three minutes of a locked four-on-the-floor groove, the picture's rhythm is a loose, drifting second rhythm. I would not call it deliberate counterpoint without evidence; it may simply be cutting to the shot's natural length (Int).
2. **The plateau is long.** 3:00 to 4:36 is about 96 s of backs, bibs and hands in similar tones. It is effective as immersion but it is repetitive, and the viewer's relief (the canal shot 3:46, the sidecar, the blown-out golden skater 4:11 to 4:14) shows the editor knew it needed breaths. The weaker stretches are 3:09 to 3:20 and 3:50 to 4:00, where blurred close-ups blend (Int).
3. **No followed protagonist.** The bib numbers recur only in adjacent shots (dossier). So the film's emotional investment never narrows onto one person. I think that is a decision, and a defensible one, but it caps how much we can care about any one skater.
4. **Text ambiguity.** The opening title board, "De winter van 1963 in Friesland", is the only on-screen text; nothing says the footage is from race day. The dossier admits nothing on screen proves it (Q4). The film's authority rests on the narrator's voice and our assumption. A viewer might read pre-dawn people, graves, airfield lights and floes as the same day. I cannot tell whether the juxtaposition is deliberate dislocation or loose archive matching (speculation).
5. **Narration over footage may not be synchronous.** Where words and image agree ("een schaatsmarathon" over skaters at 0:51; "200 kilometer" over a long road at 1:01 to 1:13; "monsterrit" over a file of skaters at 1:52 to 1:57), it is close enough to be literal; I mark this as the one place the film leans on illustration.

## 5. Three questions for the panel

1. **To the music analyst(s):** the hole at 1:24 to 1:28.6 is one of the few moments where music and picture feel co-designed, yet the picture does not cut on the drop. Do you think the music or the narration was fitted to the film, or the reverse? Is there any evidence in the structure (the 30-bar-plus-one-beat grid, the 24-bar echo at 3:24.8) that the film was cut to the music's bar structure at the joints I list (0:28, 2:36.8, 4:36.8) rather than the other way round?
2. **To the lyric/word and context panellists:** is the narration's sparse, factual register (date, distance, numbers, nationalities, "only the strongest") enough to establish a human story, or does the film depend on what a Dutch viewer already knows about 1963? If the narration stops at 2:36, who is the viewer meant to be for the final 2:30?
3. **To everyone:** the ending turns from people to floes and ducks, with a final full-level hit after the groove stops. Is that grief, relief, or the film simply running out of people? I read it as withdrawal; what would change your mind? And is "elegy" fair when the music's loudness rises 15 dB and never recovers to the intro's level?
