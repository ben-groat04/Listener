# First-timer (panellist: the first-time viewer), Round 1

I am reading this with no special expertise, from the dossier, the frames I opened (f_0049 at 0:48, f_0211 at 3:30, f_0302 at 5:01) and the music reading's measurements as summarised in the dossier. Nobody here has heard the audio; where I talk about sound I am trusting the measurements, and I mark that.

## What it is about

It is a memorial for an ordinary-people event. The footage is old colour cine film of the 1963 Elfstedentocht, the 200 km skating race through Friesland. The narrator gives the facts (date, cold, distance, 575 racers, about 9,500 tour skaters), then stops at 2:36 and never returns. After that it is just a loud, steady beat and people's backs. I do not think it is about winning: nobody wins, nobody finishes, there is no finish line. It is about the grinding, anonymous effort, and the way the world goes quiet after.

## How it makes me feel, and where

- **0:00 to 0:40, quiet and cold.** Forty seconds of frost and empty snow before any person. Patient, almost bold for a music video. I like it. A friend with a short attention span would reach for the skip bar, though.
- **0:48 (f_0049).** The first colour: a pink-gold ice road, a red sun, tiny figures. After the blue-white this is a real lift, and it is the first moment I felt something warm. It looks like a postcard from someone's grandparents.
- **0:41 to 0:46.** The date spoken over a snowed-under churchyard. Funereal. Whether that was chosen deliberately I cannot tell (the dossier says it may be the footage's own placing), but the effect is that a date becomes a death notice.
- **1:22 to 1:30, the hole, then the drop at 1:28.6.** The tiny figures on an empty dyke, the music drops away, and then everything comes back on one beat. This is the biggest thrill in the piece. The picture does not react (the editor lets the shot ride on), and strangely that is better: it feels like the music is the weather and the people are just walking through it.
- **1:30.** The first crowd: the lorry, the headscarf woman, the people on the rail. A jolt of humanity after all that emptiness.
- **2:36 to 2:44.** The voice stops, the loop shifts, and the picture cuts at nearly the same moment to a hazy drive down a frosted road. It feels like an exhale. The dossier says this is the best-engineered transition. I felt it before I read that.
- **2:44, the red lamps and the aeroplane.** Most surreal image in the film. I do not understand it (an airfield, says the dossier) and I love that it is odd.
- **2:58 to 3:00.** One skater coming at the camera, then the flare bleaches her into orange. Lovely: a single person pulled out of the crowd and dissolved. This is the most tender thing in it for me.
- **3:09 to 4:36, the long middle.** This is where it divides me. The pack from inside, bibs, backs, no faces (f_0211 is typical: blurred, bent, anonymous, and I like that the bib numbers become the people). It is tense and hypnotic for a while. After about two minutes of steady groove and the same kind of shot it starts to feel samey, and I am not sure I am being carried or just kept going. The dossier says the beat does not change at all and the build is a slow brightening; I could believe that without being able to hear it, and honestly that is what I felt: pressure with no event.
- **4:10.** A young man in a fur collar looks right at the lens, mouth open. After minutes of backs, a face. It jolts me, and it is the nearest thing to a "hello" in the film. I would show a friend this one second.
- **4:28.** The control point: a red-hatted skater held up by an official, onlookers around. The only moment the cost of the thing is plain.
- **4:36 to 5:06, the unravelling.** The picture turns calm, the music thins to gated stabs, and then ducks, broken ice, open water. f_0302 (5:01) is just pink water and floes. I did not expect that and it moved me: the surface that carried everyone is now breaking up. The sound ends first, one last hit, then fade, no credits. It does not resolve; it withdraws. I found the ending the best part.

## What is clever

- Holding back people and voice for the first 40 s, and then removing the voice for good at 2:36 so the facts give way to feeling.
- The silent hole before the drop, and not cutting on it.
- Showing the race from inside the pack, as numbers, rather than as heroes.
- Ending on thaw instead of a finish. It reframes the whole thing as a season closing.
- The cut statistics say the cutting is not locked to the beat. I think that is a strength: the film is not a pop video, it breathes with the phrases.

## What is weak, or left me cold

- The middle plateau (about 3:00 to 4:36) is too long for my taste on repeated viewing; the sameness of shot type and beat is a risk. (Taste, not a fault of craft.)
- I cannot follow the Dutch. The facts are a lot of the first half's meaning, and a non-Dutch viewer without subtitles gets mood only. That is a real accessibility gap, not necessarily the makers' problem.
- No one is followed. I wanted a person to attach to and never got one (the bibs do not carry through, per the dossier). I accept it is deliberate; I still felt the lack.
- The tint swings and flares are lovely, but I cannot tell whether they are damage, stock or grading, which weakens how much credit I can give the makers.

## What I would tell a friend

"Watch the first minute, the drop at 1:28, the face at 4:10 and the last thirty seconds. It is a quiet, odd, sad thing about people skating themselves silly in 1963, and the ending is the point."

## Three questions for the other panellists

1. **For the music person:** the beat never changes for three minutes, yet I feel pressure building. What exactly is doing that (the brightening layer, the fragments from 3:28, the loudness rising about 15 dB), and would a listener hear it as building or as relentless? Is "calm" in the press quote honest at all?
2. **For the editing/film person:** the picture is not cut to the beat, but it lands together at a few joints (0:28, 2:36, 2:57). Was that planned, or is it what you would expect from chance plus a good editor? Does it matter to the feeling either way?
3. **For the context person:** how much of what I felt (the graves, "only the strongest", the thaw at the end) was made by the people who pieced this together, and how much is the 1963 footage and narrator speaking for themselves? Do we know whether the narration was ever paired with these pictures originally?
