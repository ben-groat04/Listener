# The Symbolist (poet and symbolist), Round 1 reading of video2, "Elfstedentocht Part 2"

Sources: dossier, music reading, and frames I opened myself: f_0003 (0:02, frosted crown), f_0085 (1:24, the hole), f_0251 (4:10, the stare), f_0300 (4:59, floes). Obs = seen or measured; Int = my reading. Nobody has heard the audio; the music reading's measurements are my ground.

## 1. What it is about (Int)

On the surface: the 1963 Elfstedentocht. Beneath it, I think the piece is about **anonymity and endurance as a form of love**: how a nation's memory is made of people we cannot name, whose only legible marks are numbers on their backs. The title board says "De winter van 1963": a season, not a race. The winter is the protagonist; the skaters are its weather.

Three ideas run together.

1. **The voice that gives way to the crowd.** A dated, dry, official voice states facts (18 January, 200 km, minus 16, 575 and 9,500). Then, after 2:36, it never speaks in sentences again; it becomes bar-locked fragments (music reading: fragments from about 3:28.6, wordless in my reading, L-M). History as *testimony* becomes history as *breath*. The archive voice, once outside the machine, ends up inside it. The record dissolves into the thing it recorded.
2. **Persistence against a world that is itself ephemeral.** The surface that carries the crowd is ice, and the film ends on ice breaking (4:52 to 5:05). Every stride in the piece is a bet on something that will melt. That is the philosophical core: the heroic act happens on a loan.
3. **Film as ice.** The footage is itself a fragile surface: tint swings, burnt-out frames (2:08), flares (2:55 to 3:00, 3:36.7), emulsion rot (2:35). The medium decays visibly just as the subject does. Obs: the damage is real; Int: its resonance with thaw is mine, and the dossier rightly says damage "is not meaningful by itself". I claim only that the structure invites the rhyme.

## 2. How it makes me feel, and exactly where

- **0:00 to 0:28, reverence and held breath.** Frost as lace (f_0003: the whole frame is a net of branches with sky showing through in holes; it reads as a screen, a veil, almost a negative of a crowd). Pad only, one thud. Obs. The feeling is of entering a church that is also a weather system.
- **0:41 to 0:46, the date over graves.** The first human voice names the day over a snow-buried churchyard and headstone (0:41 to 0:46). Whether PSB chose that pairing is not established (dossier), but it is what the viewer receives: the date arrives already as an epitaph. This is the first place the piece tells me it is an elegy, and it does so before a single skater appears.
- **1:24 to 1:28.6, the hole, and the picture's refusal to flinch.** f_0085: an empty drifted road, one tiny figure, a round road sign, footprints already filling in. The drums and bass vanish (music reading, H) while the narrator names the race. Then the kick returns about 50 dB louder on one downbeat and the *picture does not cut* (cut at 1:30.3). I find that the cleverest decision in the piece: the music gets its explosion, the image stays in the quiet. The viewer is made to feel the force in the body while the eye still sees emptiness. The pack, the crowd, is somewhere in the sound but not yet in the frame.
- **1:30, the crowd arrives.** Sepia bridge, lorry, row of spectators. A human jolt after the empty road. Warmth as memory (Int).
- **2:36.1 to 2:36.8, the last sentence.** Voice ends, loop changes, a layer mutes, and the picture cuts to the modern, rime-covered road within about 0.2 s (dossier, H on times). An exhale: the facts are over, now feeling.
- **2:44 to 2:48, the red lamps.** Five then seven red globes against blue-violet, a small aircraft (dossier f_0165, M). The most dreamlike image; red as a pulse in a cold body. I do not know what it is for; its strangeness is the point.
- **2:57 to 3:00.5, the woman in the flare.** A single skater coming toward the lens, bleached to an orange-cream silhouette as the light-leak swamps the frame, on a section boundary in the music (0.3 s). Dossier: looks like a woman, M-L. This is the nearest the film comes to transcendence: an individual lifted out of the mass at the moment the film stock itself overexposes. The person is saved by being erased by light. Int.
- **3:09 to 4:36, the pack from inside.** Bibs, backs, hands clasped behind backs, no faces (bibs 201, 211, 504, 481, 145, 170, 529, 157...). This is where the title idea is carried: the person is reduced to a numeral and a back, and yet the repetition of the posture, hands folded behind, reads to me as a collective prayer or a bearing of weight. The brightening layer (about +14 dB in 24 s from 3:26) puts pressure on the image with no cue to say when. Obs for the measurements; the "prayer" is Int.
- **4:10, the stare.** f_0251: a young man in a fur-collared coat, mouth open, eyes straight into the lens, other bodies behind him in blue shadow. After about a minute of backs, one face addresses us. It is the single moment the dead look back at the living. The music is at its loudest block (peaks around -9.9 dBFS at 4:24 to 4:36), so the confrontation is *loud*, not tender, which makes it feel like an accusation or a plea rather than a greeting (Int). Strongest moment for me.
- **4:36 to 4:49, the sag and the calm shot.** The picture turns calm 0.8 s before the music sags; the glitter drops out while the machine goes on (music reading). The flag against cyan sky (4:45, M-L as Dutch tricolour) is a civic lift with nothing under it.
- **4:52 to 5:05, ducks, floes, open water, black.** f_0300: straight-down view of broken floes in rose-brown water. No people, no credits. The sound stops at 5:00.8, then one more full-level hit at 5:02.3, then the fade starts (about 5:02.5). The final hit is the heart of the ending for me: after the stop, one more footfall nobody asked for. The machine cannot quite stop on cue. Where an elegy would soften, this strikes. Then the surface the whole film rested on is open water. Int: the thaw closes the winter; Obs: whether this is the same race's reel is unknown (dossier Q16).

## 3. Motifs and visual rhymes

- **Veil and net.** Frosted branches (0:02, f_0003) against the bibs and backs of the plateau: both are dense patterns that hide the individual. The first image of the film is, structurally, the same as the long middle: a lattice of dark marks on pale ground. Int.
- **Empty road and file to the horizon** (1:01 to 1:13, 1:22 to 1:30, 3:25 to 3:28, 4:36 to 4:42). The leading line is the journey and also the vanishing point: everyone in this film is going away from us. The one exception is the man walking towards the lens at 1:44 to 1:48 and the stare at 4:10. Obs for the placements; Int that the toward-the-lens moments form a deliberate pair (I cannot show intent).
- **Hands behind the back** (2:52, 3:20 to 4:34): burden carried, or hands tied. Ambiguous on purpose, I think; the posture is practical long-distance technique (dossier), so I treat the symbolism as the viewer's gift, not the maker's claim.
- **Red as the single warm word in a cold language** (lamps 2:44; hats 3:36, 4:10, 4:21, 4:28 to 4:34; flag 4:45). Blood, alarm, or just stock colour; mark of the individual rather than the event.
- **Pad as frame.** The same dark pad opens and rings out at the close; the thud at 0:00 answers the hit at 5:02.3. A breath taken and let go.
- **Frost to floes.** Ice as lace at 0:02, ice as rubble at 4:59. The film's first and last images are both ice in its *delicate* state, one about to be made solid by cold and one unmade by warmth.
- **Machines at the margin** (cars 2:04, tractor 2:54, sidecar 3:46): modernity hovering at the edge of an old effort. The tractor clearing the lane (2:54) is quietly moving: even a heroic race needs someone to sweep the way.

## 4. What is clever

1. **Withholding the picture of the drop (1:28.6).** Described above. A restraint that lets sound and image disagree productively.
2. **Subtraction as the climax.** The piece adds nothing audible after about 3:50 and spends its last 24 s taking away. A build that ends by unmaking itself is the right grammar for a piece about endurance: the true cost is paid at the end, not at the peak. Obs for the sequence (sag, gate, stop); Int for the meaning.
3. **The voice's trajectory** from sentence to fragment to a single stab per bar (4:40 to 4:59). Narrator to crowd to heartbeat to silence. This is the structural poem of the piece.
4. **Declining to resolve.** No cadence, same pitch set throughout (music reading, M-H). Harmony that returns to the B pedal every 12 s says "settled but straining": the effort never arrives, only continues. That matches an event whose point is the going, not the winning. I note nobody is named a winner anywhere in the film (Obs: no winner shot or caption in the dossier timeline).
5. **The one long hold per act** (the 12 s frost at 0:12 to 0:24, the 8 s road at 1:22 to 1:30, the 8.8 s wide ice at 3:00 to 3:09): the editor lets the world be empty just before it fills.

## 5. What is weak or doubtful

- **The middle is long and the picture's variety is thin.** From 3:09 to 4:36 (about 87 s) the imagery is backs, bibs and blurred close-ups over an unchanging pulse. Dossier statistics show no beat lock, and I do not demand one; but the same posture repeated at the same distance risks monotony, and monotony can read as meaning *or* as filler. I think the stare at 4:10 and the control point at 4:28 are doing a lot of work to rescue it. Int.
- **The film asks me to feel mourning but gives me little to mourn.** We learn no one's name, no outcome, no stakes beyond numbers. If the intent was commemorative, the anonymity is the argument; if the intent was documentary, it is a gap. I cannot settle which (dossier Q17).
- **The thaw ending may over-egg a point.** Ducks and floes are lovely, but if they are from another reel (Q16, unknown), the elegiac "winter closing" is the edit's argument rather than the footage's, and the sentimental reading is easy to accept without noticing it is borrowed.
- **Dutch narration was not heard by us in any trustworthy way.** Date, 16 below, 200 km, 575 and 9,500 are secure; the "only the strongest" line, my favourite, is M on its gist and L on its last verb (dossier). I lean on it for the "thesis" feeling and should be corrected if a Dutch speaker disagrees.

## 6. Claims I decline to make

- That the picture is synchronised to the beat. It is not (dossier: 34 % within 80 ms against 32 % chance on the true grid). The piece meets music at joints, not pulses.
- That the 3:28.6 fragments are archive voices. Source undecided.
- That the graveyard pairing at 0:41 to 0:46 is PSB's intent. It is what I receive.
- That the final hit at 5:02.3 is "meant" as an echo. I only know it is full-level and mono, and falls just before the fade.

## 7. Three questions for the panel

1. **To the music analyst:** The kick and drums do not change level after 1:28.6, and the pad's brightness rises about 24 to 28 dB while the harmony stays on a B pedal. Can you tell me whether the felt "pressure" from 3:24 to 4:36 could be a filter opening alone, and if so, is the piece more *about* gradual tightening (like a held breath) than about any event? And what, in the sound alone, would make the final full-level hit at 5:02.3 read as a *stumble* rather than a *salute*?
2. **To the film and edit specialist:** The stare at 4:10 is the only moment a face addresses the lens in the plateau. Is it in the source reel, or did the editor choose it and hold it for exactly that effect? Does the cut-rate evidence (cut rate versus loudness r about 0.23 to 0.27) show a deliberate escalation there, or is my feeling of confrontation mostly mine?
3. **To the historian or context panellist:** If the narration (1963 voice) was recorded for a different reel from the pictures we see, how much of what I take as "words and images agreeing" (the date over graves, "half zes" over the bobble-hatted figure) is the film's own, and how much is PSB's pairing? Does it matter to the reading that the cuts are not locked to the beat while the *meaning* is so carefully locked at the joints (1:28.6, 2:36.8)?

*One-line summary of my reading: a winter that remembers itself by erasing its people into numbers, then erases the surface they stood on, and ends on one more footstep after the music has stopped.*
