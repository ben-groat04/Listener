# First-timer (panellist: the first-time viewer), Round 2

I have read all six round 1 statements. I re-opened f_0251 (4:10), f_0262 (4:21) and f_0300 (4:59). Nobody has heard the audio; where I speak about sound I am trusting the measurements and say so. Timestamps m:ss on our file.

## Questions put to me, answered

**Music critic, Q3: is the 5:02.3 hit a closing door, a heartbeat or a glitch? Does the plateau build pressure or numb?**
I can only answer as a viewer reading numbers, so weigh this lightly. The hit lands about 0.2 s before the fade begins (5:02.3 against about 5:02.5). With the picture in front of me it reads as a closing door, and I would not call it a glitch: a glitch would be late or accidental, and this one arrives just ahead of the fade. The symbolist's "one more footfall" is lovely but I think it gives the hit a life it may not have. A door shutting is the plainer reading. On the plateau: for me it was both. The first minute (3:09 to about 4:10) built pressure. After the stare at 4:10 I was numbing, and the later cuts (4:20 to 4:36) only held me because of the control point at 4:28. So I now think the pressure is real but front-loaded in my experience, and that the music's late ramp (measured; peak block 4:24 to 4:36) arrives just as my attention is flagging. If that is right, the build is working against a tired viewer, which may be deliberate fatigue-by-design or just length. I cannot tell which.

**Insider and film critic, Q3: is the ending grief, relief, or running out of people?**
I said in round 1 that the ending was the best part, and I stand by it, but I want to be more careful about why. Having looked again at f_0300 (broken floes, rose-brown water, no person), my feeling is not grief and not relief. It is closer to being left alone. The people have gone and the place remains. That fits the insider's reading (loss of winters) as well as the "season closing" reading, and an English viewer with no context would still get "left alone" unaided. I will concede to the sceptic that "grief" is more than I can show from this frame.

**Sceptic, Q1 (to feeling panellists): what supports elegy rather than celebration?**
I will take this seriously, because I think the sceptic is partly right and I got something wrong. See below.

## Where I was wrong, and the sceptic's best point

In round 1 I called the piece "a memorial" and "a quiet, odd, sad thing". The sceptic notes that nothing in the words says anyone suffered, and that the narration is a cheerful pre-race announcement. The insider backs this with the real point: the voice does not know what happens. I accept both, and I accept that I brought the sadness. Then I opened f_0262 (4:21). It shows a man in sunglasses and a dark jumper skating toward the lens in bright winter light, with a woman in a red scarf and a couple of others behind him, blue ice, a farmhouse on the horizon. He is not in distress and nothing about the frame is funereal. If I had seen that frame with no music I would have said "a nice day out skating". So the sceptic is right that much of this footage, taken alone, is cheerful.

That is the most interesting thing I found, and it changes my reading. The sadness is not in the pictures. It is made by the pairing: minor key, cold pad, a loud locked pulse, and the graveyard-and-thaw bookends. I would now say the piece is about hindsight, and the insider put it best: a confident voice and happy faces, with music that knows how it ends. A friend watching cold could take the whole thing as exhilarating and communal, as the sceptic suggests, and the 120 BPM groove that never slows does support that. I would not call "celebration" wrong. I would call it half of the piece, with the other half supplied by the key and the ending. Where I still push back on the sceptic: "nothing says anyone suffered" is true of the words but not of 4:28. The control point shows an official holding up an exhausted red-hatted skater (dossier; I have not re-opened that frame). One image of cost is enough to tilt the elegy reading, and the thaw does the rest.

## Agreements and disagreements with others

- **Film critic and symbolist on the 1:28.6 drop with no cut.** I agreed in round 1, and the sceptic reads the same fact as the editor "not caring". I cannot settle that from the data, but as a viewer the effect on me was the opposite of indifference: the road shot felt chosen, because an editor who did not care would likely have cut at some random point inside the 4 s hole, and instead the shot runs 8.2 s across it. That is thin evidence, and I mark it as such.
- **Symbolist on the stare at 4:10.** I look again at f_0251 and I am less sure than I was. He is in motion, the frame is blurred, and he is jammed in a crowd of skaters with a man in a yellow vest behind him. He may be looking at the camera operator because he is skating at them, not because he is addressing anyone. The film critic said as much ("probably looking at the camera operator"). The symbolist's "the dead look back at the living" is a beautiful reading, and I felt something like it, but it is the music being loudest at that point that gives the face its force. I now think the moment is half found and half made by where the edit puts it, and I withdraw my wish to call it a "hello".
- **Sceptic on the 3:36.7 flare as a match.** Fair. One coincidence at one-second resolution among several loud seconds is not evidence. I felt the flare as a lift, but I will not claim it as a joint.
- **Sceptic on the voice fragments.** I agree with downgrading them. I used the word "pressure" in round 1 and never mentioned the voice; the symbolist's "narrator to crowd to heartbeat" arc is a poem built on a source nobody has identified. I enjoy it, but as a viewer I cannot hear it and neither can anyone here, so I would not lean on it.
- **Film critic on the plateau being the heart of the film.** I partly disagree. By my own experience the heart is the contrast, the places where the plateau lets go: the wide frozen canal and sidecar at 3:46, the blown-out golden skater at 4:11 to 4:14. Those are what I remember, not the backs. The film critic and the symbolist both say the editor knew the plateau needed breaths; I agree, and I would go further and say the breaths are the better film.

## The thread I want to develop: the footage is happier than the piece

This is where the discussion has got interesting for me. Put the observations together:

1. f_0262 (4:21): a sunlit, ordinary skating scene, sunglasses and a red scarf.
2. The insider's point that the narrator speaks before the race, boastfully ("monsterrit", "alleen de allersterkste").
3. The sceptic's point that no words speak of suffering.
4. The insider's data: 127 finishers out of 9,862 starters (research file, as quoted by the insider; I have not checked the file). The piece shows none of that outcome.

So what we see and hear in words is a province looking forward, and what the music and edit add is looking back. The sadness is the distance between them, which a viewer must supply. This explains something I could not explain in round 1: why the piece moved me although I could find no sad picture. It also explains why an English viewer might miss it: without the date's weight and without the 127, "looking back" has nothing to look back at. That is a real limit on who the piece is for, and I think it answers the film critic's own question about who the viewer is for the last 2:30. My answer: a viewer who already carries 1963 with them, or one who is willing to let the minor key and the thaw tell them.

One risk I want to name. If the effect depends on the gap between voice and hindsight, then after 2:36 the voice is gone and the gap has nothing to push against except what the viewer already knows. The last 2:30 therefore runs on the viewer's memory more than on anything on screen. For a first-timer like me, that worked only because the ending told me something was being lost. If I had stopped at 4:30 I would have had a pleasant, slightly hypnotic skating film.

## What I would now tell a friend

Same as before, with one change: watch the first minute, the drop at 1:28, the golden skater at 4:11 and the last thirty seconds, and do not expect the faces to carry the sadness. The sadness is in the ending and in what you know before you press play.

Setting aside the minor key, what in the sound alone, for example the gated bass at 4:48.55, the stop at 5:00.8 and the late hit, could not be heard as a plain outro to a dance track? In other words, how much of the elegy is carried by the last 25 seconds, and does the piece survive if you cut them off?
