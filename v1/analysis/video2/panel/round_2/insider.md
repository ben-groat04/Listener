# Cultural insider and historian: Round 2 response (video2, "Elfstedentocht Part 2")

Basis: all six round 1 statements, the dossier, `elfstedentocht_1963.md`, `cultural_context.md`, `archive_sources.md`, and frames I opened for this round (f_0179, f_0204, f_0238). I have not heard the audio, and nobody has. Timestamps are m:ss on our file. Where I rely on general knowledge rather than a repo source I say "(background, unchecked)".

## 1. Questions put to me

**Music critic: is the narration from the same film as the pictures, and does the 1963 outcome change what the unmaking at 4:36 to 5:02 should mean?**

Same film: I do not know, and the repo cannot tell us (`archive_sources.md`: films and narrator not confirmed). What I can add is a probability argument. The one concrete colour candidate is P.J. Sturm's colour film; Kleare Kimen is an amateur film club (M). Amateur colour cine film of 1963 was normally silent (background, unchecked), so a continuous commentary talking about "the twelfth Elfstedentocht" and "what everyone is preparing for" most likely comes from a separate, produced item: a regional or sponsor film such as "Dit is Friesland", a newsreel, or radio. The title board ("De winter van 1963 in Friesland", 0:00) is a club-film habit, so pictures and voice probably started life apart, and PSB (or the archive's own compilation) paired them. That is inference, M-L. Consequence: when I wrote in round 1 that words and images "agree" over graves (0:41) and the bobble-hatted figure (0:59.6), I was crediting the reel with a match that is probably the edit's.

On the outcome: yes, it changes the reading, but only for a viewer who brings the outcome. The piece itself never mentions suffering (I concede this to the sceptic below). What the 1963 outcome adds is that the sag and stop (4:36.8 to 5:00.8) can be heard as the end of the *race*, not a generic fade: the one surviving record of that day is a field that thinned from about 9,862 starters to 127 official finishers (research; the 69 figure counts differently). I would still not call the open water "the thaw after the freeze" of 1963. The 1963 race was run in hard frost, and any thaw footage is from some other day (see section 4).

**Sceptic, film critic, first-timer and symbolist all asked, in different words: how much of this is the footage, how much is PSB?** My best split:
- The footage's own: the facts, the pride of the commentary, the look (tint drift, scratches, flares), the bibs, the spectators.
- Probably PSB's: the order (frost, then voice, then no voice); the stopping of the narration at 2:36 for ever; the decision to put the date under a churchyard (the churchyard is itself ordinary, see below); the choice to end on water, not on a finish.
- Unknowable from here: whether the narration was recorded over these pictures at all.

## 2. Where I agree and where I concede

**Concession to the sceptic (cheerful announcement).** I wrote that the narrator "speaks with the confident, slightly boastful pride of a province". The sceptic is right that the words themselves are a plain pre-race announcement ("Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt", 1:23.8) and say nothing sad. My claim of a "gap between voice and music" was an inference from what I know of 1963 and from the music reading's mood, not an observation. I should have framed it as: *the Dutch audience supplies the gap*. The piece makes room for it (the voice stops, the pulse carries on) but does not state it. I keep the reading as an inheritor's reading, flagged Int, and drop "prophecy" as a description of the film.

**Concession on the churchyard (0:41).** The sceptic says a snowed churchyard is simply what a Frisian village looks like in January. In the Dutch village the churchyard surrounds the church in the village centre (background, unchecked), so a camera panning a village will often pass graves. So the footage's own presence of graves is not evidence of funereal intent. What remains is the pairing: the editor had 40 s of landscape and chose to drop the first spoken date on this shot. I still call that an invitation to a memorial reading, but I withdraw "key to the whole piece". Three of six panellists (first-timer, symbolist, film critic) took it as epitaph; that tells us what viewers *receive*, not what was intended. The sceptic and I agree on that.

**Agreement with the sceptic on motifs.** Red, machines and windmill are what any 1960s Frisian winter film contains. I would add that this is a point for my side of the panel too: ordinary content is the content, so it can be read as "the film is a family object", but it should not be counted as author's motif.

**Concession on my own round 1 claim about "tour skaters" in the plateau.** I wrote that the backs and bibs from 3:09 to 4:36 are "the faces of the great majority", meaning toerrijders. I opened bibs this round: f_0238 (3:57) shows a white bib with black numerals, "57", cut cleanly as a sports bib; f_0204 (3:23) shows striped jumpers and bare backs with hands clasped, no number. The dossier lists the legible numbers: 201, 211, 504, 440, 481, 145, 170, 529, 236, 238, 157, 57, 24x. Every one is below 575, the narrator's own count of *wedstrijdrijders* (2:25.5). If the 9,294 tour skaters also wore numbers, with a pool that size I would expect some four-digit numbers among eleven or more readings, and there are none. That does not prove these are racers: I do not know how, or whether, tour skaters were numbered in 1963 (background, unchecked; this needs a source), and eleven readings from one cameraman's position are a small sample. But it weakens my claim that the plateau is "the tour skaters' piece", and it raises the opposite possibility: the pack from inside may be the racers' main field. I flag this as an open question, not a finding.

**The woman at 2:58 (answering the dossier's Q11).** I opened f_0179. It shows a single skater coming towards the camera in a long dark coat with a skirt-like hem to the knee, a close hat, a pale scarf or collar, arms out. A woman is the more natural reading (M-L); a long overcoat on a man is not excluded. The repo says women could not officially enter the tour until 1985 (`cultural_context.md`, citing schaatshistorie.nl). So if she is a woman she was not a competitor in the 1963 race. That is a useful historian's point: she is a local skater on a lane, which supports the sceptic's and the dossier's view that not all footage is race-day. `cultural_context.md` also lists a NOS piece on "the 1963 women skaters" (M); I have not read it, so I do not claim some women skated unofficially.

## 3. Pushback

**To the film critic (and the symbolist): the stare at 4:10 as "address".** I opened f_0251 in round 1. I now want to push back on both of us treating the stare as an act of the *edit*. People filmed on a route stare at the camera; it is the commonest thing in amateur film of crowds (background, unchecked). The young man in a fur-collared coat is almost certainly a spectator who noticed the lens, and the cameraman or the editor kept it. What the edit chose is to hold it for about a second inside a stretch of faceless backs. That the cut-rate evidence is flat (r 0.23 to 0.27 vs loudness) says the escalation is not engineered shot by shot; the stare may be a lucky find. I still rate it the best image, but its force is an effect of position, not of intent.

**To the symbolist ("the winter is the protagonist; the skaters are its weather"; "anonymity and endurance as a form of love").** I like it as a reading. A Frisian objection: the Elfstedentocht is not at bottom an anonymous event for the people who lived it. The tradition is *named*. Every Dutch family tells "my grandfather skated 1963 and had to give up at Dokkum". The anonymity belongs to the film, not to the memory. So I would turn your reading round: the piece offers a blank into which each Dutch viewer writes a name. That is why the lack of city names and faces is not a weakness for an inheritor (I called it one in round 1; I now think it is a feature for Dutch viewers and a loss for foreign ones).

**To the first-timer ("nobody wins, nobody finishes").** True of the film, but a Dutch viewer will not feel absence of a finish as an omission. "De Hel van '63" is known as a race of survivors. The withheld finish is only visible to people who know there was one (Paping, the Brinta, the queen's helicopter at the Grote Wielen). For them it is a pointed omission.

## 4. The most interesting thread: the ending, and what a 2013 Dutch audience brought to it

Round 1 asked whether the open water at 4:52 to 5:05 is the end of the race, of winters, or of a reel. The sceptic and the symbolist rightly note that the ending footage may come from another reel (dossier Q16; the sceptic's f_0299 shows abstract pink-brown ice and water). I accept that. So the "climate elegy" reading I offered is carried by *outside knowledge plus context of the commission*, not by the footage. Let me say precisely what that context is, using the repo's sources.

- The commission is for the 50th anniversary, released in November 2013 (`context.md`).
- The last tour was 4 January 1997 (research). Counting winters 1997/98 to 2012/13 gives 16 winters without one when the piece appeared (my arithmetic from the Weeronline count, 28 winters by February 2025). The 22-year gap before 1985 was the earlier long drought; 1963 stayed "the reference point for a generation" (research, M).
- Most to the point: in February 2012, 21 months before release, the tour was planned for 11 Feb and cancelled on 8 Feb because the ice at Stavoren was about 2 cm thick (research, H). The narrator's "alle steden zijn klaar" (1:19.7, low confidence on the first words) and "waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt" (1:23.8, H) are therefore lines a Dutch listener in 2013 had very recently heard *in the present tense*, in the news of 2012, and then watched not happen. That resonance belongs to the audience, not to the footage or, so far as we know, to PSB. I put it as a hypothesis: the 2012 near-miss would make the waiting-and-silence at 1:24 to 1:28.6 and the open water at the end land *harder* in 2013 than in 1963's terms. No source in the repo shows reception in these terms (reception is thin, `context.md`), so this is a reading to be tested, not a finding.

My answer to my own round 1 question: the three readings are layered, not exclusive. A viewer with only the film gets the third (reel ends, ice breaks). A viewer who knows the race gets the second reading as "the winter closes". Only a viewer with the recent news of 2012 and the long drought gets the climate-loss reading. The piece does not fail if the viewer stops at the first layer; it simply stays private to those who go further. The sceptic is right that this is thin ground for a strong claim of intent.

## 5. Where the data side and mine meet

The sceptic asks what supports "elegy" rather than "determined, exhilarating, communal". Not my question, but I can offer a cultural datum: Dutch commemoration of the tour is itself *celebratory and sombre at once* ("Hel van '63" is told with pride). So the two readings are not opposites in Dutch memory. The measured facts (loudness up 15 dB, constant pulse, fall of the top band at 4:36.8) do not fix which the maker meant. The honest statement is that the piece supports both, and the ending's subtraction (4:36.8 to 5:00.8) and open water are the only places that lean elegiac without needing outside knowledge.
