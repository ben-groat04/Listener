# The Sceptic: Round 2 on video2 ("Elfstedentocht Part 2", PSB)

Basis. I re-read my round 1, then all five other round 1 statements. New this round, I opened f_0251 (4:10), f_0269 (4:28) and f_0270 (4:29) myself, and re-read `sync/sync.json` for the chance tests. As before, nobody has heard the audio; "sound" means measurement. Times are m:ss on our file.

## 1. Where I was answered, and where I concede

**My question 1 (elegy versus celebration).** Nobody supplied measurement for "elegy"; they supplied context. The insider did the honest thing and said so: the elegiac reading comes from the *date* (a Dutch viewer brings "De Hel van '63", research file: 127 finishers) and from the churchyard shot, both outside the sound. The symbolist and film critic lean on the same shot (0:41 to 0:46) and on the ending. I concede part of my case: the insider's point that the narration is a *before-the-race* voice (1:49 "16 graden beneden nul", 0:58 "half zes in de ochtend", 2:25 the entry numbers) is a real, checkable observation about the words, and it does give the irony a mechanism that my "cheerful announcement" reading lacked. I still say the irony exists only for a viewer who already knows the outcome. Without that knowledge the same data reads as exactly the "determined, communal" piece I proposed. Both readings fit; the music's constant 120 BPM, B Aeolian, no cadence does not choose between them. I now state it as: *elegy is a reasonable reading conditional on outside knowledge; it is not carried by the signal.*

**My question 2 (which joints survive chance).** Answered only indirectly. Nobody named a number of candidate cuts. I will do the arithmetic myself. `sync.json` gives 86 cuts in 305.9 s, one every 3.6 s on average. A cut falling within 0.25 s of any one named instant therefore has a chance of roughly 0.5/3.6 = 14 % per joint. For the five joints I listed: 0:28 (0.05 s), 2:36.6 (0.2 s), 2:57 (0.3 s to a section boundary), 4:36.0 (0.8 s early, so outside any tight tolerance), 1:28.6 (cut 1.7 s late, a miss). Only 0:28 and 2:36.6 are inside 0.25 s, and the chance of *at least two of five* landing is about 15 %, not negligible. Note too that `sync.json` shows section boundaries with the nearest cut as far as 4.3 s (75.7 s), 2.8 s (85.0 s) and 1.6 s (88.65 s). The joint list is a selected one. I do not say 0:28 and 2:36.6 are chance; I say they are two hits among the section boundaries listed in sync.json, most of which have no cut within 0.5 s, which is what a thoughtful editor *or* luck would give. The film critic's and the music critic's "narration placed with care" is therefore M at most.

**My question 3 (voice fragments).** The music critic and symbolist both keep it, with proper labels (L-M, "wordless in my reading", "I credit the gesture, not the technique"). The music critic's evidence is the bar-lock: 17 of 21 onsets 0.1 to 0.2 s before the bar line. That is a real measurement, and I accept that something with speech-like timing is bar-locked. But bar-lock is what *any* sequenced layer does; it is evidence against "free-timed archive voice" and for "sliced or synthesised material", which actually weakens "a witness becomes a texture". A voice that has been chopped to the grid is the producer's, not the witness's. The symbolist's "history as breath" is a fine sentence about a layer whose identity is unknown. I hold my round 1 position, and add that the bar-lock makes my downgrade stronger, not weaker.

## 2. Things I checked in frames, and what they show

**4:28 "official holds up an exhausted skater" (first-timer, film critic, insider; from seg 14).** I opened f_0269 and f_0270 (4:28, 4:29). In both I see a skater in a red hat and dark sweater at the left with a second skater in green; skates are visible and the red-hat figure is upright, mid-stride or walking on blades. Two officials in peaked caps stand a short distance to his right, apart from him; no visible hand on him, no sagging posture. A row of about a dozen onlookers stands along the right. So the frames support "control point or rest point with officials and onlookers" and do *not* support "held up" or "exhausted". Three panellists built on the seg note ("steadied by an official", "appears exhausted, being held up") and the film critic labelled it "dossier, not re-checked". The first-timer called it "the only moment the cost of the thing is plain". On this evidence that moment is not plain at all. I would strike it from the list of evidence for suffering. Seg 14 itself also says elsewhere "a man in a dark coat apparently pushing or helping another" at 4:25 to 4:28, a different shot; that is also tentative.

**4:10 "the stare" (symbolist, film critic, insider, first-timer, all "strongest moment").** f_0251: a young man in a fur-collared coat in the foreground, lit and in focus enough to see his face, turned toward the camera, mouth slightly open or in mid-breath. Behind him a yellow-vested man and others in blue shadow, on snow. I agree he is looking in the direction of the lens. But look at the geometry: the camera is among the group (the frame is hand-held, blurred at the edges, with people at both sides), so the operator is working at close range, and in amateur film people near the camera looking at it is the *default* behaviour. The film critic is the only one to say it ("probably looking at the camera operator, not at us"), and I side with him. The symbolist's "the dead look back at the living … an accusation or a plea" and the insider's "someone I could be descended from" are read from a frame of about one second in which a man glances at a cameraman. It is a good shot because it is the first face in a minute. The reading "the stare is deliberate address by the editor" is unproven: what the editor chose was to *keep* a frame that happened to hold a glance. I answer the symbolist's and the insider's question directly: I cannot say it is in the source reel and not a PSB insertion, but nothing in the data suggests insertion, and a one-second glance in a hand-held reel is the null hypothesis. Also, "the loudest block makes it an accusation": the sync data give r = 0.23 to 0.27 for cut rate against loudness, and picture motion against onsets r near zero. Nothing measured says the picture was timed to that loudness.

**Insider's claim: the plateau shows the toerrijders, the 9,500, not the racers.** The insider labels this "background knowledge, unchecked" and I am grateful. I want to test it with what we have, and it points the other way, at least tentatively. The bib numbers the notes read in the plateau are 201, 211, 504, 481, 440 (or 44x), 145, 170, 529, 157, 236, 238 (dossier lines 75 to 82). Every one is below 575, the narrator's count of *racers* (wedstrijdrijders), while the tour skaters were about 9,500. If numbering ran 1 to 575 for the race and higher for the tour, these are racers, or at least the numbers are consistent with that. I do not know the 1963 numbering scheme and it is not in `research/` (I searched for "bib", "nummer", "startnummer" and found only descriptive mentions). So: this is a *testable* point, not a finding. If the insider can find the numbering, it decides whether "the anonymous majority" reading of the plateau stands. Until then, "these are the tour skaters" is an assumption stated as a fact, and the piece's emotional alignment "it belongs to them" rests on it. Separately the footage is not necessarily from the race at all (the dossier admits it; archive_sources calls the reel unresolved, and downgrades a newsreel source to L).

## 3. Where I push back on others

**Symbolist, "the picture's refusal to flinch" at 1:28.6.** I said in round 1 the picture does not mark the drop, and I read it as indifference. The film critic, symbolist and first-timer read it as a deliberate decision ("the cleverest decision in the piece"). The two readings predict different things. If it were designed restraint, the picture should do *something* purposeful near the drop (a held shot of a chosen length). What we have is a static shot from 1:22 to 1:30.3 and a cut 1.7 s after the drop. A long shot that happens to straddle a loud event looks the same in the data as a "refusal". I would only credit intent if shot lengths near the drop were *longer than the film's norm*. I computed what I could: 86 cuts over 305.9 s gives a mean shot of about 3.6 s; the static road shot from about 1:22 to 1:30.3 (the film critic's and dossier's timings; I did not re-measure the start) is about 8 s, more than twice the mean. I concede that this is a point in the critics' favour: a hold of that length is not typical. But the opening shot (shot 4, about 12 s) and other long holds show this editor *likes* long holds of empty landscape in the first half, so the hold needs no special explanation. I stay at "plausible, unproven", where the others say "cleverest".

**The "withdrawal, not resolution" ending (all five).** Agreement is complete and suspicious; they share one data pipeline and one dossier. The four specific sound events (4:36.8 sag, 4:48.55 bass gate, 5:00.8 stop, 5:02.3 last hit) are measured, and I trust them. What I question is the step from "layers removed" to "unmaking". Removing layers is the standard way to end a loop-based track; the *last hit after the stop* is the only unusual feature, and the music critic and symbolist differ on what it is (door closing; stumble; salute). That divergence is itself the evidence that nothing in the signal selects the meaning. A quieter explanation, which nobody has excluded: a final impact placed for the outro, or an automation tail on the rendered master. I mark it L and I say the "heart of the ending" is a hypothesis about a 0.1 s event.

**Symbolist's "film as ice" (damage rhymes with thaw).** The symbolist honestly flags it as the viewer's gift. I accept that framing and have no quarrel, except to note that tint swings and flares tell us about the stock and the telecine, and that "the medium decays just as the subject does" is a poem, not an observation about the piece. The same goes for "hands behind the back as prayer". The dossier itself records that the posture is standard long-distance technique, and the notes show the first instance at 2:52.

**First-timer's "I felt the transition at 2:36 before I read it".** A note of caution: this person had read the dossier before writing (they say so), and later "felt" statements follow the dossier's priorities. I do not doubt their honesty; I say a first-timer who has read the master dossier is no longer a first-timer, and their reaction cannot count as independent confirmation of the joints. This applies equally to my own cold readings.

## 4. The thread I find most interesting: before-the-race voice, after-the-race music

The insider's one big claim (the voice does not know the outcome; the music, written 50 years on, does) is the best idea on the panel because it is *falsifiable in part*. What would we expect if it were true? The music should *comment* on the voice, with something in the sound that registers knowledge (a change on specific words), not merely a mood. The data give three candidate points: (a) the drop on "dat is de Elfstedentocht" (1:28.3 to 1:28.6), (b) the clearing at the end of the numbers list (2:36.1 to 2:36.8), and (c) nothing on "monsterrit" (1:52.5) or "alleen de allersterkste" (0:58 to 1:10) that the notes record. So the music responds at the *ends* of sentences and at the name of the event, and not at the words that carry the proud promise ("only the very strongest", "monster ride"). That is a mixed result. At (a) the drop is arguably a celebratory fanfare, not a hindsight comment; to a mind looking for hindsight the *silence before* is the grave and the *drop* is the bravado. I would grant the insider this: the drop at the name of the race reads as pride, and the 4:36 to 5:02 withdrawal as the aftermath, so a two-act "pride then reckoning" shape fits the signal without any outside knowledge, whereas "elegy throughout" does not. That is a stronger and cheaper claim than the panel's. It is also testable on the other video in the set (video1), where the same producers do the same thing with a different narration, if that video shows the same pride-then-withdrawal shape.

## 5. Revised plain summary

A patient loop-based electronic piece with one well-built drop (1:28.6) and one tidy transition (2:36.8) laid under amateur colour archive film and a pre-race Dutch voice. Its shape is *announcement, endurance, withdrawal*. Whether the endurance is mourned or admired is not settled by the picture or the sound and depends on what the viewer knows. I have downgraded or struck: the 4:28 "held up skater" (not visible in the frames), the 4:10 stare as addressed to us (probable glance at the operator), the "tour skaters" identification of the plateau (bibs all below 575; numbering unknown), and "voice becomes breath" (bar-locked material is the producer's). I have upgraded: the narration's before-the-race status (insider; checked against the words note), and the long hold at 1:22 to 1:30.3 as at least unusual in length.
