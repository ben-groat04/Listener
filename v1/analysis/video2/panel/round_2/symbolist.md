# The Symbolist (poet and symbolist), Round 2 on video2, "Elfstedentocht Part 2"

Basis: all six round 1 statements, the dossier, and frames I opened for this round: f_0046 (0:45, graves), f_0106 (1:45, the walking man), f_0172 (2:51, the pack), f_0253 (4:12), f_0293 (4:52, ducks). Nobody has heard the audio; sound claims rest on the music reading. Obs = seen or measured; Int = interpretation.

## 1. Answers to the questions put to me

**The Sceptic's third question** (on what basis "the voice moves from witness to crowd to breath", and what would show the fragments are only a synth in the vocals stem). Conceded in part, defended in part.

- The music reading marks the 3:28.6 fragments L-M on "wordless" and undecided on source. I wrote "wordless in my reading" and then built a metaphor on it. That was too much weight on a source nobody has identified. I withdraw "the archive voice ends up inside the machine" as a claim about the sound.
- What survives is smaller and testable: from 3:28.6 an upper layer with speech-like timing starts, bar-locked (17 of 21 onsets 0.1 to 0.2 s before the bar line), with lengths growing (1.5, 2.2, 3.6 s), and the last 25 s have one unpitched stab per bar. That is a measured trajectory of an *event density*, whatever the sound source. I can call it "the piece's upper line stops being a sentence and becomes a pulse" without saying whose voice it is.
- What would change my mind: a spectrogram of the fragments showing formant movement against a fixed synthetic spectrum, or a comparison of the fragment pitch with the pad's scale (B Aeolian). If it sits on the scale and has no formants, it is a synth and "voice" is a listener's projection. That projection is itself worth noting (listeners hear speech where there is none: Whisper did the same), but it is then a fact about hearing, not about archive material.

**The Film critic's third question** (is the ending grief, relief or the film running out of people; what would change my mind). I read it as withdrawal-as-grief, but I now doubt the *thaw*. See section 3: I opened f_0293 and the open water holds a crowd of ducks and gulls on what looks like a hole in the ice, which in winter is ordinary, not a sign of spring.

**The Insider's third question** (race end, end of winters, or end of the reel). I give a ranked answer in section 2, because the Insider handed me the best thread of the round.

**The Music critic's third question** (closing door, heartbeat or glitch for the final hit at 5:02.3). Addressed to the human-response reader, but it was my own favourite moment, so: I cannot say how it sounds. What I can say from the data is that it is full-level and mono, arrives 1.5 s after the groove stops and about 0.2 s before the fade begins, after about 2 s of pad ring-out in which the ear has already accepted the end. By that structure it has to be heard as a late, unrequired event. A heartbeat needs repetition, and there is one hit. A glitch needs a fault, and the level is the deliberate full level. So "door closing" or "one step after the music" are the two live options; I prefer the latter and cannot prove it.

**To the Sceptic generally.** You say the funereal tone is something the viewer brings, and that the narration is a cheerful pre-race announcement. I agree on the second point and it strengthens my case rather than weakening it (section 2). On the first: the minor key and cold palette are not "the viewer's"; they are the maker's two largest choices, and PSB chose them for a piece commissioned to mark an anniversary. Brought-in or not, they are in the text.

## 2. The thread I want to develop: the voice that does not know

The Insider's point (round 1, section 1) is, to me, the most important sentence in the round: **the narration is a before-the-race voice**. Every line is pre-start: the date, "half zes", "alle steden zijn klaar", "waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt", 16 below, the entry numbers. Nothing in it is retrospective. The Sceptic reads the same fact as "cheerful announcement" and uses it to deflate "elegy". I think the two readings are the same fact looked at from two ends, and together they give the piece its actual form.

My round 1 theory was that the *voice dissolves into crowd*. The better theory is **dramatic irony carried by structure**:

1. *0:41 to 2:36.* A confident, proud, present-tense voice (Insider: the province talking to itself) over a band that is already minor, already cold, already carrying a pad that does not resolve. The band knows. The voice does not.
2. *The date over the graves (0:41 to 0:46).* Here I must be careful, and I have now looked again (f_0046): the frame is a snow-buried churchyard, headstones leaning out of drifts, nobody in shot. Obs. The Sceptic is right that a village churchyard in January looks like this on any reel, and that the pairing may be the reel's or PSB's. But irony does not need intent to work on a viewer who knows the date; and for the viewer who does not, the date arrives as an epitaph anyway. I keep it as a *received* effect and no more.
3. *2:36.1 to 2:36.8.* The facts stop. The last fact is a head-count: 575 and about 9,500. The Insider adds what the viewer who knows brings: 127 finished (research file). The number the film does not say is the one that makes the film. The picture over that sentence is faces of four or five men in knee-breeches, neutral or tired (dossier 2:34 to 2:36). It is a list of the soon-to-be-lost, read out by someone who cannot know.
4. *After 2:36.* No words that mean anything. The film has "nothing left to say" in the precise sense that the only voice it had was an innocent one.

This also answers the Sceptic's challenge about measurable evidence for "elegy": the evidence is not a dB value. It is a **structural fact about the text**: a voice from before the event, framed by music made fifty years after. That is a measurable property of the artefact (source dates, commission for the 50th anniversary: `context.md`), and it does not depend on the fragments' source. It was the Insider who saw it; I take it as the spine, and I give up my "voice dissolves" thesis as the spine and reduce it to a decoration.

Ranking the three readings of the ending the Insider asked about, by what a viewer needs to reach them:

| Reading | Needs outside knowledge? | Reachable unaided by an English viewer? |
|---|---|---|
| Closure of a film or reel (sound stops, fade, no credits) | No | Yes |
| End of the race or the season (floes, open water) | A little (what the event was) | Probably, if they absorbed "200 km" and "16 below" |
| End of winters (climate elegy) | Yes (1963 as one of the last reliably hard winters; 2012 near-miss at Stavoren, research file) | No |

I accept the Insider's claim that the third is what a Dutch viewer will bring, and the table says the piece works on all three levels only if you bring everything. I would not say it has "failed" for an unprepared viewer; I would say it holds a **layered ending** that is honest at each depth. But I place the weight on the second reading, the end of a season, because it is the only one both made by the film's own picture and not dependent on an argument that PSB is not documented as making (no artist statement was found, dossier Q17).

## 3. Correcting my own round 1 claims, with evidence

**3.1 "Everyone in this film is going away from us" (retracted).** I wrote that the leading line is a vanishing point and the toward-the-lens moments (1:44 to 1:48 and 4:10) were the exceptions, forming "a deliberate pair". Checking frames, this is much too strong.

- f_0172 (2:51): the pack of about eight skaters leans *towards* the camera, hands behind backs, gold on the blades.
- f_0253 (4:12): skaters approach the lens down a glossy lane; a standing figure at right, a golden sky.
- The dossier lists approaching figures at 2:12 to 2:23, 2:57 to 3:00.5 (the woman), 3:00.5 to 3:09 (the pack growing from tiny to large), 3:46 to 3:50 (7 to 9 striding at us), 4:20 (sunglasses man).

So approach is common; recession is too. The real distinction is not direction but **face**: almost all of the approachers have heads down, hats, hoods or blur, and only a handful show a face. I withdraw "a deliberate pair" and keep only the Obs that faces are rare and the few that look at us (4:10) stand out. The Film critic's gloss on it is better than mine: the 4:10 man is "probably looking at the camera operator, not at us". I find that more affecting, not less. The look is not an accusation across sixty years; it is a man looking at the person who is making the record at the very moment it is made. The record is looking back at the record-maker. That is the piece's whole subject (a found document) folded into one second.

**3.2 The 1:46 walking man (correction to the dossier's gloss and to my "history approaching").** In f_0106 he is a middle-aged man in a long dark coat and cap, boots, walking through trampled snow towards us with the lower face pale and indistinct (a scarf or the overexposure; I cannot say). The dossier says "expressionless"; at this resolution I cannot read expression. His behaviour is the opposite of the pack's: no skates, off the ice, walking against everyone else. I keep him as a mild counter-figure (a walker among skaters) but not as an emblem.

**3.3 The thaw (partly retracted).** I wrote that the film ends with the surface "the whole film rested on" breaking up. Having opened f_0293 (4:52), I see a crowd of ducks and gulls on a round patch of open water, with two bleached circles of film damage. Ducks on a hole in the ice are a normal sight in a hard winter, and the dossier already says the thaw reel may be from another year (Q16). The later shot (floes on open magenta water, f_0302 per the dossier and the Film critic) is better evidence of broken ice, but a viewer can also take it as a river in winter with ice floating. So: **the ending shows ice that is not solid; it does not show spring**. My "heroic act on a loan" thesis survives (the film chooses to end on the unsolid ice), but I should not say "thaw" as if it were observed. I call it "the ice losing its solidity" and mark the seasonal reading as Int.

**3.2b Frost as net (hold, with a scope limit).** The Sceptic is right that motifs are over-counted. Mine ("veil and net" joining frost branches and bibs) is a visual rhyme I find, not one I can show the makers intended. I keep it as a reader's rhyme. I would not defend it against someone who simply does not see it.

## 4. Where I agree and disagree with specific panellists

- **Agree with the Sceptic** that "elegy" and "exhaustion" are named without a measured cause in several round 1 statements, mine included ("heroic act on a loan"; "prayer"). Tempo never slows. I now say: the *form* is a subtraction (sag, gate, stop), which is measured; the *word* "exhaustion" is a reading; and "withdrawal" is the one I can defend because stop and fade are structural. Disagree that a "breakdown then outro" is "equally good and simpler": it explains the music but not why the picture leaves people four seconds before the sound does (4:52 onwards, ducks first, then floes). A plain outro does not need the picture to change subject.
- **Agree with the Film critic** that the editor's non-decision at 1:28.6 is the best decision in the piece. I disagree with the Sceptic's "editor not caring": the cut at 1:30.3 is to the first crowd, the first warmth and the first bridge, 1.7 s after the drop. The dossier's own counts do not support a claim of care *or* indifference. I can only say the pairing produces the effect; I cannot say it was designed.
- **Agree with the Music critic** on the late kick as the oddest decision, and on "emotion by timbre, not harmony". The no-cadence observation is also the most persuasive objective support for my "effort that never arrives" reading: the harmony does not arrive, and neither does the picture (no finish line, no winner, per the Insider's "no finish").
- **Push back on the First-timer** gently: "No one is followed. I wanted a person to attach to." The film does hold one thing that behaves like a protagonist, and it is not a skater: it is the **ice lane itself**, which appears in nearly every act (the swept road at 0:48, the cleared lane at 2:49, the glossy cracked lane at 3:00, the dark teal lane at 3:25, the long dark blue track at 4:36). Whether the lane is a character is my metaphor; that it is the recurrent *place* is observable in the dossier's motif table (the road or lane to the horizon).
- **Agree with the Insider** that the tour skaters are the majority and the piece "belongs to them", and add the symbolic inversion: the narrator names 575 racers first, but the camera follows the 9,500.

## 5. A line I want the panel to test: the walkers

The dossier's 1:22 to 1:29 figures are "2 or 3 tiny dark figures walking towards us, one bent forward" on a dyke road, and the Insider asks whether they are racers or onlookers. I do not know. I note that the film puts *walkers* at its two stillest points, the road at 1:22 (before the drop) and the lane at 2:23 to 2:36 (the last sentence, behind four or five men), and puts the *skaters* where the music is loud. If the walkers are spectators, then the film has chosen to read the event's greatest statements (the naming of the race and the final head-count) over the people who are not yet in motion. I mark that as Int and weak: it rests on two shots.

## 6. Two new questions

1. **To the Insider (cultural historian):** you say the narration is a before-the-race voice with a regional pride. If the narration really comes from a regional or sponsor film made *before or around* the event (as `context.md` leaves open), could the same reel also contain the graveyard, the pre-dawn figures and the final floes, so that the voice and the pictures *were* paired in 1963 and PSB's only act was to remove the original music and add its own? Or does a Frisian viewer hear the graveyard-under-the-date as a later pairing? Tell me which shots (0:41 to 0:46, 1:30 bridge, 4:52 ducks) you would stake money on as 1963 race-week footage and which you suspect are other years, because the irony I now rest on depends on the voice being innocent of the outcome, not on the pictures being so.

2. **To the Music critic:** you note the bass "limps" (three-sixteenth grid, meeting the kick only on beat 3) and that the narrator floats unducked over a locked groove. If I accept that the voice is the innocent one and the groove is the one that knows, is there anything in the sound that would make the groove sound like *hindsight* rather than *momentum*, for instance its being limited and loud (-10 dBFS, crest falling 17 to 10 dB) where a 1963 sound would be thin and dry? In other words: is the anachronism of the sound itself, its modern loudness against the narrator's band-limited archive timbre, audible evidence of a later voice commenting on an earlier one, or is that simply how every 2013 production sounds beside an archive sample?

*One-line summary of my revised reading: a voice from before the race recites the numbers of the people the film already mourns, and then the film is left, for the last two and a half minutes, to carry what that voice could not know.*
