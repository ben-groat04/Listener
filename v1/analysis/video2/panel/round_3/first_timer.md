# First-timer: Round 3 (final), video2 "Elfstedentocht Part 2"

I read all six round 2 statements and re-opened f_0269 (4:28) and f_0262 (4:21). Nobody has heard the audio; where I speak of sound I rely on the music critic's measurements. Timestamps m:ss on our file.

## Concession first: the "exhausted skater" at 4:28 is not in the frame

In round 2 I leaned on the dossier's "official holds up an exhausted red-hatted skater" to say that one image of cost tilts the piece towards elegy. The sceptic said this was not in the frames. I have now opened f_0269 myself. I see a skater in a dark jumper at the left with a red item at his shoulder, standing or walking on skates; two men in dark coats and one in a peaked cap stand a little way to his right, not touching him; a row of about a dozen onlookers lines the right edge, with a tent and a building behind. It reads as a control or rest point. Nothing shows him held up or spent. I withdraw the claim, and I withdraw my sentence that the control point is "the only moment the cost is plain". I had repeated a note I had not checked. That removes my one piece of on-screen evidence of suffering, which strengthens my round 2 conclusion rather than weakening it: the sadness is not in the pictures.

## Answers to what was put to me

**Sceptic: a first-timer who has read the dossier is no longer a first-timer.** Fair, and I accept it. I read the dossier before watching the frames, so my "felt" reactions are contaminated by its priorities (the 1:28 drop, the stare, the thaw). What I can still offer is narrower: which frames look ordinary to a person with no context. f_0262 (4:21) looks like a nice day out; f_0269 looks like people standing about in coats. Neither looks like an ordeal. That observation survives the contamination because it is about what is in the frame, not about what I felt.

**Symbolist: "no one is followed; I wanted a person to attach to", and the ice lane as protagonist.** I like it and the dossier motif table supports the recurrence of the lane (0:48, 2:49, 3:00, 3:25, 4:36). But a viewer does not get attached to a road. What I actually attached to was the sunglasses skater at 4:21, who skates straight at us and is gone. That is one stranger, not a protagonist. I would say the film gives you no one to follow and that is what makes it feel like a record rather than a story.

**Music critic: could the last hit be a "residual reflex" rather than a door?** I accept "last heartbeat or residual reflex" as an equal image. I said "door" because it arrived just ahead of the fade; the critic's data (full level, mono, unpitched, 1.5 s after the groove stopped) is better evidence than my viewer's feel. I no longer insist on the door.

**Music critic, on pressure from a filter alone.** Taking the point that the drums stay flat from 1:28.6 and the build is one layer opening, my "pressure" in 3:09 to 4:10 was partly the film critic's quantified thing: cuts about 40% faster, a visual density rising while the pulse stays level. That fits what I experienced and I am happy to credit the picture for it.

## My own round 2 questions, as answered by others

I asked the insider if the 4:21 footage could be race-day footage. I did not find a direct answer in the insider's round 2 text, and I will not pretend to one. Checking the frame again: bright sun, sunglasses, a farmhouse, a woman in a red scarf; the music critic and sceptic say nothing that contradicts a leisure reading. So I keep it as an open question, and I mark my own view as speculation: some of the plateau footage may be ordinary skating on the day or on neighbouring days, not the ordeal the 1963 numbers imply. If so, the "inside the pack" description is only half right, and the hindsight reading gets stronger, not weaker.

On whether the elegy survives without the last 25 seconds: the music critic's answer is that nothing in the final hit is celebratory, tonal or tailed. I take that as "not a plain dance-track outro", but it does not prove grief. My honest answer is that without the thaw (f_0300, broken floes, no person) I would have called it a pleasant hypnotic skating film, as I said. The ending is carrying a lot.

## Where I still disagree

- **The sceptic's "celebration is as good a reading".** Half agreed, as in round 2. The footage and the narration are forward-looking and warm. But a viewer cannot be wrong about being left alone by the last shot; that is what I felt and what a context-free English viewer would, I think, feel. I will not call it grief.
- **The symbolist's "the dead look back" at 4:10.** I stay withdrawn on this. The man is skating at the camera; the loudness around him gives the face its weight.
- **The film critic's "plateau is the heart".** I still say the breaths are the heart: the wide canal at 3:46 and the golden skater at 4:11 to 4:14. The plateau is what makes them land.

## Settled reading in five sentences

1. A confident period voice gives the date and the numbers of a race that has not yet happened for the speaker, and then leaves the film at 2:36.
2. The picture after that is mostly ordinary, even cheerful, winter footage (f_0262, f_0269); the unease is made by the pairing with a minor-key, loud, unvarying pulse and by the slow brightening and tightening that the data show.
3. Picture and music meet at a few joints (0:28 clearly, 2:36.6 probably, others are weak against chance), so the feeling comes from shared shape, not from cutting on the beat.
4. The piece moves a viewer who brings, or is given, a sense of looking back; without that it is a hypnotic skating film, and the ending does most of the telling.
5. What it leaves is not grief or triumph but being left alone with a place after the people have gone.

## The single moment

The last shot, f_0300 at about 4:59: broken floes and rose-brown open water, no person, with the groove stopped at about 5:01 and one unpitched hit at 5:02 before black. It is the one place where picture, silence and the hindsight idea all say the same thing at once.
