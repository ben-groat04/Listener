# Music critic / producer: Round 3 on video2 ("Elfstedentocht Part 2")

Basis: all six round 2 statements, my own round 2, and two quick measurements I ran this round on `stems/other.mp3` and `stems/vocals.mp3` (raw FFT over 6 s windows and 0.05 s envelopes, so rough). Nobody has heard the audio; "sounds like" means "the numbers imply". Timestamps m:ss. Confidence H/M/L.

## 1. Answers to the questions put to me

**Film critic (and my own round 2 claim): does the brightness ramp start before the picture tightens at 3:09?** I ran the check, and it overturns my "picture leads, music follows" claim. The 4 to 8 kHz energy of the "other" stem, in 6 s windows, reads about 30 dB at 2:42, 32 at 2:48, 35 at 2:54, 37 at 3:00 to 3:06, 39 at 3:12, 40 at 3:18, 45 at 3:24, 47 at 3:30, 52 at 3:36, 53 at 3:42, 59 at 3:48, then 60 to 63 flat or creeping to 4:30 and a fall at 4:36 (51). So the ramp begins by about 2:45, roughly 25 s before the picture tightens, is gentle until about 3:20 and steepest from 3:20 to 3:50 (about +14 dB). The music started first; the picture's tightening at 3:09 comes inside an existing rise. That fits the film critic's own words ("tightens exactly where the pad brightens") but it reverses my lead-lag story: the picture did not lead. What survives is the milder statement that the cut rate quickens during a ramp already under way (M, since this is one stem, coarse windows, and a mp3 separation). The film critic's "editor followed the music" is the better-supported reading, with no beat reference, as they said. I withdraw "picture leads at each turning point".

**Sceptic: do the fragments repeat identically bar to bar, and is it speech or a vocal-style patch?** I looked at the vocal stem's envelope in 2 s bars from 3:28.8 (my bar grid assumed from the 120 BPM reading, so offsets are approximate). They do not repeat identically. Envelope correlations between neighbouring bars run mostly 0.6 to 0.9 but with several bars near 0 or negative, lengths vary (a burst of 0.2 to 0.5 s at the bar start in some bars, 1 to 2 s in others, a long one at 4:12.8), and the pattern changes at 4:00 to 4:12 and again at 4:24 to 4:30. So it is not one looped sample (against the strongest form of "producer's object"). But two cautions. First, a recurring heavy block at each bar's last 0.3 s in many bars (3:36 to 4:08) looks like kick or bass bleed, not voice, which is why I call this stem leaky. Second, "differs" does not prove speech: a played or sequenced line that varies also differs. The only speech-like evidence I have is band-limit and lengthening phrases, both circumstantial (L-M). So my position: not a single repeated loop, source undecided, and the sceptic's point stands that bar-locked material is the producer's even if it began as speech. I keep "witness becomes texture" only as a reading of what the listener may feel, flagged optional, not as a finding. Concession: partly to the sceptic.

**First-timer: how much of the elegy is the last 25 s, and does the piece survive cutting them off?** Cut at 4:36 and you have a loud, minor, four-on-the-floor trance-adjacent track with a spoken first half; it would read as determined, grave and rather cold. The last 25 s add three things: the bass gate (4:48.55), the stop (5:00.8) and the late full-level hit (5:02.3). Subtraction as an ending is the standard way to close a loop-based track, as the sceptic says, so a DJ would hear a plain outro in the first two. Only the hit is unusual. So yes, it survives; and honestly most of "elegy" is minor mode plus the picture plus the ending, as I conceded. What the ending does is withhold a cadence, which the drop and the plateau had already promised not to give.

**Insider: narration from the same film, and does 1963 change what the unmaking means?** The insider's probability argument (amateur colour cine film of the period normally silent) is persuasive and I accept it: the pairing is probably PSB's or an archive's. That strengthens my 2:36.1 to 2:36.8 point as a producer's choice (an edit of the voice to the loop, not a reel's own rhythm), and removes any claim that the commentary "belongs" to the pictures. On 1963, the sound alone cannot know the outcome; the piece cannot be "about" hindsight unless the listener supplies the date. The date is given (0:41.7), so a listener who knows the race supplies it. I leave it there.

**Symbolist: is the groove's modern loudness audible evidence of later voice commenting?** Partly. The contrast is real and measured: voice band-limited at 4 to 5 kHz and free-timed; mix near -10 dBFS and crest falling about 17 to 10 dB; the groove is modern in loudness and sound. But I cannot separate "a later voice commenting" from "how any 2013 production sounds beside archive audio", and I think the second is the default. The commentary reading needs an extra clue, and the only candidate is the hand-over at 2:36.1 to 2:36.8 and the lack of return. So: anachronism is audible; commentary is optional (M-L).

## 2. Agreements and pushbacks

**Agree with the sceptic** that the shared data pipeline makes "unanimous" suspicious, and that of the sync joints only 0:28 and 2:36.6 are tight. My own chance arithmetic (about 6 % for both ends of loop B) was too generous: the sceptic's version for two of five joints is about 15 %. I now rate the picture-to-music care as L-M outside 0:28.

**Agree with the film critic** that the drop is held and answered by a content change (1:30.3, the crowd), and that the editor's long shot over the drop and the long shot after the stare burst form a pattern. I still cannot say whether that is design.

**Push back on the symbolist** over "effort that never arrives". The harmony does not arrive (no cadence, pedal return every 12 s), but the loud, bright plateau also never changes key or pitch, so it arrives at intensity without a destination. That is not the same as failing to arrive; "arrives and stays" is the safer description.

## 3. Developing the thread: what the ramp ordering means

With the corrected lead (music ramp from about 2:45, picture tightening at 3:09, steepest brightening 3:20 to 3:50), the plateau looks like three layers escalating at slightly different times on a pulse that does not move: brightness first and slowly, cut rate next, fragment length and top-band steepness last. That is a standard producer's staging by overlapping ramps, and it is why no single moment announces the build. It also changes my "fader move" complaint: a single-layer filter opening is cheap, but here it is staged over 65 s and meets a picture that tightens halfway. Whether the picture was fitted to it or the other way round cannot be told, and I will stop trying to tell it.

## 4. Settled reading (5 sentences)

1. This is a five-minute, loop-built piece in B Aeolian at 120 BPM whose real craft is subtraction: a hushed pad, a dry Dutch archive voice over a walking pulse, a drop at 1:28.6 whose tension is carried by speech rather than a riser, and an ending built by taking things away.
2. The build is a slow brightening of one layer over an unmoving groove, so intensity rises without an announced moment, and the only change of kind is the shift from free-timed narration to bar-locked, source-undecided fragments.
3. The music is determined and grave in colour, not obviously exhilarating or mournful; "elegy" is what the minor mode, the graveyard and grey-blue pictures, and the ending add.
4. The relationship of music to picture is loose: one tight joint at 0:28, one near 2:36.6, and a plateau where music and picture escalate together without evident locking.
5. The one decision that most clearly shows a producer's hand is the full-level, mono kick-and-bass hit at 5:02.3, 1.5 s after the stop, which is neither salute nor stumble.

**The moment that best captures the piece:** 2:36.1 to 2:36.8, where the last factual sentence (575 skaters, 9,500 entrants, the nationalities) ends and, 0.7 s later, the next loop begins and the voice never returns as speech: a list of figures followed by something that is not a list.
