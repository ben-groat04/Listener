# The Symbolist (poet and symbolist), Round 3 on video2, "Elfstedentocht Part 2"

Basis: all six round 2 statements, my own round 2, and frames I re-opened: f_0269 (4:28), f_0262 (4:21), f_0302 (5:01). Nobody has heard the audio; sound claims rest on the music reading and the measurements. Obs = seen or measured; Int = interpretation. Times m:ss.

## 1. Answers to the questions put to me

**Insider (Q1, round 2 was to the sceptic; but the Insider also asked me about anonymity).** The Insider turns my "anonymity as a form of love" round: the anonymity is the film's, while Dutch memory is *named* ("my grandfather gave up at Dokkum"). I accept this and it improves my reading. The film leaves a blank; each Dutch viewer writes a name into it. That is a better account of why the piece is a *vessel* than my "the winter is the protagonist". I keep one point: for a viewer without a name to write, the blank is simply a blank, and the piece then offers only the ice and the pulse.

**First-timer (Q1, the footage at 4:21).** I opened f_0262. Obs: a man in dark glasses and a dark jumper skating at the lens, a woman in a red scarf behind, blue ice, a farmhouse and snow fields. It is a bright, ordinary skating day, as the First-timer says. This is the best discovery of round 2 and I build on it in section 2. The Insider's question to the dossier stands (is this race day?); nothing on screen answers it.

**Music critic (Q2, hindsight in the sound: loudness and "modern" sound against the thin voice).** The Music critic answers me partly: there is no detuning, pitch-drop or tempo change under the confident lines, so the sound does not *comment*. I accept that. The remaining audible-evidence candidate is the contrast of a band-limited dry voice against a limited, loud machine, and the Music critic is right that this is how any 2013 production sits beside an archive sample. So "anachronism of the sound" is not evidence of a commenting voice. What I keep is the structural fact (a before-the-race voice framed by music made fifty years on), which does not depend on sound design.

**Sceptic (Q3, round 2 follow-up: would the fragments repeat bar to bar?).** That was put to the Music critic, but it bears on my metaphor. I have already withdrawn "the voice ends up inside the machine" as a sound claim. I now go further: I will not use "voice" for the 3:28.6 layer in my settled reading at all.

## 2. Where I changed my mind, and why

**2.1 "Elegy" was the wrong word, or at least the wrong order of words.** The Sceptic's best argument, and the First-timer's f_0262 discovery, converge: the pictures alone are mostly cheerful or neutral (sunglasses, red scarves, a village churchyard that is merely a village churchyard). The Music critic concedes the same of the sound: grave in colour, determined in gesture, not mournful in any measured way. So neither channel on its own mourns. What mourns is the **gap between channels and what the viewer knows**: a voice that looks forward, pictures that look ordinary, music that is minor and keeps going, and an outcome (127 finishers, `elfstedentocht_1963.md`, per the Insider) that is never named. I now call the piece not an elegy but an **unspoken afterword**: it is built to hold a sentence it never says. This is more modest and more accurate than "elegy", and it is also testable against the Sceptic's own formulation ("announcement, endurance, withdrawal"), which I accept as the *form*. The disagreement that remains is only whether "withdrawal" is felt as loss. For those who carry 1963, yes (Insider); for others it is a quiet leaving. I say both are honest.

**2.2 The 4:28 "held up skater" is not in the frame.** I did not use it in round 2, but three panellists did, and I checked. f_0269 (4:28): a skater in a red hat and dark clothes at left on skates, two officials in peaked caps standing apart, a row of onlookers at right along a glossy dark lane. No hand on him; he is upright. The Sceptic is right. The control-point reading stands; "exhaustion shown" does not. This matters for my own reading: there is **no image of cost** in the film that I can defend. The cost is wholly in what is withheld. That strengthens, not weakens, the "afterword" reading.

**2.3 The thaw, third pass.** I opened f_0302 (5:01). Obs: a high-angle or aerial view of broken pale slabs on dark rose-brown water, edges visible, no people, no horizon. This is better evidence of broken ice than the ducks (f_0293). It also looks unlike every other shot in the film (no horizon, no human scale, high viewpoint), so the Film critic's and Insider's caution stands that it may be from another reel and another year. What I keep: the film *chooses* to end on ice that is no longer a floor. What I do not claim: that it is spring, or that it is 1963.

**2.4 The stare (4:10).** I concede fully to the Film critic's shot list: 1.32 s inside a burst of six shots of 0.7 to 1.6 s. It was not held, and a man near a hand-held camera looking at it is the default. I keep only this: the First-timer's and Insider's phrase, "half found, half made by position", is right, and what the edit made is the *context* (minutes of backs and bibs, then one face). A found glance that works as a face because of its neighbours is itself a small image of the whole piece: the record is mostly backs, and the presence of a person is an accident the record could not plan.

**2.5 The Insider's bibs point.** The Sceptic and Insider both note all legible bib numbers are below 575. Obs: I cannot add to the numbers. It affects my round 2 table in one way: my remark that "the narrator names 575 racers first, but the camera follows the 9,500" is *not safe*. Withdrawn. If the plateau shows the racers, the piece's "anonymity" is the anonymity of the field, not of the mass; I find that no less moving (a numbered body in a pack is anonymous by design) but it is a different claim from "ordinary people".

## 3. Agreements and disagreements

- **Agree with the Sceptic** that the "pride then withdrawal" two-act form fits the signal without outside knowledge, and that "elegy throughout" does not. This is the best compact statement of the structure and I adopt it. Where I differ: I think the act break is not the withdrawal at 4:36.8 but the **end of speech at 2:36.8**. Pride is voiced; everything after is the voice's absence. The pulse continues, which is the point.
- **Agree with the Music critic** that the last hit (5:02.3) is neither stumble nor salute: full-level, mono, unpitched, after the stop. I accept "the machine's last reflex" as equal to my "one more footfall". I would now caution myself, as the Sceptic does, that it is a 0.1 s event and that nothing in the signal decides what it means. As the single moment, I rank it second (below), because it is where meaning is least fixed.
- **Agree with the Music critic's lead-lag discovery** (picture escalates at 3:09 with cuts at about 2 s median, the 4 to 8 kHz layer brightens from about 3:26; the picture turns down at 4:36.0, the music sags at 4:36.8). If it holds, it is the best new fact of the round, and for the symbolist it is rich: the eye goes first and the ear follows, as if the sound were remembering what the picture had just seen. I mark this Int and note the Music critic's own caution that the 17 s offset is unmeasured by him; the Film critic's shot table (median 3.2 s to 2.0 s) supports only the picture side.
- **Disagree with the Sceptic's "a first-timer who read the dossier is no longer a first-timer".** Correct as a point of method, but it also applies to the whole panel: all of us read the same dossier. The agreement on "withdrawal" is therefore weak evidence. I accept that discount on my own reading. What survives is what survives the discount: structural facts (voice ends at 2:36; subtraction at 4:36 to 5:02; final stop and one hit), not the feelings.
- **Disagree mildly with the Film critic's "a held breath followed by a human cut" at 1:28.6** as design. The Sceptic computes the shot as about 8.2 s against a 3.6 s mean, and the Film critic shows the editor likes long holds. I accept "unusual in length, intent unproven". The Music critic's added observation is the one that persuades me: the drop is carried by *the sentence ending*, not a riser, and the cut to the first crowd follows 1.7 s later. The pairing produces the effect; I say no more.
- **Answering the Insider's own question to the Film critic**, as it matters to me: if cuts follow the narration's phrase ends rather than the music (the Insider's alternative), the narration, not the music, is the picture's clock. Two data in the dossier suggest the narration and music meet at 1:28.3 and 2:36.1 only, so this cannot be tested further from here.

## 4. The thread I develop: what the piece will not say

Putting round 2 together, the most interesting finding is a negative one. Nobody has found in the piece an image of a person suffering (4:28 fails), a word of suffering (Sceptic), a tune of grief (Music critic), a finish or a winner (Insider, First-timer), or a place named after the opening (the narration names only the event and, in the Insider's reading, the province). Everything that would let a viewer *say* what happened has been taken out, and the one outcome the viewer may know (127 of 9,862) is the thing not stated. The piece is built from absences in a very exact order: first the narrator's certainty is given (0:41 to 2:36), then it is taken away (2:36.8), then the layers (4:36.8 to 5:00.8), then the people (4:52 on, ducks, then floes with no one), then the sound (5:00.8), then one last reflex (5:02.3), then the picture.

That is a reading about **subtraction as the only honest form for a loss one cannot show**. It does not require anyone's intent: a loop-based track ends by removing layers, and an archive montage ends on landscape. But the pairing of the two is a coincidence of conventions that happens to produce the shape of a thing being withdrawn from the viewer's hands, and I find it the most defensible thing to say that the piece is *about* (in the sense of "what it makes available") rather than *because of*.

A concession to the Sceptic's method: I am aware that "about" is a word I supply. Test of my reading: if someone cut the first 4:36 from this film and showed only 4:36 to 5:06, would the loss still be felt? I think much less so; the subtraction needs something to subtract from. This predicts the First-timer's own remark ("if I had stopped at 4:30 I would have had a pleasant, hypnotic skating film"): the ending works by what precedes it. It is the one prediction of mine a fresh viewer could actually confirm or deny.

## 5. Settled reading (five sentences)

1. The piece is a pre-race voice, a post-race music and a silent middle: a Dutch province announces its own great day with confident pride until 2:36, after which nobody speaks again, and the film is left carrying what the voice could not know.
2. Its form is announcement, endurance, withdrawal, and its engine is subtraction rather than arrival: no cadence, no finish, no winner, no image of cost, only a pulse that holds while layers, then people, then sound are taken away.
3. Taken separately, the pictures are mostly ordinary (a sunlit skater in sunglasses, a village churchyard), the words are cheerful and the music is grave in colour but determined in gesture, so the grief, where it exists, lives in the gap between channels and in what the viewer already knows (1963, 127 finishers, the thin ice of 2012), not in any one of them.
4. I hold the symbols lightly: the road to the horizon and the lane are recurrent places (Obs), faces are rare and backs and bibs are the norm (Obs), the ice ends the film as something that is no longer a floor (f_0302), and everything beyond that, including "prayer", "thaw" and "witness", is a reader's gift, not a finding.
5. What it is about, then, is the way a record keeps the preparation and loses the event: the film shows a people at their best moment, leaving, and makes the audience supply the name.

**The single moment that best captures it: 2:36.1 to 2:36.8.** The last fact is a head-count, 575 racers and about 9,500 more, and 0.7 s later the voice that spoke for the province is gone for ever while the pulse carries on, a measured, checkable joint (cut at 2:36.6, within 0.2 s) over faces of men in knee-breeches who do not know what the number will come to. Everything after it is the film being left alone with the figure. (Runner-up, and the one where meaning is least fixed: the single unpitched hit at 5:02.3, after the stop.)
