## 0. Headline

A relentless, swaggering, grinning club-rap party track, about 3:08 long, at about 152 BPM on the beat tracker [SUM] (felt as roughly 76 BPM half-time: bass hits come about every 0.7-1.2 s [w0060, w0080]). Its harmony hardly moves: A is the permanent brightest chroma row [OV], so the feeling is driven by what the layers do (bass in or out, drums dense or sparse, voice chopped or rapped), not by tune or chords. The emotional design is a loop of tension and release: **bass-off "dry" sections where the floor falls away and the voice is exposed, then bass-on "slams" that hit like a drop**. Confidence high on that shape (measured), medium on the emotional reading (my interpretation as a listener).

That is a strong physical cause of the "slam" feeling (high confidence on the numbers; medium that the squeeze is perceived as impact, which is how I would hear it).

## 2. Moment-by-moment journey

**0:00-0:12. Cheeky, bare, anticipating.** A voice arrives at about 0:01 [LVL vocals -37 to -23 dB, w0000] over a quiet looped bed ("other") and one drum crack per bar, spacing about 1.55 s [w0000 onsets]. No bass at all. Transcript: "Aye, man, we got this new dance... watch me do that bird walk" [LYR 0:00-0:07]. Feeling: a chatty, playful announcement; like someone calling the room to attention before the music starts. The one-hit-per-bar gaps create a suspended, held-breath quality: you are waiting for the beat. The mix is 6 dB quieter and wide, so it feels distant and loose. Confidence high on the structure, medium on "playful" (the voice is chatty and the words are an invitation; CLAP is weak evidence).

**0:12-0:20. The drop: relief and jolt.** Bass rises from -69 to -14.6 dB, drums become a full pattern with a burst at about 0:12.2, the mix gets 5 dB louder [w0000, LVL]. Feeling: the floor arrives. A physical "now we're dancing" lift. This is the first and the template instance of the track's main pleasure. High confidence on cause.

**0:20-0:47. Groove-lock, hypnotic and a bit hard-edged.** The mix is at its narrowest (width 0.08-0.15) with bass steady at -14 dB and sparse, clap-like drum hits [w0020]. The voice becomes a steadier, near-monotone half-spoken line around D4 [w0020 lead]; the lyrics are a boast and a call to dance ("we don't out dance no more, we just bird dance at home"; "lean with it, rock") [LYR 0:28-0:39]. Feeling: head-nodding, confident, repetitive, almost trance-like, because nothing harmonic changes and the bass pulses at a slow steady rate. Slightly menacing, because the bass is so heavy and close. Medium confidence; mood tags from CLAP (aggressive/energetic) agree but are weak.

**0:40-0:47. Hook call-and-response.** "I'm a show you how it go, do that bird walk" repeated [LYR 0:39-0:46]; the voice is chopped into 0.2-0.4 s bursts that lock to the drum cracks [w0040]. Feeling: chant, crowd-participation, the catchy peak of the first block.

**0:47-0:58. Sudden vacuum.** Bass collapses by 0:48 (-71 dB), drums are isolated about every 1.4 s, and the width opens to 0.53; "other" turns into wide bursts in time with the hits [w0040, LVL]. Voice keeps chanting "that bird walk, ey". Feeling: a pulled rug; air and space, a little comic and a little tense, the way a DJ cuts the low end to make the crowd wait. This 11 s hold is the longest tension build in the first half, and the re-entry is the payoff. High confidence on measurements, medium on the DJ-like reading.

**0:59-1:10. The slam, chant at low pitch.** Bass returns at 0:59.4 (-17.9 dB to -14), drums dense (about 63 onsets in 20 s), and the voice chants low, A2-C3, narrow-range [w0060 lead notes]. Feeling: the biggest "yes" moment so far; the low chant is both comic and heavy.

**1:10-1:56. The verse: boastful, punchy, steady-state euphoria.** Rapped verse starts about 1:10.4 ("I step up in the pub in my all white ones... I always get cake like it's my birthday") [LYR]. Loudness is the highest and flattest of the track (-12.7 to -13 dB, width as low as 0.06 around 1:20-1:30) [LVL]. Inside it there are micro-events that keep it alive: the "other" layer drops out for about 10 s from 1:23 and returns around 1:33 with a rising diagonal sweep, a riser, as the drums thicken [w0080]; at 1:41-1:44 the bass blinks out for 3 s and the drums reach their loudest (-16 dB) [w0100]. Feeling: cocky, cheerful aggression; the verse "bounces" with brief gasps (the bass blink at 1:41 is a mini-drop that makes the return at 1:46-1:47 feel like a kick). The long loudness plateau is satisfying but could feel relentless; the small dropouts relieve that. Medium confidence on the mood (lyrics plus stable loud energetic mix), high on the events.

**1:52-1:59. Wind-down into the cut.** Drums thin from 1:52 (-21 to -28 dB), "so icy, so nice, so whitey, whitey" in the transcript [LYR 1:51-], a final bass hit at 1:58 then everything but voice and "other" drops at 1:59 [w0100]. Feeling: a deliberate set-up, the music holds its breath again.

**2:00-2:10. Second vacuum, the bare-and-wide echo of the intro.** Bass absent (-71 dB), drums one hit per bar at 2:01.3, 2:02.9, 2:04.5... (spacing 1.57 s = 4 beats) [w0120], mix -18.5 dB, width 0.54. This is nearly identical in layout to 0:00-0:12, so it feels like a "reset" or a callback; the ear recognises the pattern. Feeling: breathing space after the long verse; anticipation again, with a slightly reflective, nearly cool-down quality. High confidence on layout, medium on the "reset" feeling.

**2:11-2:23. Return of the groove.** Bass back at A1 (about 55 Hz, YIN, steady) [w0120], drums thicken to a kick/snare/hat pattern, level to about -18 dB. Feeling: familiar, settling, comfortable; like the first drop, a rejoining.

**2:24-2:35. The breakdown: voice alone with ghosts of the band.** Bass silent from 2:24 and drums by 2:24 [LVL, w0140]; the voice is the loudest stem, chanting "that bird walk" in short syllable bursts; "other" is chopped; width 0.44-0.46, mix quieter (-16 dB). From about 2:32.5 a diagonal sweep climbs or falls across 0.5-6 kHz over three seconds [w0140], leading into the 2:35.7 re-entry. Feeling: the most "held" part of the song, the crowd-chant moment; the sweep is a classic tension riser, so the 2:35.7 hit lands with maximum release. High confidence on the dropout and the sweep, medium on "chant" (whisper gives "burr-wop" vs "bird walk" for the same phrase, so the exact word is uncertain; the repeated hook shape is not).

**2:36-2:48. Final push.** Bass and drums back (bass -13 to -16 dB), voice chopped into 0.2-0.4 s bursts, "other" jumps from about -35 to -23.5 dB at 2:47 [w0160]. Feeling: the loud, full, swaggering finale; the heaviest, narrowest part, a victory lap.

**2:48-3:00. Voice leaves, band stays; drums become heavier.** Voice falls to -58 dB at 2:48 and silent 2:49-2:55; drums brighten in the low end with a kick-like line near 100 Hz and reach about -16 dB (10 dB louder than before) [w0160]. For 7-8 s you hear only rhythm section and "other". At 2:56.5-2:59 a falling vocal glide (about 1.5 octaves, C5 to B3 per the lead tracker) returns [w0160]. Feeling: a stripped, hard, almost menacing outro; the lack of voice makes it feel like the track is walking away on the beat, the glide like a "wheeee" or tape slide, a playful punctuation. Medium confidence (the pitch fall is clear, what it is is not).

**3:00-3:08. Fade-out.** Bass and drums continue 2-3 s, then all layers sink about 30 dB in order: "other" first, then bass, then drums [w0180]. Feeling: not a conclusion but a withdrawal, the volume knob turning; it ends with the party still going somewhere else. Final chord labels lean F#m rather than A [w0180], which is low-confidence evidence for a slightly unresolved end. The measured nature of the fade is high confidence; the "unresolved" tag is low.

## 3. What a listener keeps (the feeling in five sentences)

1. The track's main pleasure is the bass re-entry (0:12, 0:59, 2:11, 2:35), which is both a loudness jump and a width collapse. Four times the same trick, each preceded by a drier and wider section that makes the listener wait.
2. The mood is playful bravado: chanted nonsense hook plus boasting verse, with gaps, shouts ("aye"), and a falling glide that gives it a cartoonish wink. The measurements cannot say it is humorous; the chant and the sweeps suggest a lightness under the hard bass (medium).
3. Harmony supplies no emotion beyond a static A (minor/major ambiguous: A minor 0.50 vs A major 0.47 [SUM]), so there is no sadness or joy from chords; the slightly "dark but not sad" colour comes from the bass register (A1 at 55 Hz) and the minor-ish F and F#m flickers. Confidence medium.
4. It is tiring as well as exciting: 12.7-13 dB mix loudness held for most of 1:00-1:58 with a width as narrow as 0.06, and the tempo tracker's wandering values [OV] show the groove is ambiguous between half and double time. A listener may feel pummelled; the dropouts are the only relief.
5. Closing: no resolution, a fade, a feeling of the party going on.

## 4. Where I am unsure or limited

- I cannot confirm instruments, the actual words in the hook ("bird walk" vs "burr-wop"), or whether the dropouts are played or edited. The 12-13 dB loudness plateau might partly be a compression artefact of the file, not a creative choice.
- The mood words (cheeky, swaggering) come from my reading of vocal chopped rhythm, lyrics transcribed by Whisper (which hallucinated "whitey" for about 28 s at 1:51-2:19, so treat that stretch as untrustworthy) and loudness behaviour; CLAP mood tags are noisy.
- Stem separation leaks voice into "other", and some of what I attribute to "other" (e.g. the 0:47 wide bursts) may be voice or drum bleed.
- I did not run new Python beyond recomputing 10 s means and bass-off spans from [LVL]; I did not independently verify the 1:41 dip or the 2:32.5 sweep against the audio, only through the window notes and the described window panels.
