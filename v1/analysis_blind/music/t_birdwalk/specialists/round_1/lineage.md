# t_birdwalk: LINEAGE specialist, round 1 (blind, measurements only)

Evidence keys: WNN = music_views/window_00NNs.png (W80 = 1:20-1:40 etc.); OV = audio_features/overview.png; LV = per_second_levels.json; CH = chords.json; LN = lead_notes.json; CLAP = clap_tags.json (weak); SUM = audio_features/summary.json; LYR = lyrics/whisper_medium_*; win_*.md = the ten window notes (all read). My own run: v1/scripts/agents/lineage_bass_hats.py (pyin on bass.wav and onsets on drums.wav, 1:02-1:20).

## Verdict in one paragraph
On sound alone this is a **Southern US hip-hop track in the crunk / snap / early-trap lineage (mid-2000s to early 2010s), most likely a dance-craze novelty record** ("do that bird walk"). The ingredients are: a close, dry, shouted-chanted male rap on a near-single pitch; a sub-bass note tuned to A1 (about 55 Hz) that hits as short, pulsed 808-type notes; a half-time, very sparse programmed clap/snare pattern; a one-chord (A) static harmony with no progression; and "drop / breakdown / drop" arrangement logic borrowed from club and EDM-era rap. Confidence: **high** for "hip-hop, programmed, Southern-club-leaning rap delivery"; **medium** for the crunk/snap/trap sub-lineage and the "dance-craze" function; **low** for any dating, region or named artist.

## 1. Evidence, by trait

**a) Rap delivery, not song.** Vocal stem is continuous and loudest (LV vocals -14 to -25 dB; W80 vocals panel shows dense syllable bars with harmonic ladders, about 4-6 syllables per second). The pitch tracker (LN) shows near-monotone recitation around D4 from 0:25 (win_0020: about 90 per cent within a semitone of D4), chromatic speech-pitch elsewhere, and a low "chesty" register (G#2-C#3) in intros. No sung tune anywhere; lead is a rhythmic hook ("bird walk") plus ad-libs ("aye", "yea", "man": LYR mix, 0:00-0:07). The lyrics (LYR mix 0:28-0:39: "we just bird dance at home", "the club get crunk", "lean with it rock") name the club and dance, which fits the genre; the transcript is mix-only and partly garbled, so I use it as support, not proof. High confidence for rap; medium for "chant/shout" flavour. CLAP voice labels ("no vocals" 0.95 song average) are plainly wrong and discarded.

**b) The low end is the genre marker.** The bass stem is not a played bass line: it is short, sharp-attack, quick-decay notes (W80 bass panel: vertical stabs with a bright click then a low tail), pitched near A1. My pyin run on 1:02-1:20: median 55.3 Hz, 88 per cent of frames within a semitone of 55 Hz; win_0120 (YIN) also reads a steady 55.1 Hz at 2:12-2:19. The sub-60 Hz share of mix energy jumps from 1.4 per cent to 35 per cent at the first drop (win_0000 citing the sections file); at 2:47-2:56 about 90 per cent of mix energy is below 150 Hz (win_0160). This "one tuned sub note per hit, with a click, in the centre" is the signature of a **Roland TR-808-style kick/bass** (or a software imitation) as used in Southern rap. Identity of the exact sound cannot be proved from a spectrogram; a synthetic 808-like bass is my best reading. Medium-high confidence. Note on my own check: a stable f0 only says the tuning, not a glide; I did not measure pitch bends on the notes.

**c) Sparse, half-time programmed percussion.** Drum stem is single broadband cracks (about 100 Hz to 4-8 kHz) with long silences between them: about one hit per 1.4-1.6 s in the sparse sections (win_0000 onsets 0.81, 2.28, 3.81 ... ; win_0120 onsets every 1.5-1.6 s), and very low drum levels (-30 to -68 dB) between. In the dense stretches (1:40-1:52: 78 onsets in 20 s, win_0100) there is a busier pattern and, in my 1:02-1:20 test, a median inter-onset interval of 0.24 s with some intervals as short as 0.05 s, which is compatible with fast hat/roll-type bursts and fills (win_0000 17.2-17.7 s; win_0120 2:17-2:19). Whether these are claps, snares or snaps cannot be separated; "snap" is ruled neither in nor out. The tempo tracker says 152 BPM (SUM) with local readings 144-163 (OV bottom panel), but the bass/clap spacing (0.7-1.5 s) is half or quarter of that, so the felt pulse is likely **a half-time groove near 76 BPM** with fast subdivisions on top: the standard rap/trap relation (the "152" is the double-time ruler, "76" the head-nod). Medium confidence on which one a human taps; high that the pattern is machine-quantised (metronomic beat grid 0.35-0.40 s, win_0160) rather than a played kit.

**d) One chord, one pedal.** Chroma (OV third panel; every W image) is one saturated A row for 3:08 with brief G, F, D, F#m flickers. CH labels (Am 40 per cent, A 20 per cent, F#m 10 per cent, N 48 per cent of time, summary.json in music_views) are chroma-template noise; the key estimate A minor 0.50 vs A major 0.47 (SUM) is an essentially tied third. Rap production of the crunk/trap family is characteristically built on a drone or a two-note minor riff rather than chord changes, so the static harmony is a genre cue, not a gap. The brief "F" and "G" and the D4 recitation vs A tonic (a minor seventh/tenth over the tonic) give a blues-adjacent, slightly sour colour (win_0020), but I cannot hear a blues scale. Medium confidence.

**e) A hooky mid-range "other" layer.** Stab-like, narrow bars between 500 Hz and 2 kHz in 'other' (win_0020; W80 other panel; plucky pitched bursts, bright resonance near 800-1000 Hz). It resembles a **sampled or synthesised plucked/stab riff** in the production style of crunk and ringtone-era rap, but demucs 'other' also receives voice bleed (win_0000, win_0020 both flag this), and CLAP's instrument labels flip between "banjo", "brass", "synthesizer" (CLAP song average: drum machine 0.41, sampled voice 0.19, banjo 0.18, synthesizer 0.12). I treat "banjo" as a spurious or at best a timbral resemblance (pluck, not a lineage claim). Low confidence on instrument identity.

**f) Arrangement by subtraction and drop.** The structure is a series of stripped-back passages with bass absent and voice exposed, ended by a hard bass re-entry: bass off 0:00-0:12, 0:47-0:59, 1:23-1:33 (stripped), 1:41-1:46, 2:00-2:11, 2:24-2:35; returns marked by a falling or rising vocal sweep (0:09-0:12, 2:08-2:11, 2:32-2:36, 2:56-2:59; W0, W120, W140, W160). OV loudness panel shows the same ABAB-like "thin, then 5-7 dB louder" alternation (sections B, C, A, C, E, C, A, D, A, D). Mix level swaps between -18 to -20 dBFS (thin) and -11 to -13 dBFS (full) (LV); the stereo width also flips from 0.4-0.7 (thin, 'other' wide) to 0.05-0.3 (full, bass/kick centred). That "voice solo, riser, drop" template is a feature of club rap after about 2005 (crunk, then the EDM-hybrid and trap era). It is also the way DJs and dance-craze records are built to cue a dance move. Medium-high confidence in the device, medium in the dating inference.

**g) Production era and finish.** Bright to a hard ceiling that varies by window (about 6-7 kHz in W20, up to 10-16 kHz in W100: the roll-off is inconsistent, so I take it to be a lossy encode or a source that varies, not one fixed "lo-fi" choice). The mix is very loud and flat in the full sections (-11 to -14 dBFS RMS, little dynamic variation; win_0100), and very close and dry (no long reverb in any window). Compressed, centred, bass-heavy, sparse: consistent with a digital (DAW) rap production of the 2000s onwards, not with a live-band or sampled-break-based record. CLAP "vinyl crackle" (0.46 average) and "ice skating", "bells" labels are not credible as evidence. "Hip hop" is the top genre in CLAP (0.58, song average) and "playful" the top mood (0.60); "country" 0.38 is most likely the banjo/pluck pull and is not backed by any other measure (no twang, no steel, no 4/4 shuffle, no sung vocal). The window-level CLAP "hip hop" is consistent with, but adds almost nothing to, my own reading.

## 2. What it resembles, what it does not
- **Resembles (medium):** Atlanta/Southern crunk and "snap" era party records and dance-craze singles; the chanted, call-out vocal and the dance-name hook are the strongest resemblance. The trap-style 808 plus sparse half-time claps with hat bursts is the second. Also resembles YouTube/meme-dance novelty rap in its drop-and-hold template.
- **Does not resemble (medium-high):** boom bap (no sampled break, no swung dusty drums, no pitched piano/horn loops), R&B or singing-led hip hop, G-funk (no synth lead melody or live-bass line), drill (no sliding 808 glides measured; no dark triplet hat pattern shown), EDM proper (no four-on-the-floor; kick is not on every beat), reggaeton (no dembow; claps are not on the 3+3+2 grid, though win_0020 floated a 3+3+2 reading at low confidence), Appalachian/country (despite CLAP).
- **Open:** whether the 'other' layer is a sample from an older record. The measurements cannot say.

## 3. Mood in lineage terms
Playful, cocky, call-to-the-floor (CLAP "playful" 0.6 is weak but consistent). The sparse drums plus 55 Hz weight give physical bounce rather than melody-led emotion. The static A tonality with unresolved third gives a cheeky, slightly menacing colour typical of party rap (not sad).

## 4. Limits
I cannot hear timbre, so "808", "clap" and "stab synth" are shape-based inferences. Stem separation leaks voice into 'other' and sub-bass into the vocal stem (win_0160 notes this at 2:50-2:58). Chord labels are weak. Lyrics are from Whisper on the mix and are partly mis-heard. I have not found measurements that fix region, year or artist; any such claim would be invention.

## 5. Questions for fellow specialists
1. **To rhythm:** Is the felt pulse the 76 BPM half-time (claps and bass every 0.7-1.5 s) or the 152 BPM ruler, and do the bass and clap hits fall on a trap-like grid (e.g. hat bursts at 1/8 triplets or 1/16) or a shuffle? A confident answer separates "crunk/trap" from "snap/other" readings.
2. **To sound:** Do the bass note envelopes (attack click, decay length, any measurable pitch glide on the notes) look like a synthesised 808 (tuned sine with a click) or a sampled/played bass? And is the 'other' stab layer a synthesised plucky riff or a sample, i.e. is there any sign of a loop (identical repetition) in it?
3. **To voice:** Is the near-monotone D4 chant (0:25-0:40) and the shouted ad-libs ("aye", "yea") a call-and-response with a second voice (backing shouts), and is the vocal's pitch contour consistent with a single rapper or more than one (e.g. register changes at 0:24, 0:40-0:46 to C5)? A group chant would point to a crunk "crowd" model; a lone voice to a solo novelty rap.
