## 1. Headline

A very loud (RMS -13.5 dBFS overall, peak reaches 1.0 full scale [S1]), band-limited, sub-bass-dominated, dry, near-mono hip-hop-style production. A rapped/chanted voice sits dry and front-centre over a short-note sub-bass (about A1) and a punchy, noisy drum track, with a thin mid-range "other" layer of stabs, risers and filtered sweeps. The form is built by subtracting and returning the sub-bass, and the stereo image breathes with it: the bass is mono, so when it leaves, the width opens. [high on the overall picture; medium on instrument identity]

## 2. Instrumentation, with evidence

**Voice (vocals stem).** Close, dry, harmonically clean (clear harmonic ladders in the vocals panel, W0000, W0040 [high]). 56% of vocal-stem energy lies at 500 Hz to 2 kHz, 27% at 150-500 Hz, only 4% above 2 kHz, centroid about 1.2 kHz [S2, S1]. That is a chesty, mid-forward chant/rap, not a bright sung line. Vocal-stem level is the steadiest thing in the mix, about -17 to -22 dB nearly throughout [LV]. It is the loudest layer for most of the track. Stem crest 19 dB means it has real transients (syllable attacks) rather than being flattened, though the mix as a whole is compressed [S1] (medium).

**Sub-bass.** The key sound-design fact. The bass stem's per-note spectral peak sits at 55-57.5 Hz (A1 = 55 Hz; histogram of about 750 active frames: 280 at 55, 373 at 57.5, minor at 47-50) [S2], and 47% of the whole mix's energy lies below 60 Hz and 24.5% at 60-200 Hz [S1]. The bass-stem centroid is about 112 Hz [S1]. Bass note envelopes [S2] start at about -10 dB, fall steadily by about 1-2 dB per 0.1 s to about -24 dB, then drop to below -50 dB: short decaying notes with a hard gate or cut-off, a repeated pattern of about 1.4 s cycles (about 0.7 s in the denser 1:00 section per WN). A pitched, decaying, gated sub tone is the classic 808-style or synthesised-sub behaviour. The W0040 bass panel shows each bass hit as a vertical onset with a harmonic click at its start (energy up to about 1-2 kHz in the first few ms) over a long low tail at the bottom of the plot [high that it is a sub-bass of this kind; low-medium that it is literally an 808].
Absence: no bass at all 0:00-0:12 (LV bass -70 dB; sub below 60 Hz is 0.1% of the energy in 0-12 s [S1]), out again 0:47-0:59, 1:50-2:10 partly, and in 2:20-2:40 attenuated (-43 to -50 dB) [LV, WN].

**Drums.** 78% of drum-stem energy is below 150 Hz [S2]. In a stem this is the kick and the sub-tail leaking in. Another 12% is at 2-5 kHz (snare/clap crack) and only 2% above 5 kHz [S2]. The W0040 drum panel shows broadband vertical streaks from 0 to about 9 kHz at each hit with little tail (dry, gated), then a sparse hit every about 1.4 s in the breakdown, about the same cycle as the bass. I see no sustained hi-hat wash. The WN windows disagree about hi-hat-like noise (W0020 note vs W0040 note); the band shares say hats are a small component (2% above 5 kHz), though stem separation puts hats partly in "other". CLAP "drum machine" 0.96 at 0:00 and "hip hop" 0.85-0.96 [CLAP] agree weakly. Medium that this is programmed (machine) drums; the exact count-in click at 0:00 on the drum stem (-30 dB, [LV]) looks like a sample start (low).

**"Other" layer.** Band shares: 37% below 150 Hz (leaked sub), 31% 150-500 Hz, 27% 500-2 kHz [S2]. It is mid-range, not shimmering. W0040 other panel shows a repeated plucked/stabbed tone at about 0.8-1 kHz in a regular rhythm at 0:40-0:47 (the horizontal line bursts at 1 kHz), then broad rectangular noise blocks aligned with the drum hits in the breakdown (a gated pad/noise or sample bed). The chroma is nearly a pure A pedal throughout [AF overview, W0040], so this layer carries timbre and rhythm, not harmony. CLAP's banjo/sampled-voice scores [CLAP] are consistent with a plucky, clipped mid sample but cannot identify it (low). I cannot name the instrument.

**Sweeps/risers.** Diagonal streaks in the mix, vocals and other panels at about 0:09-0:12 (before the first drop), 0:20-0:23, 0:45-0:47 (W0040, x 4-7 s, a diagonal falling/rising line across the mix and vocals panel), 1:35-1:38, 1:55-1:57, 2:08-2:11 [WN, W0040 seen]. Each precedes a change of texture, so they work as transitions. Whether these are risers, filter sweeps or a vocal pitch effect is unclear (the sweep is visible even in the vocals stem, which suggests either bleed or an effect applied to the voice); medium-low on the mechanism.

## 3. Timbre, bandwidth and "era"

- **Hard band limit.** Spectrogram panels show a black band above about 9-10 kHz in every panel (W0040, spectrogram.png); 99.9% of energy lies below 5.4 kHz and everything above 10 kHz is about -74 dB or lower relative to total [S1]. This is a source limit (sample-rate-converted, low-bit-rate lossy copy, or a low-pass on a sampled loop), not a creative filter, since it is static across the whole 3 minutes [high that the limit exists; low-medium on cause]. Practical effect: dull, warm, no air; any "sparkle" on the track comes from the 1-4 kHz vocal/snare presence.
- **Spectral balance.** Bass-heavy: 71.9% of mix energy below 200 Hz, 18.5% at 200-800 Hz, 8% at 0.8-3 kHz, 1.4% at 3-6 kHz [S1]. That is far heavier at the bottom than classic mixes and sub-heavy in the way of modern trap/bass-music. The 99.9% roll-off at 5.4 kHz and overview.png centroid mostly at 800-2000 Hz agree.
- **Distortion/saturation.** 286 samples at or above 0.99 full scale and 746 above 0.95 in the full file [S2]. That is limited-to-the-ceiling but not heavily clipped (peaks reaching 1.0 only a few hundred times in 8.3 million samples, so about 0.003%). Drums show broadband noisy streaks, consistent with a saturated or sample-crushed kick/snare. I cannot measure harmonic distortion on the voice; the harmonic ladders look clean (medium-low that the voice is lightly distorted; high that it is not heavily distorted).
- **Era/style.** Sub-heavy, dry, vocal-forward, dance-chant hip-hop of the 2000s-2010s "crunk/club" mould; the lyrics "the club get crunk" read from the mix transcript [WN win_0020] fit. Tempo 152 bpm in the tracker [AF] may be a double-time reading of about 76 bpm (the half-time bass/drum cycle of about 1.4 s supports this); that is rhythm's call. Low-medium on era: the production cues (sub, dry, mono centre, band-limited loop) are era-ambiguous, since lo-fi bandlimiting could also be a deliberate or distribution artefact. CLAP "hip hop" 0.58 averaged (country 0.38, which I discount) [CLAP].

## 4. Space and reverb

- Dry. In W0040 the silences between vocal phrases are dark with no wash, and drum hits decay fast (a few tens of ms) [high for no long reverb]. Short room or slap cannot be excluded.
- The only "space" is the stereo opening. Mid/side ratio and L/R correlation by section [S1]: 0:00-0:12 side/mid 0.49, correlation 0.61 (wide); 0:14-0:45 0.17, 0.95 (near-mono, bass and vocal centred); 0:48-0:58 0.54, 0.55 (wide); 1:00-1:40 0.18, 0.94 (near-mono); 2:01-2:15 0.41, 0.71; 2:20-2:50 0.33, 0.80. Per-second [LV]: width 0.04-0.11 at the loudest points, 0.5-0.7 when bass and drums drop out. Since the sub is the centred element, wherever it leaves, the stereo content of the "other" layer (and any wide sample) is exposed. The wide parts are therefore the mid-range bed/room of a sample, which is plausible as a stereo loop, while the groove sections are near-mono (medium-high).

## 5. Mix balance and loudness curve

Overall: -13.5 dBFS RMS, crest 13.8 dB, 1s-RMS spread p10 -26, median -15, p95 -9 dB [S1]. Per 10 s block [LV, mix dBFS / vocals / drums / bass / other]:

- 0:00-0:10: -19.9 / -22.7 / -42.9 / -70.5 / -31.1. Voice is by far the loudest part; no bass; the voice is 8-20 dB above the rest (high).
- 0:10-0:50: -12 to -14; bass at -14 dB (the loudest instrument) with voice at -17 to -19. The sub enters at about 0:12 with a jump of about 5 dB in the mix [WN win_0000]. The voice drops below the bass here. Mix is flat to within 1-2 dB (hyper-compressed/limited behaviour, overview.png RMS blocks).
- 0:47-0:58 breakdown: -18 dBFS, bass gone (-66), drums and voice only. The mix rises back to -13 at 0:59.
- 1:00-1:50: steady -12.7 to -13.6, the loudest consistent section, with drums rising to -16 dB around 1:40 [LV] and a bass dip at 1:40-1:50 (-34 to -21).
- 1:59-2:00: sudden dip to -18.5 (hard stop). 2:00-2:10: sparse, bass out (-70). 2:10-2:20: bass back, -13.6.
- 2:20-2:40: -16 dBFS, voice still -19.6, drums almost gone (-54), bass -43 to -50. This is the quietest "full-voice" section with the vocal in front.
- 2:40-3:00: bass and drums back, voice nearly out (vocals -30 then -60 dB: instrumental tail), mix -13; the last 8 s decay to -25 at the end (3:00+ -25.6) [LV]; overview.png shows the loudness decaying from about 3:05 to nothing, so a real fade-out or a ring-out, not a cut (medium).

Balance summary: voice mid-forward and steady; sub-bass is the loudest instrument and dominates the energy; drums are punchy but sit lower in the stem levels (-15 to -40 dB per second) because their energy is spiky (crest 22 dB), so they look quiet by RMS but are audible as transients. CLAP's "no vocals" 1.0 at 0:00 and 0:40-1:00 is wrong given a -23 dB clear vocal stem, a reminder of how weak the CLAP set is.

## 7. What the sound does to a listener (sound-only reading, medium)

The dry, close, centred voice sounds like someone talking at you directly in the club; the 55 Hz sub gives a physical push (felt in the chest or a speaker's cone rather than heard as pitch), and the short gated notes make the groove bounce rather than drone. The intro's wider, bass-less, mid-heavy sound feels small and anticipatory, and the entrance of the sub at 0:12 is the biggest dynamic event of the track (about +5 dB mix, +45 dB low-end). Each breakdown (0:47, 1:59, 2:20) removes the sub, so the room feels suddenly wider and lighter; the return then reads as a release. Because the harmony is nearly a pure A pedal, these timbral and stereo changes are the real arc. The ceiling at 9-10 kHz keeps the sound warm and slightly boxy, rather than hi-fi.
