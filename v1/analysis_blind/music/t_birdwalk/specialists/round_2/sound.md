## 1. Answers to questions put to me

**Feeling and Form: does the width collapse at each bass entry come from the bass/drums being mono, or from the mix?** Per-stem stereo measurement [S3] (L/R correlation / side-to-mid ratio):
- Bass stem: 1.00 / 0.00 in every steady groove segment (0:14-0:40, 1:02-1:20, 1:35-2:00, 2:15-2:23, 2:56-3:06). Fully mono. [high]
- Drums stem: 0.98-1.00 / 0.04-0.09 almost everywhere (0.91 at 1:24-1:32). Effectively mono, centre-panned. [high]
- "Other" stem: correlation about 0.0-0.15, side/mid 0.86-1.08 in 0:02-0:09, 0:14-0:40, 0:50-0:58, 1:02-1:20, 2:15-2:23, 2:32-2:38. That is a near-decorrelated, very wide layer (a stereo sample bed, or a wide chorus/doubling treatment). [high on the numbers; medium on what produces them]
- Therefore both explanations are partly right, and they combine: the mono bass and drums are always in the centre, and the width of the mix is decided by how much of the wide "other" layer is present against them. When bass and drums are low (breaks, 0:50-0:58: mix correlation 0.55, side/mid 0.54) the wide bed is exposed; when the 55 Hz sub returns (about 47% of the energy sits below 60 Hz [S1]) the centred energy swamps the side signal and the mix drops to 0.91-0.95 correlation (0:14-0:40, 1:02-1:20, 1:35-2:00). The collapse is mostly an energy-ratio effect rather than a mastering move (no evidence of a "mono-ing" bus: the "other" stem itself stays wide when the bass is present, 0.15 at 1:02-1:20). [high]
- The "squeeze" texture: I would call it close, dry, centre-weighted and compact, with a hard-panned-looking narrow focus; the width opening in breaks is a relief of that pressure. Deliberate or consequential: the arrangement drives it, I cannot tell whether the mixer intended it (low).
- Form's 1:23-1:33 "other layer removed" case is confirmed independently: in 1:24-1:32 the "other" stem correlates at 0.91 (side/mid 0.21) and the mix at 1.00 (side/mid 0.03), i.e. only a mono remnant (bleed or a centred element) is left, so the wide bed is gone and the mix is effectively a mono beam. [high]

**Form: is the 0:48 / 2:00 block a literal repeat?** Mel-spectrogram pattern correlation between 0:48-0:58 and 2:00-2:10 [S3]: vocals 0.96, "other" 0.96, drums 0.89. **Harmony: is the bass a pitch-dropping 808 tone, and does "other" betray a third or fifth?**

This agrees with Harmony's 1.2-semitone start settling on A1 and with the 55-57.5 Hz histogram in my round 1. That is a short downward pitch drop on a decaying sub: the 808 signature. Correction to my round 1, which said I had not looked for glides. [medium-high]
- Harmonic content: the second harmonic is 28 dB below the fundamental and the third 31 dB below [S3]: a near-sine tone with a click, not a rich sawtooth bass. The audible "weight" is physical sub rather than pitch clarity. [medium-high]
- "Other" stem, strongest peaks in 150 Hz-3 kHz [S3]: 0:40-0:47: 883, 988, 904, 942, 966, 1047 Hz (a dense cluster from A5 (880) through B5 (988) to C6 (1047), not a single line); 1:02-1:20: 226, 880, 363, 293, 441, 331, 902, 988 Hz; 1:40-2:00: 226, 883, 293, 363, 315, 441, 904, 164 Hz. That supports a third-less "power-chord" colour and a layer that is more than one repeated pitch, but those peaks are averaged over many seconds and cannot show order. 363 Hz is about F#4 flat (370) and 988 is B5, so a stray sixth or ninth is possible; low confidence. In short: the layer is not an A-only loop; it carries A, D and E at least, with a bright cluster at 0.9-1 kHz that I would read as a plucky/stabbed sample played in the A-D-E area. [medium-low]

## 3. Connections to the other specialists

- **Form:** the form is a layer map; its three levers are mono-centre (bass, drums) versus wide-side (other layer, hook vocal). Because the centre is where the energy is and the sides are where the space is, every subtraction of the bass is automatically a stereo-open event. So loudness, width and layer in/out arrive together and cannot be separated.
- **Rhythm:** a short, gated, falling sub on every half-bar hit gives the groove its "bounce" (decay of about 0.7-1.4 s fills the 1.5 s hit spacing), so the bass is rhythm as well as pitch. The speed-up Rhythm sees (152 to about 160 BPM after the intro) coincides with the first sub entry at 0:12; if the pulse is steady the sub's sheer weight could make the tracker lock onto a faster reading, so I would treat the speed-up as medium-low.
- **Harmony:** an A1 sub (near-sine, falling 0.7 semitone) plus a bed whose peaks are A, D, E gives a power-chord sonority, third-free, in agreement with Harmony's "third-less" reading; the colour contrast Harmony hears at 1:20-1:23 (G) should show as a shift of the "other" peaks; I did not test that.
- **Voice:** the dry mono verse voice is the most intimate thing in the mix; the wide hook voice sounds bigger and more crowd-like. Texture therefore supports Voice's "crowd versus one man talking".
- **Feeling:** the loudness stays flat (-12 to -14 dBFS) while the width and the sub come and go, so the listener feels the impact as bodily (sub energy, mono focus) and as spatial contrast, not as rising level. The ceiling at 9-10 kHz leaves the sound warm and boxy.
- **Lineage:** falling-glide sine sub, dry, centre-mono drums, wide sampled bed, band-limited ceiling: all consistent with a mid-2000s to 2010s Southern club/crunk or snap production. Era remains low-medium confidence.
