## 2. Answers to questions put to me

**RHYTHM (Q2): do verse boundaries sit on the loop grid; is the delivery pushing or laying back?** Answered in section 1, points 1-3. One more link to your 1:22 and 2:58 "off-grid moments": 1:22 falls inside verse 2 (not a boundary of the voice), where my quiet-run list shows only brief dips at 1:53.9, 1:54.8, 1:57.5 [M2]; 2:58 is the last falling vocal glide [V1], not a phrase end that runs into a block, because the voice has left (2:47.7-2:56.9) and returns once for 1.5 s. So both are better read as "the backing changes, the voice does not respond", which matches [V1] section 6.2.

**HARMONY (Q3): is the delivery consciously pitched, and is C versus C# a deliberate blue third?**
- Verse 1 (0:24-0:45): pYIN pitch classes D 0.40, C# 0.25, C 0.13, D# 0.10 [M1], median offset from A=440 of +9 cents. The note choice is a near-monotone chant on D with a semitone below and above. That is "pitched" in the sense of a held reciting note, but D4 over an A pedal is not obviously picked from the track, it is a natural reciting pitch for a male voice (my read, interpretation) and the +9 cents says it is not tuned to the A1 sub. Not tuned: medium.
- Hook (0:48-0:58, 2:00-2:11, 2:35-2:46): the three passes give almost identical pitch-class shares: A 0.36-0.38, C 0.15-0.17, A# 0.11-0.14, G# 0.10-0.12, C# 0.07-0.10 [M1], and a median offset of -1 cent from A=440 in **all three** [M1]. Counting by frequency window, A2 142-148 frames, C3 46-51, C#3 23-33 [M1]. So C is about twice as frequent as C# in the hook, consistent with [H2]'s "faint minor lean". But C# is not rare (about 8-10 per cent). Two readings fit: a deliberate minor-third bounce, or speech intonation that flexes between them. I cannot separate these on one tracker; the voice's own -1 cent median against A=440 is the more surprising number. A -1 cent offset is better than most human voices hold over 10 s and suggests either tuned or very stable pitch, or (more likely given the loop) that the same take recurs. I keep **speech melody with a stable low-A centre: medium**; **"deliberate blue third": low**. The instrument layer gives no third [H1], so the voice is the only source of the minor colour, which is what makes it matter.
- Verse 2 (1:12-1:58): G 0.30, G# 0.17, F# 0.16, F 0.12 [M1], centre about 9 cents sharp of A=440. That is a contour a fourth to a fifth up and chromatic, again speech inflection. It coincides with the G colour [H1], but because the pitch classes are a chromatic cluster centred on G, I cannot claim the voice "plays" b7.

**LINEAGE (Q3): call-and-response, backing shouts, one or several voices?**
- Stereo test of the hook passages: left and right channels of the vocal stem correlate at about 0.52-0.56 with zero lag between them (peak at 0 samples) and a side/mid ratio of 0.53-0.56 [M1, M2]. In the mono verses correlation is 1.00 and side/mid 0.012-0.06. In the hook, pYIN on the left and right channels agrees to a median 20 cents (verses: 0 cents) [M2].
- Reading: in the hook the two channels carry the **same pitch**, so there is no evidence of a second voice on a different melody. A 20-cent disagreement with the same fundamental is what you would get from a lightly doubled or stereo-processed take, or from stereo "other" layer bleed in the vocal stem [S1]. Zero inter-channel delay argues against a Haas-style double. Therefore: **one voice at the pitch level, stereo-widened or doubled in the hook, dead-centre in the verses (high for the mono/wide split, medium for "single speaker")**. I cannot rule out a second voice speaking in unison with the first (a crowd shout would not show up as a different pitch).
- Ad-libs ("aye", "yeah", "aye, man") appear in the transcripts between hook lines [V1 section 3]. I looked for gaps in the vocal stem where a response could sit and found only short dips (0.15-0.3 s each) at 0:43.7, 0:48.0, 1:10.8, 1:53.9, 1:54.8, 1:57.5, 1:59.6, 2:00.0, 2:41.1, 2:42.2 [M2]. There is no 1-2 s space for a call-and-response exchange; the hook is continuous speech. So any "response" is within the same delivery, not alternating. The crunk crowd model needs backing shouts I cannot detect; the solo novelty-rap reading is better supported, but cannot be proven from the stem. Medium-low.

**SOUND (Q3 to me): rap versus sung; are the sweeps voice effects or bleed?**
- Rap/chant versus sung: speech contour throughout. The only place where the voice holds a long steady pitch is the D4 recitation (0:24-0:45), which is a monotone chant rather than a tune; the hook's A2-C3-A2 bounce is narrow (a minor third). Nothing resembles a sung melody. Medium-high.
- Sweeps: the falling chirps at 0:09-0:12, 2:32-2:35, 2:44-2:47 and 2:56-3:00 [V1] are present in the vocal stem and have a 3 kHz to 1 kHz fall over 0.7-0.8 s. I cannot tell whether they are a vocal effect or a bleed from a synth riser that demucs put into the vocal stem; the fact that 0:45 and 1:35 sweeps (sound's list) occur **in the "other" panel as diagonal streaks** while the chirps I picked occur in the vocals panel suggests two kinds of sweep, rising synth sweeps in "other" and falling chirps in the voice. I could not test pitch content with enough confidence to say harmonic versus noise. Low.

## 3. Correction: how sure am I that the hook is a pasted audio copy?

Round 1 said r 0.7-0.9 by normalised cross-correlation of 3 s vocal blocks, and medium-high that the hook is a copy. I re-ran at 16 kHz [M3]:
- 3 s raw-waveform correlation, best lag within +/-0.3 s: 0:49 against 2:25.4 gives r 0.27; against 2:01.2 r 0.10; against 1:01.1 r 0.15. These are **not** the 0.7-0.9 of round 1. I cannot reproduce that number; either it used a different normalisation or a different resampling, so treat "r 0.88" as unverified.
- Log-mel spectrogram correlation of the same blocks: 0.96 (0:49 vs 2:25.4), 0.96 (0:49 vs 2:01.2), 0.72 (vs 1:01.1), 0.55 (vs 1:13.4). Control: 0:49 against every 0.25 s offset in 2:00-2:42 has median 0.57, and against verse 2 median 0.46, maximum 0.64 [M3]. So the matches at 2:01.0, 2:25.4 and a few others stand out (0.84-0.97) from background, but spectrograms of a loud chant are inherently similar (median 0.57), so this is evidence for "same words in the same rhythm", not for sample-level copying.
- Onset pattern: of the 37-47 vocal onsets in a 12 s hook block, 83 per cent of those at 2:00-2:11 fall within 40 ms of the 0:47-0:59 pattern at a fixed 72.0 s offset; at fixed 12.0 s steps for 7, 8 and 9 steps the match is only 37-39 per cent [M2]. The reason is that the repeat period is not exactly 12.0 s: the best lag offsets I found are 12.15, 24.42, 72.00 and 96.37 s, which is 12.15, 12.21, 12.00 and 12.05 s per loop [M3]. A sequencer loop would have one period. A re-performed hook would drift like this.
- Pitch: the three hook passes agree with each other to within 2 per cent on pitch-class shares and all sit at -1 cent from A=440 [M1]. That favours the **same take or a very tightly rehearsed refrain**, not a loose live repeat.

Revised reading: the hook is "the same words, the same rhythm, and nearly the same pitch track, recurring with 12.0-12.2 s spacing"; **whether it is a pasted audio clip or a tightly re-performed refrain is undecided (low-medium for paste)**. What stays high: the hook recurs, verses never recur (best mel-correlation of verse 2 against its own region 0.64 maximum, median 0.46), and the voice is the constant while the backing varies.

During the 2:23.5-2:35.7 rhythm stop [R1], the voice carries on, but my drum-hit test against drums cannot run there (drums silent), so I have no measurement of voice timing in that gap, only that voice level stays -19 to -20 dB [R1].
- **Voice and harmony [H1, H2].** The pedal stays on A; the voice moves: D4 (a fourth over), then A2-C3, then G3 (b7). Each register change coincides with a section change, so pitch register is the voice's formal signal, as in [V1] section 6.4. Because the voice supplies the only possible third and its C is about twice as common as C#, the minor lean is a vocal fact, not an instrumental one.
- **Voice and sound [S1].** Mono verse versus wide hook is the same split as the near-mono groove versus wide breaks in [S1], so the width change may be as much the stereo "other" layer coming through the vocal stem in the hook as a change in how the voice was recorded. If the vocal stem in the hook contains stereo bleed, then my "hook is stereo-wide" is a property of stem separation, not of the voice.
- **Voice and feeling [FE1].** Interpretation: a voice that never lets go (gaps over 0.35 s total only about 1.4 s before 2:47 [V1]) over a bass that leaves and returns reads as a dominant, boasting presence; the 9 s vocal-less 2:47.7-2:56.9 is the one place the listener is left with the machine alone, and the drum jump there (2:48.7-2:50.3 per [F1]) is felt as a release. The "swagger" word in [R1] is supported by the voice sitting on the hit rather than ahead of it: a laid-back, not pushing, delivery.
