## 4. Connections

- **Form to rhythm.** The song is a count of 12 s blocks (intro 1 | groove 3 | break 1 | groove 5 | break 1 | groove 1 | break 1 | groove ~1.5, then bass-free tail). A hit per 1.55 s with 16-beat blocks gives the cheapest possible arrangement language: subtract on the block line. Density change (drum onsets per 4 s from 3-9 to 11-18 [R3]) carries the late build, not tempo.
- **Form to sound.** Loudness is flat at -12 to -14 dBFS [S3]. The dynamics are therefore not volume: they are spectral and spatial. When the 47 per cent sub-bass energy goes, the mix gets lighter and wider at the same level. Tension is the empty floor, release is the sub landing. Because sub carries the weight physically, the return is felt in the body more than heard as pitch.
- **Form to harmony.** One tonic (A) throughout [H3]. The sound of "returning home" is the sub returning, not a chord.
- **Form to voice.** The voice does not rest and is exact at 72 s, near-exact at 12 s: the repeats are the listener's landmarks since there is no melody to remember. Hook in stereo, verse mono [V2, S3]: a second dynamic cue, the hook is wider and so "opens" the mix.
- **Form to feeling.** The feeling payoff is where I place it in the map: the drop at 0:12.4 (a pickup hit at 0:11.6, then bass from -71 to -21 dB in one frame), the 1:46 and 2:11/2:35 returns. Pleasure here is anticipation and return on a count the body learns by 0:48, which is why break 2 (identical to break 1) works as ritual [FE2].
- **Form to lineage.** Subtract-on-the-line arrangement is a producer's drop template [L2]; I agree and add that nothing like a riser exists except the vocal chirps.
