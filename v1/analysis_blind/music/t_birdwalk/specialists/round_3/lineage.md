This weakens "trap" as the sub-lineage and favours straight-sixteenth, half-time club chant grammar (crunk / snap / Southern party-rap family). Medium-high for "no triplets", low for dating.

So my "crowd chant" reading must be demoted: the hook is **crowd-joinable by design (low, simple, repeated, spoken) but not crowd-performed on the evidence**. It reads as a solo novelty boast with a stacked hook (medium-low).

## 2. Corrections to myself, others' new findings absorbed

1. **"Other" layer: A with an occasional D neighbour, not one pure note.** H3's frame-by-frame peaks (A 79-94 per cent of loud frames; D 5-10 per cent; E rare, mostly 2:27-2:30) refine my "one-note pluck". It is still a two-pitch cell, with no third, so open fourth/fifth colour and no major/minor decision. My conclusion survives: not a sampled melodic loop from an older record, closer to ringtone-era minimal production. Medium.
2. **Hook width is in the voice, not leakage.** My round-2 Q to SOUND is answered by V3: vocal side/mid 0.37-0.49 in hooks against 0.01-0.03 in verses, and the side envelope follows the voice's own mid (r 0.56-0.81), not the "other" side channel (r about -0.1 to -0.2). So a doubled or stereo-processed hook over a dry mono verse is better supported (medium). That is a standard party-rap mix habit (centred dry verse, stacked wide hook); consistent, not diagnostic.
3. **Pasted-loop claim softened.** I said the hook is "copy-pasted"; V3 shows the break block repeats at exactly 72.00 s (r 0.95, on the grid) but the late hook repeats at 12.09-12.21 s (r 0.76-0.89), i.e. 0.1-0.2 s off the rigid 12.0 s grid. So the break block is a pasted loop (high), the late hook is re-timed or re-performed over a rigid backing (medium; cause low). "Producer pasting to a 4-bar template" holds for the backing and break; "all vocals pasted" does not.
4. **No speed-up.** R3 withdrew the speed-up; I never relied on it. Tempo stays 80 BPM half-time from 12.0 s loop arithmetic (medium-high).

## 3. Connections

- **Rhythm to lineage.** A hit per 1.55 s, 80 BPM half-time, 0.19 s sixteenth hats in the dense stretches and a voice at about 5.9 syllables per second locked to drum hits 83-100 per cent [V1]: the voice is the groove, the drum machine marks the pulse. That is the chant-rap "voice is the beat" grammar.
- **Harmony to form.** A static A pedal means the only development is in layers: the arrangement does all the work. This is the lineage fingerprint of a loop-based club beat, not a limitation. Form's 12-second subtract-and-return structure is that fingerprint in time.
- **Sound to feeling.** Mono near-sine sub with click, dry mono voice, wide bright A5 pluck and chirps: heavy, dark floor and toy-like top, which is the "playful with menace" [FE2] colour. The thrill comes from the sub returning, not a level rise (flat -12 to -14 dBFS [S3]).
- **Voice to feeling.** Low reciting pitch (A2-C3), spoken not sung, cheeky C over A: a swagger boast, not a lament.

## 4. What the measurements cannot tell me

## 5. Settled conclusions

- **Programmed Southern-leaning club rap, crunk / snap-derived and minimalist** (high for "programmed club rap", medium for sub-family, low for date, region and artist). It does not resemble boom bap, G-funk, drill, reggaeton or EDM four-on-the-floor.
- **Rhythm grammar: 80 BPM half-time, one hit per 1.55 s, straight (non-triplet) sixteenth hats, voice carrying the subdivisions.** Evidence: 12.0 s loop arithmetic, R3 IOI histogram (triplet bin 9 of 418).
- **Sound: tuned, falling-glide sine sub with click (about 1 s), dry mono voice, wide plucked A (with a D neighbour) bed.** Static harmony is a genre fingerprint, not a lack of ideas; the "other" layer is not a sampled melody (medium).
- **Form: arrangement by subtraction on a 12.0 s (four-bar) grid.** Break block pasted exactly (72.00 s lag, r 0.95); late hook slightly off-grid (12.1-12.2 s), so re-timed or re-performed (medium).
- **Function and feeling: a crowd-joinable but, on the evidence, solo chant** (one voice with ad-libs and a widened hook), cheeky and playful with a heavy floor; "dance-craze" stays medium-low because the measurements show no crowd and no instructions.
