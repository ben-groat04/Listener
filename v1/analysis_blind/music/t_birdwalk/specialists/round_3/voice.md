## 3. Connections

- **Words and music.** The transcripts show the track is built from two kinds of text: the refrain ("that bird walk, watch me do that bird walk") with very few distinct words, and verse 2 (1:10-1:56) with boasts and rhymed end-words (bums/gummy/bummy, "money money money", "best/worst", "birthday/your birthday" [T]). The refrain has the same words as the rhythm part: it names the gesture. The verse is where words carry the content; the refrain is where the voice becomes a rhythm instrument on the hit. The repetition scheme in the verse is also internal (money x3, bummy x3, "show you" x3), so the voice supplies its own loops even where the backing offers none. High for the text structure, as it comes straight from the transcripts (accuracy of individual words medium).
- **Voice and form.** Hook = refrain at A2-C3, wide; verse = recitation at D4 (verse 1) or G3 (verse 2), dead-centre mono. Register and width change together at each section, so the voice defines the form as much as the bass entries do. That agrees with [F2] where the voice marks the sections (hook 0:12, verse 1 0:24, break 0:47, verse 2 1:11, break 2:00, final hook 2:11).
- **Voice and rhythm.** Break block locks to 12.0 s, late hook slips 12.1-12.2 s: so over the final minute the hook drifts about 0.15 s per pass against the rhythm grid [R2]. Interpretation: that small slip is exactly the "human" element on a rigid backing.
- **Voice and harmony.** The voice supplies the only minor third (C versus C# about 2:1 in hooks [V2]); a stable -1 cent median across three passes [V2] is the same-take indicator. Not a blue third by choice (low).
- **Voice and feeling.** A close, dry, mono verse voice at one's ear versus a wider hook voice is a shift from "someone talking to you" to "a chant around you" [S2]. The voice-free 2:47.7-2:56.9 block, and the quiet 2:58 falling gesture (about 15 dB under the chant), read as the one place the voice lets go; as a feeling it is wry or deflating rather than triumphant. Interpretation only; medium-low.

## 4. Settled conclusions (5 bullets)

- **The voice sits on the beat, not ahead or behind (medium-high).** In the sparse hook passes every drum hit has a vocal onset within 50 ms (8 of 8, 7 of 7 against about 35 per cent by chance); between hits the syllables are free (phase-locking to the 0.19 s grid is only 0.09-0.36).
- **The hook is one refrain ("that bird walk, watch me do that bird walk") recurring with the same rhythm and nearly the same pitch (high); whether it is a pasted clip is not proven (low-medium).** The break block repeats at exactly 72.00 s (r 0.95); the late hook repeats at 12.1-12.2 s (r 0.76-0.89), not at the 12.0 s grid.
- **The hook's width belongs to the voice, not to leakage (medium).** Vocal side-channel envelope follows vocal mid envelope (r 0.56-0.81) and not the "other" stem's side channel (r about -0.1 to -0.2); verses are mono (side/mid 0.01-0.03). One voice widened or doubled; a crowd is neither shown nor excluded (low).
- **The voice carries the form and the feeling (medium).** Register, width and words change at each section edge; the only voice-free stretch (2:47.7-2:56.9) and the 15 dB-quieter 2:58 falling glide (processed sweep, sample or shout, undecidable, low) are the track's one moment of release, with a dry, close, boastful presence the rest of the time.
