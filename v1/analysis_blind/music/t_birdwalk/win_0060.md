## Summary

After a stripped-down passage with no bass (about 0:47 to 0:59, MV 0040s bass panel empty from ~7 s to ~19.4 s of that window), the bass and a drum groove slam in at about 0:59.5 and stay for the whole window. ## What a listener feels, and why
1. **A jolt of release and weight at 1:00 (high):** bass appears from silence (-71 to -18 dB) and the whole mix rises about 5 dB, after a thin passage. Low-end weight plus returning drums make this the "drop".
2. **Swagger, bounce, playfulness (medium):** syncopated half-time groove (bass about every 0.7 s with irregular drum stripes), a simple low chant that repeats, in a static A tonality with an occasional F. Static harmony gives a trance-like loop feel rather than a "story" progression.
3. **Tension and drive at 1:06 to 1:11 (medium):** drum roll runs (0.2 s intervals) build into the bar at 1:08 and again at 1:10 before the verse. The roll-then-switch is a standard "here comes the verse" cue.
4. **A shift to confident, conversational attitude at 1:10 (medium-high):** the pitch rises about a fourth to a fifth, the delivery becomes rhythmic, dry and closer, and the stereo field narrows to near mono, like someone stepping up to the microphone.
5. **Something a little menacing under the fun (low):** the low minor-leaning A drone with C and F colour. Given the third ambiguity, this feeling rests on weak evidence.
