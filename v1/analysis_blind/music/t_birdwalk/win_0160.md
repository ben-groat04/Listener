# t_birdwalk, 2:40-3:00 (window_0160s): blind listening notes

Method: window_0160s.png (plus 0140s and 0180s for continuity), chords.json, lead_notes.json, per_second_levels.json, clap_tags.json, beat_times.json, both Whisper passes, and a short probe (`v1/scripts/agents/win_0160_probe.py`, plus an inline RMS-dip and band-share check). Times are m:ss; "(w x s)" means x seconds into this window. Context.md and research were not read.

## 1. What is playing (layers)

- **Chopped voice, 2:40-2:47 (high).** The vocals stem (window image, 0-7 s) shows a dense run of short, formant-banded bursts. The mix spectrogram shows separate vertical blocks. Vocal-stem onsets come every ~0.2-0.4 s (160.1 to 167.0 s, probe). Per-second vocal level is -18 to -24 dBFS, then falls to -35 at 2:47, -58 at 2:48 and about -70 (stem silent) from 2:49 to 2:55. Voice is therefore **fully absent for about 7-8 s** (2:49-2:56). It returns only as a gliding sweep, 2:56.5-2:59 (see 5).
- **Bass (high for presence, medium for pitch).** The bass stem sits at -13 to -16 dB for 2:38-2:49, then drops to -19 to -22 at 2:50-2:55 and recovers to about -17 from 2:56. Pitch from pyin on the bass stem is a steady ~55 Hz (A1) at 2:40-2:49; after 2:50 pyin finds no stable pitch, which is consistent with a sub tone below its range or with low-register noise. The bass spectrogram shows short, vertical, tightly gated low pulses rather than sustained notes. The lead-notes tracker, reading bleed in the vocal stem, reports C#2 (~69 Hz) repeatedly 2:50-3:01, so the sub may have shifted from A to C#. This is low confidence because that is a tracker reading a stem that has no voice.
- **Drums (high for the change).** In 2:40-2:47 the drum stem is sparse and mid/high (level -24 to -37 dB, centroid ~3.3-3.5 kHz, almost no energy below 150 Hz). From 2:49-2:50 it brightens in the low end (a short kick-like line near ~100 Hz in the drum panel from w10 s) and the level jumps to about -16 dB at 2:51-2:55, roughly 10 dB louder than before. Low-frequency share of the drum stem rises from 0.00-0.02 to 0.22-0.31 (probe). The drum stem has about 75 onsets in the window, about 1.5 per beat. This reads as a kick/snare/hat pattern rather than a plain four-on-the-floor, but I cannot tell the drum-machine sounds apart from measurements alone.
- **"Other" (guitar/synth/sample layer, medium).** The "other" stem is quiet (-32 to -38 dB) until 2:46 and then jumps to about -23.5 dB at 2:47 as the voice leaves. It stays near -27 to -31, with another peak of -22.9 at 2:58. Its panel shows a repeated mid-band (~800-1100 Hz) stripe with harmonic lines up to ~6 kHz. That is a pitched sustained or chorded layer plus stabs, whose identity I cannot name. The CLAP guesses (below) are not reliable on this.
- **Sweeps (medium).** Diagonal, falling streaks appear in the mix at about w5 s (2:45) and w17-18.5 s (2:57-2:58.5). The second is in the vocals and "other" stems and is a downward gliss. The lead tracker follows it as C5 (2:58.45), D#4 (2:59.14), C#4, B3, a fall of about 1.5 octaves in about 1.5 s.

## 2. Harmony and melody (medium-low)

- Chroma (panel 6) has a bright, unbroken **A row** across the whole window, with weaker C, E and G. This is an **A pedal**. Whole-song key estimate is A minor 0.51 vs A major 0.46 (audio_features/summary.json), and this window's labels are mostly Am (2:39.7-2:44.4, 2:49.6-2:50.7, 2:51.5-2:52.6, 2:54.1-2:54.9, 2:56) with N (no chord) between.
- Moves away from Am: **F#m** labels at 2:53.0-2:53.4 and 2:57.2-2:58.8, a **G** glow in chroma at 2:47-2:48.3 and 2:57.5-2:59, and an **A (major)** at 3:01.8 (just outside). I read this as a one-chord drone on A with brief passing colour (b7 = G, a 6th/F#). F#m labels may reflect B-less F#-A-C# overlap from the sub, so trust them weakly.
- No tune in the usual sense. The only melodic shapes are (a) the speech contour in 2:40-2:47, whose pitch tracker values (E3-F#3 and C#3-A2, a range of about a fifth, midi ~44-51) mostly reflect speech inflection, and (b) the falling sweep at 2:58-2:59 from ~C5 to B3. The pitch track around 2:47 (F4, D#4) is one rising/falling blip at the point where speech stops.
- Do not read the "lead_notes" in 2:50-2:58 as melody: they sit at ~C#2 with no vocal in the stem, so they are the sub-bass leaking into the vocal-stem pass.

## 3. Rhythm

- Beat tracker interval in 2:38-3:02 is a median ~0.37 s, about **161 bpm locally** (beats are not far from the tracker's whole-song figure of 152 bpm, which is a mean). Intervals are very steady (0.35-0.40 s), so the pulse is metronomic, with no audible tempo drift in these data.
- 2:40-2:47: speech bursts fall near the beat grid (onsets spaced ~0.37 s), so the voice acts as the rhythm (a vocal-chop hook). Drums are sparse clicks and noise bursts, not a groove.
- **Near-silent gaps (the stutter)**. Short RMS dips of 14-31 dB occur in the mix at 2:42.75, 2:44.7, 2:45.8, 2:48.6, 2:50.3, 2:51.4, 2:53.3, 2:54.5, 2:56.4 and 2:57.4. From 2:50.3 the spacing alternates 1.1 s, 1.9 s, 1.1 s, 1.9 s (170.30, 171.41, 173.34, 174.47, 176.36). This is about a 3-beat + 5-beat pattern, repeating every ~3.0 s (8 beats). Medium confidence (the 172.4 and 175.4 gaps are weaker, ~6 dB). It is a cut-off rhythm: the sound is hard-stopped on a grid, which gives a punching or lurching feel.
- 2:50-3:00: denser and heavier, since drum onsets rise to roughly 4 per second (probe: 39 in 2:50-3:00) and the kick/sub weight gets loud.

## 4. Sound

- **Loudness.** Mix level is nearly flat, -11.5 to -14.2 dBFS from 2:40 to 3:00 (per_second_levels), the loudest portion of the video. There is no fade. The "build" is by layer change, not by level. Whole-window dynamic is heavily squashed, so it reads as a dense wall.
- **Low-end weight.** Energy share below 150 Hz in the mix rises from 0.77 in 2:40-2:47 (voice-led) to 0.93 in 2:47-2:50 and 2:50-2:56, so the mix is almost all bass/sub in power terms. In 2:56.5-3:00 it is 0.91, with the high-band (>4 kHz) share about doubling (0.007 to 0.017) and spectral centroid rising from ~1.1 to ~1.55 kHz, because of the sweep and hi-hat/noise.
- **Brightness.** The panels cut at about 7-8 kHz. Mix and "other" show broadband noise-like bursts in 2:40-2:47. These are the vocal sibilance and clicky transients.
- **Stereo width.** Width falls from ~0.26-0.35 (2:40-2:46) to ~0.15-0.18 at 2:47-3:00 (per_second_levels). When the voice leaves, the picture becomes narrower and more centred, consistent with bass/kick-dominated mono-ish material. Medium-high confidence in the number, low in its cause.
- **Space.** No obvious long reverb tails in the panels: dark gaps between blocks are deep (up to 31 dB), so the sound is dry and edited rather than washy. Vinyl crackle is CLAP's top "sound" label (0.83-1.0) from 2:40, which I read as a generic noise-floor/crackle texture rather than a proof of a vinyl sample. Treat as weak.

- Treatment: the speech is cut into rhythmic, beat-synchronised fragments and sits very near the front of the mix (vocal stem -18 to -24 dB beside a -13 dB mix), so it works as a **percussive vocal-chop instrument**, not a lead singer. In 2:56.5-2:59 the voice becomes a **gliding falling sweep** with harmonic streaks. It reads like a pitched "whoop/slide" or a processed vocal fall. I cannot say whether it is a human vocal gesture or a synthesised sweep.

## 7. What a listener would feel, and why

1. **Urgency and playfulness at 2:40-2:47** (medium). A near-constant ~161 bpm pulse with a voice chopped into 0.2-0.4 s bursts, plus the CLAP mood "playful" 0.65 at 2:40 and "aggressive" 0.35. Cause: vocal-stem onset density and the steady beat grid.
2. **A drop or punch at 2:47-2:50** (medium-high). Cause: the voice vanishes in about a second (-23 to -70 dB), the "other" layer jumps +12 dB, and 77-93 % of mix energy sits below 150 Hz.
3. **Weight, drive and swagger from 2:50** (medium-high). Cause: drum stem +10 dB with kick-range energy, a mix that is flat at about -13 dBFS, and the cut-off 3+5 gap pattern that adds a lurching groove. CLAP mood goes "energetic" 0.996 at 2:50 and "aggressive" 0.91 at 2:55, which agrees but is weak evidence alone.
4. **A lift and release at the falling sweep (2:56.5-2:59)** (medium). Cause: a descending gliss of about 1.5 octaves, with more high-frequency energy, which points to the next section.
5. **Drone stasis under all of it** (medium). Cause: the unbroken A pedal in chroma. The ear gets a held tonal floor, not a harmonic journey, so the excitement comes from rhythm and layer swaps, not chord changes.
