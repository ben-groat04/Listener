## 8. Production-era judgement (rule 1), INFERRED
**Judgement: modern production (limited/loud master, loop-free but DAW-compatible), not a single period-consistent old recording. No archive layer detectable by measurement. Confidence: medium-high that the final master is modern; medium that the material is not older.** Figures:
1. **Peak level**: sample peak 1.0 linear (0.000 dBFS), 235 samples at or above 0.999 and 299 at or above 0.99 across the whole file; 4x-oversampled true peak 1.059 (+0.50 dBTP) (m). Window notes record +0.3 and +0.04 dBFS float overs at 2:30-3:10 and 4:20-4:40. The mix is limited to the ceiling, with intersample overs: a modern limited/loud master (high).
2. **Loudness and crest**: median 1 s RMS -16.9 dBFS (60-340 s), 10 s crest factor 14-17 dB through the loud body; blocks 3:40-4:00 at -13.7 to -14.6 dBFS (m). Compressed and loud, consistent with modern mastering, though -15 dB RMS is not "brickwall extreme" (medium-high).
3. **Loop regularity**: the five stabs at 1:00.8, 1:04.8, 1:08.8, 1:12.9, 1:16.8 (refined on the envelope: 60.829, 64.843, 68.848, 72.885, 76.768 s) are spaced 4.013, 4.005, 4.037, 3.884 s, a spread of 0.15 s (3.9%). At 123 BPM, 8 beats = 3.90 s, so the spacing is 8.2 beats on average. Waveform cross-correlation of the 1.5 s after successive hits is only 0.17-0.33, though their average spectra match (cosine 0.91-0.94). So the cycle is **timbrally constant but not a sample-exact copy**; it is not a rigid loop of the identical audio, and its period does not lock to the tracker beat. A DAW loop of a sampled stab would give about 1.0 correlation at exactly repeated spacing. INFERRED: played or varied material, medium-low.
4. **Kick spread from the grid**: tracker beat interval 0.488 s (122.95 BPM); low-band drum-stem peaks have IOI clusters at 0.10, 0.20-0.30 and 0.45-0.50 s, so they include fills and eighth-note activity. A best-fit fixed-tempo test of these peaks is weak (mean |dev| 62-118 ms against the 61-122 ms expected for random phase on the 244 ms eighth and 488 ms beat grids; my first-hit phase test cannot tell grid-locked from free). In win_0060 the note found kick onsets at +10/-20/+30 ms from the beat grid (n=3, low). **My drum-stem onsets do not demonstrate a quantised grid; neither do they disprove one**, because separation bleed and fills dominate. Confidence low. Do not use this for or against a DAW.
5. **Band-limited sample against full-band mix**: I looked for a narrow-band, noisy or low-fi layer sitting under or inside the full-range mix. Result: **none**. The bandwidth is uniform: all sections show the same ~17 kHz lowpass cliff (section 3), the opening collage and the loud body alike; the only narrow-band material is the mid-band speech-like residue at 5:38-5:42.8 (1-4 kHz 66-77% of power, centroid 3.5 kHz), which appears only because the pad fades away, not as a separate low-fi layer. The only speech-like layers (2:22, 5:12, 5:17, 5:30-5:42) were measured as short and mid-band, and no consistent vinyl-like band appears. CLAP "vinyl crackle" is constant and discounted (medium-high).
6. **Pitch tuning**: "other" and final pad are 10-25 cents flat of A=440 and bass about 0.2 semitone flat (harmony specialist's figures, not re-measured by me). A DAW-locked synth sits at 0 cents; a flat reading is mild evidence for played or tape-sourced pitch (INFERRED, low-medium; carried from harmony, not mine).
7. **Both ends are cut hard** (section 7) and the whole file carries a ~17 kHz lowpass: a lossy-codec generation, which implies the audio was extracted from a compressed video, not delivered from a lossless master (INFERRED, medium-high). It does not date the recording.
Net: the figures that settle the case are peak 0 dBFS with +0.50 dBTP, 14-17 dB crest at -15 to -17 dBFS RMS and the absence of any band-limited layer. ## Not recoverable by measurement

- **Whether the distortion is amplifier fuzz, digital clipping or plain density**: no THD can be separated from stem-model artefacts.

- **Intent behind the hard cut at 5:42.8**: the measurement shows a 30 ms ramp to zero, not why.
