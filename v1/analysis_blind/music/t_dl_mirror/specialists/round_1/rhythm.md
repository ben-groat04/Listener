Octave resolution. The beat is about 94 BPM (beat 0.6385 s). The alternatives are 47 BPM (half) and 188 BPM (double). The tempogram top candidates in `audio_features/extra/music_extra.json` include 92.3, 95.7, 184.6 and 47.0, so all octaves are present. I take 94 for three reasons: (a) the kick IOIs sit on a 0.16 s sixteenth grid and the mid-band backbeat hits sit 1.28 s apart in strict alternation (section 3), so a pulse of about 0.64 s is the natural unit; (b) a hip-hop-range 94 BPM is also what the density of sixteenth hits (about 6 per second in the 1.5-5 kHz band, 5.7 onsets per second measured) implies if counted as sixteenths; (c) the vocal syllable rate of about 5 per second (`stems_extra.txt`, 5.25 onsets/s) matches sixteenths at 94 BPM (6.3 per second) or dense eighths at 188 BPM. A half-time reading (47 BPM, backbeat on every second bar-half) is available to the ear and a colleague suggested it (win_0060.md, tagged INFERRED low). I keep 94 as the counting tempo and treat "half-time feel" as a perceptual option, not a measurement. Confidence: tempo octave high; 94.0 vs 94.3 medium.

Stability. The bar lag is 93.96-94.01 BPM in every 20 s window from 0:20 to 3:50 (23 windows, range 0.05 BPM). That is far steadier than a human drummer and is consistent with a fixed-tempo machine or sampled loop. The tracker's own interval scatter (std 21 ms, 92 per cent of beats within 5 per cent of its median) is mostly its frame quantisation. No tempo drift, rush or drag is measurable. The last window (3:48-3:56, fade) still gives 2.556 s (93.91 BPM). Tempo change: none. Measured, high.

Correction to a colleague figure. Window notes at 1:40-2:00 and 3:40-4:00 quote "about 94 BPM" from the tracker while the rest quote 95.7; they are both from the tracker's quantised spacings (0.627 s versus 0.6385 s). ## 3. Drum pattern

Method: drums stem (`stems_raw/htdemucs/song/drums.wav`), onset detection in four bands at 1.45 ms and 5 ms resolution, 0:30-3:35 only (to avoid the intro and the fade).

- **Kick band (35-130 Hz):** 558 raw onsets, which collapse to 298 groups once the repeated 0.11 s double triggers are merged (each kick shows two close rise points; I treat that as one hit with a short body, not a drum roll; measured, medium). The 298 groups give about 4.1 kicks per bar (2.55 s).
- **Kick inter-onset intervals (IOI, after merging), in sixteenth-note steps at 94.0 BPM:** 1 step: 8; **2 steps: 72; 3 steps: 107**; 4: 3; **5: 36; 6: 37**; 7: 1; **8: 33**. So kick spacings are 0.32 s (2), 0.48 s (3), 0.80 s (5), 0.96 s (6) and 1.28 s (8 steps = two beats) almost exclusively; a spacing of exactly one beat (4 steps, 0.64 s) is nearly absent (3 of 298). Measured, high. This is the grid for these statistics: a fixed 16th-note grid at 94.0 BPM (0.1596 s), and it is checked against the kick IOI because it is built from it. Deviation of the IOIs from integer multiples of 0.1596 s: standard deviation 20.2 ms, mean absolute 14.2 ms; for random times the expectation would be 46.1 ms standard deviation. On the tracker's grid (95.7 BPM, 0.157 s) the figure is 20.6 ms against 45.2 ms random, but the cumulative-drift test (section 2) rules that grid out. So the kick is on a sixteenth grid at 94 BPM with a spread of about 20 ms: neither a metronome (which would be a few ms) nor loose.
- **Kick pattern, read from 1:40-1:51 (collapsed onsets, 0.01 s resolution):** 1:40.34, 1:40.83, 1:41.15, 1:41.63, 1:41.96, then 1:42.9, 1:43.7, 1:44.18, then 1:45.45, 1:45.93, 1:46.4, 1:46.71, 1:47.05 ... Within a bar (2.554 s) from the first hit that is steps 0, 3, 5, 8, 10; the next bar 0, 5, 8 with a gap of two beats; the cycle repeats at 5.108 s with variation. A 3+2+3+2 grouping (dotted-eighth and eighth alternating) is the same figure as the widely used "tresillo-like" boom-bap kick. INFERRED (style reading, medium); the step positions are measured, medium (single detector). Confirmed again at 2:40-2:51: IOIs of 8, 3, 2, 3, 2, 6, 5, 3 steps.
- **Mid-band hits (1.5-5 kHz, snare-crack and tom region):** 1059 onsets (5.7 per second); 899 of the IOIs are one sixteenth step, 154 are two. This is a continuous sixteenth-rate comb with accented positions. The fold on a single beat (48 bins) shows peaks at the beat and the half-beat for the 150-400 Hz and 1.5-5 kHz bands (maxima 4.4-5.0 per cent per bin against about 2 per cent average) with a dip just after each peak, which is the hat/snare "tick-tack" of eighth-note pairs. Measured, medium; I cannot separate backbeat snare from hats with certainty.
- **Hi-hat band (6-10 kHz):** the same comb at lower contrast; peaks at beat and off-beat.
- **Swing.** The beat-fold peaks for the mid-band sit at bins 5-6 and 29-30 of 48 (0.50 beat apart within about 0.02 beat) so the off-beat eighth is on the straight position: no swing figure of the 2:1 type. The 20 ms deviation above bounds any swing/feel to about 30 ms. Straight sixteenths, slightly loose. INFERRED, medium.
- **Density per bar:** about 4.1 kicks, 14 mid-band hits (5.7 per second times 2.55 s), and a hat comb at 0.16-0.17 s. Median onset IOI of the whole drum stem is 0.174 s (win_0180 and win_0200 notes, 84-86 onsets per 20 s), so 6 onsets per second. A single bar-level silence or fill is not seen.
- **Variation.** Adjacent-bar correlation of the 2-9 kHz drum envelope over 67 bar pairs: mean 0.64, median 0.72, min 0.12, max 0.85. Four-bar spacing: mean 0.42. So the pattern recurs at the bar and varies in detail (extra hits, which colleague notes call ghost notes). Measured, medium.
- **Where the detector failed.** Several window notes (0:20, 2:20, 3:00, 3:20) report the low-band picker as random on every grid. My result differs because I merged the double triggers and used IOIs rather than absolute phase, which does not depend on the tracker's off-phase beats. I also could not get a stable bar-phase fold: the comb phase wandered across 12 s blocks, which tells me the drum pattern is not strictly the same every bar (it shifts hits within a two-bar frame), not that the tempo wanders. Treat the fold-based figures as unavailable.

Summary of the groove (INFERRED, medium): a boom-bap/break-style loop at 94 BPM in straight sixteenths with a syncopated kick on steps 0, 3, 5, 8, 10 (or close variants), a backbeat on beats 2 and 4, and a constant 16th hat comb. This reading rests on band onsets from separated stems; "snare on 2 and 4" is the least certain part.

## 5. Loop regularity and production-era cues (rule 1)

Figures measured:
- **Waveform repeat at the bar lag.** I cross-correlated 2.554 s snippets of the drums stem with the stem one, two and four bars later, searching +/-80 ms. At 1:00, 1:40, 2:00, 2:50: one bar later r = 0.64, 0.66, 0.59, 0.53 at a lag of 0.5 ms; four bars later r = 0.85 (2:00, lag 2.1 ms), 0.75 (2:50, 2.1 ms). A scan of all lags 1-21 s for 2.5 s snippets at 1:02, 1:40, 2:05 and 2:52 finds the strongest peaks at 10.15 and 10.22 s (r 0.76 and 0.83) and at 5.08-5.14 s and 2.555 s (r 0.43-0.66). Waveform correlation at that level, for stems that are demucs estimates with vocal bleed, means the same drum recording (or near-identical hits) recurs. Near-identical repeats of a bar or phrase suggest a loop machine or DAW. INFERRED, medium confidence (not high: some windows show r only 0.2-0.5 and lags of -25 to -70 ms, e.g. 2:30: r 0.19; 0:40: lag -46 ms).
- **Lag steps.** The best repeat lags scatter in roughly 23-25 ms steps (0.5, -25, -45, -70 ms), and the long-lag peaks fall at 5.083, 5.109 and 5.135 s, 10.146 and 10.218 s. So the loop is re-placed with a jitter of a few tens of milliseconds, not sample-exact on a single grid. That fits a sampled phrase re-triggered by hand or a rhythm played against a grid with human feel; it does not fit a purely quantised sequencer. Medium-low.
- **Kick onset spread** (rule 1): 20 ms standard deviation on the 94.0 BPM sixteenth grid (mean absolute 14 ms), against 46 ms for random onsets. Tight but not machine-exact (a quantised kick would be within a few ms). I do not have an unquantised reference, so this is not proof of either live playing or a sequencer.
- **Mix peak:** sample peak 0.0 dBFS over the whole file (window notes quote 0.0 dBFS and 48 samples at or above 0.999 in a 20 s window), mix RMS about -10.5 dBFS, crest factor about 10.5 dB (win_0060, win_0100; I recomputed the whole-file sample peak as 0.00 dBFS). That is the profile of a limited, modern master. Measured, high for the figures; INFERRED medium-high for "modern mastering or limiting" (true peak not measured).
- **Band-limited layer:** colleagues measured 99.9 per cent of the energy below 7.9 kHz in 0:00-0:10, a 40-46 dB cliff above 8-9 kHz, and a mono image (L=R to 0.0005; side/mid -90 to -109 dB per `audio_features/extra/music_extra.json`) for the whole track. The drum stem itself carries broadband transients up to about 8 kHz, then nothing. A band-limited, mono source in a mix whose peak is at full scale suggests older or lo-fi source material (a sampled break and a sampled drone bed) carried inside a modern master, but the whole file is mono and band-limited, so there is no full-band layer to contrast it against. INFERRED, medium-low.

Judgement on production era (INFERRED, confidence medium): modern limiting (peak 0.0 dBFS, crest 10.5 dB) on a file that is also mono and band-limited at 8-9 kHz, built on a looped rhythm (waveform repeat r up to 0.85, bar lag steady to 0.05 BPM for 3.5 minutes). That reads as a sampled or programmed hip-hop-style track assembled in a DAW, probably from older, band-limited source loops, or encoded with a low-pass. I cannot tell from rhythm alone whether the break is archive material.

## 7. Accelerations, stops, and what the pulse does to the feeling

- **Accelerations:** none measured. The bar lag is constant to 0.05 BPM. The tracker's occasional 0.51 s and 0.72 s intervals near 0:18-0:19 (win_0000.md) lie inside the turnaround at 0:16-0:20 and are more likely tracker error than a tempo move, because the drums stem bar lag at 0:20-0:30 is 94.0. Low confidence on the 0:10-0:20 window itself: its bar lag reads 2.508 s (95.7 BPM) but with r 0.36 and only four bars; I cannot exclude a short tempo difference in the intro (speculative).
- **Stops:** none in the drums. The voice and bass drop out for 0.6-1.3 s at 0:11.3-0:12.3, 2:13.3-2:14.0, 2:25.7-2:26.8, 2:29.3-2:29.95, 3:13.8-3:15.1, 3:24.6-3:25.2, 3:34.2-3:35.5 while the drums and bed play on. These stuttered "pull-outs" are the nearest thing to stops; the mix dips 7-12 dB for at most a quarter second (the quietest 0.25 s mean is -22.5 dB at 3:14.75).
- **Feeling.** (INFERRED; the causes are measured, the feeling is my reading.) A restless, forward-pressing but unhurried groove: 94 BPM is a head-nod pace, but the constant sixteenth comb (about 6 onsets per second) and a vocal at about 5 syllables per second fill every sixteenth. The kick avoids the beat (almost no one-beat IOIs) and lands on the dotted positions, so the beat is felt through the backbeat and the bass stabs more than through the kick, which gives a rolling, slightly off-balance push. The bass on/off cycle (about 10 s, with the on-part 2.5 bars) builds and releases tension without any drum change: when the low end is out the groove thins to hats and voice (a "ground gone" feeling), and the return of D2 stabs after a 4-second absence acts as the lift. The groove therefore feels relentless and trance-like on the surface, with structure delivered by subtraction. The whole file is mono and nothing above about 9 kHz, which makes it feel close and boxy rather than wide.
- **Half-time option.** A listener can hear it at about 47 BPM with the backbeat on every second bar-half (win_0060.md). Evidence for that is weak; the kick pattern's two-beat gaps (8 steps, 33 times) support a slower anchor, but the 16th comb works against it.

## 10. Not recoverable by measurement

- **Whether the drums are a sampled break, a drum machine or a played kit.** Waveform repeats (r up to 0.85 at four bars) and a 20 ms kick spread fit more than one source; stems are separation estimates.
- **The exact tempo beyond 94.0-94.5 BPM** (and therefore the "true" tempo the producer entered): the tracker's 95.7 is a measurement artefact, my range comes from drum-stem autocorrelation and a drift fit, not from a click track.
- **Which hits are snare, clap or hat.** I separated bands, not instruments; "backbeat on 2 and 4" is the weakest part of section 3.
- **Whether the "double rise" on each kick is a real second hit.**
- **Whether the bass gating is an arrangement choice or a separation or mixing artefact** (the mix level changes only 1-3 dB when the bass leaves, so the bass stem may be catching partly shared low-end energy with the kick).
- **Whether the half-time or the 94 BPM reading is the one the music "means".** Both are available to a listener.
- **Recording or release date, and whether the rhythm loop is archive material.** Mono, band-limited and a 0.0 dBFS peak can be produced by a modern lo-fi workflow as well as by an old source.
- **Performer intent behind the vocal pull-outs** and whether the unplayed beats are deliberate "breaths" (metaphor) or edit points.
