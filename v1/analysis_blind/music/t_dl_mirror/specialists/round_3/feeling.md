## 3. Why it feels this way (the chain)

1. Floor that never moves: drums stay within about 1.6 dB between hooks and verses and the pulse is steady to 0.05 BPM, so the listener's body is locked in throughout (measured, high).
2. All drama is subtraction and return under a hard ceiling: no loudness build (mix 10 s RMS -10.1 to -10.5 dB), so tension is made by bass withdrawal and return on a 10.16 s loop, plus voice holes (measured, high).
3. The voice switches job: fast monotone narrator (verse) against slow sung refrain (hook); the hook is where the only harmony moves (measured, medium-high).
4. Sound keeps it intimate and claustrophobic: dry, dual-mono, 8 kHz roof, 0 dBFS peaks (measured, medium-high; feeling INFERRED, medium).
5. Repetition teaches the form: eight bass walks, four hooks and four hole patterns of identical shape, so the first is a surprise and the rest are anticipated (INFERRED, medium).

## 4. Where I might still be wrong

- Equal hook levels may come from the limiter, so "no louder voice" may describe the master, not the performance.
- Demucs bleed affects the voice-hole depths and stem-level claims.
- A listener hearing half-time (47 BPM) would feel the groove as slower and heavier; the data give the counting pulse only.
- A listener who follows the words may find the track far darker or more narrative than the measurements suggest.

## Not recoverable by measurement

- How any real listener feels: these are responses the structure invites, not measured ones.
- Performer's intent, irony or meaning of the words: stems give pitch, level and timing; Whisper is unreliable.
- Instrument identity and whether drums or bed are samples: spectra do not name sources.
- Recording date or whether any layer is archive: the 8 kHz ceiling and mono image are the file chain.
- Whether holes, stutters and the 3:34 thinning are performed, gated or edited: the waveform shows the event, not the hand.
- Whether the 2.77 s digital zero belongs to the released track: the file cannot say.
- True tempo (94.0, 94.5 or 95.7): three baselines, three figures.
