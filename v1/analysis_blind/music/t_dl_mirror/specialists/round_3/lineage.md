**Sound Q (round 2 to lineage, via my own question): is the bass a sine-like sub, and does it share a source with the bed?** Sound round 3 (Welch of the bass stem, 1:00-2:00): 60-90 Hz holds -1.7 dB of 20-2000 Hz power, 20-45 Hz only -19 to -22 dB, with real energy at 130-400 Hz (-9 to -11 dB per band). Peak 74.7 Hz is the largest by 14 dB. Harmony round 3 agrees: bass-stem energy above 100 Hz is 5 dB above that below 100 Hz in 3:14-3:30. So the bass is **not a pure sub-sine**; it is a low tone centred at 60-90 Hz with a harmonic skirt. **I withdraw my round-2 octave labels (D1, G1, G2, F2) as facts**: a 74 Hz peak can be the second harmonic of a 37 Hz fundamental, and the labelled walk "G1, F2, D2" may be one octave off. What survives is the pitch-class path G - F - C - D (harmony round 3, medium) and the shared sharp offset (+13 to +30 cents; sound and mine agree in sign, differ in size by pyin and FFT resolution). Shared source with the bed: possible, not shown (low). Whether it is a synthesised bass or a re-pitched sample: not decidable (sound, INFERRED, low; I agree).

**Form Q: hook as "statement, answer, answer without walk"; is the intro built from verse or hook bass density?** Harmony round 3 puts walks at +4.4 to +5.0 s and +14.6 to +15.3 s into each hook (10.2 s apart, one 4-bar loop at 94.5-94.7 BPM). I do not have the intro's bass density measured by me; carried: rhythm round 2 gives verses 27-29 per cent and hooks 84-88 per cent bass-on, form gave the intro as drums and bed with bass in gaps (about 25 per cent). That is verse-like in its density. **So the 4-bar intro is a verse-density stretch with a chanted vocal over it, and the first hook at 0:10.4 is the first thing the bass fills.** Lineage reading, revised: not "hook first, single-style opening" as I put it in round 2, but "a drum-and-bed intro that the first full-bass section follows". INFERRED, low. The hook-before-verse single convention is demoted to speculative.

**Feeling (implicit): does the lineage change the feeling?** Feeling round 2 says it does not need lineage; I agree and add the only lineage-dependent point that matters: a thirdless drone with a short side-step bass is a bed type found in dark rap beats, in rock and funk vamps and in minimal electronic loops, so the listener's "dark" or "tense" feeling comes from the drone and the voice's low register, not from a named genre (INFERRED, low-medium).

## Not recoverable by measurement
- **Instrument identity** of bed, bass and drums (synth, sample, organ, played): only partials, pitch classes and envelopes are visible.
- **Whether any layer is a sample from older music, or the source of one:** a sample processed with the rest would show no separate band-limited layer.
- **Whether the drums are sequenced or played:** kick phase is at chance on this detector, so nothing about timing precision can be shown.
- **Whether mutes, gates and gaps are edits, gates or performance, and why:** the level drop shows, the cause does not.
- **Whether the +13 to +30 cent offset is deliberate, speed-related or tracker error:** a pitch figure cannot show intent.
- **Bass octave and whether the G chord is major or minor:** harmonics and weak chroma cannot separate them.
- **Recording or release date, scene, region, performer, and which artists the track descends from:** resemblance in sound is not provenance.
- **Words and what they mean to a listener:** only unstable ASR transcripts exist.
