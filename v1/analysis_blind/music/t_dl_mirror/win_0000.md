## 8. Production-era cues (INFERRED)

1. **Modern limiting or clipping: INFERRED, high confidence.** Sample peak 0.0 dBFS with 6,720 samples at 0.999 or above in 20 s; mix RMS -10 to -11 dB; crest factor 11.9 dB over 0:00-0:10. I cannot separate "modern master" from "loud hip-hop mixdown"; both are post-1990s practice.
2. **Narrow-band, mono layer under or inside the mix: INFERRED, medium-high confidence.** Over 0:00-0:10, L=R to within 0.0005 and 99.9% of energy below 7.9 kHz, with a 40 dB cliff above 8 kHz. That is the profile of a band-limited, mono sampled source (old record, broadcast or lo-fi sample) that carries the drums and the other layer. The full-width arrival at 0:10-0:20 (side/mid -19 dB; 99.9% below 9.3 kHz; the 0:10.67 and 0:18.45 bursts reach beyond 9 kHz) shows the later layers are wider and slightly brighter than the first 10 s. So there are at least two sources: a narrow-band mono bed and a voice recorded or processed separately. The measurement cannot say the bed is archival, only that it behaves like a bandwidth-limited sample. CLAP's "vinyl crackle" is near 1.0 for every 5 s window; it is a weak, nonspecific tag (it is always 1.0) and I give it no weight.
3. **Loop regularity: INFERRED, low-medium confidence.** Drum waveform correlation between consecutive bars (4 beats = 2.53 s from 0:00.28, 0:02.81, 0:05.34) is only 0.04-0.08, and for the other stem 0.10, -0.16, -0.15. So the repeats are not waveform-identical, which argues against a sample-exact DAW loop of one drum recording. But the onset-envelope autocorrelation at the bar (2.55 s) is 0.32 on the drums, so the rhythm recurs. The separation algorithm and phase drift between hits would also lower waveform correlation. I cannot settle this.
4. **Kick onset spread: not reliably measured.** I band-passed the drums stem at 40-120 Hz and picked onsets 0:00-0:20: 90 onsets with a standard deviation of 84 ms from a 16th grid at 94.8 BPM and widely scattered inter-onset intervals (0.03 to 0.65 s), so that detector is picking up more than kicks (sub bleed, ghost notes, bass leakage). I cannot report a trustworthy kick-grid spread from this stem in this window, and I am not using it to claim either tightly quantised or loose timing. The tracker's beat intervals (0.58-0.65 s) suggest the pulse is not rigid (see section 3).
5. Overall judgement: modern production (heavily limited, voice added in 0:10) laid over a band-limited, mono sample-like bed. INFERRED, medium confidence.

- Overall: urgent but restrained, a groove piece before a verse. Feelings are my inference from the measurements; I cannot verify them.

- Whether the mono, 8 kHz-limited bed is a vintage recording, a broadcast clip or just a filtered sample: the band limit shows the bandwidth, not the age or origin.

- Whether D versus Dm is a real major/minor choice: the chord estimator flips on the missing third; the key is only D minor in the whole-song estimate.
- The recording date, performer, and intent (why the bass vanishes at 0:06): not in the audio.
