## 7. Production-era cues (all INFERRED)

- Peak level: sample peak reaches 0.0 dBFS in the window and across the file; per-second RMS of the mix is -8 to -13 dBFS. For 95 percent of whole-track seconds the peak is above -1 dBFS (0.89 linear). That says the master is normalised or limited to full scale, which is typical of modern mastering (and of a loud-mastered re-release of older material). RMS crest of about 9 to 13 dB: not heavily crushed. Confidence medium that it was processed to full scale; low-medium that this is "modern".
- Band-limiting: the whole mix has a hard cliff at about 9 kHz (46 dB in 1 kHz) and 0.02 percent energy above 8 kHz; and it is mono (width 0.0). A narrow-band, mono mix inside a full-range master would point to old or low-fi source material, but here nothing full-range sits above it: the entire mix is band-limited, so I cannot split "old layer" from "modern layer". This is what a low-bitrate or low-sample-rate source, a deliberately lo-fi master, or a mono sample-based track would give. CLAP's "vinyl crackle" at 0.7 to 1.0 is weak support for old-sounding material only. Confidence medium that the audio is band-limited and mono at source; low that this means "old".
- Loop regularity: bar-to-bar drum timbre correlation 0.997 (the same hits re-used) but rhythm placement correlation 0.32; the drum placement varies. Neither a fixed DAW loop nor sync to a strict grid (kick spread equals chance, section 3). INFERRED, low-medium: sampled drum sounds, played or re-sequenced with variation (human or a chopped break).
- Overall: the track reads as a deliberately produced hip-hop-style recording of a loud mastered file with a mono band-limited character; I cannot call it either a single-period recording or a modern layer on old archive material from these figures. Confidence low.
