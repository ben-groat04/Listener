## 3. Rhythm
- **Tempo:** tracker 95.7 BPM (`audio_features/summary.json`); beat IOI in this window 0.627 s (measured, 31 beats). Kick-band onsets (drums stem low-passed at 150 Hz, my detector) have a **median IOI 0.313 s**, i.e. exactly half the beat: they sit on the **8th-note grid of the 95.7 BPM tracker**, which is the correct comparison grid. Because the low-passed drums stem also catches toms and snare body, treat "kick" as "low-drum onset" (medium).
- **Kick onset spread:** deviation from the tracker 8th grid: mean absolute 83 ms, s.d. 95 ms (measured). That is large, but the tracker beat phase may sit off the true downbeat (a constant offset inflates this number), and I did not remove the offset. So this is not yet a statement of looseness. This is a **syncopated, varying kick pattern**, not four-on-the-floor. A strictly quantised grid would show repeating IOIs; the varied IOIs fit a sampled or played hip-hop kick pattern with swing or off-beat placement.
- **Density:** about 59 low-drum onsets in 20 s, roughly 3 per second; the pattern thickens in 1:16 to 1:20 (IOIs 0.1 to 0.2 s at 1:16.29 to 1:16.67, 1:17.9 to 1:18.4): a short hi-density burst, the closest thing to a fill (medium).
- **Pulse:** steady throughout; no tempo drift visible in beat IOI. Bass gating, not the drums, creates the rhythmic variation.
- **Feel:** a 95.7 BPM mid-tempo head-nod pulse, with a half-time feel if heard as about 48 BPM backbeats (INFERRED, low).

## 7. Production-era cues (INFERRED)
- **Peak and loudness:** native sample peak 0.0 dBFS exactly, window RMS -10.5 dBFS, crest 10.5 dB. This reads as a **modern, limited master** (confidence medium-high; a 1990s-style hip-hop CD master could also be at 0 dBFS, so the era within the modern range is not fixed).
- **Loop regularity:** the "other" layer shows the same harmonic ladders for the whole window and the chroma holds D and A (almost unchanged for 17 s). That is DAW or sample-loop behaviour (INFERRED, medium). The rapped vocal is not looped (different words every bar, lead notes do not repeat).
- **Kick onset spread:** the low-drum IOIs (0.15 to 0.65 s, 8th-grid deviation s.d. 95 ms with the phase uncorrected) are too irregular for a quantised drum machine alone. Either a played or sampled-groove drum part, or a tracker phase offset. INFERRED, low. I cannot call it "live drummer": the measured spread does not discriminate.
- **Band-limited layer:** the mix has a hard roll-off near 10 kHz (0.0 % of energy above 10 kHz; image top band dark). A cut at this frequency in all stems is more consistent with a lossy-codec or a lowpassed source file than with an old recording; it is a property of the file, not of the musical layers separately. No separate narrow-band/noisy older layer is measurable in the "other" stem, whose spectrum is clean harmonic ladders (INFERRED, medium).
- **Mono:** L/R correlation 0.99999: a mono source or mono mixdown. Mono delivery is common in older material and in downmixed uploads; I can't separate the two (low).
- **Overall:** a modern-sounding hip-hop-style production (drum-machine/sample drums, mono, loud) rather than archive material layered under it. CLAP: "hip hop" 0.97 to 1.0 and "drum machine" 0.69 to 1.0 at 1:00 to 1:20 (weak evidence, but agrees with the tempo, the rapped lead, the bass-heavy spectrum). CLAP's "ice skating" and "vinyl crackle" labels (0.87 to 1.0) are noise-model artefacts and not evidence of vinyl: I found no crackle measurement.

- **Mood:** I'd call it a nervy, wry, determined bounce, partly because of D minor (INFERRED, medium) and the fast spoken delivery. The comic effect of the text is not from the music.
