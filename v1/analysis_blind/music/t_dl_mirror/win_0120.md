- Loop regularity: drum-envelope bar correlations 0.15-0.75 (mean about 0.46), so not sample-identical bars; the pitch contour of the hook repeats but is not note-identical. This window continues that for 12 s, then the rap verse ends on the word "blood" (2:11.7) and the track breaks into the hook: a stutter-edit at 2:12, a 0.7 s vocal gap at 2:13.3-2:14.0, and a sung, higher chant (G4-A4 versus A#3) with a bass that stays in. So this window is release-from-verse into hook, with tension carried by the vocal gap.

## 9. What a listener feels, and why

- Drive and a steady, slightly swaggering groove: constant drum pulse at about 94 BPM, with 8th-note chatter on top and kick on every beat (measured IOI 0.64 s, 0.32 s). The loose kick timing (std 58 ms) adds a human, swinging feel (low-medium confidence).
- Tension and stasis through 2:00-2:11: the D-A drone never changes (chroma D and A constant), the rap melody stays within 3-4 semitones, and the bass appears and vanishes. Dropouts of the bass (-50 dB) make each return feel like a weight landing.
- A jolt then release at 2:12-2:14: a 0.25 s mix gap (-9 dB) and a 0.7 s vocal gap (-42 dB) set up the hook, which then arrives higher (8-10 semitones up) and slightly more distant in the mix: the feeling of lift, opening and a slightly dreamy, circling chant.
- A faint lift or leaning-forward at 2:17.7 as the harmony finally moves (Gm to F to Am to Dm): the first harmonic change after 40 s.
- Loud and compressed: peaks at 0 dBFS and 10 dB crest give a dense, pushing, "in your face" feel; the mono, band-limited sound (no energy above 10 kHz, near-zero side) makes it feel centred and a little dry or lo-fi rather than airy.
- Mood: CLAP says "playful"/"hopeful" in 5 s windows (0.4-0.99) but that is weak evidence; the narrative (a road fight, blood) and the minor drone would otherwise read as tense and dark. I would call the mix tense-but-bouncy; mood is not firmly supported by measurement. low.
