## 0. The shape in one paragraph
This is a 3:09 piece that never stops moving and never changes key. It is a long, steady build and release carried almost entirely by texture and low-end, not by tune or chord progression. Drums run for the whole file (LV drums -16 to -20 dB, no gap except a tiny dropout near 2:47.8 and the final stop). A spoken voice appears in short bursts (a sampled announcer, no singing). The emotional arc comes from four things: the bass coming and going, the "other" (guitar/synth wall) swelling and thinning, stereo width opening and closing, and the voice stepping in and out. Mood overall: urgent, grey, determined, quietly heroic, with an undercurrent of wistfulness; closing with a hard cut, not a resolution.

Whole-track curve (LV, 10 s means): mix level -14.6 dBFS at the start, -11.6 by 0:20, a plateau of about -11 to -12.5 to 1:10, a dip to -14.3 at 1:20 (bass gone), peak -11.0 at 1:40, a trough -15.1 at 2:00 (the quietest section), then a climb to -11.4 and a plateau to the end. Width: 0.31 at the start, 0.5 to 0.55 through the middle, a narrow 0.29 at 2:00, a wide 0.78 at 1:30 and 0.71 to 0.75 for the final 40 s (high confidence in the numbers; medium that width drives felt "openness").

## 1. Moment-by-moment journey

**0:00-0:09, ignition and suspense (high on events, medium-high on feeling).** Drums alone at 0:00 (mix -19.9 dBFS, drums -20.4, bass and vocals at floor, LV t=0). A dense harmonic "other" layer slams in at about 0:01.3 (other -60.6 to -30.5 dB at t=1; WN 0000), and the bass creeps up over about 16 s (about -44 dB at 0:03 to -19.5 at 0:20). Harmony is a pedal D with little movement (WN 0000, CH). Feeling: a film-opening pulse, motion without a destination, tension from a slow swell with nothing yet resolved. The 78 BPM beat with about 4 drum onsets per beat (0.19 s gaps; WN 0000/0020 onset passes) is a slow pulse under a nervous fast one: it makes the music feel patient and impatient at once.

**0:09-0:32, the announcer and the groove settling (medium-high).** The first chord change (G, C, D) at about 0:08-0:10 and the voice at 0:10 (LV vocals about -22 dB) give a human on top of the machine. The voice is speech, centre, band-limited (WN 0000/0020; MV vocals panel), so it reads as documentary or archive reportage, "someone telling us what is about to happen". The stoic, slightly cold colour comes from the D major/minor flicker (F vs F#), which CH and AF both show as unresolved (medium; chord estimators are noisy on distorted mixes). The mix reaches its loudest early point at 0:20-0:30 (-11.6 dBFS).

**0:32-0:40, release into the groove (medium-high).** The voice leaves at about 0:31.7 (vocals to about -64 dB), width climbs about 0.36 to 0.5, and the bass gets brighter and louder from about 0:35 (bass share above 300 Hz 15% to 29%, WN 0020; bass about -15 dB at 0:33-0:38). Feeling: the music "takes over"; it feels rawer and the room bigger. A modest payoff without any change of chord.

**0:40-1:00, the slow tightening (medium-high).** Three speech bursts (0:41, 0:50.8-0:53, 0:54-0:56.5) sit on a groove that never ducks. The "other" stem rises about 10 dB from -24 at 0:40 to about -14 at 0:57-0:58 (LV, WN 0040) while the bass steps back 3 to 5 dB. The effect is rising pressure and a slightly grim, wind-in-the-face determination. CLAP's "nostalgic" at 0.95 to 1.0 and "triumphant" 0.58 in 0:45-1:00 are weak but agree with a stoic-wistful reading of a D with a flat seventh.

**1:00-1:15, steady effort, gentle melancholy (medium).** Loud (-11 dBFS), dense, no dropouts; width rises about 0.4 to 0.7 by 1:06-1:14 (WN 0060). The D to Bm sway and the third's ambiguity give a bittersweet colour inside the drive.

**1:15-1:27, hollowing-out, then an announcement (high on events).** The bass is cut within about a second at 1:15 (LV t=75 -28.7, t=76 -65.2) and the mix drops 4 to 5 dB. This is the first real "held breath" of the track. Then the voice returns at 1:18-1:20 (chord moves A to C, WN 0060) and reads numbers and groups (WN 0080; the nationality list is unreliable as two transcripts disagree). Feeling: exposure and loneliness, then formality, a roll-call.

**1:27-1:40, the surge without a floor (medium-high).** At 1:27 the voice stops, "other" fills 100 Hz to 8 kHz and jumps -22/-27 to -17/-15 dB, width nearly doubles 0.4 to 0.8 (LV t=87 width 0.62; 1:30 block mean 0.78). The bass is absent for 12 s (LV 1:20-1:39 about -62 dB), so this is an open, floating, yearning wall of sound over a pedal D (Am, C, Dm alternating, WN 0080). I read it as hovering suspense with a heroic tint: wide and big but weightless.

**1:40, the single strongest "drop" in the track (high).** Bass from -53.8 dB at 1:39 to -19.1 at 1:40 (LV t=99/100), mix up to -11.1 then -10.3, width narrows from 0.70 to 0.40 as the low end locks everything together. After 12 s of weightlessness the song suddenly has a floor: the felt effect is gravity and punch, the sound of the whole band arriving.

**1:40-1:55, locked-in power with two interruptions (medium-high).** Drums peak at 1:51-1:54 (-14 dB). Voice bursts at 1:46-1:48 (intrusion on the groove) and about 1:57-1:58.5 (a call).

**1:55-2:13, the trough, the quiet heart of the track (high on events, medium on feeling).** Bass off at 1:55-1:56 (-33.5 then -62.5), "other" falls -14.6 at 1:55 to -25.9 at 1:58 and about -31 to -33 at 1:59-2:00, width collapses to about 0.2-0.3. Feeling: a narrow, cold, hypnotic space, the ear waiting, each bass note a body-felt thump and release. This is where the music feels loneliest and most like a long march across empty ground.

**2:13-2:19, lift (high on events).** "Other" steps up from about -25/-31 to -19/-22 dB at 2:13 and the chords start to move for the first time in over a minute: Dm, Am, D, G, D, Dm (WN 0120, CH). Feeling: "here we go", a payoff. CLAP "triumphant" 0.996 at 2:10-2:15 agrees, weakly.

**2:19-2:40, the pumping eight-beat cycle (medium-high).** Sub-bass bursts every about 6.1 s (2:19.5, 2:25.7, 2:31.9, 2:38.3; dominant about 43 Hz; WN 0140) with a long Dm on each, then quick one-beat chords (Am D G D G; Em D Gm Am C). **2:38-2:48, arrival of the continuous bass (medium-high).** The bass becomes continuous at 2:38 (-29 to -19 dB) and stays to the end. The announcer speaks 2:44.3-2:48.2 and then, at 2:47.8, a 0.35 s dropout (about 12 dB dip; WN 0160) gives a final held breath.

**2:48-3:07, locked, wide, heavy (medium-high).** After the dropout the "other" layer is brighter to about 9-10 kHz, width goes to about 0.7 and then 0.75, the chord is a pure D drone (WN 0160/0180), 78% of the mix energy is under 500 Hz (WN 0180), and drum, bass and "other" onsets coincide (0.186 s median gap). A broadband hiss (CLAP "vinyl crackle" 0.92-0.98; low confidence) adds a worn, filmic grain. Feeling: heaviness, trance, power, a sense of arrival with no new information, the sound of being fully inside the engine. Rising urgency in the last 3 s (drum density doubles from 3:06.25, chroma lifts to G/E; WN 0180).

**3:08.3-3:08.7, the cut (high).** Level falls -19.5 to -57 dB in about 0.2 s with no tail. The ending is a "slammed door": the harmony never cadences and the groove just stops. This feels abrupt, unfinished, and invites the next part (consistent with this being a first part of a two-part piece, which I do not claim from the measurements).

## 2. Cross-cutting causes of the feeling
1. **Constant 16th-note drum stream at 78 BPM** (about 4 to 5 onsets per second; WN 0000-0180): the engine of "drive, machine, march". It never rests, so every change in feeling must come from the other layers. High confidence.
2. **Bass as the emotional valve.** Bass absent: exposure, lightness, suspense (1:15-1:40, 1:55-2:13). Bass present: weight, safety, punch. Its on/off edges at 1:15, 1:40, 1:55 and 2:38 are the biggest felt events. High confidence in timings, medium-high that they are the main emotional levers.
3. **Pedal D with an unresolved third.** Held tonic plus D major/minor ambiguity (AF: 0.76 vs 0.756) gives "stoic, determined, wistful-heroic" and prevents either joy or sadness. Medium; the chord labels are weak under distortion and speech.
4. **Spoken voice instead of melody.** No tune anywhere (lead_notes are speech inflection or bleed, WN 0000-0140). The listener's human anchor is an announcer: factual, archival, distance. High on speech, medium on the "archive" interpretation.
5. **Width as scale.** Narrow (0.2-0.3) for intimacy or coldness, wide (0.7-0.8) for expanse. Medium.
6. **Compression and density.** The mix sits at -11 to -12 dBFS most of the time; the dynamic range is small, so the track feels like a wall pressing forward rather than a breathing one, apart from 1:15 and 1:55-2:13.

## 3. Overall character
Urgent, mechanical, determined, cold-bright, quietly heroic; endurance rather than celebration or grief. It is more trance-like and cumulative than song-like. The nearest everyday feeling is a long effortful journey in poor weather with something being announced over a loudspeaker, a mix of grit, tension and a little awe. Interpretation, not measurement.

## 4. What measurements cannot tell me
- Instrument identity: "guitar", "synth", "drum machine" are CLAP-derived guesses and spectral texture only.
- True major or minor (the third is ambiguous), and whether D/Dm flips are real chord changes or detector artefacts.
- Whether the felt tempo is 78 or 156 BPM (the 16th stream could be heard as 8ths at 156).
- The actual words and tone of the narration (Whisper passes disagree), hence whether the speech sounds warm, formal or sombre.
- Any riff: the lead tracker follows speech and bleed, not a melody.
- Subjective reaction: everything under "feeling" is inferred from typical responses, so confidence is medium at best.

## 5. Three questions for fellow specialists
1. **Harmony:** Is the F/F# flip in the D chords a genuine alternation (a riff using both thirds, a modal mixture) or a detector artefact on distortion? Does any sustained third or a b7 (C natural) dominate the long D-pedal sections (1:27-1:40, 2:48-3:07)? That decides whether I call the colour "wistful" or "neutral".
2. **Rhythm:** Is the felt pulse 78 BPM with 16ths or 156 BPM with 8ths, and do the 6.1 s bass phrases (2:19.5, 2:25.7, 2:31.9, 2:38.3; 8 beats) and the 1:15/1:40/1:55 edges fall on bar boundaries (a 4/4 grid and 16-bar or 8-bar blocks)? This affects whether the drops feel "on the one".
3. **Sound:** Can you tell, from spectral shape, whether the "other" wall is one saturated instrument or several (including the 1:27 and 2:48 brightening to 8 to 10 kHz), and is the hiss after 2:48 a deliberate vinyl/film layer or distortion byproduct?
