# video1: RHYTHM specialist, round 1 (blind, measurements only)

Evidence keys: WN = win_NNNN.md notes (all ten read); BT = audio_features/beat_times.json and summary.json; MV = music_views/window_NNNNs.png; LV = per_second_levels.json; CL = clap_tags.json. Own measurements: v1/scripts/agents/rhythm_r1.py, rhythm_r1b.py, rhythm_r1c.py (drum stem from stems_raw/htdemucs/song/drums.wav, mix from audio/song.wav). Times m:ss. I cannot hear; "kick/snare/hat" below means band-limited energy in the drum stem, not identified instruments.

## 1. Headline

A **metronomically steady 78.0 BPM, 4/4, with an unbroken 16th-note drum stream** (about 4.5 onsets per second, which is 4 per beat), carried for 3:08 with **no tempo drift, no tempo change and essentially no stop** except one half-beat cut at 2:47.8 and a roll-and-cut ending. The feel is "slow beat, fast engine": a heavy, rolling, mechanical churn. The pulse never changes; the music's drama comes from layers entering and leaving over it (bass swell, spoken sample, the 1:15 bass dropout, the 1:27 wall, the 2:20-2:40 bass bursts), and from how many 16th slots are left empty.

## 2. True tempo and the octave question

- BT: 78.3 BPM, 218 beats, median gap 0.766 s, gap std 0.019 s (my read of beat_times.json). The summary's local-tempo percentiles (10/50/90 = 78/108/152) show the tracker flipping between 78 and about 152-156 and sometimes 104-ish; that is octave/triplet confusion, not real tempo change.
- My own fit: folding the drum-stem onset envelope at candidate beat periods in six 30 s blocks gives the best period **0.769 s (78.0 BPM) in every block** (0:00, 0:30, 1:00, 1:30, 2:00, 2:30: 78.02 / 77.97 / 78.02 / 78.02 / 77.97 / 78.02). Whole-track 16th-grid fit: 0.1923 s = 78.02 BPM. That is dead flat to within measurement noise, so the track is grid-locked: sequenced, or a click-tracked performance with the drums quantised. Confidence high.
- The tracker's odd moment around 1:25-1:28 (beats at 84.82, 85.59, 86.38, 87.31, 87.93: gaps 0.77, 0.79, 0.93, 0.63) coincides with the 1:27 texture change (WN win_0080). My fold shows no tempo change there. I read it as a tracker artefact (medium-high).
- **Octave resolution.** Autocorrelation of the drum onset envelope (rhythm_r1.py): peaks at 0.197 s (rel 0.32), 0.383 (0.54), 0.766 (0.69), 1.544 (0.61), 2.31 (0.64) and **3.077 s (0.72, the highest)**. Strong periodicity at 4 beats (one bar of 4/4, 3.08 s) and weaker at the 8th (0.38 s) than at the quarter, with the 16th weakest, so the metrical hierarchy is 16th < 8th < beat < bar. That supports **beat = 78 BPM, 4/4, 16th subdivision**. The alternative reading (156 BPM with steady 8ths, or 8ths treated as the pulse) is a legitimate way to *tap* it: the 16th stream is so even that a listener may well feel a fast 156 BPM "8th-note" motor with half-time weight on 78. I rate: 78 as notated tempo, high; 156 as a common felt tap, medium. Harmonic rhythm supports 78: chord labels change at 0.77 s grid and bass bursts recur every 8 beats (below).
- Meter: 4/4 (bar 3.07 s) is favoured by the 4-beat autocorrelation peak and by the 8-beat cycle seen in 2:20-2:40; 2/4 or 8/8-at-156 cannot be excluded from the measurements (medium).

## 3. The drum pattern

- **IOI histogram** (rhythm_r1.py, 734 onsets, median 0.197 s): modes at 0.16-0.20 s (about 490 intervals) and 0.36 s (177 intervals), tiny at 0.08 and 0.32/0.56. So the pattern is a **16th-note stream with roughly one in four hits left out (a single missing 16th, giving 0.38 s gaps)**, not a straight unbroken 16th hat. WN win_0040, win_0140 describe the same "0.19, 0.19, 0.37" cell.
- **Where the empty slots are.** Folding the drum envelope into one beat (32 bins) shows four onset peaks per beat spaced exactly a 16th apart. Folding into one bar (16 steps, rhythm_r1b.py) shows the mid and high bands strongest on the four beat positions (steps 0, 4, 8, 12 roughly equal, mid 0.9/0.8/0.9/1.0 in 0:10-1:15; no strong 2-and-4 backbeat contrast) and **consistently empty at steps 6 and 11** (high-band 0.31/0.02 in 0:10-1:15; 0.11/0.16 in 1:30-2:00; 0.04/0.09 at 2:10-2:45). The same dips recur in the low band (0.12, 0.13). So the bar has a recurring syncopated shape: the hits cluster on the beat, then the "and-of-2" and "a-of-3" 16ths are skipped. This is my best reading of a groove; the step numbering depends on my phase choice (step 0 is the first strong slot, not a verified downbeat), so the exact bar position is **medium-low confidence**, while the existence of recurring gaps is high.
- **Kick-like (30-150 Hz drum-stem band) energy is busier and more syncopated than the mid band**: in 0:10-1:15 the low band peaks on steps 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10, 13 (about 0.8-1.0) rather than just on the beats. So the low drum content is off-beat heavy (a dotted/offbeat kick figure) while the mid/high content states the beat. Medium confidence: demucs drum stems leak bass and the three bands are crude.
- Hat-like high band (5-10 kHz) is strongest at 0.97-1.0 on the beats and falls to 0.2-0.5 on the weak 16ths: an accented stream, not flat.
- **Pattern evolution** (fraction of IOIs near 0.19 s / near 0.38 s, per 20 s block; rhythm_r1c.py): 0:00 0.71/0.21; 0:20 0.74/0.23; 0:40 0.70/0.30; 1:00 0.61/0.38; 1:20 0.69/0.27; 1:40 0.78/0.22; 2:00 0.62/0.32; 2:20 0.51/0.36; 2:40 0.73/0.13; 3:00 0.39/0.32 (plus 18 % sub-0.15 s hits, the final roll). Onset counts per 10 s: 40-46 up to 1:50, 32-39 from 1:50-2:30, 29-44 later. So the pattern is **densest in the first minute (about 4.3/s) and in 1:40-2:00, and sparsest around 2:20-2:40 and the last ten seconds**. The mid-track "thinning" is the same pulse with more rests, not a tempo change.
- **From about 0:33 onward the drum stem shows a one-16th hole in nearly every beat** (drums-stem dropouts of 0.15-0.20 s recurring every 0.75-0.8 s from 0:33.1 to at least 1:22, rhythm_r1c.py), whereas 0:00-0:32 has no such holes. This lines up with WN win_0020's observation that the drum centroid jumps from about 1.6 kHz to 3.3 kHz at 0:32 and the voice leaves: the groove changes character, from a fuller (probably kick/snare body plus hats) to a brighter, hat-led, more gapped pattern. Confidence medium (stem separation could account for part of the centroid change).

## 4. Structure of the pulse in time (what happens when)

- **0:00-0:00.9 pickup.** The drum stem rises smoothly from -67 dB to -35 dB over about 0.7 s (a reverse-swell or riser: unpitched, smooth ramp), then the first hit lands at about 0:00.74 at -9 dB, with further hits from 0:00.95 onward; the first beat in BT is 0:00.95 (rhythm_r1d probe). WN win_0000 calls it a "broadband smear"; the smooth monotonic ramp followed by a sharp hit is what a riser into a downbeat looks like. Medium-high. The "other" layer enters at about 0:01.3, drums are audible and rhythmic for the whole rest of the window.
- **0:09-0:20 spoken sample over a locked grid.** WN win_0000 notes phrase starts at 0:10.2, 0:13.9, 0:16.6, 0:18.9, near beat times (10.22, 16.35, 18.67/19.43); I treat this as weak evidence of deliberate placement; the speech is not itself rhythmic.
- **0:20-0:32 and 0:32-0:40.** Steady; at 0:32 the brightening and holes appear (above).
- **0:40-1:00.** Beat grid steady (0:40.2, 0:41.0, ...), brief rhythmic hiccup at 0:42.7-0:43.0 where the "other" cuts (WN win_0040; low-medium).
- **1:00-1:15.** Loudest, steadiest groove; drums -19 to -20 dB; 21/18/20/21 onsets per 5 s (WN win_0060). **1:15: the bass is cut abruptly** and the "other" thins to held tones; the drums carry the pulse across unchanged (WN win_0060). Rhythmically this is a subtraction, not a change in the pulse; the drum fraction of the mix rises by about 2-3 dB (drums -17 to -18 dB).
- **1:27 "arrival".** Voice stops and the dense, wide texture appears; drum level steady (-18 to -20 dB). The surge is harmonic/spectral, not rhythmic (WN win_0080).
- **1:40-2:00.** Drum intensity swell at about 1:51-1:54 (+4-6 dB, WN win_0100: medium), bass dropout at 1:55, "other" collapse at 1:58. Onset density is the highest of the later sections (fraction near 0.19 s = 0.78 in 1:40-2:00). No stop.
- **2:00-2:20.** Periodic dark gaps in the drum panel at about 2:02.7, 2:07.1, 2:09-2:10 and 2:15, matching bass entries (WN win_0120); no total stop.
- **2:20-2:40: the 8-beat bass cycle.** Bass bursts start at 2:19.5, 2:25.7, 2:31.9, 2:38.3 (WN win_0140; from MV and per-second bass levels). Intervals 6.2, 6.2, 6.4 s, versus 8 beats = 6.15 s at 78 BPM (8 x 0.769). So the bass is entering on **a two-bar (8-beat) cycle**, locked to the grid. That is the clearest measured evidence of bar-level structure in the track: one bar is 3.08 s, two bars 6.15 s. In this section the drums are at their sparsest (16th fraction 0.51), with a visible thinning at 2:32-2:35 (stem RMS dips to 0.02-0.04 while the voice peaks) and again at 2:37-2:38, re-filling as the bass returns at 2:38.3.
- **The stop at 2:47.8.** My 20 ms RMS scan: mix at about -10/-13 dB until 2:47.76, falls to about -20 to -25 dB between 2:47.8 and 2:48.2 (about 0.4-0.45 s, roughly **half a beat**), then returns at -10 dB with a hit at 2:48.24; the drum stem decays continuously from about 2:47.6 to -55/-60 dB by 2:47.9-2:48.2 and re-enters at 2:48.23 at -11 dB (rhythm_r1d probe). So it is a **short drum-and-everything dropout, not a silence** (the mix keeps a residual about 12 dB down, probably tails of the sample/reverb; medium-low on the cause). The nearest beats are 2:47.14 and 2:47.97, i.e. the cut ends within about 0.25 s of a beat and its end lands off the 0.769 s grid by 0.075 s (low significance given tracker precision). High confidence it is deliberate. It falls at the end of a spoken phrase (WN win_0160).
- **After the stop (2:48-3:06).** Drums come back a bit lower (about -17 to -20 dB per-second versus -13.7 to -16 before) but brighter (WN win_0160). Isolated one-second dips in drums/bass at 3:00-3:05 (WN win_0180: low confidence, possibly separation artefacts).
- **The fill and ending (3:06-3:08.6).** From 3:06.25 the drum onsets compress to 0.09-0.11 s spacing (WN win_0180) for about 1.5 s: **32nd-note roll**, with the sub-0.15 s fraction rising to 0.18 in the final block (my IOI measure); the drum stem level sits near -15 to -16 dB then falls to -26, -48, -66 dB over the last 0.5 s of the stem and the mix falls from -12 dB (3:08.0) to -24 (3:08.4), -46, -58 and silence by 3:08.6. The track therefore **ends on a roll into a fast fade or hard cut, not a ritard**. High confidence on the roll, medium on "hard cut vs 0.4 s fade".

## 5. How the pulse drives the feeling

- **Relentless, journey-like momentum (high).** A 3-minute fixed tempo with a drum stream that never stops, 4 onsets per beat, is the sensation of travelling at constant pace: no rubato, no breath. The slow 78 BPM beat keeps it from sounding frantic, so it reads as **determined and heavy** (a march or a slog) rather than excited.
- **Machine-like, not human (medium-high).** Grid drift is below 0.1 % across 3 minutes, the 16th stream is flat in timing and there is no sign of swing (the 0.19 s IOIs are evenly spaced); CL says "drum machine" at 0.6-1.0 in many windows (weak evidence, noisy model). This fits sequenced or tightly programmed drums, but a very good drummer playing to a click cannot be excluded.
- **Syncopation as a limp/stagger.** The recurring empty 16ths (steps 6 and 11 in my bar fold; the 0.37 s IOIs) give a stuttering, skittering quality over the stable beat; because the gaps always fall in the same places, it comes across as a riff, not a looseness. WN win_0140 calls it "skittering/stuttering".
- **Suspense from stasis, release by subtraction and addition.** Because the pulse never changes, tension is made by harmony-and-layer events: the first 7 s drum-and-pedal intro, the bass cutting at 1:15, the 8-beat bass bursts, the half-beat stop at 2:47.8. The stop is the loudest rhythmic event in the track because everything else is constant; it works as a gasp.
- **Gradual thinning toward the end** (lower 16th fraction 2:20-2:40, fewer onsets 1:50-2:30) gives a slight loosening, then the closing roll tightens it again and cuts.

## 6. What the measurements cannot tell

- Whether the drums are a machine or a kit; which strokes are kick, snare or hat (bands are only a proxy; demucs leaks bass and "other" into drums).
- The true bar downbeat (step 0 in my fold is the first strong slot, not verified) and whether the strongest mid-band beat is a snare backbeat (the four beats look equal, so I see **no standard 2-and-4 backbeat contrast**, which is itself a finding: the beat is stated evenly).
- Whether a listener feels 78 or 156 BPM (both defensible).
- Swing: the IOIs are even, but I did not measure sub-20 ms timing deviations per hit.
- Whether the 2:47.8 residual -22 dB is reverb tail or a held sample.

## 7. Cross-checks and disagreements with window notes

- WN win_0000 interprets the 1.5 s recurring wider gaps as a "two-beat accent"; my fold shows the gaps are every beat from 0:33 but not before, so that early 1.5 s pattern is probably the same one-16th hole pattern in a different phase (medium-low).
- WN win_0160 says "I did not check whether the pulse is 78 or 156": I did (section 2) and prefer 78, with 156 as a felt tap.
- WN win_0120 reports "periodic dark gaps every two bars" while WN win_0140 gives 8-beat bass bursts; I confirm the 8-beat (two-bar) cycle numerically (6.2 s).
- WN win_0000 says drums are "a pickup" of about 1 s; I read it as a 0.7 s riser into a hit at 0:00.74.
