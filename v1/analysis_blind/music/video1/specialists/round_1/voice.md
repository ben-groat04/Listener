# video1: VOICE specialist, round 1 (blind, measurements only)

Evidence keys: **VS** = vocals stem (`stems_raw/htdemucs/song/vocals.wav`); **PSL** = `music_views/per_second_levels.json`; **MV** = `music_views/window_NNNNs.png`; **LN** = `lead_notes.json`; **LY-T / LY-E** = Whisper medium Dutch transcribe / translate in `lyrics/`; **CLAP** = `clap_tags.json`; **BT** = `audio_features/beat_times.json` (78.3 BPM, beat 0.766 s); **mine** = `v1/scripts/agents/video1_voice_r1.py` and `video1_voice_r1b.py` (my own VS runs: RMS segmentation, pyin pitch, spectral roll-off, beat-phase of onsets). I read all ten window notes (win_0000 to win_0180) and re-checked their voice claims. Blind mode kept. I cannot hear; "sounds like" below means "the measurements look like".

## 1. Headline
There is **no singing anywhere in this track** (high confidence). Every sound in the vocals stem is **speech**: a **single-sounding male Dutch announcer**, in about 27 short bursts (my segmentation of VS, threshold -45 dB, gaps under 0.35 s merged), between 0:09 and 2:48. After 2:48 the voice never returns (VS about -62 dB to the end at 3:08.7, mine). The voice is **dropped on top of a continuous instrumental groove like a found document**, not woven into it as a hook, and it is placed with **no rhythmic quantisation** and **no audible ducking of the band**. Words give the track its subject and its "reportage" feel; the music (D-centred, 78 BPM, drums never stopping) answers the words by **taking over whenever the voice leaves**.

## 4. Treatment and placement
- **Level.** VS runs -18 to -26 dB when active (PSL; peak -18.3 dB at 0:41, -18.5 at 0:53-0:55, -19 at 2:44-2:48). Typical stem levels in the same moments: other -16 to -24, drums -15 to -20. The voice is therefore **comparable with the drums and the "other" layer, not far above them**, and it stays intelligible because it lives in a different band (mine: 95% of VS energy in speech seconds lies below roughly 4.3-5.3 kHz; under 1% below 200 Hz; sibilant energy above 4 kHz 1-5%). Confidence medium-high.
- **No ducking.** The mix level changes by only about 1 dB when the voice starts or stops (0:32 exit: -10.9 to -12.4; also win_0020/0040 notes, PSL). The band does not lower itself for the speaker. This is the clearest sign of a **layered, "pasted over" approach**, in contrast with a mixer-led voice-over (high confidence).
- **Band-limited, dry.** The speech has the look of a narrow, radio/film-sound recording: formant stripes up to about 4-5 kHz in MV, 95% roll-off about 4.3-5.3 kHz (mine), no visible reverberant tail on the bursts (MV windows 0000, 0040, 0140). Medium confidence that it is archive-like or is processed to feel so; I cannot hear grain, noise or hiss, and **I cannot prove it is an archival recording as opposed to a new read or an effect**. CLAP "radio announcer speech" (about 1.0 in many 5 s windows) is weak, but it agrees with the clipped delivery; note CLAP "vinyl crackle" rises only after 2:48 (0.33 at 2:50, 0.92-0.98 from 2:55), i.e. it tags the voiceless noisy outro rather than the speech.
- **Centre-panned.** When the voice leaves (0:32, 1:27) stereo width jumps (0.36 to 0.5 at 0:32; 0.4 to 0.8 at 1:27; PSL). The voice therefore occupies the centre and "closes" the image; its removal "opens" the room. High confidence on the measurement, medium on the cause (a centre sound also removed with the bass/other re-balance).
- **Pitch.** pyin on VS gives a **median f0 about 130 Hz at 0:10-0:16, 146 Hz at 0:20-0:31, 175-178 Hz at 0:41-0:56, 161 Hz at 1:19-1:27, 196 Hz at 2:24-2:36** (mine). That is a **male speaking range that rises through the track**: the late list is about 4-7 semitones above the opening. Possible reasons: the announcer is **more animated in the list** (high confidence that stress is raised in a list), a **different excerpt/source**, or a pitch/speed treatment (cannot tell; low confidence). Curiously the 0:20-0:31 median (146 Hz) is about D3 (146.8 Hz) and the track's tonic is D, but this is a median of a speaking voice and I rate "voice tuned to the key" as **low/probably coincidence**. The few LN "lead notes" in these bursts are speech inflection (about C#2-C5), not melody; LN's low notes (C#2, D2, E2) during silence are bass bleed (VS about -65 dB there), so I discard them.
- **Rhythm of placement.** I measured each burst's start and end against the beat grid (BT, 78.3 BPM). Offsets are spread across the beat: about 27 starts from -0.45 to +0.48 beat, with no clustering on the beat (only 0:27.9, 0:41.05 and 2:24.1 are within 0.1 beat). Bar position (beat index mod 8) is spread over all eight values. So **the speech is free-timed against the 78 BPM grid** (high confidence for the measurement). That matches a pre-recorded document laid over, not a chopped, quantised vocal "instrument". Two exceptions are musically significant, see section 5.
- **No chopping or stutter.** I find no short repeated fragments, no looped syllables, no pitch-shifted vocal hook: bursts are whole phrases of 0.4-4.8 s. The place-name list (2:21-2:36) is the only place where bursts get as short as 0.44 s (2:27.4, 2:30.5), and even there they look like separate **words in a read list**, not edits (medium confidence; I cannot rule out cuts).

## 5. How words and music answer each other
1. **Phrase end locked to the groove, once.** The 0:27.9-0:31.7 sentence starts and ends **on the beat** (start +0.00 beat, end +0.00 beat; beat 35 to about 40; mine). It lines up with the D hold at 0:28.7-0:31.0 (win_0020 note). It is the one place the speech looks **placed to the bar** (high confidence on the alignment, medium that it is intentional). This is the "event name" sentence, so the music "lands" with the title words: a strong inference that the producers placed the key sentence to the grid.
2. **The title sentence comes back at 2:44.3-2:48.2** with near-identical length (3.83 s vs 3.85 s) and the same words. Envelope correlation after alignment is only about 0.3-0.5 and an MFCC time-warp cost is 0.135 against 0.205 for an unrelated phrase (mine): so it is **similar but not demonstrably the same audio take**. Verdict: the track returns to its opening sentence for its close (high confidence); **same recording re-used: medium-low**. It is the **frame**: the voice opens (date, setting, title phrase) and closes with the title phrase, then the music alone closes out (2:48-3:08).
3. **Voice leaves, music takes over (the main dialogue).** Every long speech block is followed within seconds by a **rise in width and brightness**: at 0:32 the drum centroid jumps from 1.6 to 3.3 kHz and the bass brightens (win_0020); at 1:27 width jumps 0.4 to 0.8 and the "other" wash and, after 1:40, bass enter (win_0080); after 2:48 the "other" brightens to 9-10 kHz and noise appears (win_0160). So the structure is **statement (voice) then answer (the band, louder/wider)**, three times: 0:32, 1:27, 2:48 (high confidence on events; "answer" is interpretation).
4. **Alternation with the bass in the list.** In 2:20-2:40 the voice is quieter at 2:21-2:22 and 2:35.5-2:37.5, where the 8-beat bass hits and chord changes sit (win_0140 note); the bass bursts begin every 6.1-6.2 s, the voice fills the gaps. The place-name list thus behaves like a **call-and-response with the sub-bass**, still not beat-locked (the beat offsets above), medium confidence.
5. **Content versus arc.** Facts are given in the order of an introduction: when (0:09), what (0:19), the name (0:27), the number (0:41), the cold (0:51), distance (0:54), field size (1:19), then, 1 minute later, **places (2:15)**, then the name again (2:44). A listener gets setting, then scale, then route (the towns), then title: the voice does narrative work the instruments cannot. The place list begins when the "other" stem is lifting by 5-7 dB (2:13) and ends as it peaks (about 2:34, win_0140), so **the towns pass while the music builds**, a plausible "route and momentum" reading (inference; medium).
6. **Silence as a sound.** The 48 s speech-light middle (1:27-2:15, only 1:46 and 1:57) is where the track is most purely musical: wide wash, bass entry 1:40, drone D. The two speech intrusions in it are quieter (-23 to -26 dB) and short; they act like **echoes of the story** rather than new information (medium).

## 6. Feeling (what a human would hear, with cause and confidence)
- **Documentary distance and authority (high).** Cause: calm, centred, band-limited male speech, declarative, giving date, temperature, distances; no melody, no emotion in pitch. (Measured: flat speaking range, 110-200 Hz, no sung pitches.)
- **Archive atmosphere (medium).** Cause: narrow bandwidth (95% roll-off about 4-5 kHz), dryness, "radio announcer" CLAP. Not proven; the feeling of "voice from another time" depends on timbre I cannot hear.
- **Ceremony / roll-call building (medium).** Cause: the list of town names, rising pitch (about 196 Hz), growing frequency of bursts, over a build in the "other" stem. CLAP "triumphant" in 2:15-2:35 is weak but consistent.
- **Cold, effortful, "monster" scale (low-medium).** The words "16 graden beneden 0" and "200 kilometer" carry it; the music's driving 16th-drum pulse and steady, never-stopping groove supply the "effort" (the drums run 4-5 onsets per second through all speech).
- **Release and widening when the voice stops (medium-high).** Cause: width +0.15 to +0.4, brightness, bass; this is felt as relief or lift, though loudness hardly changes.

- win_0020 calls the 0:32 drum change a result of the voice leaving; the drum centroid does change then, but I treat "voice leaves, drums brighten" as co-incident, not caused (they are separate stems).
- win_0120 and win_0140 treat the voice as a rhythmic instrument "one per beat group"; my beat-phase measurement does not support a grid lock (offsets scatter); it is **free-timed, only list-like**.
- win_0140: "Dm voice pauses at 2:35.5-2:37.5": VS has a burst at 2:35.1-2:35.9, then silence to 2:44; I agree with a pause but note the list is **finished at 2:35.9 (Leeuwarden)** and the voice is absent for 8 s (2:36-2:44), not just two.

- win_0100 says the burst at 1:57-1:58 has strongest energy below 2 kHz in "harmonic stripes": agree, and note it is 1.3 s long and untranscribed, so I cannot tell whether it is a word or a vocal effect.
