## 1. Corrections to my own round 1

1. **Loudest point.** I wrote "peak -11.0 at 1:40". That was a 10 s mean. Per second, the loudest moments are 1:52 (-9.5 dBFS), 1:54 (-9.7), 1:51 (-10.1), 1:48 (-10.3), 0:58 (-10.3), 1:41 (-10.3) (LV, my run). R1-form is right: the second peak is the 1:40-1:55 stretch, topped at 1:52 where the drums also swell (R1-form; R1-rhythm). So the "bass slam" at 1:40 is the biggest *event* but the *loudest* moment is 12 s later. Felt consequence: the track is still climbing after the drop; it does not release at 1:40, it gains weight and keeps building, then is pulled apart at 1:55. High confidence on the numbers.
2. **"Pedal D with a flat seventh" and "wistful".** R1-harmony shows the third is mostly absent (root plus fifth, F 0.19-0.57 and F# 0.34-0.57 of D, similar everywhere) and that the low bass shuttles D2-C2-D2. I said the colour came from an unresolved third. Better: the colour comes from an *open fifth with a b7 shuttle*, which feels neutral-to-stoic (modal, "folk-rock, Mixolydian-ish"), not major-minor flicker. I withdraw "wistful from the F/F# ambiguity" as a cause; "wistful" can stay only where Bm (1:07-1:15, 2:47) and A-to-C (1:15-1:19) are audible. Medium.
3. **Tempo feel.** R1-rhythm's bar autocorrelation (3.077 s peak 0.72 against 0.69 at the beat) and 78.0 BPM in every 30 s block settle the grid. I earlier hedged 78 vs 156. I now say: notated 78, but a listener will probably tap 78 or half of 156's eighths; either way the 16th comb is a texture, not a tempo. The "patient and impatient at once" line stands.
4. **The 2:47.8 event.** I called it a "dropout about 12 dB dip" and a "final held breath". R1-rhythm measured it with 20 ms RMS: roughly half a beat (about 0.4 s), mix about 12 dB down, not silence, drums re-enter with a hit at 2:48.24. Consistent with mine.
5. **Hiss "worn archive grain" after 2:48.** My new spectral check: share of mono-mix power above 8 kHz is 0.0008 at 0:20-0:30, 0.0035 at 1:00-1:10, 0.0020 at 1:28-1:38, 0.0043 at 2:05-2:15, 0.0037 at 2:30-2:40, **0.0071 at 2:50-3:00** and 0.0046 at 3:05-3:08. So the last minute is the brightest in the top octave, but only about 2x the 1:00 level, and 2:05-2:15 is already similar. R1-sound says the "vinyl crackle" CLAP tag has no matching spectral evidence. I accept that: I **downgrade "worn, filmic grain" to low confidence** and will not use it as a feeling cause. What remains is "a brighter, wider final wall". Medium.

## 2. Answers to questions put to me

**To feeling, from R1-form (does my section map match? is 1:55-2:13 a "middle eight"?).**
My section-wise means from LV (mix dBFS / width): 0:00-0:09 -14.7/0.31; 0:09-0:32 -12.2/0.36; 0:32-0:50 -12.4/0.50; 0:50-1:15 -11.2/0.54; 1:15-1:27 -15.2/0.46; 1:27-1:40 -12.7/0.76; 1:40-1:55 -10.7/0.53; 1:55-2:01 -14.1/0.50; 2:01-2:13 -15.2/0.30; 2:13-2:38 -12.6/0.50; 2:38-2:48 -11.6/0.49; 2:48-3:08 -11.5/0.73. These reproduce R1-form's table within 0.1 dB, so my map and the form map agree. On "middle eight": 1:55-2:13 does the *job* of a contrast section (the quietest, narrowest, bass-gated passage; static chords per R1-harmony), but it is not a song-form middle eight: it has no new tune, only subtraction. For a listener it is a **hush/breakdown**, then the 2:13 lift is the feeling of "return". I would call 1:15-1:27 a short breakdown and 1:55-2:13 the main breakdown, with the track alternating "full" and "hollow" states, as R1-form says.

**To feeling or sound, from R1-sound Q3 and R1-voice Q3 (are the real boundaries 0:32, 1:15, 1:27, 1:40, 1:58, 2:13, 2:38, 2:48? is the 2:47.8 gap the start of the climax, the widest and brightest minute?).**
Ranked by how strongly I would expect a listener to *feel* a boundary (size of the change in LV, number of simultaneous changes):
- **Strong (several layers move together):** 1:15 (bass out, mix -4 dB), 1:27 (voice out, other +10 dB, width doubles), 1:40 (bass +35 dB, width -0.3), 1:55-2:00 (bass out then wall out, width to 0.2-0.3), 2:38 (bass returns for good).
- **Medium:** 2:13 (other +6 dB, first chord motion per R1-harmony and CH), 0:32 (voice out, width +0.14, drums brighter per R1-rhythm).
- **The 2:47.8 gap** is felt as an *accent*, not a boundary of equal rank. Before it the bass has been continuous since 2:38 and the closing line has just ended; after it the voice never returns, width rises from about 0.5 to 0.7+ and the >8 kHz share roughly doubles (my run). Both R1-form and R1-voice treat it as the start of the final wordless wall. I agree it begins the last section, and I agree the last 20 s are the **widest, voice-free, bass-on** passage, the only one with all three (R1-form's point). But I would not call it "the climax" in the sense of the loudest: the loudest moment is 1:52, and the final section's mean of -11.5 dB is only 0.8 dB above the 1:40 stretch's -10.7 (it is in fact 0.8 dB *quieter*). So the ending is a **plateau of arrival** (a long held "everything at once"), not a peak. Felt: resolve through stillness, as R1-harmony says (no cadence, single D label for 20.7 s).

**To feeling, from R1-voice Q3 / Q2 (do the speech bursts cluster at boundaries? archive or modern?).**
I looked at timing only (voice entries/exits from R1-voice and LV). The voice leaves at 0:32 (width up), 1:27 (other up 10 dB, width doubles) and 2:48 (width up, final wall), so **each long voice block is followed by an instrumental opening-up**; R1-voice's pattern "talk, then the band answers" matches my journey. Not every boundary is a voice event though: 1:15 (bass out), 1:40 (bass in), 1:55 and 2:38 are bass events with the voice either absent or incidental. So voice marks about half the boundaries; bass marks the others. On archive versus modern: R1-voice and R1-sound both measure narrow band-limited, dry, centred speech (95% below about 4.5-5 kHz). I can add only this: a narrow, dry, centred voice set against a wide, loud, saturated band is, for a listener, a **distance contrast** (small human at the centre, big machine around it) regardless of whether the source was radio. Whether it reads as "period recording" is something measurements cannot decide. Confidence: medium on the contrast, low on archive.

## 3. Connections to the other specialists

- **Rhythm serves the feeling of travelling.** R1-rhythm shows zero tempo drift (78.02 BPM in all six 30 s blocks) and an unbroken 16th comb with a recurring empty slot (steps 6 and 11 of the bar). I accept their "limp or stagger" reading: it is *why* the groove feels like a riff and not a metronome, and the fixed pattern is the reason the mood is "march" rather than "dance". It also means the **only rhythmic events are removals**: the 2:47.8 half-beat gap and the 3:06.25 32nd roll. These are the two places where rhythm itself, not texture, produces a gasp and a lunge. I had listed the roll only as "drum density doubles"; R1-rhythm's 0.09-0.11 s spacing for about 1.5 s into a cut makes it a real lunge. High.
- **The bass-gate period locks to the bar.** R1-rhythm (bursts 6.2, 6.2, 6.4 s apart vs 6.15 s) and R1-harmony (the Dm 3-beat anchor at the same times) confirm the 2:01-2:40 pulses are a two-bar breathing cycle. That is why I felt it as "breathing"; I can now call it a 2-bar swell-and-release that repeats five or six times, and the lengthening gaps (6.2, 6.2, 6.4) are within tracker error so I treat the cycle as regular.
- **The sub-F bursts.** R1-harmony measures about 42.6 Hz (F1 about 40 cents flat) over D/A upper layers and R1-sound says it is a fixed sine, no glide. For feeling: an F sub (b3) beneath a bed with no third is more *weight* than *colour*; it will be felt in the body and read as "dark" without being identified as a note. If the F is real and the bed has F# (R1-harmony: F# 0.57 at 2:00), a clash is possible; I cannot tell whether it is heard. Low.
- **Sound design and the feeling of closeness.** R1-sound: dry, no reverb tails, 77% of power under 500 Hz, crest 11-14 dB. A dry, bass-heavy, compressed mix gives the "in the room, pressed on" quality I called a "wall pressing forward". It also explains why my feeling curve moves so little in loudness (-9.5 to -16.5 dBFS only) and so much in width and bass: the listener is steered by *weight and space*, not volume.
- **Form and feeling together.** R1-form's two states (F full, H hollow) map directly onto my "pressure" and "exposure": F states feel weighted and forward, H states feel suspended. The felt strongest events are state *switches* (1:15, 1:40, 1:55, 2:38), not what happens inside a state. The three peaks (about 0:58, 1:52, 2:48-3:08) are, for feeling, a rise (0:58), a gain-then-strip (1:52 then 1:55-2:13), and a plateau (2:48).
- **Lineage (R1-lineage).** The post-rock grammar (layering, subtraction, wall, no sung vocal, documentary spoken sample) predicts exactly the arc I describe: patient build, hollow, surge, long hush, final wall. It does not need a chorus because the reward is the re-entry of weight. I treat lineage as consistent with, but not evidence for, my feeling reading.
