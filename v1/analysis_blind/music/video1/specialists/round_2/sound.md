## 1. Questions put to me, answered

**Q (harmony, rhythm-adjacent): is the 2:01 to 2:40 sub a sine, and does it glide?** Re-measured with an 8x zero-padded FFT on the bass stem at 2:01.5, 2:07.5, 2:13.7 and 2:19.9: peak 42.66 Hz in all four (fixed, no glide; high). Round 1 showed harmonics 38 to 48 dB down, so it is near-sinusoidal. It is below F1 (43.65 Hz) by about 40 cents. Harmony reads it as "F, about 40 cents flat"; I agree on the number. Whether it is a sounded b3 against the D drone or only weight: a 42.7 Hz fundamental is at the edge of audibility on most playback and is almost never heard as a pitch; its upper harmonics are too weak to carry pitch (see round 1). So I rate it as a felt texture first, harmonic colour second (medium). One more clue: the rest of the track's bass sits at D2 (about 73 Hz). A sub that is a dissonant minor third below the octave-lower D would clash if heard as pitch, which makes "weight tone, not chord member" more plausible (low-medium).

**Q (rhythm, form): is the "other" wall a guitar or a synth, and is the 8 kHz roll-off a source property or a production filter?** Correction to my round 1: I called the 8 kHz roll-off a "hard shelf". Measured per 15 s block, the 99 % roll-off of the other stem is not fixed: 9.1 kHz (0:00), 7.3 (0:15), 10.3 (0:30), 7.4 (0:45), 6.5 (1:00), 12.7 (1:15), 6.7 (1:30), 7.3 (1:45), 13.0 (2:00), 7.6, 6.7, 8.3, 7.5 kHz. So it is content-dependent. In the dense riff blocks the band above 8 kHz is 30 to 45 dB under the mids (e.g. 1:35 to 1:40: 100 Hz to 500 Hz at 47 dB, 8 to 12 kHz at 2 dB). In the thin blocks (1:15 to 1:30, 2:00 to 2:15) the top end is relatively larger because the low content has gone. That is what a source with natural high-frequency decay (an amplified, speaker-limited or low-passed instrument) does, rather than a fixed brick-wall in the master. I can say: not a fixed production filter on the whole mix (the whole mix reaches 18 kHz; round 1). I cannot say guitar versus synth: a speaker-cabinet guitar and a low-passed saw would both fall off like this. I keep "guitar-like or saturated synth, undecided; leaning guitar-amp-like because the top end is soft and the roll-off varies with playing density" (low). Honest "rock band versus electronic" verdict: not decidable from this evidence, and the sub plus the grid-locked drums (rhythm: 78.02 BPM in every block) argue for at least a sequenced element.

- **To form:** the sound supplies five boundary kinds, not one. 0:32: voice off, drum centroid doubles, bass bites. 1:15: bass off and top end off. 1:27: top end on (+26 dB at 2 to 5 kHz). 1:40: bass on. 1:58: other falls 10 to 15 dB. 2:13: body on (+11 dB low). 2:48: full cut then brightest wall. - **To rhythm:** the sub's 6.1 s spacing (8 beats at 78.02 BPM = 6.15 s) is exactly rhythm's 8-beat cycle: the sound's slowest event is locked to the grid, and the sine's constant pitch plus gated envelope say it is sequenced. A fixed-pitch gated sub on a bar grid with a dry sixteenth drum stream is a machine-like backbone with a distorted layer on top.
- **To harmony:** the held "D5 unbroken" at 2:40 to 3:08 is, in sound terms, the other layer at its widest, brightest and loudest; so harmonic stillness is also sonic saturation. Stillness plus maximum width is why the end reads as an arrival by weight, not a resolution by cadence.

The sound design gives speech a clear centre lane and takes it back when the wall widens.
- **To feeling:** the felt oppression comes from low-heavy balance (77 % of power below 500 Hz), flat loudness, dryness and the pure sub; the felt "opening" at 1:27 and 2:48 comes from the top end (2 to 12 kHz) arriving, not from a rise in level.
- **To lineage:** the production does not date the song strongly. Dry, loud, 0 dBFS peaks, synth-like sub and a sampled spoken voice are consistent with any time from the late 1990s on; the old-sounding element is the voice only.
