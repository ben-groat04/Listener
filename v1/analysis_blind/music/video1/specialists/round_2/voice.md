## 1. Correction to my own round 1: the place-name list IS bar-paced
Round 1 said all bursts were free-timed against the grid. Re-measuring with BT (beat index counted from BT[0]) shows that was true for the opening and the numbers, but **wrong for the list**. Starts of the first bursts in 2:15-2:31 are 2:15.0, 2:18.2, 2:21.2, 2:24.1, 2:27.5, 2:30.5 (VS segmentation, -45 dB). All fall at **beat index mod 4 = 2** (the third beat of a bar), offsets +0.24, +0.39, +0.27, +0.06, +0.46, +0.38 beat (a steady slight lateness, mean about +0.3 beat = 0.23 s). Gaps between them are 3.2, 3.0, 2.9, 3.4 s against a bar of 3.06 s. So the roll-call advances **about one name per bar**, at a constant place in the bar, then speeds up after 2:31 (bursts 0.4-0.8 s long, gaps about 1-1.5 s). Confidence: high on the measurement (seven starts, all mod 4 = 2, none opposite), medium on intent (a producer could have hand-placed a read-aloud list on the bar, or the source reader simply paced it; a free-running read would not hold a bar to within 0.2 s over 20 s by accident, but seven points is small). This **corrects the window notes' "rhythmic instrument" claim only partly: it is bar-paced, not beat-locked and not chopped**.
The 0:27.9-0:31.7 sentence is the other bar-locked event (starts -0.01 beat, ends -0.05 beat; drum-stem level at 0:27.9 jumps from -21 dB at 0:27.5 to about -14.8 dB, and 0:31.7 is -13.7 against -23.4 at 0:31.2; mine). So **drums are loud exactly on the speech start and end**; that is the drum stem answering the entrance, or a shared cut. Together: the voice is grid-free in sections of "reportage" (0:10-0:26, 0:41-0:56, 1:19-1:27) and **grid-aware in the two moments that matter** (the event name at 0:28 and the route list from 2:21).

## 3. Connections to other specialists
- **Form + Rhythm + Voice:** the three voice-bearing arcs share one device: speech arrives in a thin, narrow or bass-light state; the band answers with a bar-grid event (0:32 drum centroid change; 1:27 wide wall; 2:48 re-entry). The voice is the "statement", the groove is the "reply".
- **Sound + Feeling:** a dry, narrow, centred voice over a wide, saturated wall produces the "two eras" feel Sound names. Its narrowness (4 kHz ceiling) is what keeps it clear while only 3-8 dB above the stems, so no ducking is required; the arrangement leaves the 1-3 kHz window to the voice (the "other" wall is 100 Hz-8 kHz but the voice sits in the quieter intervals: "other" about -22 dB while speaking at 0:20-0:32 and -27 at 1:20-1:27; louder wall only when the voice is gone).
- **Lineage:** "no flow-like alignment" (hip-hop rejected) holds for most bursts, but the bar-paced list is a **collage-lineage detail** (a read-aloud inventory as a metronome), not rap.
