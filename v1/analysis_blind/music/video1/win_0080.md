- **Bass (high on timing, medium on role)**: bass stem is essentially absent (-65 dB floor) for almost the whole window. Exceptions: a short low event at ~1:28-1:29 (x≈8.8-9.5 s; bass -43 then -55 dB; lead tracker catches a D2 at 1:29.14), which is a single low D "drop"-like hit, and the real entry at 1:40 (second 100: -19 dB, ~46 dB jump, then -16 to -19 for the following seconds). So for ~12 s after the voice goes, the harmony is carried without a bass foundation, then the bass slams in as the window ends.

## Sound
- Loudness (dbfs): about -15 dB through the speech part, rising to -12 to -13 dB from 1:27 and up to -10 to -11 dB from 1:40 (bass entry). So 1:20 to 1:40 is a ~4-5 dB overall rise.
- Stereo width: 0.42-0.53 before 1:27 (some dips to 0.34-0.38), then jumps to 0.62-0.83 from 1:27 and stays about 0.7-0.83 until 1:39, then drops to 0.40-0.47 at 1:40 when the bass (centre) arrives. So the wash is very wide, and the sound feels like it opens out when the voice leaves.
- Brightness: the mix panel has a clear break at x≈7.2 s (1:27) where the energy above 2 kHz becomes stronger and denser: a fuzzy, bright, distorted texture, with strong low-mid energy at 100-500 Hz (D, A fundamentals) but no sub-bass (bass stem absent). The "wind/ distorted" CLAP sound tags (wind 0.84-0.95) are probably the fuzz/noise floor being heard as wind; vinyl crackle 0.99 at 1:30 and 1:35 may be hiss or broadband texture, not literally vinyl (low confidence).
- Space: no isolatable reverb measurement; the broad horizontal smearing of the "other" panel and the stereo width suggest a sustained layered pad/guitar with some spaciousness (low confidence).

## Movement from the previous window
- Before (1:00-1:20, see window_0060s): voice-led and busy. In this window the voice finishes its list at 1:27 and the music takes over: this is a handover from narration to instruments. It is a release (the sparse narration over a driving beat becomes a full, wide, louder instrumental) and also a build (loudness +3 to +5 dB, width up).
- It then leads to 1:40, where the bass enters (+46 dB in bass stem), the width narrows and the mix gets another 1-2 dB louder: the last 12 s of the window is an anticipatory section, held on D with no low end, which makes the bass entry feel like a weight dropping in.

## What a human listener would feel, and why
- **Informational, brisk, documentary** at 1:20-1:27: spoken numbers over a steady beat (announcer speech 1.0; voice stem bursts).
- **A lift or opening-up (confidence medium-high)** at 1:27: voice stops, a dense bright wall appears, loudness up ~3 dB and stereo width almost doubles (0.4 -> 0.8). People hear this as a surge in scale, an "arrival".
- **Drive and momentum (high)**: unbroken, regular drums at 78 bpm, no stop; the slowish tempo with dense hits gives a determined, marching/pulsing feel rather than a fast rush.
- **Suspense or yearning (medium)**: sustained D drone, Am and C alternating with D major/minor ambiguity, no bass. Open fifths and an unresolved third give a hovering, wistful-heroic quality; CLAP mood "nostalgic" ~1.0 and "triumphant" 0.91 at 1:25 (weak evidence but consistent).
- **Anticipation then impact (high)** at 1:40: the lack of low end for 12 s followed by a large bass entry makes the final second feel like a drop in weight and a gain in punch.
