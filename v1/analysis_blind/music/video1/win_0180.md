# video1, window 3:00 to 3:09 (blind music read)

Note on range: the file ends at 3:08.66 (audio_features/summary.json duration 188.66 s), so the window is 8.7 s long, not 9 s. window_0180s.png shows the same: the panels stop at x = 8.7 s. This is the **final stretch and ending** of the track.

Evidence used: music_views/window_0160s.png and window_0180s.png, chords.json, lead_notes.json, per_second_levels.json, stems/stem_rms_per_second.json, clap_tags.json, audio_features/summary.json, lyrics/whisper_medium_mix_nl_*.json, and my own onset/band measurements (v1/scripts/agents/win_onsets.py, run on song.wav and drums/bass/other stems, 3:00 to 3:08.66).

## 1. What is playing (layers)

- **Drums, bass and "other" play together for the whole window; none enters or leaves until the end.** Per-second stem RMS (stem_rms_per_second.json, t = 180 to 188): drums 0.03 to 0.17, bass 0.015 to 0.12, other 0.12 to 0.26. Confidence high.
- **Drums:** the drums panel shows a dense run of vertical broadband strikes plus a steady bright wash above 2 kHz. That wash is hi-hat or cymbal-like (the window-0160 image shows it returning at about 2:48.2). The drum stem carries about 13% of its energy above 2 kHz in 3:00 to 3:08.7 (my band measurement: 0.073 for 2 to 6 kHz, 0.083 above 6 kHz), and low thumps below 150 Hz at 58%. A kick-like low thump with a bright top layer is the best reading. Exact kit identity is not recoverable. CLAP calls it "drum machine" at 0.97 (3:00 to 3:05 window), which is weak evidence; the machine-like evenness of the onsets (see rhythm) is more convincing than the label. Confidence medium.
- **Bass:** 97% of the bass stem's energy is below 150 Hz (my measurement), centroid about 320 Hz. It is a sub/low register line locked to the drum onsets (bass and drum onset times agree to within about 20 ms: for example 180.21/180.19, 180.77/180.77, 181.53/181.53, 182.11/182.11). Its level dips and recovers between seconds (-17.5 to -22.5 dB per-second), as in a pumping, riff-following line. Confidence high on register, medium on role.
- **"Other" (guitars/synths):** the loudest stem throughout (-14.6 to -17.6 dB). It is broadband: 50% of energy at 150 to 500 Hz, 13% at 0.5 to 2 kHz, 17% at 2 to 6 kHz, nothing above 6 kHz, and a hard roll-off near 8 kHz in the "other" panel. Sustained, thick, band-limited texture with fast repeated onsets (50 detected, median gap 0.186 s) is consistent with chugging distorted guitar and/or synth. I cannot tell guitar from synth. CLAP says "distorted guitar" at 3:05 (0.99) and "drum machine" at 3:00; the model is flip-flopping, so I treat it as weak. Confidence medium that it is a distorted, riff-playing layer.
- **No voice.** The vocals stem is at -65 to -66 dB for every second 172 to 188 (per_second_levels.json), which is the floor. The bright texture in the vocals panel of window_0180s.png is the plot's auto-gain on leakage, not singing. See section 5.
- **The end (3:07 to 3:08.7):** see section 3 and 4. Drums and bass stop first; the last notes decay by about 3:08.3 and the file is silent by about 3:08.5.

## 2. Harmony and melody

- chords.json gives one chord for this whole range: **D (2:47.97 to 3:08.66, one 20.7 s stretch)**. The song-level key is D (D major 0.76 vs D minor 0.756, summary.json); for 3:00 to 3:09 the key estimate is **D minor 0.645 vs D major 0.594**, so the third is ambiguous.
- The chroma panel of window_0180s.png agrees: D is a constant bright bar, A is frequently lit, C (natural) is lit at the bottom on and off, and F-sharp is not clearly present. This pattern of root + fifth + occasional flat seventh suggests a **D power chord/drone with a bluesy or modal flavour (D5, with C natural)** rather than a plain major triad. The "D" label is a template match and the missing third makes major/minor unresolvable. Confidence medium-high for D as the tonal centre, low for major vs minor.
- **Last 2 seconds (about 3:06.5 to 3:08.6):** the chroma panel shows energy moving up from D to G (about x = 6.8 to 7.3), then E and F-ish rows (x = 7.3 to 8.6). Likely a closing rise of the riff or a final gesture away from the tonic, but the chord labeller does not register it (still D) and the chroma is blurred by the decaying tail. Confidence low.
- **Lead line (lead_notes.json):** it is the vocal-stem pitch tracker, and with the vocals stem silent it is tracking leakage, so these are not sung or spoken notes. Notes between 3:00 and 3:08 sit in **C-sharp 3 to A-sharp 3 (about 139 to 233 Hz), clustered at D3 / D-sharp 3 / C3 / C-sharp 3 / B2**, i.e. chromatic wobble of a semitone either side of D3 (for example 3:00 to 3:01: C3, C-sharp 3, D3, A-sharp 2, B2, C-sharp 3, D3, C3, D3, D-sharp 3). Most likely this is the pitch tracker latching onto the repeating low riff, whose shape is "circle around D with a flat seventh and a flat second". Very low confidence as melody. Single outliers (G3 at 3:06.8 and D-sharp 3 at 3:07.3) match the chroma move towards G.
- There is no clear tune above the riff here; the interest is rhythmic and textural.

## 3. Rhythm

- Tempo: the global beat tracker gives 78.3 BPM (beat gap about 0.77 s). The median onset gap in all three non-vocal stems is **0.186 s**, which is almost exactly one quarter of a 78 BPM beat (0.766 / 4 = 0.19 s). So the stems move in **steady sixteenth-notes at about 78 BPM, or equivalently steady eighth-notes at about 156 BPM**. The tempo-percentile statistics (10th/50th/90th: 78 / 108 / 152 BPM) show the tracker oscillating between these two readings over the song. A human would most likely tap the half-time pulse (about 78) with the drums feeling driven; I cannot decide which from measurements. Confidence high that the pulse is metronomic; low on which tempo a listener taps.
- Drum onsets (my detection on the drum stem): 180.19, 180.56, 180.77, 180.95, 181.16, 181.37, 181.53, 181.72, 182.11 ... regularly about 0.18 to 0.21 s apart, with occasional gaps (for example 181.72 to 182.11, 183.65 to 183.83) that suggest a repeating groove with some missing hits. Very even spacing, no audible-looking swing, consistent with machine-like or tightly quantised playing.
- **Fill at the end:** from 3:06.25 the drum onsets compress to about 0.09 to 0.11 s spacing (186.25, 186.36, 186.46, 186.64, 186.73, 186.83, 186.92, 187.01, 187.11, 187.20, 187.31, 187.48, 187.71), i.e. roughly double the density, around 32nd-note rolls. The 2 to 8 kHz streaks in the last 1.5 s of the drums panel agree (a tight roll/crash build). Per-second drums RMS peaks at 0.17 in 3:07 then collapses to 0.01 in the last partial second (3:08). Confidence high for denser hits.
- **Dropouts:** drums RMS falls to 0.026 in second 185 (3:05) and 0.06 in 182, bass to 0.015 in 182 (3:02). These are single-second dips (the mix level in those seconds stays near -11 dB), so they may be separation artefacts or very short breaks; I cannot tell. Low confidence.

## 4. Sound

- **Loudness:** this section (158.7 to 188.66 s, "B" in sections_auto) is the song's loudest (mean RMS 0.2588). Per-second mix level is steady at **-10.9 to -12.0 dBFS** through 3:00 to 3:07. There is no build in level; the drive is constant.
- **Brightness:** spectral centroid of the mix about 2.76 kHz in this window (per-second brightness about 2.9 to 3.0 kHz at 3:00). Energy: 45% below 150 Hz, 33% at 150 to 500 Hz, 7% mid, 13% at 2 to 6 kHz, 2% above 6 kHz. So full, heavy low end with a clear cymbal/guitar presence band.
- **Distortion:** the "other" panel shows a dense, flat harmonic stack from the bottom up to about 8 kHz with no empty gaps between harmonics, which is what overdriven/saturated sources look like. Medium confidence.
- **Stereo width:** 0.62 to 0.86 (per_second_levels.json), averaging about 0.72 for 3:00 to 3:07, much wider than the 0.24 at the song's start. Widest at 3:05 (0.855). It is wide, enveloping, with the low end presumably central. Medium confidence.
- **Space:** I cannot isolate reverb. The drum panel shows a short tail in the final second only (drum energy decays smoothly 3:08.0 to 3:08.5), which looks like a natural/room decay or a reverb tail after the last hit.
- **The ending:** mix RMS (my 0.1 s RMS of song.wav): about -10 to -12 dB until 3:07.4, easing to -14 / -16.7 / -14 / -19.5 dB at about 3:07.6 to 3:08.0, then **-40.6 at 3:08.1, -57 at 3:08.2, -66 at 3:08.3, -81 at 3:08.4**. That is a 20 dB fade followed by a very fast (about 0.2 s) cut to silence, an abrupt stop with at most a short ring, not a long fade. Confidence high.

Confidence high that no real voice is present here.

The narration stops at about 2:48 and does not return.

## 6. How this window moves the song on

- Previous window (2:40 to 3:00, window_0160s.png): the narration over a drum pulse (2:44 to 2:48), then a brief hole in everything at about 2:47.8 to 2:48.2 (panels go black), then the band re-enters at full weight with a bright cymbal wash and a continuous D pedal in the chroma. At 3:00 the same full-band D-pedal groove is simply still running.
- So this window is the **sustained peak/last section**, not a build: constant loudness, constant chord, constant sixteenth pulse. The only changes are internal: a denser drum fill from about 3:06.3, a slight harmonic lift towards G/E at about 3:06.5 to 3:08, and the sudden stop at about 3:08.1. It is a payoff followed by a hard stop, a "driving to the end, then the cut" arrangement.

## 7. What a human listener would feel, and why

- **Drive and relentlessness (high confidence).** Cause: metronomic sixteenth/eighth-note onsets (0.186 s median spacing in drums, bass and other alike), a locked bass and drum pair, and unchanging loudness (-11 dBFS +/- 0.6 dB).
- **Heaviness and power (high).** Cause: 78% of mix energy is below 500 Hz, a loud sub-bass line, the loudest section of the song, and a distorted-looking mid layer. CLAP gives "aggressive" 0.99 at 3:00 to 3:10, which agrees but is weak by itself.
- **Hypnotic, locked-in trance (medium).** Cause: one chord (D) for over 20 s and an ostinato-like repeating pattern; no vocals or new melodic material to pull attention away. The chromatic wobble around D3 (low confidence) would add a slightly menacing, circling feel.
- **Immersion/width (medium).** Cause: stereo width 0.6 to 0.86 vs 0.24 at the start, plus cymbal wash and broad "other" layer.
- **Rising urgency near the end (medium).** Cause: drum roll density doubling from 3:06.25, chroma rising to G/E at about 3:06.8 to 3:08, drums level peak in 3:07.
- **A jolt/sudden full stop (high).** Cause: mix level collapses from -19.5 dB to -57 dB in 0.2 s after a short 20 dB decay, no sustained tail. Listeners would feel an abrupt "slammed door" ending rather than a resolution; the harmony never cadences (D throughout), so the feeling is of cutting off rather than closing.

## Limits

The "melody" in lead_notes.json for this window is pitch-tracker leakage on a silent vocals stem, not a sung line. Major versus minor is unresolved because the third is mostly absent. Whether the felt tempo is 78 or 156 BPM is undecided. Whisper's last line is almost certainly an artefact.
