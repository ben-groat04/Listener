## 1. The shape of the journey in one paragraph

A hushed, dark hall (0:00-0:28), a sudden kick into a steady dark groove (0:28), a long stretch where a calm male spoken voice tells a story over a thin or half-formed beat (0:42-1:28), a release into a full, driving, locked groove (1:28.6), a physical heaviness step (1:40), then three and a half minutes of near-constant, loud, narrow, motorik-like drive in which the feeling is carried by pressure and slow minor/major colour changes rather than by a tune (1:40-4:58), with the voice returning as a chopped, rhythmic texture (3:28-4:58), and finally a hard, theatrical stop and a single dark closing hit (5:00-5:03). The key emotional fact is that this is a track of **accumulation and plateau, not of peaks**. The mix level climbs 16 dB over the track and then stays flat. Overall level, my 20 s means [LVL]: -26.4 (0:00), -20.6 (0:20), -18.1 (0:40), -17.7 (1:00), -17.0 (1:20), -12.1 (1:40), -11.6, -12.1, -13.1, -12.2, -11.5, -10.8, -10.3, -10.2 (4:20), -12.7 (4:40). Stereo width falls from 1.20 to about 0.33 by 1:40 and recovers only to about 0.5 [LVL]: the sound goes from a wide, enveloping room to a narrow, centred wall. High confidence on all these numbers.

## 2. Moment-by-moment, with causes

**0:00-0:28, awe, stillness, a ceremonial hush (high on the mood, medium on the causes).**
Quiet (-29 to -21 dBFS), no bass (-85 to -91 dB), no voice, no pulse, and a wide sustained pad (width 1.2-1.5 at 0:01-0:08) [WIN 0000, LVL]. The only events are deep, long-ringing low strikes at 0:00, 0:08, 0:12, 0:20 and 0:24 (peak -26 to -21 dB, decaying 20 dB in 1 s and 50 dB in 3 s) [WIN 0000, 0020]. To a listener these read as a tolling bell or a slow heartbeat: each hit makes the silence between feel bigger. Harmony is B minor with a held flat sixth (G) and a four-second lift towards D major at 0:08-0:12 [WIN 0000, chroma]; the G against B gives a yearning, slightly sorrowful colour, and the D lift is a brief glimpse of hope that sinks back. Caveat: [CH] labels only "Bm" for 42 s, so the chord movement is from chroma, not from the tracker. CLAP "calm" and "nostalgic" agree but are weak.

**0:28, the kick-in (high).**
Bass jumps from -89 dB to -22 dB and drums from -82 dB to -24.5 dB in one second; the mix rises about 7 dB [LVL t=27 to t=28]. There is no riser: the drum panel is black until the hit [WIN 0020]. The feeling is a physical jolt, then a determined, marching drive. Width collapses from 1.1 to 0.7 and the centroid falls to about 490 Hz [WIN 0020]: darker, heavier, more focused. The slow, long-held chords (Bm to Em at 0:26, G at 0:32, D at about 0:36.5, about 4 s each) give a "descending then lifting" loop: weight, then hope.

**0:42-1:28, the narrator, a documentary calm (medium-high).**
Spoken (not sung) male voice enters at about 0:42 and returns at 0:51, 0:59, 1:04-1:10, 1:20-1:28 [WIN 0040, 0060, 0080]. The words I trust are a date ("18 January 1963"), a title mentioning a marathon, "200 km", "6.30 in the morning", "only the strongest" and a Dutch-sounding sentence about a twelfth "Elfsteden"-type event [LY, weak, Whisper passes disagree]. Feeling causes: a voice with no pitch movement, sample-like edges, set over slow chords at the pace of a sentence. The beat thins while he speaks (drums stem -56 to -75 dB at 1:00-1:04 and 1:10.5-1:16, [LVL]), so the voice is a held breath, not a melody. Tension peaks at 0:53-0:56 where the chroma suggests B major (a dominant-like chord) resolving to Em [WIN 0040]: a small "will it resolve?" question answered at 0:56. Medium-low, because the chord tracker has gaps and the major third (D#) is only a smear. A lift at 1:15-1:16 (G to A to D with the pulse restarting) is a gathering of momentum [WIN 0060].

**1:20-1:28.5, hollow suspense (high).**
The quietest and emptiest stretch after the opening: voice at -21 to -23 dB, bass and drums at about -76 to -80 dB (t=85-87), mix -19.8 to -22.5, long static Em, width about 0.55 [LVL, WIN 0080]. This is a deliberately engineered pause: everything except the voice and pad is removed, which the ear reads as "something is about to happen".

**1:28.6, the release (high on time and structure; the image shows full-width vertical lines starting at x = 8.6 s [IMG 0080s]).**
Drums, bass and "other" return together and the mix jumps from -21.5 to -16.6 dB in one second (t=87 to 88). I disagree slightly with how some window notes cast this as the song's biggest drop. Measured by level, it is a medium step (+5 dB). The bigger, slower change in weight is at **1:40**, when bass rises about 10 dB (to -16 dB mean) and the mix goes to about -12 [LVL]. So: 1:28.6 is the emotional **release** (talk gives way to groove: a train pulling away, per the notes), and 1:40 is the **gaining of body** (the groove becomes heavy, locked and relentless). A listener will feel the first as relief and the second as being gripped. Evidence for "train/skater" imagery is my interpretation of a steady 0.37 s pulse with 0.12 s subdivisions, not a measurement.

**1:40-2:20, locked-in cruising with one spoken interruption (high on drive, medium on the rest).**
Drums flat at -19 dB, bass -14 to -16 dB, width 0.3-0.35 [LVL]: dry, tight, centred, pressure rather than space. Chords turn about every 4 s (Em, G, D, Bm and Em/E, later Em-G-A-D-Em-G-A-Bm) [WIN 0100, 0120]. At 1:49-1:56 a sample ("temperature 16 degrees below zero, a monster ride of more than 200 kilometres", medium-high on sense, from two English-forced Whisper passes) rides over the groove with the bass ducked 4-6 dB under it [WIN 0100]. The words give cold and effort; the minor chords under them (D to Bm/B) add chill. Medium confidence on the emotional reading, because it depends on words I cannot verify by ear. At 1:56.5 the voice stops, "other" rises about 5 dB and harmony lands on E: a small payoff, "open road". At 2:08 there is a one-second broadband fill and level maximum, a punctuation [WIN 0120]. The feel is sustained intensity with no peak.

**2:25-2:36, the list (medium).**

## 4. Disagreements and corrections with the window notes

- The notes call 1:28.6 the "drop"; levels show a larger sustained weight step at 1:40. I think the track has two arrival points, release (1:28.6) and weight (1:40).
- Several notes read CLAP "aggressive", "playful", "krautrock" as support. These are single-window, noisy labels; I use them only when they match a measured cause.
- The notes disagree on whether the late voice is speech or singing. Measurements (no sustained pitch, gated bursts, tracker notes 0.1-0.4 s) say chopped speech-like fragments or a vocal chop. I cannot say which, nor whether the words "Stuck/End of the road" are real.
- Lead-pitch notes in the first minute are not melody (vocal stem is leakage); no tune exists anywhere that I can confirm. The feeling is all texture, harmony and pulse.

## 5. What the measurements cannot tell me

Instrument identity (organ, choir, synth, guitar, timpani vs tom); the true wording and language of the voice; whether the voice is archival; reverb type; exact bar count (160 bpm vs 80 bpm felt in half time is ambiguous in the first 42 s, when there is no beat grid); what the listener's culture or mood brings. Emotional claims are inference from cause, not a recording of response.
