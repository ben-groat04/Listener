## 1. True tempo: 120 BPM in 4/4, not 161
**Verdict: the felt pulse is 120 BPM, four beats to a 2.00 s bar. The 161.5 BPM in the summary is a tracker artefact (high confidence in the 120 grid; medium that a listener would tap exactly it).**
- [AC] In every 20 s window from 0:30 to 4:50 the drum-onset autocorrelation has peaks at 0.499, 0.998, 1.997, ~3.00 and ~4.00 s. Ratios are exact (0.5 : 1 : 2 : 4), so the grid is 120.0 BPM within measurement error. A tempo estimator run on the same windows returns 120.2 for all but the last. 120 BPM is the only grid that explains 0.5 s, 1 s and 2 s all being strong.
- [BT] The tracker's 0.372-0.375 s spacing (84% of beat intervals within 20 ms of 0.375) is exactly 3/4 of 0.5 s, i.e. three semiquavers (a dotted quaver) at 120 BPM. 161.5 is 4/3 of 120. Several window notes noticed "doesn't divide into bars" (win_0040, 0060, 0120, 0140): the cause is this 3:4 relation, not odd bars. The chord changes every 4 s (win_0060 to 0160) are 2 bars of 4/4; 8 s = 4 bars.
- Why the tracker locks to 0.375: the drum cell is full of 3+1 semiquaver pairs (onsets about .12 / .50 / .63 / 1.00 s, intervals 0.38, 0.13, 0.37: three semiquavers then one) [WIN 0120, 0160, 0180; PH]. A dotted-quaver stream is therefore genuinely present in the hi-hat/ghost layer, as a cross-rhythm over the 120 grid; the tracker follows it. It is not the metre. A human would tap the kick (see below).
- Half-time at 60 or double at 240: the 0.5 s peak is present in every window, and the kick sits at 0.5 s steps (4 on the floor). 120 is the sensible tap. The "80 BPM half-time" suggested in several window notes is excluded: 80 BPM would need a 0.75 s beat, and there is no 0.75 s autocorrelation peak.
- Stability: folded phase of the 0.5 s grid is constant to within about 30 ms across 1:30-5:00 (0.125 s to 0.09 s offset, [PH]); there is no audible-scale drift. It is a quantised, machine-steady tempo (consistent with CLAP "drum machine", weak [WIN 0100]). Local-tempo percentiles 96-161 in [AF] come from the tracker flipping between readings, not from the music speeding or slowing.

## 2. The groove (1:40-4:50, the main pattern; medium-high confidence on positions)
Folding drum onsets on the 2 s bar [PH], in semiquavers (0.125 s) from the bar start:
- **Kick (30-150 Hz band): on every beat (0, 4, 8, 12), plus an extra pick-up on the "a" before beat 4 (semiquaver 11).** Four-to-the-floor with a syncopated push into the last beat. Same shape at 1:40 and 3:20, so one pattern runs the whole second half.
- **Snare/clap (150 Hz-2 kHz): hits on alternate beats (0 and 8 in my phase; with the kick-heavy beat as reference these are the backbeat, beats 2 and 4),** with weaker ghost notes at 6, 10 (semiquavers) and 11. Which beat is "1" is not determinable from the stems alone; backbeat reading is my interpretation.
- **Hat/high band (5-10 kHz): hits at 0, 3, 6 and 9-ish semiquavers (the 3+3+3 dotted pattern), plus bright accents on a 2 s grid** [WIN 0100: bright bursts at 1:40.02, 1:42.02, 1:44.01]. This is where the 0.375 s pulse lives.
- Density: about 4 onsets/s at 2:00-3:20 [WIN 0120-0180], 5-5.5 /s from 3:40 and 4:20 on [WIN 0220-0260]. Mean gap 0.14 s (about a semiquaver). So the later minutes are busier by extra ghost and hat hits, not a faster tempo.
- Feel: a motorik/four-on-the-floor stomp (steady kick every half second) with a skittering, syncopated top layer. It is stiff and relentless in the bottom, jumpy in the top. CLAP "krautrock", "drum machine", later "drum and bass" (0.93-0.99 at 4:45-4:55, [WIN 0280]) are weak but the "drum and bass" label is probably the busy semiquaver hats at the tracker's 161 reading, which is near D&B pace (160-175); it is a reading the tracker also makes, not evidence of breakbeats. Low confidence.

Rubato-feeling to a listener, metric underneath (medium: five points, but all fit).
- **0:28.1 onset of the beat.** Abrupt entry with no riser [WIN 0020]; 0:28.14 is exactly 14 bars from 0:00.14. First dense cell 28.14, .37, .51, .87, 29.01, 29.5 (intervals 3,1,3,1,... semiquavers: the dotted pattern). A "drop" onto the grid, a downbeat.
- **0:40-1:00 groove with 4 s accents.** Loud drum seconds at 0:40, 0:48, 0:52, 0:56 [LV]: 4 s = 2 bars, and 0:40 to 0:48 is 4 bars. Stereo width dips at the drum hits [WIN 0040], so the kick/snare are centred. Grid phase in this stretch is 0.456 s into the half-second, [PH]; this is about 0.33 s (nearly one dotted quaver) later than the post-1:28 grid (0.12). So when the groove re-enters at 1:28.6 it is **re-phased by about a dotted quaver (3 semiquavers) relative to the 0:30-1:00 grid** (medium confidence: phase estimate; strong 0.5 s peak both sides). Listener effect: the pulse "lands somewhere new", plausibly a displacement of the whole pattern (a bar that feels shortened by three semiquavers, or a pattern re-barred). Question below.
- **1:00-1:16 thinning.** Drums fall away around 1:00-1:03, return harder about 1:04, then drums collapse to under -50 dBFS from **1:10.0** to **~1:16.4** [LV drums: -29 at 1:09.0, -65 at 1:10.0; revival -35 at 1:16.5]. ## 4. How the pulse drives the feeling
- **Shape of the track in rhythm terms:** a free, unmetred, slow-bell intro (a clock heard only in hits 4 s apart) -> abrupt lock to a hard 120 grid at 0:28 -> two stops (1:10, 1:24.5) that tear the groove down and rebuild it, re-phased -> three minutes of an almost unvaried relentless stomp, varied only by fills and the entry/exit of layers -> a drum stop and three-hit cadence. The long middle is trance-like rather than dramatic: the push comes from repetition, the bass and fills; the listener's relief or tension comes from harmony and voice above it.
- **Stiff/skittering contrast:** a rigid four-on-the-floor kick at 2 steps per second with a nervous 3+1 hat layer gives a feeling of marching (feet) and running (hands) at once. Forward pressure rather than swing. Syncopation is local (the kick pick-up, the 3-sixteenth hats), not swung; no triplet swing is evident (0.124 s = 1/3 of 0.372 is the same number as 1/4 of 0.5, so the tracker-based "triplet" readings in [WIN 0080] are ambiguous; the 120 grid favours straight sixteenths).
- **Stops as drama:** the drum stems vanish for 6.5 s and 4 s in the first half; the listener loses the beat entirely, so the return at 1:28.6 is felt as a hit, and it works as the track's biggest rhythmic punctuation. It also places the groove on a new phase, which may be felt as the beat "turning round".
