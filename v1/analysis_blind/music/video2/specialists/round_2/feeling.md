## 2. Answers to questions put to me

**Sound asked: does a 16 dB climb plus a flat drum stem feel like escalation or numbness?**
My measurements [P]: from 1:40 to 4:37 the mix 60 s means are -11.8 (1:40-2:37), -12.4 (2:37-3:37), -10.4 (3:37-4:37); standard deviation of the per-second level is only 0.6-0.9 dB inside each block, and the whole 1:40-4:37 stretch spans 5.2 dB (the 20 s means run -12.1, -11.6, -12.1, -13.1, -12.2, -11.5, -10.8, -10.3, -10.2). So after 1:40 the loudness is not an escalation at all: the 16 dB rise is done by 1:40 (-26 to about -12) and the next three minutes add only about 2 dB. A listener will feel the first 1:40 as escalation and what follows as **plateau, trance or numbness depending on attention**. What keeps it from going numb is not level: it is (a) the slowly thickening "other" layer (+7 dB, [R1-form], [R1-sound]), (b) the voice returning at 3:28, (c) the drum fills every 4-8 bars [R1-rhythm], and (d) the withdrawals (2:36, 4:37). My honest reading: a listener who enters the groove feels hypnotic, pressured, "locked in"; one who resists feels a long sameness, because pitch, tempo and loudness barely change. The flat drum stem is the cause of the "machine" feel; the +2 dB drift is the "tide coming in" feel. Medium confidence on the experiential split (an inference), high on the numbers.

**Form asked: is 1:40 a boundary as important as 1:28.6, and is 4:48 a distinct outro?**
- 1:28.6 is a boundary of **kind** (talk-and-air becomes groove: drums and bass return from -75 dB in about one second, mix +5 dB [LVL, R1-form]). 1:40 is a boundary of **weight** (bass +10 dB, width 0.45 to 0.30, [R1-sound] agrees on 1:39-1:41). Both are felt, differently: relief, then grip. I would draw 1:40 as the start of the real "A section" and 1:28.6 as the start of the groove. [AF] puts a boundary at 1:41.3, which supports mine. High on levels, medium on how a listener would parse sections.
- 4:48 as an outro: partly. The level step is at 4:37 and the bass becomes one stab per 2 s at 4:48.4 [R1-form, R1-rhythm]. I would call 4:37-5:00 one **thinning outro in two stages**: 4:37 the floor falls, 4:48 the floor is replaced by a spare pattern; the feeling goes from "pressure releasing" to "mechanism ticking on". Medium.

**Rhythm's point on re-phasing at 1:28.6 (about a dotted quaver).** I had no way to feel this before. If true it adds a small disorientation to the "release": the pulse arrives, but "somewhere new". I cannot confirm it myself (my fold [P] over 1:40-4:50 gives a single bar-phase peak at 1.125 s, which does not test the earlier grid). Low-medium; I would only claim it as a possible contributor to the "lurch" feel.

## 3. Cross-links between my area and theirs (how causes make feelings)

- **Rhythm to feeling, stops as drama.** [R1-rhythm] measures two drum-stem stops: 1:10.0-1:16.5 (6.5 s) and 1:24.5-1:28.0 (four seconds, two bars at 120). They explain why the 1:20-1:28 hollow is the tensest minute of the first half: the beat is removed on a bar line, so the ear counts silently and the 1:28.6 hit lands on a predicted downbeat. "Subtract, then release" [R1-form] is therefore a rhythmic fact, not only a loudness one. High.
- **Sound to feeling, narrow and dry.** [R1-sound] measures L/R correlation +0.76 (1:50-1:58) and width 0.3: a centred, dry kit and bass with no tails. That is the cause of my "pressure, claustrophobic" reading; the wide, wet opening (correlation -0.22 at 0:05-0:10) is the cause of "hall, awe". The width widening to 0.5 from 3:00 as "other" grows [R1-sound] is why the plateau feels "enveloping" rather than louder. Medium-high.
- **Sound to feeling, the stop.** [R1-sound]: a wide wet tail (width 0.92) and then a dry, mono hit. That is the cause of the "gun-shot / conductor's cut-off" feeling at 5:02.3: loneliness of a close, dry bang after open space. High.
- **Harmony to feeling.** [R1-harmony]: Aeolian B minor, an upper Bm pedal with a moving bass (E, G, D, B, A under it). Cause of two feelings I had merged: the **constant minor seriousness** (the pedal never leaves) and the **shifting warmth** (G under Bm gives a Gmaj7-like warmth, D under it a settled "inversion", A a soft seventh). So "hope" in my round 1 (D lifts) is better restated: the bass moves, the minor ground does not; the lifts are changes of weather, not changes of key. Medium.
- **Voice to feeling.** [R1-voice] gives pYIN median f0 118-142 Hz (low male speaker, voiced fraction 0.1-0.3), so Part A is speech. Speech at -17 to -20 dB is a foreground figure; chops at -25 to -35 dB are background texture. So the narrator gives **meaning and distance** (newsreel), while the chops give **human presence without meaning**. The loss of words after 3:28 is itself a feeling: story told, now only the body of the sound remains. Medium-high.
- **Lineage to feeling.** [R1-lineage]'s motorik and film-score frames fit my "accumulation and plateau" reading: motorik repetition is designed to be trance-like, so "numbness vs hypnosis" is part of the idiom rather than a flaw. Medium; I defer to lineage on the labels.
