## 1. Answers to questions put to me

**Form (is 1:28.6 a true change of pulse, "tempo drop"?) No. High confidence.** [PER] A fine scan of the drum-onset period (0.490-0.510 s, step 0.5 ms) gives 0.5000 s (120.0 BPM) in 0:30-1:00, 1:30-2:30 and 2:30-4:50 alike. The 120-to-160 "local tempo" in the audio_features summary is the tracker flipping to the 0.375 s hat cross-rhythm ([R1] section 1). What changes at 1:28.6 is the drum pattern and density, not the pulse. Call it a "density/pattern drop", never a tempo drop. Strongest single piece of evidence: coherence of the 0.5 s grid rises from 0.42 (0:30-1:00) to 0.62 (1:30-2:30) because the post-1:28 kick lands on every beat, while the earlier kick does not.

**Harmony (do chord roots land on bar downbeats; where is bar 1?).** I cannot answer this from [CH]. I checked chord-change times modulo 2 s from 0:00.14 in three stretches (0:28-1:28, 1:28-2:37, 2:37-4:50): the phases are scattered across the whole bar (for example 0.4, 0.6, 0.9, 1.4, 1.9 s), because the chord tracker's frame resolution is coarse (about 0.37 s hop). So the chord file cannot confirm or refute bar alignment. The better test is the bass-stem note onsets; harmony already has these ("each bass note is 2 bars"). Where I can help: the drum grid is stable (below), so any bass change time that is a multiple of 4.000 s from a drum downbeat is a two-bar change. Bar 1 is not determinable from the stems alone (see section 2); I put the downbeat on the weaker-snare beat as a convention.

**Lineage (motorik backbeat or pure four on the floor; delay copies or programmed hat?).** [FOLD] 1:28.6-4:50, bins relative to the kick beat (kick at bins 3, 7, 11, 15):
- Kick: 0.14, 0.17, 0.11, 0.16 on the four beats, plus a strong extra at bin 10 (0.10-0.12, one semiquaver before beat 3). Four-on-the-floor, confirmed. Note this corrects [R1]: I labelled beats as bins 0, 4, 8, 12 with the pick-up on 11; the same pattern, re-labelled to where the kick actually peaks, has the pick-up before beat 3, not before beat 4.
- Snare band: 0.09, 0.18, 0.11, 0.18 on the four beats (1:30-2:30; 0.08, 0.14, 0.08, 0.14 in 3:20-4:50). Beats 2 and 4 are about 1.6-1.8 times stronger than 1 and 3 in every stretch. So it is **four-on-the-floor kick with a backbeat accent on 2 and 4**, not a pure floor-only beat: the structure of disco/house/motorik alike. It cannot separate those (all share kick on every beat and snare on 2 and 4). Medium confidence for the backbeat labelling (it depends on my choice of which beat is 1), high for "alternate beats accented".
- Hat: not delay copies. [HAT] After the 203 strongest hat events in 1:40-3:20, the hat envelope at lags 0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.5, 0.75, 1.125 s is 0.11, 0.22, 0.40, 0.34, 0.30, 0.30 of the event's own. A delay repeat would give decaying copies at 0.375 and 0.75 and 1.125 s (a falling sequence above baseline); I get a modest excess only at 0.375 (0.40 against a baseline near 0.30) and none at 0.75 or 1.125. So the 3-semiquaver hat is better read as a programmed (or sequenced) hat line with a few hits at +0.375 than as an echo. Low-medium confidence (onset-strength of a noisy band; leakage possible).

**Sound (programmed/quantised?).** Yes, strongly. [PER] period 0.5000 s with no resolvable drift across 1:30-4:50 (kick-band phase stays within about 18 ms over 200 s; a human-played kit would be expected to wander by tens of ms and show beat-to-beat jitter). The kick-on-whole-seconds idea in your question: the kick is on every half-second; whole-second structure comes from the snare accent (beats 2 and 4 are 1 s apart) and from the 2 s hat/vocal accents. High confidence for a machine grid (sequenced or looped), low for the identity of the drum sound.

**Feeling (is the 160 bpm half time at 80 in 0:28-1:28; is the 4 s accent cycle a 2.7-bar pattern?).** Neither. The pulse is 120 throughout, with no 0.75 s autocorrelation peak ([R1] section 1). The 4 s cycle (0:40, 0:48, 0:52, 0:56) is exactly 2 bars at 120; the "2.7-bar" figure is 4 s divided by 1.5 s, that is, counting at the tracker's wrong 0.375 s. The 0:28-1:28 stretch is fully metric: the felt difference from later is that the kick is syncopated (below) so it feels looser, not slower.

**Voice (do the chops lock to the grid; does the 4:46-4:59 2 s call cross the bar?).** [PER] Vocal-stem onsets at 3:28-3:50 (208.6-228.6 s in the file) have a phase on the 0.5 s grid of 0.05-0.16 s for 11 of 15 onsets, where the drum grid sits at 0.11-0.12 s, so most are on the beat within about 30 ms; four sit at 0.37-0.46 s (early by about 0.05-0.15 s, i.e. a semiquaver ahead or pre-beat). So **grid-locked with some pre-beat pick-ups**, which supports "rhythmic instrument" over "floating intrusion", with medium confidence. In 4:46-4:59, onsets at 4:46.65, 4:48.65, 4:50.65, 4:54.62, 4:56.62, 4:58.66 are spaced 2.0 s with phase 0.12-0.16 s on the 0.5 s grid: **exactly one per bar, and on the beat of the drum grid.** Compared with the kick fold, the vocal lands on the beat that carries the weaker snare, which is the beat I call 1: a call on the downbeat each bar. (Note that the vocal stem here is partly the instrument; the call is only as reliable as the separation.) Medium confidence.

## 3. How rhythm serves the others' areas

- **Form:** form boundaries should be cut on the 2 s bar. Rhythmic events that are structural: 0:28.1 (grid entry), 1:10.0-1:16.5 (drum stop, 3.25 bars), 1:24.5-1:28.6 (second stop, 2 bars), 1:28.6 (pattern becomes four-on-floor), fills at 2:08, 2:24, 2:36 (16 s then 12 s: 8 then 6 bars), 2:48, 2:56, 4:48 and 5:00.9 (stop). The 8-bar phrase default is a reasonable working assumption; none of the later fills deviates from the bar lattice by more than a beat.
- **Feeling:** pre-1:28 is a "skipping", off-balance feel (kick off the beat, hat 3+3+3), post-1:28 is a "stomp" (every-beat kick, backbeat on 2 and 4, pick-up before beat 3). That matches feeling's reading of a release at 1:28.6: the release comes from the beat becoming regular, with no change in speed. The long middle plateau is a physical, trance-like pulse; the escalation felt after 2:48 is the extra hat/ghost density (4 to 5.5 onsets/s) and the growth of "other", not tempo.
- **Voice:** one chopped word per bar from 4:46, on the beat, turns the voice into a rhythmic accent over the same grid; the voice does not cross the bar.
- **Harmony:** the pulse is too regular to give a clue about chord roots; the 4 s chord/bass cycle sits as 2 bars of 4/4. A drum fill coincides with each chord-block change in [R1] [WIN] notes (rhythm marks the turn, not the other way round).
- **Lineage:** kick on every beat plus accented 2 and 4, a skipping 3+3+3 hat and a machine-exact tempo reads as dance/electronic or motorik-by-programming. I find no evidence for human feel (no jitter, no swing). A guitar-band lineage cannot be excluded from rhythm alone (a drum machine can be used by a band).
