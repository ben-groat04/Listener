## 1. What changed in my view

So the "width collapse" at 1:28.6 is not the pad becoming narrow: the wide layer keeps its width, and the centred bass and kick come in on top of it and dominate the sum. That is a mix-balance effect (adding centred sources), not a source change. High confidence. It answers [R1-lineage Q2, second half]. It also fixes my round-1 line that "other" widens at 3:00: its own correlation moves from about 0 to 0.25-0.5, so it is slightly less wide late on, and the mix width rise at 3:00 (0.3 to 0.5) is because the centred drums/bass fall in relative level (bass -14 to -19 dB while "other" rises 10 dB), not because "other" spreads.
2. **The "other" stem is wide in every band, so no evidence for two separate layers.** Correlation by band at 1:40 and 3:20 [P2, 8 s clips]: 0.0 to 0.3 in 80 Hz-9 kHz at 0:05, 0:40, 1:40; 0.1-0.3 at 2:30; 0.3-0.5 at 4:10. 4. **The 2:48 jump is a ramp, not a step.** 0.5 s RMS of "other" 2:44-2:52 [P2]: -31, -29, -26, -29, -24, -24, -27, -31, -26, -23, -22, -21, -20, -21, -21, -21 dB. ## 2. Answers to questions put to me

- **[Rhythm Q2] Is "other" an arpeggio locked to the 3+1 pattern? Is the bass on-beat quavers before 4:48?** Onset-envelope autocorrelation of "other" at 3:00-3:40 [P2] is nearly flat across lags: 0.125 s 0.67, 0.25 s 0.70, 0.375 s 0.71, 0.5 s 0.69, 1.0 s 0.64, 2.0 s 0.73. Dense, continuous, with at most a weak preference for 2 s and 0.375 s. So I cannot show a sequenced arpeggio; the wall is continuous with a bar-length (2 s) periodicity at most. Low confidence either way. The bass is not on-beat quavers: its onset autocorrelation 3:00-3:40 is -0.1 at 0.125 s, -0.07 at 0.25 s, -0.01 at 0.5 s, -0.04 at 1 s, 0.19 at 0.375 s and 0.37 at 2 s. It re-attacks mostly once per bar, plus the occasional dotted-quaver (0.375 s) accent, with sustained notes between. That matches [R1-harmony]'s "held 2-bar roots with short bursts" and means the bass is a drone-with-accents, not a driving quaver line. High-medium.
- **[Harmony Q2] Is the bass overdriven enough to explain the D#/third ambiguity?** Bass stem 3:20-3:26 [P2]: 30-150 Hz about 99 dB, 150 Hz-1 kHz about 87 dB (12 dB down), 1-4 kHz about 64 dB (35 dB down). So the bass is fundamental-heavy with a real mid band of harmonics, moderately saturated, not fuzzed. A fifth harmonic of B1 (61.7 Hz) lands at about 308 Hz = D#4, so a 150 Hz-1 kHz band at -12 dB is large enough for harmonic D# to show in chroma. This supports the harmony specialist's "D# is an overtone, not a major third" (medium); it cannot prove it, because the same chroma would arise from a played major third.
- **[Lineage Q2] Guitar or synth for the wall?** The measurements cannot separate them. For guitar-through-effects: very wide and decorrelated (double-tracked or stereo-delayed parts do this), a gradual brightening with drive, flatness rising to a noisy broadband state by 4:20. For synth: a unmodulated horizontal harmonic ladder at 1:40 (flatness 0.0004, nearly pure tone-like) and an intro pad that is a clear synth/organ-like sustain. I see no odd/even harmonic or pick-attack evidence strong enough to choose. I put it at 50:50 and would call it "a distorted wide chordal wall" (low). The only point I add: the same stem is very tonal (flatness 0.0004) at 1:40 to 3:20, which is more typical of a sustained synth, organ or heavily compressed guitar drone than of a transient-heavy strummed guitar.
- **[Feeling Q] Does the 16 dB climb feel like escalation or numbness?** Sonically, both, and the data split them. Escalation: the mix rises 16 dB and "other" brightens by 20 dB in the top band between 3:00 and 3:50. Numbness: the drum stem is flat at -19 dB (+/-1.5) from 1:40 to 4:50, the crest factor shrinks to 10-12 dB, and the mix width is flat at 0.3-0.5. My reading: the rhythm section is a fixed floor and all growth is in the bright upper texture, so the feeling is "rising heat over a constant stomp", with the voice the only varying human element (interpretation, medium).
- **[Voice Q1/Q3 about sound] How is the voice sitting?** In my stems the narrator is band-limited and centred (round 1). I cannot say if the chops are 'found' or 'produced', but the chop gating from 3:28 is cleanly gated (sharp starts and stops), a production gesture, not a recording property.
- **[Form Q3]** answered in section 1, points 3 and 5.
