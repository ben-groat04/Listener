**Harmony (are 4:29.9-4:35.6 and 4:42.7 real voiced material, and where do they sit?).** Real, not artefact (medium-high). In 4:44-5:00 they align with the bar, not with a harmonic change (chords there are mostly Bm/B).

**Sound (are the chops pitched or tuned to the chord, or is the tracker reading "other"? archival or produced?).**
- Not tuned: pitch centres wander G4, G#4, A4, A#4 irrespective of chord (above). They are a recorded voice's own pitch, not a retuned instrument (medium). A stuck G4 against Bm to D is a held colour, not a chord tone.
- Not "other": see the pYIN comparison and correlations above.
- Narrator or not? Sound asks about "the narrator cut into chops". I disagree with the premise, medium-low: median f0 is 118-142 Hz for the speech and 390-415 Hz for the chops (round 1 and [R2]). A pitched-up male voice is still possible (a formant-shifted sample), but spectral roll-off does not support a simple speed-up: the speech's 85% roll-off is 0.94 kHz and 2.4 kHz (0:64-70 and 1:50-1:56) with centroid 0.73-1.34 kHz, and the chops' are 1.9 kHz and 2.3 kHz with centroids 1.1-1.3 kHz. A pitch-up of two octaves would multiply the centroid by up to four; it rose by about 1.0-1.8 times. That argues against a straight repitch of the narrator (medium-low) and for a different, naturally higher voice or a synthetic one.
- Archival versus produced: the speech has energy to 3.7 kHz (95% roll-off) and nothing beneath about 200 Hz in round 1; that is mid-band and abrupt-edged, consistent with archive audio but also with a stem estimate losing bass. Measurements cannot choose (low). The chops are not band-limited in the same way (their 95% roll-off 3.8 kHz is similar), so I cannot separate their origin by bandwidth either.

## 3. How my area serves the form and the feeling

- **Form.** Voice marks the two halves: speech (0:42-2:36, loop A, free of the drum clock, entering as the band thins) and chops (3:28-5:00, loop B, pitched high, floating then locking). The 52 s silence (2:36-3:28) is the vocal-less middle where the "other" wall rises (Sound: +7 dB over 2:47-3:50): the music grows while the voice is absent, then the voice returns into a loud mix at -25 to -35 dB, so it comes back as colour, not as a lead.
- **Rhythm.** Speech is free over a rigid clock (round 1: it leaves the band surging at 1:28.6, 1:56.5); chops start free and end as a metronomic one-per-bar backbeat marker on 2. Free human gesture over machine groove, and then the human is absorbed by the machine: this is the narrative of the voice. (Interpretation; medium.)
- **Feeling.** The newsreel gravity of the first half is made by the spoken block's flat pitch and dry hard edges. The second half's "faceless yearning" (round 1) is now sharper: a high, floating, mostly non-verbal line that finally turns mechanical. A listener would likely feel the loss of the human story as the tempo and loudness climb.
- **Lineage.** Spoken archival-sounding sample plus a repeated wordless female-or-high voice over a guitar-wall and hard 120 four-to-the-floor is a recognisable recipe, but the measurements do not give me names; I leave identification to Lineage and flag only that "sample as narrator, then as texture" is a known strategy.
