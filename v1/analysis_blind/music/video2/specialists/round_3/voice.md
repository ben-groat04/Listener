## 2. Answers to questions put to me

- **Rhythm and Form: do the earlier chops (3:28-4:22) fall on bar starts, or on 6-bar cycle starts?** [V3], 36 bursts 3:28-4:39, same lattice. Many sit 0.01-0.18 s early (1.82, 1.83, 1.94-1.99), and four are exactly 4 s apart (3:32.43, 3:36.43; 3:44.56, 3:48.44). So the chops show a **tendency to land on, or just before, a bar start**, with free fill-ins between; this revises my round-2 "float" (measured on a different origin, against beats) to "loosely bar-anchored, early rather than late" (medium-low; burst detection is noisy and I use one threshold). On 12 s cycle phase they scatter (0-12 s all visited; 7.8 s appears three times: 3:36.43, 3:48.44, and 4:38.44 at 9.8). So **no 6-bar lock** (medium). The first dense chop (3:32.43, 3:36.43) is at cycle phase 3.8 and 7.8 s, not at a cycle start; Form's 3:36.6 edge is marked by density and the loop restart, not by an on-the-cycle vocal entry (the 3:36.43 burst is 0.17 s before the edge: a pickup, medium).
- **Lineage and Sound: reverb tails or processing on the chops?** [V3] time for the vocal-stem level to fall 20 dB after a burst peak: 0.14-0.59 s for 3:28-3:39 chops (peaks -15 to -17 dB, weaker repeats at -37 to -39 dB fall in 0.17-0.19 s), and 0.46-0.57 s for every call at 4:44-4:53 (peaks near -21 dB). I did not measure vibrato; pYIN voiced fractions are too low (0.16-0.67) to give a clean contour. So "human singer" versus "processed sample" remains open (low). What the measurements say: the late calls are uniform in level (-21 +/- 1 dB), spaced exactly, and equally long: they behave like a triggered sample on a sequencer, not like a performance (medium).
- **Sound: does the bass accent fall on the same beat as the calls?** I cannot resolve a bass accent time (rhythm's own bass onsets were unusable, [R3-rh]); the safest statement is that the voice shares the kick lattice's bar start and, per [R3-rh], not the snare beat. Low.
- **Harmony: does a G# in bass or "other" justify the voice's G#?** Answered by [R3-ha]: G# is below 0.3 in bass and about equal to G in "other"; I accept that the Dorian colour (G#/A over an unmoving Bm) belongs to the voice alone, with the usual caution that the pitch is uncertain by an octave and some pYIN glide (medium-low). I add that a voice free of the chords, but timed to the bar, is the typical behaviour of a sampled loop that is looped against a sequencer: timing locked to the clock, pitch not.

## 5. Settled conclusions (five bullets)
- **Two voices, two regimes (high):** spoken, low (f0 118-142 Hz), flat, dry, free of the bar clock narration from 0:42 to 2:35.8 over loop A; then a higher (390-415 Hz, G4/G#4/A4, octave uncertain), mostly wordless, gated chop voice from 3:28 to 5:00 over loop B, with a 52 s vocal-free middle (2:36-3:28) while the instrumental wall grows.
- **Voice is timed to the machine more and more (high for the end, medium for the middle):** narration none, chops 3:28-4:39 loosely anchored on bar starts (about half within 0.2 s, early rather than late), final calls 4:46.6-4:58.6 exactly one per 2.000 s bar at bar phase 0.00-0.03 on the kick-only beat. This corrects my earlier "beat 2".
- **Pitch is not tied to the chords (medium-low):** chops wander G4-A4 over Bm/D/A/F#m; G# colour is in the voice alone, since bass and "other" chroma lack it ([R3-ha]).
- **Treatment (medium):** gated, dry, uniform at the end (about -21 dB, 20 dB decay in 0.5 s, no long tail), consistent with a triggered sample; origin (archive, sung, synthetic) and language cannot be decided from measurements (low).
- **Role (interpretation, medium):** the human narration is the first half's gravity, then the voice becomes a texture, and finally a clock tick absorbed by the machine at the end, so words give way to rhythm as loudness and brightness climb.
