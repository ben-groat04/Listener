## 4. What a viewer feels here

OBSERVATION first: the images become paler, emptier and calmer at the exact time the music gets louder.
INTERPRETATION: Tension and cramped, jittery closeness at the table (1:20-1:26: dark, glare, hands, paper, the crowd pressing in), then a loosening into huge, silent, white space. The tiny file of skaters on the white void (1:30-1:33) gives an impression of smallness before nature: humans as specks, a long, cold, collective effort. The lone figure at the posts (1:37-1:38) brings loneliness and endurance. Cold is conveyed through tone: nearly everything is white-grey, flat light, wind-bent reeds, frost. Music louder yet less percussive suggests a rising, steady, determined march rather than excitement. Confidence: moderate, from features, not hearing.

## 5. Symbols, motifs, visual rhymes to track

- **Numbers and counting:** bib "157" and the narrator's 575 / 9,500; individuals vs. the mass. The person becomes a number. Watch whether the numbering returns at the end (finishers, or someone's number recalled).
- **Hands, pens, signatures:** registering, a commitment or pact before the ordeal; check whether hands return at checkpoints.
- **Black vs white:** the table scenes are black-heavy, the countryside is white-heavy; the film's tonal palette flips at 1:27.
- **Tiny figures in vast white space:** recurs here at 1:30 and 1:37 (human scale versus landscape); likely returns.
- **Line of skaters vs lone skater:** group formation breaking into individuals.
- **Reeds / wind-bent grass:** shows the wind direction and hardship; a "weather" motif.
- **Evenly spaced posts / fence line:** route markers; a possible "path" motif.
- **Drawbridge and church tower:** the town / human order left behind as the skaters enter the wilderness.
- **Film damage (scratches, specks):** the visible age of the material; the film says "this was then".
