## 4. What a viewer feels here, and why

- **Pressure, claustrophobia and relentless effort.** Close-ups of backs, hats, jumpers, bibs, no faces: anonymous bodies in a pack, being swallowed by the lens. Loud steady bass and dense texture add to the sense of a machine of effort. The viewer is made to sit *inside* the slipstream.
- **Relief and loneliness at 3:24-3:28.** The wide shot of the empty glassy lane, the lone dark figure ahead, soft lilac sky: a moment of beauty and vastness, a person against the whole winter landscape. Also a small unease: where is everyone going? The glossy blue ice and the golden reeds are the prettiest images in the range.
- **Cold and tenderness.** The warm bobble hats and rough knitted wool against the bitter blue ice. Wool texture reads as human, homely.
- **A surge at 3:36-3:38.** The orange film-burn flash after the dark almost-black frame feels like a breath of heat, a rupture, or a memory flaring up. With the loudest second of the range, it lifts the mood: from grind to something like release or arrival. (Interpretation; the intent could also be technical flare.)
- Overall mood: gruelling, determined, awe at human endurance, nostalgic (the film stock signals "the past"), with little joy visible; nobody smiles because nobody is seen.
