Three sources are home-made fan-style clips (a trio dancing in a pale room, a lone dancer in a yellow-walled bedroom, a person in a yellow poncho with a box on their head on a driveway); one is a bright close-up of a young man in a yellow, orange and pink striped hooded towel, who looks to the camera, mouths and gestures like a performer; the rest is older-looking footage of a giant yellow bird puppet and a person-sized bird costume (it resembles a well-known children's-TV character; identity unverified), a dark grainy crowd clip, and small children in yellow beaked hoods. The music is a count of 12-second blocks in which the sub-bass is repeatedly taken away and put back; the voice is a dry, close rap, hook ("that bird walk, watch me do that bird walk") alternating with boasting verses. The picture does not follow the beat (cuts sit between beats), but it does lock loosely to the big structural edges, and every one of the three returns of the bass lands on or beside the hooded man. The feeling is cheeky, swaggering, daft and affectionate: a deadpan joke (children's-TV bird against club-rap boast) wrapped in a promotional frame. It ends by fading the music out at 3:08 and then showing 13 more seconds of silent picture.
