# t_birdwalk: the definitive reading (blind mode)

Moderator's synthesis of the checked music reading, the dossier and all three panel rounds (film critic, first-timer, insider, music critic, sceptic, symbolist). Blind mode: no context.md, research or web; nothing here comes from outside the data and the panel. Nobody on the panel can hear; every statement about sound rests on the music reading's measurements and on Whisper transcripts (medium to low reliability). Tags: [seen] checked in frames; [data] measurement files; [read] music reading; [heard-ish] Whisper; [panel] panel finding or calculation; [interp] interpretation; [spec] speculation. Times m:ss. British English.

Corrections adopted from the panel that supersede the dossier: (1) the last frame at 3:20 is fully lit, not "one corner lit then black"; the corner was the contact-sheet thumbnail (symbolist and sceptic, checked on f_0201 and sheet 11) [seen]; (2) the trio is at least two different takes, not one clip reused eleven times (film critic, first-timer); (3) the hooded man at the bass returns is two of three, the first about 0.5 s late, and is within chance (see section 4).

## 1. The piece in one paragraph

t_birdwalk is a 3:21 promotional video for a rap single about a dance called the "bird walk" [seen: caption "Soulja Unit / BIRD WALK / AVAILABLE NOW on iTunes"; the credit card also gives FD Entertainment and a MySpace address]. The music is a programmed, bass-heavy track on a single note (A) in which the whole drama is a count of 12-second blocks: the sub-bass is taken away and put back, loudness stays flat within each state, and the feeling comes from the floor dropping out and the stereo image opening wide, then the sub landing and the image snapping narrow [read]. Over it runs a collage of about nine recycled sources in 52 shots: home-made dancing (a trio in a pale room, a dancer in a red shirt in a yellow bedroom, a person in a yellow poncho with a box on their head), a close-up man in a yellow, orange and pink striped hooded towel, a giant yellow bird puppet, a person-sized bird costume, small children in yellow beaked hoods, and a dark grainy crowd clip [seen, dossier]. The picture does not lock to the beat at the half-beat, bar or 12 s level, and avoids the tracked beat (a weak off-beat clustering, section 4A), yet the whole thing hangs together, through one colour world (yellow, orange, pink) and the repeated bird. What it does to you: it is cheeky, swaggering, daft and affectionate, a deadpan collision of children's-bird imagery with a club-rap boast, framed by a sincere sales message. It ends with a fade at 3:08 followed by 13 seconds of silent picture, which many viewers feel as an odd, slightly forlorn tail.

## 3. Meaning and themes (consensus)

**1. A dance being offered to the viewer. [Strongest consensus]** Words promise "I'ma show you how it go" and "show you what it do" (Whisper) and the picture shows it: flapping arms, bent knees and a shuffle (poncho 0:09, feet 1:04, sneakers 1:47, trio 1:51, children 0:54, 3:01) [seen]. The out-of-step trio at 0:05 reads as an invitation rather than a failure (insider). The caption turns the offer into a sale ("AVAILABLE NOW on iTunes") [seen]. Symbolist's framing, accepted as interpretation: home-made clips show bodies dancing for nobody, among themselves; the packaged layers (puppet, hooded man, caption) look at the viewer; the film negotiates between the two.

**2. The bird in many registers, tied together by yellow.** Puppet in a studio, person-sized costume in a car park, a box on a head with a drawn face, children in beaked hoods, a man in a striped towel hood, the logo in the caption [seen]. Yellow, orange and pink recur through hood, poncho and feathers, so cuts between the registers never feel like a change of film (film critic). Whether these are the same character is unproven [spec].

**3. Affectionate comedy, not demonstrated parody.** The strongest comic items are visible regardless of intent: the box-head with a drawn mouth, the solemn child, the puppet before a lettered wall while a boast plays [seen]. Panel consensus: it is funny to a viewer and affectionate; the makers' intent to be funny cannot be proved from frames, and the caption is a sincere sales layer. Sincerity about being "available now" is part of what makes the gags land (symbolist).

**4. Subtraction as the form.** One tonic, no progression, a rigid 12 s count; the voice is the continuity layer and the bass the punctuation (music critic) [read]. Once the listener learns the count after break 1, the return becomes a pleasure of prediction rather than surprise (music critic, first-timer).

**5. Home-made-looking, domestic, social-web promo. [Medium-low]** Bedroom, driveway and pale room are ordinary domestic settings, though some clips (puppet, studio set) look professionally or televisually made. A MySpace address and an iTunes sales line place the piece in an era when both were in common use (inference from the text alone; the year is not established). A driveway with hedge and garage, a car park with a red vehicle (pickup or SUV) and tall trees (palms unconfirmed) look suburban and English-speaking; country is not established.

## 4. Live disagreements

**A. Is the picture tied to the music?**
- *Cuts avoid the tracker's beat.* Cuts within 80 ms of the tracked beat: 21.6 per cent against about 42 per cent expected [data]. At the 152 BPM beat grid they cluster at the off-beat phase (about 0.5; n 49, R 0.255, Rayleigh p 0.04; weak and uncorrected for multiple tests). No lock was found at the half-beat (p 0.84), bar (p 0.18) or 12 s level. Re-tested at the felt half-time grid (0.725 s: 11 cuts against about 10.6 expected, film critic) and at 0.75, 1.5, 3.0 and 12.0 s with permutation tests (p 0.36 to 0.86, music critic). Cut rate against loudness r = 0.27 at about 4 s lag; motion against onsets r = 0.30; warmth against loudness r = 0.38 [data]. Sceptic: picture is mostly independent of the arrangement.
- *A loose, human feel.* Five of seven bass edges have a cut within 0.9 s; the picture is often early (cuts at -0.4, -0.27, -0.86 s before three edges; towel lifted 0.7 s or more before the 2:35.5 slam); an editor who knew the 12 s count and cut by eye (insider, film critic). Counter: n is 5 to 7, one case is late (+0.54 s), and for the 2:11 and 2:23.5 edges no cut is within 2 s. The film critic dropped "the editor leads the music" to "a habit worth noting".
- *Moderator's resolution:* the picture does not follow the beat at any tested pulse; its loose link to the section-scale shape is suggestive, not strong. Viewers largely supply the sync themselves (first-timer): whatever is on screen at a drop takes the credit.

**B. Does the hooded man meet the bass returns by design?** Round 1 said all three. Corrected: at 0:59.25 the children are on screen and he arrives 0.54 s later; at 2:11.25 he is on screen mid-shot; at 2:35.5 he is at the end of his towel gesture. Two of three is about 15 per cent by chance given about 23 per cent of running time (symbolist, first-timer, music critic). Sceptic: post hoc. Sides now agree: a pattern a viewer may feel, not evidence of design.

**C. Was the dark crowd clip placed at the exits as a marker?**
Seen at 0:12 (a return, first drop), 0:47 and 1:58 (exits), 2:36 (a return); at the 2:23.5 exit the foam-block puppet appears instead. Symbolist's "floor" reading and the early "dark at exits, bright at returns" rule were downgraded by film critic, sceptic and insider to tendency or chance. No one defends it as design now.

**D. Ending: gesture or accident?**
- *Accident of length:* audio decodes to 188.15 s, the container says 201.4 s; the credit card returns at 3:06 and stays only until about 3:15 (f_0196 shows it, f_0197 at 3:16 does not), so the last ~5 s are card-free; the card does not show the picture was finished to 3:21, and the "single laid under a longer video" account is an inference only (sceptic, adopted by music critic, film critic, first-timer, symbolist). The music itself fades in an orderly way, so the music was designed to end at 3:08.
- *Effect regardless:* the silent tail works on a viewer (symbolist, film critic): bodies flinging arms at no sound. The first-timer hears it as a cut-off, "an unfinished edit"; the symbolist feels sadness from departures never reversed (voice at 2:47.5, caption at 3:04 and 3:19, sound at 3:08).
- *Insider remains agnostic* between a planned silent tail and audio that ran out.
- Moderator's resolution: most probably a length mismatch; unproven either way; the effect is real. The sceptic's "card pasted over a longer clip" argument is weakened: the card ends about 6 s before the picture does. Slowing of the cut rate from 3:01 (shots of 4.6, 6.5, 6.9 and 2.2 s) is probably an edit decision (film critic, [data]).

**E. The last frame.** Symbolist: the last face is looking slightly away, not performing at us, and the editor kept it. Film critic, first-timer, sceptic: it looks like a swinging handheld close-up at the end of a take, "a smudge of warm colour with an eye in it" (first-timer); no fade, hard end. Both agree on what is seen; they differ on whether placement is a decision. Film critic: probably a "take runs out" choice made to end on the face the film kept returning to.

**F. Break 2: padding or ritual?** Music critic: a literal paste (statement, repeat, variation), "where groove tips into padding", but also what a loop-based arranger does; the paste does not harm the A, A, B shape. First-timer: felt as padding; break 1 taught the game, break 2 said "again", break 3 woke them up.

**G. The 2:47.5 voice exit.** Music critic (leaning medium-low): the last hook loop ran out (late hooks repeat at 12.09 to 12.21 s intervals, so the last would end about 2:47.6 to 2:47.7); a backing-track count completing, not a gesture. Alternative: a mix choice. Data cannot tell them apart; "eerie" and "wry deflation" are withdrawn as claims.

**H. Is the hooded man the voice?** Never established. Mouth open at 0:26, 0:31, 0:42, 0:52 and shut at 2:46-2:48 when the voice stops (dossier); but his mouth is shut at about 2:20 while the hook voice is on, and sync at 1 fps proves nothing either way (sceptic). He is a man performing to camera; whether he is the act, a fan or a stand-in is open. The panel stopped treating him as "the voice".

**I. Earnest or parody?** Sceptic: the box-head is evidence of humour in the source footage, not in the video's framing; both survive. Insider: the caption is sales copy and the picture is "deliberately cheap and good-humoured" in fit, not provable in intent. Symbolist: affectionate comedy that is funny whether or not meant.

**J. Is "faces for the quiet" a structure?** Symbolist reads children (0:54), foam tower (2:22), solemn child (2:44) as the picture's offer of a face where the floor is out; first-timer: three picks from a film with dozens of faces, and a tower is not a face. Treated as a viewer's reading, not a finding.

## 5. Craft notes

**Music**
- One tonic A. Sub-bass is near-sine, a falling glide of about 0.7 semitone on A1, about half of the mix's spectral energy (about 55 per cent over 0:15-0:45; about 0 per cent in the bass-free intro) below 60 Hz, mono; the "other" bed is wide, so removing the bass opens the image from 0.04-0.35 to 0.37-0.71 and changes level, weight and space as one event [read, high on structure].
- Loudness is flat inside each state (about -11 to -14.5 dBFS in grooves, about -18.5 dBFS in breaks) [read].
- The voice is speech, not melody, with register jumps: hook A2-C3 (about 113 Hz), verse 1 around D4 (about 286 Hz, near 40 cents flat), verse 2 around G3 (about 194 Hz) [read]. Whether it is one performer is undeterminable.
- Felt tempo about 78-80 BPM; the tracker's 152 BPM is the double-time reading. Phase of the 0.75 s pulse is inferred from bass edges (medium-high) [read].
- Dense drum passages use straight sixteenths, no triplet signature [read]. Breaks are preceded by thickened drums (13 onsets in the last 3 s before 1:59.25; 8 before 2:23.5); returns are bare, hit, gap, bass [read].
- Six of the seven bass edges lie on a rigid 12 s grid (0:47.25, 0:59.25, 1:59.25, 2:11.25, 2:23.5, 2:35.5); this is the intentional structure in the piece (sceptic: "no coincidence produces" it).
- Production is dry and close with a band-limited top; "808-type" is inference only (low-medium); the style reads as programmed, bass-led club-style rap, with period and region not derivable from the measurements (the word "crunk" in the Whisper transcript, if correctly heard, is a lyric reference only).
- Break 3 differs because drums go with the bass, leaving the voice and plucked stabs alone; the voice itself does nothing new there (music critic).

**Editing**
- 51 cuts, 52 shots, about nine sources. About 20 of 52 shots come from three sources (trio 11, bedroom 5, crowd 4) [dossier counts]. The craft is in re-use: alternating "registers of the same gag" and a controlled colour world (film critic).
- Brightness alternates between bright cream faces (200+) and dim archive (50-60) every 2-5 s, which reads as texture (film critic, [data]).
- Cut rate: fastest in the first full groove (4.1 per 10 s), slowest in breaks 1 and 2 (1.6) and in the closing stretch [data].
- The only micro-scale editing is the 0.8 s puppet flashes at 0:17.45 and 0:20.4 and the 0.57 s jump-cut at 2:13.4; no audible hook was verified for any of them. The flashes are bird gags, "the closest thing to comic timing in the cut" (sceptic).
- The one clearly crafted passage is 2:21-2:36 (blocks, loosest trio, towel gag, slam) (film critic, first-timer).
- Long holds: 9.5 s trio take (1:30.8) and the 3:01 onwards run.

**Archive and found material**
- A dark, grainy clip of a costumed bird among people appears at 0:12, 0:47, 1:58 and 2:36; a chequered floor is visible only at 0:12 and 2:36 (0:47-0:50 and 1:58-2:03 show a bird costume among people, a hard hat at 0:48-0:50, in a dark greenish room). Same source is plausible but unconfirmed [seen]. It looks grainy and low-fi, which suggests older or lower-grade source material (impression only). The puppet and costume are a large yellow bird; the puppet sits before a wall with alphabet and numerals, in a set that reads as children's-programme-like (visual impression); origin unknown. Sound on those clips may have been replaced, untested.
- The puppet's beak opens during "I always get cake" and "Today you hate"; sync not measured.

**Structure**
- Intro (no bass, 0:00-0:12) / hook / verse 1 / break 1 / groove B / verse 2 / break 2 (copy) / groove C / break 3 (variation) / groove D / voice-free / glide / fade / silent picture [read, dossier]. Credit card at 0:02-0:11 and again from 3:06; caption strip with logo from about 0:14 to 3:04 [seen].
- Insider's small clue: the strip prints the title in quotation marks while the card does not, so the two pieces of text were made at different times or by different tools; a sign of quick assembly (low).

- **A sales frame on every frame.** The strip says what the thing is for; the dance is offered, not just performed.
- **Era and place (low).** A MySpace address and an iTunes sales line place it in an era when both were in common use (text inference only; year not established, and the production-era reading from the music is wider still); the suburban setting looks English-speaking, but country is not established. "Soulja Unit" and "FD Entertainment" are the credit card's text; nothing else is known.
- **Production.** Much of the footage looks domestic and amateur; some looks professionally or televisually made. Why the picture ignores the beat is unexplained.
- **The audio/picture length mismatch** (188.15 s audio against about 201 s of picture; 201 frames at 1 fps agree) changes how the last 13 s should be read: possibly an artefact that happens to work (cause unproven; see 4D).
- **Genre convention** (opening spoken tag, one-note boast, fade-out) is outside the data; the music critic flagged it as [spec].

- "Sad" at the end is a viewer effect on some panellists (symbolist); others find the tone playful and not sad (first-timer, music critic). Overall mood in the music is medium confidence.
