# First-timer (panel, round 1): t_birdwalk

Blind mode. I read the dossier and the checked music reading, and opened contact sheets 03 (0:40-0:59) and 09 (2:40-2:59) myself. I cannot hear; sound statements come from the music reading's measurements. Everything about who these people are or where the clips come from is unknown to me.

## What it is about

A promo video for a song called "Bird Walk" by "Soulja Unit" (the on-screen caption, seen on every sheet I opened: "Soulja Unit 'BIRD WALK' AVAILABLE NOW on iTunes"). The idea is a dance craze: a man raps that he has a new dance and keeps telling you to "watch me do that bird walk". The picture answers with a pile of yellow birds: a puppet, a person in a costume, small children in beaked hoods, a home-made box-on-the-head poncho. It is mixed with home-video dancers and a young man in a striped yellow-orange hooded towel who looks into the lens and mouths along. My reading: it is a joke and an advert at once. A cheap, affectionate, slightly daft thing, which I find charming rather than slick [interp].

## How it makes me feel, and which moments do it

- **0:00-0:12, the deadpan opening.** A yellow bird puppet's beak opens and a hype-man voice says "we got this new dance". The music has no bass at all (music reading), only one drum hit about every 1.5 s, so it feels like someone clearing his throat. I smiled. It also made me wait for something.
- **0:12, the first bass drop.** The measured jump (bass from silent to heavy in a second, mix about 5 dB louder) is the moment the song "starts". The picture cuts to a dark, grainy night clip right then (a cut about 0.4 s early). The contrast is pleasing, though I only half-trust that it was planned.
- **0:47-0:59, the empty floor.** The bass vanishes and the picture goes dark and grainy with the bird among a crowd (0:47-0:50, I saw the star-like flash), then to the hooded man shouting and, from 0:54, the small children in beaked hoods flapping. This is my favourite stretch. Sparse beat, chanting, and a solemn child. Sheet 03 shows the faces clearly: the children look serious, which is funnier than any joke.
- **0:59, the return.** Weight comes back. I felt the release even though I could not hear it, because the reading describes it so clearly and the cut to the hooded man is right there [interp].
- **2:23-2:35, the hook with no beat.** Bass and drums stop while the foam-block tower falls (2:21-2:26) and the trio dance on, then the hooded man pulls the towel over his face (2:33) and lifts it as the slam comes back (2:35). That is the cleverest bit of picture-and-sound teamwork to me: he hides, the music hides, he reappears, the music reappears.
- **2:44-2:49, the child and the shut mouth.** A very serious toddler in a googly-eyed yellow hood (2:44-2:46) is the funniest single face. Then the hooded man shuts his mouth at 2:46-2:48 and the voice stops around 2:47-2:49. It is a lovely coincidence, though I have not tested whether it is lip-sync.
- **3:01-3:08 and after.** The music fades out like a knob turned down, with no ending, then 13 more seconds of silent picture of the hooded face. I find that oddly sad and abrupt, the sort of loose end that feels like an unedited upload [interp].

## What bores me

The music reading says the whole track is one note (A), no chord changes and no tune, only layers going on and off. For a first-timer that means that by about 1:10 I would be waiting for something new. Verse 2 (1:10-1:56) is long and repeated, and the picture there uses the same few clips again (the trio clip is used 11 times, about 54 seconds in all, per the dossier). The repeated trio dance footage, mostly arms flailing out of step, is the weakest thing in the film. If the clip returns that often, I stop reading it as a dance and start reading it as filler.

## What is clever, what is weak

Clever:
- The bass-off, bass-on count in 12-second blocks. Even without the numbers I feel it as a trick the track plays three times, and by the second time I am expecting it.
- The picture seems to track the loud and quiet changes loosely: the fastest cutting (4.1 cuts per 10 s) is in the first full groove, the slowest (1.6) in breaks 1 and 2 (dossier, from sync.json). Cuts do not lock to the beat (21.6 per cent within 80 ms against about 42 per cent by chance), so the sync is at the level of sections, not beats. I read that as an editor cutting by feel rather than by grid.
- The yellow rhyme: the bird, the towel, the poncho, the bedroom walls and the caption text are all yellow. It holds a patchwork of footage together.
- Literal pairing of words and picture: "we just bird dance at home" over dancers in a room (0:28), "show you how it go" over a shuffling pair of feet (1:04 and 1:47, per the dossier, which I did not open).

Weak:
- The tracks and caption: "available now on iTunes" runs over almost the whole film, so I never feel I am simply watching; I am being sold to. A bit cynical, a bit funny.
- Break 2 is, by the music reading, a pasted copy of break 1 (72.00 s lag). It works as a ritual repeat for the ear, but as a viewer I would call it lazy if I noticed it, and the picture is no fresher.
- The tail: 13 s of silence at the end, and an ending that fades instead of landing.

## What I would tell a friend

"It is a cheap, funny, slightly ridiculous promo where someone has stuck yellow-bird footage under a rap about a dance. Watch the first minute and 2:20-2:50. The bass drops are fun. Don't expect a tune, and don't judge the dancing."

## Three questions for the other panellists

1. **Is the joke intended?** The deadpan bird, the solemn child and the box-head poncho make me laugh, but the song itself is a boast (money, club, "so icy"). Does the collage read as parody, tribute or accident, and what in the data or frames would settle that?
2. **Does the hooded man sing the voice?** He is on screen at all three bass returns (0:59.8, 2:11, 2:35) and shuts his mouth as the voice stops at 2:47. The dossier says sync was never tested. Can anyone check the mouth against the vocal stem before we call him the performer?
3. **How much of the structure is the viewer's expectation?** The music reading says the drama is spatial (bass in or out, wide or narrow), not loudness. After the first break the listener learns the count. Does the picture help that learning, or does the film only feel structured because the music is? In particular, was the cut-to-dark at 0:47 and 1:58 (the crowd clip) chosen to match the drops, or is the match by chance, given that only five of seven bass edges have a cut within 0.9 s, which is about what chance gives (37 per cent per edge)?
