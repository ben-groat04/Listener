## 1. What the piece is about

On its face it is a promotional video for a dance-craze rap single. Burned into the picture: "Soulja Unit / Bird Walk / FD Entertainment / MySpace.com/SouljaUnitMusic" (0:02-0:11, 3:06 on), then a strip reading "Soulja Unit 'BIRD WALK' AVAILABLE NOW on iTunes" (about 0:14-3:04) [seen]. The words are a boast plus an instruction: "we got this new dance ... watch me do that bird walk", "we don't out-dance no more, we just bird-dance at home", "I'ma show you how it go" [heard-ish]. The picture's job is to show "the bird walk" being done by as many different people and birds as possible: a trio in a pale room, a lone teenager in a bedroom, a person in a yellow poncho with a box over the head, children in beaked hoods, a giant yellow puppet, a person-sized bird costume, and one hooded man in close-up who acts as the face of the thing.

Underneath, it is a joke built on collision: a children's-television bird (soft, educational, alphabet board behind it at 1:16) as the mouthpiece for a club boast ("so icy", "money, money, money", "tomorrow's my best, tomorrow's your worst"). Nothing in the data tells me whether the puppet clips are used with permission, as tribute or as parody [spec]. It is plausibly a viral-marketing piece: low cost, instantly recognisable images, one repeatable gesture (flapping arms, shuffling feet).

## 4. What is clever

1. **One gesture, many bodies.** Flapping arms plus a hopping step is shown on adults, teens, kids, a costume, a puppet and a box-head, so the dance reads as something anyone can do. That is the craze mechanism in miniature [infer].
2. **Yellow as the controlling colour** (bird, hood, poncho, bedroom walls, caption text) [seen, dossier]. It binds nine mismatched sources into one world cheaply.
3. **Counted subtraction in the music, answered by the picture.** Three bass cuts and returns on a 12 s count [read]; the hooded man is on screen at all three returns (0:59.8, 2:11 mid-shot, 2:35 end of shot) [dossier, seen at 2:35]. Whether intended or not, he becomes the "face of the drop".
4. **The deadpan reuse of the same clips** (trio 11 times, crowd clip 4) is thrifty and turns into a running gag [dossier].
5. **The towel reveal at 2:33-2:35.**

## 5. What is weak

- **The picture does not lock to the music.** Cuts cluster mid-beat (mean phase 0.51, Rayleigh p = 0.04); only 21.6 per cent of cuts within 80 ms of a tracked beat [dossier data]. For a dance song, the cutting is not dance-literate [interp]. The tracker may be double-time, so I will not say cuts "avoid" the beat.
- **The dance itself is under-shown.** Few shots hold long enough to learn a step (the 9.5 s trio take at 1:30.8 and the legs at 1:03.6 are the exceptions), and the trio is out of step [seen]. If the aim is a craze, there is no tutorial moment [interp].
- **The caption strip is a heavy-handed ad** from 0:14 to 3:04 [seen], which I read as a pragmatic marketing choice, but it flattens the comedy.
- **No ending.** The song is faded out, then silent footage for 13 s [read, dossier]; the final shape is a shrug.
- **The music is a loop with a count** [read]; there is almost no musical development except the subtraction trick, which is repeated until it is ritual.
