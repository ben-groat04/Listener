# The Sceptic: Round 1 reading of t_birdwalk

Blind mode. Sources: the checked music reading, the dossier, sync.json, and my own look at contact sheets 01, 07, 08 and 10 (0:00-0:19, 2:00-2:39, 3:00-3:19). I cannot hear; sound claims are the music reading's measurements. Tags: [seen] I checked it in frames; [data] measurement file; [read] music reading; [interp] interpretation; [spec] speculation.

## 1. What it is about (the down-to-earth version)

It is an advert for a song. The on-screen text says so, in plain words: "Soulja Unit / Bird Walk / FD Entertainment / MySpace.com/SouljaUnitMusic" at 0:02-0:11 and again from 3:06, and a strip reading "Soulja Unit 'BIRD WALK' AVAILABLE NOW on iTunes" from about 0:14 to 3:04 [seen, sheets 01, 07, 08, 10]. The picture is a collage of about nine recycled sources: a yellow puppet bird, a person-sized bird costume, a person in a yellow poncho with a box on the head, a trio dancing in a pale room, a lone dancer in a yellow bedroom, a young man in a striped yellow-orange-pink hooded towel, dark old crowd footage with a bird figure, and small children in beaked hoods. The sound is a programmed, bass-heavy rap track about a dance called the "bird walk" [read]. So the piece is about: "here is a new dance, here is our act, buy it". Everything else is craft or charm layered on that.

I would resist "meaning" beyond that. The yellow bird recurs because the song title says "bird", not because of a design scheme. Yellow is the controlling colour because the sources were chosen (or happened) to be yellow, which is an obvious thing to do when the subject is a yellow bird [interp]. That is competent, even neat, but it is not depth.

## 2. How it makes me feel, and which moments do it

Cheeky, daft, affectionate, a little threadbare. I would not claim more. Moments, with the evidence:

- **0:00-0:05, the opening gag.** A close-up of the puppet bird against flat blue; the beak opens at about 0:03-0:04 [seen, sheet 01] while the voice announces "this new dance" [read, Whisper, medium]. It reads as the bird talking in a hype-man voice. That is the single best joke in the piece, and it works because the picture is deadpan. Caveat: the beak opening is visible, but nobody has tested that it follows the voice; one open-beak frame at 0:03-0:04 is thin evidence of "sync" [spec].
- **0:12.25, first bass drop.** The music reading's strongest finding: bass goes from -71 to about -19 dB in one second, mix 5 dB louder [read, high]. The picture cut arrives about 0.4 s early onto a dark, grainy crowd clip [seen, sheet 01, 0:12]. The impact is real in the sound; the picture goes dim at that moment, so picture and sound pull in opposite directions rather than together [seen]. I find that more "coincidence of two edits" than design.
- **0:47-0:59 and 1:59-2:11, the empty floor.** The bass leaves, the image widens, the music becomes drum cracks and a chopped chant [read, high]. This is the main thing the track does, and it is a sound-design idea, not a picture idea.
- **2:11.25, the return, with the hooded man mid-shot, no cut** (sheet 07, 2:11-2:13: he is on screen and the picture does not change) [seen]. Satisfying, but see question 2.
- **2:21-2:26, the tumbling foam blocks** (sheet 08) [seen]: the puppet sits in a tower of foam blocks and it falls apart as the bass and drums cut at 2:23.5 [read]. Prettiest coincidence in the film. It is a coincidence of timing (the clip has no reason to be about the music) and I like it as a viewer, not as a claim about intent.
- **2:44, the unsmiling child in a beaked hood** [dossier, seen description; I did not open that sheet]. Deadpan comedy from a source that was not made for this.
- **3:01-3:08, the fade.** A 30 dB fade in about 7 s [read, data]; the picture goes to the poncho figure, then the hooded man and the credit card. There is no resolution in the sound [read]. The silent 13 s afterwards (3:08-3:21) is the odd, slightly deflating end: picture with no sound [seen, sheet 10]. I read it as the audio ending before the video, not as a chosen silence. The dossier's own check says the container claims 201.4 s but the audio decodes to 188.15 s: a mismatch that is exactly what an unmatched upload would produce. Down-to-earth reading: the video is longer than the music [data].

Overall feeling: amused, a little charmed by the amateurishness, and bodily moved by the bass edges. Not moved emotionally; there is no tenderness or sadness I can point to.

## 3. What is clever, what is weak

**Clever (earned):**
1. The 12-second subtraction template in the music is simple, readable and repeated until it is felt [read, high]. After break 1 the listener can predict every later cut and return.
2. Colour echo: the hood's yellow, orange and pink stripes against the bird's yellow and the yellow bedroom walls and caption text [seen]. A cheap, effective unifier of mismatched footage.
3. The literal gags: "we just bird-dance at home" over the trio at about 0:28 [dossier, heard-ish], "show you how it go" with the red-shirt dancer flailing at 0:42 [dossier]. Funny if the Whisper words are right; I cannot confirm them.

**Weak, or over-read by others:**
1. **"The picture follows the structure."** The measurements say the picture does not lock to the beat: 21.6 per cent of cuts within 80 ms of a beat against about 42 per cent expected by chance [data]. The one significant item is a weak phase clustering (Rayleigh p = 0.04, resultant R about 0.26, n = 49) [data], and the cut-rate/loudness and motion/onset correlations are r = 0.27 and 0.30, which explain under 10 per cent of the variance each, with 5 s smoothing that inflates apparent correlation [data, notes]. This is a weak, noisy relationship. A reading that says "the picture breathes with the music" is stronger than the data.
2. **"Every bass return lands on the hooded man."** The dossier counts three returns with him on screen. Three, in a film where he appears 15 times in about 201 s (the dossier's count) is not rare: on those numbers he fills roughly 20 to 30 per cent of the screen time, so three for three is about a one-in-ten to one-in-fifteen event if chance, from three trials chosen after the fact [my arithmetic, rough]. Also, the hooded man is on screen at only part of those moments: at 0:59.8 the cut is 0.54 s after the bass, and at 2:35 the bass returns at the very end of his shot. I would say: suggestive, not shown.
3. **The five-of-seven "cuts near the edges" count.** The dossier itself calls it suggestive, not strong, because n = 7 and a 0.9 s window is generous. At 2:11.25 and 2:23.5 there is no cut within 2 s [data]. Note the dark crowd clip at the edges 0:12, 0:47 and 1:58 is a pattern worth keeping, but notice the music reading's own edge precision is 0.1-0.25 s, so a "0.27 s early" cut is within the combined error in both directions, and "early by 0.86 s" is not a match at all.
4. **Mouth-sync claims.** "His mouth closes as the voice stops at 2:47" [dossier] is a neat coincidence from a single frame sequence; and the dossier itself says sync was never tested. In sheets 07 and 08 his mouth is open at 2:08-2:13 and 2:33-2:35 [seen], consistent with singing along, but that proves a man mouthing along to something, not that he is the voice on the track [spec].
5. **The puppet's identity and "tribute or parody" talk.** In blind mode the puppet is only a yellow bird puppet [seen]. Any reading about children's TV, irony or juxtaposition against club rap depends on outside knowledge or on the viewer recognising a character. The visible facts are: the clips are old-looking, soft and saturated, and the track is a modern rap boast [seen, read]. The "innocence against swagger" contrast is a fair interpretation, but it is mine, not data.
6. **Deep claims about the music.** The music reading is careful and I largely accept it. I would hold lightly: "wry deflation" at 2:57-3:01 (the file itself says sweep, sample or shout cannot be told apart, and the vocal stem there is only -33 to -56 dB, possibly bleed [read, low]); "eerie" at 2:47-2:57 (interpretation, medium-low in the reading's own words); and "hypnotic" at 0:24-0:47 (a near-monotone voice and thin drums can feel hypnotic or merely repetitive).
7. **Production values.** The footage is low-resolution, over-bright and visibly re-compressed [seen]. A good deal of the "lo-fi charm" is a fact about the source files, not a style choice. I would not praise the editing for effects that the footage's condition produces for free (the washed-out hood man, the green-tinted archive).

## 4. A plain summary

A promotional video for a programmed rap track: the music is a rigorous but simple repeating structure (bass off, bass on, in 12 s blocks, over one tonic A) with a speech-like voice and a catchy refrain; the picture is a cheap, charming, yellow-themed collage that sells the dance and the act, with a few happy coincidences of edit and sound. The relationship between picture and music is real but weak in the data. It is a joke told deadpan, and the best parts are the opening beak and the bass drops.

## 5. Three questions for the other panellists

1. **Evidence for intent.** You can point to a few coincidences (the dark clip at 0:12, 0:47, 1:58; the hooded man at three bass returns; the foam blocks falling at 2:23). For each, what would a random edit of these same nine clips look like? Give me a number or a test, or call it a coincidence. Specifically: how often would a returning bass land on any shot of the hooded man, given he fills roughly a quarter of the film?
2. **Intent behind the silence.** The audio ends at 3:08.15 and the picture runs to 3:21.4 [data]. Is the final 13 s silent picture a decision (a held credit card, a sign-off), or simply an unmatched video and audio length? What in the frames (3:12-3:19 pale-room dancers, 3:19-3:21 face close-up then black) shows it was planned rather than left over?
3. **Earnest or parody?** Everyone calls the tone "cheeky" and "deadpan". Name the single frame or second that tells me the makers are in on the joke rather than sincerely selling a dance, and say what would count as evidence against that reading. The caption strip, the iTunes line and the credit card are, on their face, earnest.
