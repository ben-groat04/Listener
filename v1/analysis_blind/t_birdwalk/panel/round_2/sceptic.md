## 1. The test I demanded, now done (answering Symbolist, Film critic, First-timer, Music critic)

Three of you asked, and I asked myself, how often a bass return would land on the hooded man by chance. Using the dossier's 15 hooded-man intervals, he is on screen for about 47 s of 201 s, or 23 per cent [calc].

- **Concession.** In round 1 I said three-for-three was "about one in ten to fifteen". That was too generous. Treating the three returns (0:59.25, 2:11.25, 2:35.5) as independent draws at 23 per cent gives about 1.3 per cent (roughly 1 in 77) [calc]. All three do fall on him, and at 2:35 he is on screen from 2:34.8 to 2:36.2, so he is there at the slam [seen, sheet 08].
- **Why I still do not call it design.** (a) The three were picked after the fact from seven edges, and the four other edges (0:12.25, 0:47.25, 1:59.25, 2:23.5) have no hooded man [calc]. If I had also counted "bass-off lands on a bird shot" I would have had another pattern to report; that is the forking-paths problem. A fair haircut moves 1 per cent to something nearer 5 to 10 per cent. (b) The shots are not independent: his shots are 2-6 s long and the edges are on a rigid 12 s grid, so the true chance depends on how his appearances happen to fall against 12 s multiples. With 15 appearances in 201 s, some alignment with a 12 s count is quite likely. (c) At 2:11 there is no cut at all (mid-shot) [data], and at 0:59.8 the cut is 0.54 s after the bass, so for two of three the editor did not "cut to a face on the beat"; the face was simply there or arrived late.
- **Verdict.** Raised from "suggestive" to "mildly surprising, not yet evidence". A viewer may feel it, because the face-returns-with-the-floor pairing recurs; I will not go beyond that.

**An unflagged pattern that is arguably stronger.** The dark crowd clip is on screen at three of the four bass-off or first-drop edges (0:12.25, 0:47.25, 1:59.25) [calc, with the 0.4, 0.27 and 0.86 s early cuts]. It fills only about 8 per cent of the film (16 s) [calc], so three of four at 8 per cent is a nominal chance of about 0.2 per cent. Same haircut applies (post hoc, grid-aligned), and I note that it is the opposite pairing to the symbolist's: the crowd clip marks where the floor *leaves*, the face where it *returns*. That is the more interesting structure on the table: **dark and old when the floor goes, bright and frontal when it comes back**. The one break that breaks the rule is 2:23.5, where the puppet and foam blocks are on screen, not the crowd clip [seen, sheet 08]. A rule with a 75 per cent hit rate on n = 4 is a tendency.

## 2. Is the hooded man the voice? (Symbolist, Music critic, First-timer, Insider)

I can add a little evidence, all weak. On sheet 08 his mouth is **open** at 2:34 (towel lifted, mid-line) but at 2:20-2:21 (white vest, head back, eyes shut) and at 2:35 (calm, lips together) it looks **closed** [seen], while the hook voice is on through all of 2:19-2:47 [dossier, read]. The chopped voice at 2:35.5 on is every 0.2-0.4 s, which no one could mouth, so 2:35 proves little. But 2:20-2:21 is a plain case of voice on, mouth shut. Add the silent end: at 3:07-3:11 the voice has been gone since 2:49 and his mouth is closed or pursed [seen, sheet 10]; at 3:08-3:11 he gestures at the lens instead.

So mouth-state does not cleanly track the voice at 1 fps. The neat "mouth closes as the voice stops at 2:47" is one of at least three mouth-closed moments, two of them with the voice running. I would withdraw "coincidence worth noting" and say: **no test supports lip-sync, one test (2:20) mildly cuts against it.** The picture supports "a man performing to camera" and nothing about whose voice it is. I agree with Music critic and First-timer that a frame-by-frame check against the vocal stem is the only real answer; I can only offer 1 fps.

## 3. The silent tail: planned or leftover? (answering my own question, with new evidence)

Sheet 10 gives something nobody used. The credit card (Soulja Unit / Bird Walk / FD Entertainment / MySpace.com/SouljaUnitMusic) **appears at 3:06 over the hooded man and stays through the cut to the trio at 3:12 and until 3:18** [seen]. The strip before it ghosts away at 3:04 [seen]. So the picture track was finished as a designed piece to about 3:18, graphics included, and its last shot (3:19, the tilted face) has no card [seen]. The audio, by contrast, is a produced fade that completes at 3:08 with -62 dB [data]. The most down-to-earth reading, consistent with the duration mismatch (the container says 201.4 s, the audio decodes to 188.15 s) [data]: **the single was laid under a video 13 s longer**, and nobody extended it. The picture tail is planned; the silence is a by-product. Film critic's "export accident" and Symbolist's "best formal gesture" are both defensible as readings; as a claim about intent, only the first is the minimal one. I concede to the Symbolist that it is *moving* regardless, but moving by accident is still accident. **Note:** this also answers Music critic's question about the ending: the fade is the song's own (it ends the file), and the 13 s are the video's.

## 4. Earnest or parody? (my own question, answered by the panel's non-answers)

No one named a frame that proves the makers are in on a joke, and I do not think one exists. What the frames show: the strip says "AVAILABLE NOW on iTunes" for 2:50 and the card carries a label and MySpace address [seen]. Everything humorous (the solemn child, the box-head with a drawn face at 3:01-3:05 [seen], the beak opening at 0:03) is also what a sincere dance-craze promo made with whatever footage was to hand would contain. A joke needs a wink; I see only deadpan, and deadpan with no wink is not distinguishable from earnest. What would count as evidence: the bird's mouth timed precisely to a lyric (untested), or any on-screen text breaking character. Neither is in the data. So: "funny to us" is solid, "meant to be funny" is unknown, and "parody of children's TV" needs outside knowledge that blind mode forbids. The Insider's line "the educational bird delivers the insult" (1:16-1:29) is a lovely juxtaposition, but note the puppet shots could equally be chosen because they were the yellow bird available.

## 5. Where I agree, and a small push-back on structure-learning

- **Agree with Music critic** that the loudness is flat and the drama is spatial, and that after break 1 the listener can predict returns. I agree with First-timer's third question that the film *feels* structured because the music is. Measured, the picture's own tie is cut rate against loudness r = 0.27 at roughly 4 s lag [data], i.e. under 10 per cent of variance. The dossier's "cuts fastest in the first groove (4.1 per 10 s)" is dominated by 0:12-0:47, which has the most **bird-gag short shots** (0.8 s flashes at 0:17 and 0:20), so it may reflect an editor opening with a flurry, not following the music.
- **Push back on Symbolist's "object permanence"**: the towel reveal is real [seen, 2:33-2:35] and charming, but he is a man in a towel on a cream wall, who appears 15 times; hiding and revealing his face is something a performer does to a camera. Also "the bass is object permanence" claims a lot of a clip whose audio link (the lift at 2:34.8, bass at 2:35.5) is a 0.7 s gap [calc].
- **Push back on "the sneakers arrive late like imitation"** (Symbolist, Insider): a 3-5 s lag comes from Whisper's loose timings (0.5-1 s) plus the dossier's guess at which line the sneakers answer. I would not build a theme on that.
- **Agree with Film critic** that the archive footage is "placed, not handled": sheet 08 at 2:36-2:39 shows the same clip untouched except for the caption strip across it [seen].

## 6. The thread I find most interesting

The **dark-clip-at-the-exit, face-at-the-return** pairing (section 1). It is the only structural pattern in the picture that has both a plausible mechanism (an editor looking at the waveform for the loud edges, and dropping a dim clip over the thinned-out section, a bright face over the loud one) and numbers. It also fits the dossier's brightness alternation (cream 200+ against archive 50-60) and the sync file's weak warmth-against-loudness link (r = 0.38 at 4 s lag) [data]: brightness may be the editor's rough instrument, tied to the sound's weight. If so, the "design" is crude and brightness-based, not beat-based or symbolic. I hold it as a hypothesis for the panel to try to break.
