# The Sceptic: Round 3 (final) on t_birdwalk

Blind mode, British spelling, times m:ss. Tags: [seen] frame checked; [data] measurement file; [read] music reading; [calc] arithmetic; [interp]; [spec]. New this round: I checked the last shots in scenes/shots.json and the final contact sheet (sheet 11, the 3:20 frame).

## 1. Answering the questions put to me

**Symbolist: was the 13 s tail cut to its own length, and is the last shot a trim or a fade?** The shot list gives shot 51 as 3:12.33-3:19.2 (6.87 s) and shot 52 as 3:19.2-3:21.43 (2.24 s) [data]. Shot 51 is a normal, full-length shot (compare 5-7 s shots at 2:49-3:12). Shot 52 is short, but nothing in the file says why; a detector would also close a file on a short final shot. On sheet 11 the 3:20 frame is a full-frame, saturated orange face (eye, nose, pink at the top edge) against a black contact-sheet background [seen]. I have to correct a shared reading: "lit in one corner" is partly an artefact of the sheet, because the thumbnail sits in the top-left cell and the rest of the sheet is empty black. The frame is not a dip to black. So the picture goes from live image to the end of the file, with no fade I can see at 1 fps. That answers the question: the data shows a hard end on a shot of ordinary-to-short length, not a designed fade. It neither proves nor rules out a trim.

**Was the trio take chosen?** It is the clip reused eleven times (dossier). Reuse cuts against choice, as the Symbolist suggests. I would say it was probably the nearest filler. But I also concede the other side: the edit chose to place it in the credit-card stretch, and a viewer cannot tell choice from convenience, so the effect (a calm, wordless ending) is real either way [interp].

**Film critic: the 0.8 s flashes and the jump-cut at 2:13.4.** I over-claimed that the opening flurry "may reflect an editor opening with a flurry". Cut rate against loudness is r = 0.27 at about 4 s lag [data]. I stand by "weak", but I accept the flashes at 0:17 and 0:20 are bird gags (seen earlier), and gags are a kind of choice. They are the closest thing to comic timing in the cut, not music-following.

## 2. Where I conceded and where I now hold

- **The hooded-man pairing.** Everyone, including me, now reports the same arithmetic: about 23 per cent of running time, so three-for-three is about 1.3 per cent if independent [calc]. The Music critic found, and I have verified from the shot list, that the first return is 0:59.25 and the cut is 0:59.79 [data], so the face arrives half a second late, and 2:11 has no cut. I hold: a post hoc pattern with a plausible haircut to 5-10 per cent, not evidence of design. The Insider's "suggestive" and the Film critic's "mildly surprising" are fair; "design" is not earned.
- **The dark crowd clip at the exits.** The First-timer found the clip also appears at 2:36-2:37, which is at a return and not a break, and the break at 2:23.5 has the puppet and foam blocks instead. So my "dark when the floor goes" rule is 3 of 4 exits and also appears at a return. I downgrade it from "the most interesting structure" to "a tendency I cannot separate from the clip simply being used often". Credit to the First-timer.
- **Lip-sync.** I agree with the room: the mouth test at 1 fps proves nothing either way. The hooded man's mouth is shut at 2:20 with the hook voice on [seen]. Nobody can claim he is the voice.
- **Earnest or parody.** The Insider has conceded the text is sales copy; the Film critic and Symbolist offer the box-head with the drawn face (3:01-3:05) as a made joke. I accept that: someone made that costume, and it is funny on purpose or by childlike play. That is evidence of humour in the source footage, not evidence the *video* is in on a joke. Both readings survive.
- **The ending.** Weight of evidence from the panel and the data: audio decodes to 188.15 s, container says 201.4 s [data]; the vocal stem is gone by 2:49 and the fade finishes at 3:08 [read]; the credit card stays up to 3:18 [seen]. The Music critic's point is fair: the music itself exits in an orderly way, so the *music* is designed to end at 3:08. What stays unproven is whether anyone meant the 13 s of silence. Minimal reading: the song was laid under a longer picture. The Symbolist's "best effect, not shown to be a gesture" is the wording I would sign.

## 3. The thread I find most worth developing: the arrangement is intended, the picture is not tied to it

The Music critic's strongest point is one I accept completely: the six bass edges (0:47.25, 0:59.25, 1:59.25, 2:11.25, 2:23.5, 2:35.5) sit on a rigid 12 s grid [read], which no coincidence produces. That is the intention in this piece, and it lives in the sound. Against that, the picture's own timing is loose: cuts within 80 ms of the beat are at about chance level and the cut-to-loudness link is under 10 per cent of variance [data]. So the best summary of the *relationship* is: a carefully built track with a picture that is mostly independent of it, but arranged by someone who sensed where to put the brightness and the faces. I note that this would mean the felt unity of the piece comes largely from the music's regularity and the yellow motif, and that viewers (the First-timer said it well) supply the sync themselves. That is a real human response and a real finding, and it does not need a hidden plan.

One caution on my own earlier remark about brightness as "the editor's rough instrument": warmth against loudness r = 0.38 at 4 s lag [data] is modest, and I offered it as a hypothesis. It remains one. I have not seen it falsified, and I have not seen it supported.

## 4. Settled reading (five sentences)

1. This is a high-spirited promotional video for a dance-craze song about a bird, in which a rigidly regular, 12-bar-style arrangement (bass leaves and returns on a 12 s grid, with the third break the only one that changes) carries a loosely related collage of yellow-and-orange footage: a man in a hood and towel, a puppet bird, small children in beaked hoods, old dark crowd footage, a box-head figure, and an advert strip across most of it.
2. The feeling is warm, silly and slightly stubborn; the humour is deadpan and I cannot show it is intended, but the box with the drawn face is plainly someone's joke.
3. The music is the structure; the picture follows it only by tendency (faces and brightness at the returns, a dark clip at three of four exits), none of which survives a sceptical count as design.
4. The strongest sound-and-picture contact is the hooded man's towel lift just before the 2:35.5 bass slam, which works on a viewer whether or not it was planned.
5. The silent final 13 s, with a credit card over a calm trio and a face that fills the frame and then ends, is most simply a longer picture under a shorter song, yet it leaves the piece on its most human and least advertised image.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 2:34-2:36.** The towel comes up off the hooded man's face, the vocal starts chopping, and the bass slams back at 2:35.5 [seen, read]. It holds the piece's whole bargain in two seconds: a regular, almost mechanical arrangement, a droll human face, and a coincidence the viewer is glad to take as meaning.
