## 1. Overview

A 6:01 instrumental music video (360.98 s) built wholly from 4:3 pillarboxed, old-looking film: grainy, faded, mixed black-and-white and early colour, with some tinted or green night-vision-style passages. It opens on two white-on-black title cards ("THE NOISE WEEKEND ORCHESTRA", then "C-SHELLS C-SHORE") over 11.3 s of exact digital silence, then the music jolts in with a close-up of what looks like a record-player pick-up, and a beach scene with a white open-topped car and a figure facing the sea. What follows is a collage of mid-twentieth-century instructional, scientific, domestic and military footage: a measuring counter, a quartz ultraviolet lamp, goggled and masked people, bodies being tested (blindfold and eye-mask routines, a garden grappling demonstration, an infant under a device), radar and aircraft, a recurring night tower crowned with a fan of tube-lights, a camera, an eye, a colour-disc test. The unifying subject is looking, sensing and being measured, and the machines and light that do it, but nothing in the frames states a thesis. The music is a stern, trance-like build on a fixed A centre: a dark drone, stabs, drums and bass arriving one layer at a time from 1:04.8, a full groove from 1:53.4, subtraction-driven changes (bass out 3:44-4:11.5 and elsewhere), a drum-led finale, then drums out at 5:17, an uneven fade from about 5:29, a one-sample cut to digital zero at 5:42.84 and 18.1 s of zero after. The picture fades to black about 5:40-5:41 and stays black. No human voice is demonstrated anywhere; every transcript line is a decoder hallucination. The picture is rhythmically busy but not beat-locked (cuts within 80 ms of a beat: 33.3 % against 32.6 % chance), yet several large cuts coincide with music edges to within about 0.1-0.3 s (section 4). The feeling is hushed, uneasy, wry and hypnotic, with a calm, elegiac close on a lamp-lit pier.
