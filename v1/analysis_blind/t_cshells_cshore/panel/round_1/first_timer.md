# First-timer (panellist, no expertise): Round 1 reading of t_cshells_cshore

Blind mode. I read the music reading first, then the dossier, and opened contact sheets 01, 10 and 16 myself (0:00-0:19, 3:00-3:19, 5:00-5:19). Everything else about the picture is from the dossier and perceive notes and is marked as relayed. Times m:ss. I cannot hear the audio; my sense of the music is the checked reading's measurements turned into feeling, and I say so where it matters.

## 1. What I think it is about

A six-minute instrumental laid under a collage of old-looking instructional and scientific film: people being tested, blindfolded, goggled, balanced, grappled, wired to machines, plus radar, aircraft, a light-crowned tower, cameras and eyes. My gut says it is about being watched, measured and signalled at, and about not being able to see. Nothing on screen says so (the only legible words are the two title cards, "TRAIL" and "FEET", a quartz ultraviolet lamp plate, and a "FIRE ... BOX" sign), so that is interpretation. The title sounds like "sea shells, sea shore", and the film does start and end at the sea (beach 0:16 and 5:01, pier and ship-side sea 5:04-5:41). That pun is my speculation.

Plainly: the music is a cold, patient machine and the picture is people being poked by other machines. The pairing works because neither explains the other.

## 2. How it makes me feel, and which moments do it

- **0:00-0:11, two title cards over nothing (checked: exact digital zero).** Slightly awkward at first, then expectant. I saw the cards in the sheet; the silence is from the reading.
- **0:11, the jolt.** A close-up of a record-player cartridge (sheet 01 shows it clearly; the print on it looks like "STEREO" to me, but that is small and blurred, so treat it as a guess) arrives with the first sound, which jumps from nothing to a nervous burst of clicks and noise. That is the best single move in the piece: the needle drops. Then the calm shot of a woman in grey with her back to us beside a white open-topped car, facing the sea (0:16). That image is lovely and lonely, and it sets the emotional tone for the whole thing.
- **0:24-1:04, the drone.** Dark, still, a bit menacing; pictures of a measuring box, a cockpit, a dim room and a lamp apparatus (relayed). This is the "something is about to be done to someone" section.
- **1:04.8, drums arrive and the picture cuts to a school of fish 0.2 s before (relayed).** I would feel the weight arrive.
- **1:53.4, the lock-in.** After two dropouts the full groove arrives, and the dossier says a cut lands within 0.13 s. Strongest picture-to-music match in the film, per the measurements. This is where I would settle in and stop waiting.
- **3:06, the infant in goggles with a metal cylinder on its back (sheet 10 confirms: undressed baby, dark eye covers, cylinder, cable).** The most unsettling image of the film for me, and it sits under a loosening bass (checked: soft from about 3:15). Then at 3:18 the tower flashes up on black (sheet 10). I like how the tower works as a hard punctuation mark after clinical footage.
- **3:44-4:11, bass out, music brighter and thinner but not quieter.** Over a 12.5 s static take of two people bending (relayed). Calm picture, exposed sound: odd and rather good.
- **4:46-4:50, a camera lifted to the eye, then a huge eye (relayed).** The film's thesis moment, if it has one: looking at looking.
- **5:00-5:06, a wave, then a hard cut back to the opening beach, the woman and the car (sheet 16 confirms), which dissolves into a night pier.** This is the moment that moved me. The loop back to the first image after five minutes of testing and surveillance feels like a memory returning, and then the day turns to night.
- **5:17-5:42, drums out, pad, fade, pier held for about 17 s (relayed), picture to black by about 5:41 and sound cut dead at 5:42.84.** Elegiac, empty. A calm after a long tension. Then 18 s of pure black and silence, which I find either bold or an export accident (undecidable, as the data say).

## 3. What is clever, what is weak

Clever:
- The music barely changes key or melody; it changes by adding and removing layers, and the level stays within about 6 dB for minutes. That gives a hypnotic, trance-like push, and the picture supplies the variety. Cutting is not tied to the beat (33.3 % within 80 ms of a beat against 32.6 % chance), yet the big edges are shadowed by cuts to about 0.1-0.3 s several times (0:24.4, 1:04.6, 1:53.3, 4:11.4, 4:46.2). I am not sure whether that is craft or chance among about 84 cuts; the dossier flags that no permutation test exists.
- Recurring motifs are well chosen and easy to spot: the tower (0:44, 0:53, 2:43, 3:18, 4:38), covered eyes, the brick wall, red alarm boxes, and the opening beach returning. A viewer who has never seen the footage can follow these.
- The refusal to use voice. Faces that look as if they are shouting (2:07, 3:30-3:35, relayed) over a track with no voice are eerie.

Weak or risky:
- The middle can feel like a very long run of the same drive. The music's own reading calls 1:53-3:44 flat within about 3 dB, and the picture there is a flurry of tests that all start to rhyme (balance, mask, grappling, bend). I would be at risk of numbness.
- The picture is not obviously driven by the music (cut rate vs loudness r = 0.25, not distinguishable from shuffled data). A viewer hoping for a drop-synchronised edit would not get one.
- The 18 s black-and-silent tail is dead air whatever the intent.
- Many images are murky or unreadable (the green night-vision footage at 4:15-4:30 and 5:06-5:13; I saw 5:07-5:08 in sheet 16 and could not tell what it was). Murkiness is atmospheric but also hides things.

## 4. Three questions for the other panellists

1. Is the 1:53 lock-in, 3:44 bass-out and 4:46 bass-return structure something a listener actually feels as a story, or is it only visible in stem charts? Does the music have a feeling of climax anywhere, given the level never really rises?
2. Do the several cut-to-edge coincidences (about 0.1-0.3 s) amount to evidence of deliberate editing, or are they what you would expect from 84 cuts and a dozen edges?
3. What should we make of the return to the opening beach at 5:01 and the pier that follows: memory, departure, or just a loop? And does the music (a pad dissolving, then cut dead) support the elegiac reading or undercut it?

## 6. BOREDOM NOTES (first person)

- I expect a drift around 2:09-2:43. A 14 s take of two men balancing on one leg, then 18 s of a masked person in a chair (relayed), under a groove that is at its most settled; this is where the film asks for patience. The music's drums thin from 2:40, which would not help.
- 2:40-3:07: the music reading names a "lull" or slackening here. I would probably glaze a little.
- 3:47-3:59: a 12.5 s take of people bending, under the sparsest music. I am not sure if I would be bored or hypnotised; honest guess: slightly bored on first watch, then curious about why it is held so long.
- The 18 s of black and silence at the end: I would reach for the stop button.
- Early 0:11-0:24: not bored, but the clicks and noise are a bit abrasive; I would be restless rather than bored.
- Caveat: I have not heard the audio and I have seen the frames only as contact sheets plus the dossier, so my boredom notes are predictions from the measured structure, not lived experience.

## 7. CHANGES OF MIND

1. At first I read the pick-up close-up at 0:11 as the "needle drop", the start of a record. After seeing the film end on a pier and the dossier's note that the same beach returns, I now think the record-player is just one more apparatus in the film's list of machines for sensing and reproducing, not a framing device. I hold that loosely.
2. I began by assuming the sparse stretch at 3:44-4:11 would feel like a lull. The measurements say the mix gets brighter and its level actually rises (about 1.8 dB, peak -13.9 dBFS short-term), so I now think of it as lightness by subtraction, not a rest. I cannot hear it, so this is a revision on evidence, not on ears.
3. I first thought the voices in the transcripts ("top of a mountain", Welsh) might be hidden samples that add a human layer. The checked reading shows they are decoder hallucinations (including "Thank you." over exact zero at 5:59.6), so I dropped that and now read the track as entirely wordless.
