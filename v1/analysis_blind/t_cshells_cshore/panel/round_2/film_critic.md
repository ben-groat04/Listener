## 1. Answers to the questions put to me

**Sceptic (Q1), first-timer (Q2), insider (Q2), music critic (Q1), cold viewer (Q2): are the cut-to-edge coincidences chance?**
I ran the test nobody had run. From shots.json I took the 83 cut times (the 84th is the opening, as in sync.json) and the music edges listed in round 1: 0:24.4, 1:04.8, 1:53.4, 2:07.5, 3:44.75, 4:11.5, 4:46.5, 5:17.1. Distance from each edge to the nearest cut: 0.09, 0.16, 0.10, 0.19, 0.48, 0.12, 0.30 s, and 22.7 s for 5:17.1 (no cut there at all). Five of eight edges have a cut within 0.3 s. I then shifted the whole cut train circularly by a random 5-356 s, 20,000 times, and counted how often five or more edges got a cut within 0.3 s. Result: about 0.003 (51 of 20,000).

Caveats, which matter. (1) I did not pre-register the edges: they were collected by the panel after someone noticed the near-coincidences, so the real p is weaker than 0.003. How much weaker I cannot say. (2) The edge list is the panel's, not an independent detector's. (3) The test says nothing about beat phase, which stays at chance (0.333 against 0.326). (4) A 0.3 s window is wide: at 83 cuts over 361 s the mean gap is 4.3 s, so a given point has about a 14% chance of a cut within 0.3 s by luck alone, which is why five of eight is only moderately surprising on its face and the shift test is what carries the claim.

My position: the picture is not cut to the pulse, but it is plausibly cut to the layer entries. Look at which edges match: the drone (0:24.4), the drums (1:04.8, 1:53.4), the bass return (4:46.5), the 4:11.5 lift. The edge with no cut is a subtraction (5:17.1, drums and bass leaving), and the one that misses by 0.48 s (3:44.75) is also a subtraction. Entries get cuts; exits get held shots. That fits the symbolist's question (layers, not pulse) and I think it is the most defensible thing I can say, though eight edges is a small sample and I flag it as INFERRED.

## 3. The thread I want to develop: entries cut, exits hold

If the shift test is even roughly right, the director uses two grammars. When the music adds something (drone, drums, bass), the picture cuts, often to something with a visible geometric centre: the converging lines at 1:53.3, the tower at 4:40-4:45 before the bass return. When the music subtracts (3:44, 5:17), the picture lingers: the 12.5 s bending-figures take, the 17 s pier. It is the reverse of how a trailer is cut, where drops get a flurry, and I like it: the picture treats additions as events and withdrawals as rest. Two things stop me from claiming it: (a) I have not checked every layer change, only the ones the panel listed, and (b) 3:44.75 is a subtraction with a cut 0.48 s away, so the pattern is leaky. Also the 4:46 shot itself matters: the woman raising a camera at 4:46-4:48 is a gesture of "bringing to the eye" over the bass return, and the very next shot is the eye (4:49). That is a three-beat micro-sequence of raise, aim, see, with the music's bass back under it. I think that is a composed micro-sequence, whether or not it was timed to the bass.

The dosage of the long hold also deserves a word. The 2:09-2:23 take (13.8 s) is the longest hold in the middle section, and the music is flat there. The film uses the static take as a rest between two cut-dense blocks (2:00-2:09 and 2:40-3:45). The rest is the mid-film's one real breath.
