Sheet 07 shows 2:07 as a woman with red lips looking up and slightly to the side, mouth a little open, and 2:08 as a woman turning her head in a crowd; sheet 11 shows 3:30 as several people in a dark doorway with arms flung out, 3:33-3:35 as close, blurred faces. ## 3. The thread I most want to develop: the cross-cut at 3:20-3:39

The music there is, per the reading, a re-energised drive from 3:07 (drums +7 dB) with the bass loosening from about 3:15, before the bass leaves at 3:44. The picture is at its most fragmented: nine shots in 20 s, alternating a steady garden take with flash cuts of red alarm hardware, a telephone, a panic. So the picture is doing something I expected the music to do, which is build tension by alternation, while the music stays level. That suggests the film's tension is carried by the picture and the music is the unbroken floor under it, a division of labour that would explain why the Sceptic finds the music alone flat and I, as a viewer, do not find the whole flat. I am speculating about intent; the observation is only the alternation, which I checked in sheet 11. Then at 3:44 the garden carry ends, the picture calms to the long static take, and the music drops its bass. Picture calming and music thinning together would be the second strong music-picture match of the film, though it would be a match of mood and structure, not of cuts. (The dossier's nearest cut to 3:44 is at 3:44.75, 0.48 s away, which the Sceptic noted.)
