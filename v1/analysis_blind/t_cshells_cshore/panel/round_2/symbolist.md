## 1. Answers to the questions put to me

**Cold viewer: "is the perception theme deliberate, or have I imposed it on a random collage?"** (it was put to everyone; I answer as the one who built most on it.) The Sceptic's counting objection is fair: covered eyes are about seven shots in some 85 cuts plus dissolves, and an editor drawing on a limited archive would repeat things. But the theme is not carried by the covered eyes alone. It is carried by the *first image*. I opened f_0013 (0:12): a mint-green cartridge with a stylus, and print on its top face that reads, to my eye, as something like "STEREO" (blurred, so OBSERVED as a short capitalised word of about six letters, the Cold Viewer's reading plausible, not certain). The piece opens on an instrument of *sensing* (a stylus that reads a groove) at the very instant sound starts. That is a choice of the first image from the whole archive, and it states the topic before any goggles appear. So I keep "perception and its apparatus" as a deliberate frame (INFERRED, medium), and I withdraw the heavier claim that the 20th-century "legibility" theme is demonstrated (see section 3).

**Music critic and Film critic: does the picture supply the missing voice?** Yes, for me, and the strongest case is 2:07-2:09 and 3:30-3:35. But I concede the Sceptic's point that open mouths are what crowd footage contains, so "chosen against silence" cannot be shown. What I can say is narrower and safer: a viewer given open mouths over a track with no demonstrated voice hears the absence. That is an effect on the viewer (my own, and four panellists reported it), not proof of intent.

**First timer: does the music have a feeling of climax anywhere, or is it only visible in stem charts?** My honest answer as a listener-by-proxy: no climax, and I should not have implied one in round 1. What I felt was *exposure*, not height. I stand by "3:44-4:11.5 is lightness by subtraction" but I now say plainly it is a clearing, not a peak.

**Sceptic: "where do you actually see a story or argument, and what would disprove your reading?"** Fair. See the next section: I give a falsifier.

**Push back on the Sceptic** on one point only: "red appears in a dozen unrelated objects" is a count I can half-accept (radar 1:16, red slab 2:03, FIRE box 3:22, child's coat 4:11, tower dot). **Push back on the Sceptic's "18 s tail is slack."** The music reading marks this undecidable, and I agree, but there is evidence on my side that the Sceptic did not use: the *picture* behaves as authored. It reaches black at about 5:40-5:41 (brightness 5.1, 1.4, 0), before the sound's last audible moment at about 5:42.7, so a person adjusted the picture fade relative to the sound. That does not prove the tail is chosen, but it does show someone was timing the exit with care. Weighted lightly.

**Disagree with myself, and the Insider's version too:** the Insider says the film is about "a mid-century faith in seeing and controlling, replayed as a strange trance". I like it, and my round 1 said nearly the same. I now think "faith" is too active. The footage is *instructional*: nothing in it argues. The irony, if any, is made by the juxtaposition of that bland confidence with the music's trance and the withheld voice. So the film is less "about" a faith than it *exposes* one by removing the narration that carried it.

## 3. A concession and a falsifier

**Concession.** In round 1 I wrote that "the twentieth century's confidence that people can be made legible" was the subtext. Nothing in the frames dates the film's *attitude*, only its stock (grain, dress, cars, equipment: all INFERRED mid-century). I cannot see a 20th-century confidence; I can see old footage of people being measured. I demote the thesis to SPECULATED.

**Falsifier (the Sceptic asked).** My "being looked at, and the machinery of looking" reading would be weakened if (a) a large share of the unreadable green footage (4:15-4:31, 5:06-5:13) turned out to show something unrelated to sight or instruments, or (b) the beach and pier were found to be the *only* footage not of looking, which would make the bookend a contrast rather than a continuation. On (b) I note that the figure at 0:16 faces the sea, which is itself an act of looking; that cuts both ways, and I cannot resolve it from pixels.

## 4. The thread I want to develop: sound as the thing being looked at

The best idea this round came from reading the Cold Viewer and Film critic side by side with my own notes. The film opens on a *stylus* (f_0013), on the instant the sound arrives. The tower is dissolved into a gramophone horn at 0:56-0:59 (dossier). Between them is a long run of devices that turn invisible things into visible traces: the radar screen (1:16-1:24), the oscilloscope curves (1:49-1:52, which coincide with the music's dropouts, 1:51.2-1:53.4), the wristwatch (2:01), the camera and the eye (4:46-4:50). So the piece may be less "about looking" than about *transduction*: signal into sense, sense into trace, trace into image. A cartridge turns a groove into sound; a radar turns an echo into a glow; an eye turns light into a picture; a camera turns an eye's view into film. The music, which the reading says has no voice and no melody and works by layers entering and leaving, is a signal too: it is *being sensed* by us.

What gives this some evidential weight is the placement. The oscilloscope trace (1:49-1:52) sits exactly where the sound is stripped to near nothing in the "other" layer (1:51.2-1:53.4, per the music reading), and the very next cut (1:53.3, the strongest coincidence, 0.13 s from the edge in sync.json) is converging white lines and a face, the moment the groove locks. I will not claim intent, but the sequence reads: sound thins to a trace, the trace resolves into a face, the pulse arrives. It would be a tidy figure for what the film is doing, a signal finding a receiver. The beach figure, standing with the sea ahead and no instrument, would then be the *unmediated* receiver, and the pier, with its lamps, the signal's last trace in the dark. SPECULATED throughout, and I record the weak point: the detector misses dissolves, and I have no frame-level data finer than 1 s for these joins.
