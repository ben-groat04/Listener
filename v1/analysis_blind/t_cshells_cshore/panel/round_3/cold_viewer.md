I cannot separate the two causes, and the music critic's caution that the lull may be partly separator behaviour means I should not blame the music alone. Honest answer: I trust the lull as "a slight loosening", not as a feature a listener would single out.

**Sceptic: would I count content as evidence for 4:46.5, or only timing?**

**Film critic and Symbolist: is the black tail authored?** I accept the film critic's split: the picture and sound fading together at about 5:39-5:43 looks paired; the 18.1 s after is an absence I cannot prove was chosen. ## 4. The thread I develop: what the film does between the edges

The round 2 tests say cuts track layer changes but not beats. So what is the picture doing in the long stretches where the arrangement is steady, like 2:09-3:07? Looking at sheet_09, my answer is: it is wandering, in a controlled way. It alternates black-and-white and colour, close and far, people and lights (the tower returns at 2:43-2:44 after earlier appearances, a punctuation mark, as the film critic says). The music holds a floor; the picture drifts above it. That is the same relation as the music critic's "different feelings on the same bed". Inference: the piece works by a fixed bed plus changing images, and the change that matters is whenever the bed itself changes, at which point the picture also jumps. That would explain why my attention snaps at the edges (1:53, 4:46) and drifts between them.

## 5. Settled reading (5 sentences)

1. This is an instrumental, voiceless piece of about six minutes, with eleven seconds of silence at the start and a faded-out, then black, silent tail, whose sound is a single A drone and open fifth built up and taken apart one layer at a time (music reading).
2. The picture is a collage of mid-century-looking instructional and scientific footage in black-and-white and colour: eyes, cameras, instruments, blindfolded or tested bodies, a lit tower, sky and sea (frames, with the date and genre as inference).
3. Its hard cuts probably follow the arrangement's big layer changes, within a fraction of a second and not on the beat (panel tests, my eye-checks at 1:53 and 4:46).
4. I take the mood as stern, impersonal and deadpan, with the human warmth coming from a few images (the family at the window, the pier), and a sagging stretch at 2:40-3:07 where neither picture nor sound gives me a thread.
5. I do not find a story or argument and I do not need one; I read it as a texture about looking and measuring, and I hold the "perception" theme as an interpretation covering about half the footage.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 1:53.** The oscilloscope-like curve on black gives way to a smiling face and converging white lines at the instant the music's dropouts end and the groove locks (reading 1:53.4; my frames f_0113 and f_0114). It contains the film's two halves in one cut: the instrument that traces a signal, then the person who receives it.
