## 3. The thread I want to develop: the film as a record of being measured, and what the music does to it

The cut-to-edge result opens something I had not considered. If the editor listened for layer entries, then the film is not just old footage laid over a track; it is footage placed to join the music's own act of building and stripping. The music is also, in its way, a measuring instrument: a counter that adds one layer at a time (drone, stabs, drums, bass, then groove) and takes them away one at a time. The picture is full of things that count, time, scan and test (counter box, watch, radar, scope, light-test discs), and the editing makes layer changes the moments when we look at something new. The "faith in seeing and controlling" I withdrew is not needed. What survives is smaller and safer: counted sound and counted bodies, under a steady A. I put this as SPECULATED, because it rests on the same after-the-fact motif counting the Sceptic rightly questions.

## 8. Settled reading (five sentences)
This is a six-minute, wordless film of mixed mid-century footage (the opening beach looks 1960s to me, the telephone and drills earlier; English-language print and a plate that appears to read Chicago, Ill.) assembled around looking, timing, testing and being tested, set over a stern, trance-like A-centred build whose layers arrive and leave one at a time. The picture does not follow the beat, but its biggest hard cuts probably follow the music's layer changes (strongest at 1:53.3), and it lingers where the music subtracts. Its attitude is deadpan: nothing in frames or sound shows affection or contempt, and I will not decide between pastiche and critique. The frame is a return to a seaside scene and its car and figure at 5:01, then a night pier fading to an 18 s black tail whose intent cannot be decided. What I can say with confidence is that it leaves a feeling of hushed unease, wry distance and a quiet, emptied ending.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 1:53.3-1:53.4.** The oscilloscope trace thins to nothing, the sound is stripped, then a hard cut to converging lines and a smiling face lands within 0.13 s of the groove locking in. It holds the two ideas of the film in one second: a machine making the invisible visible, and picture and music briefly agreeing.
