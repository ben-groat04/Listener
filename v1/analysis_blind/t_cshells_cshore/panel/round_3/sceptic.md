## 1. Answers to the questions put to me

**Cold viewer Q2 (would I count content as evidence, or only timing?).** Both, with a rule: the content claim must be fixed before looking, and it must be something a stranger could score. "Camera raised to the eye as the bass returns" is a content claim I can check, and the frames give a mixed result. The cut is at 4:46.2 (shot list; MEASURED) against a bass return at 4:46.5 (reading), so the timing is good. But f_0287 (4:46) shows a woman looking down at a folding camera held at her chest, and f_0288 (4:47) shows her still looking down, camera in both hands, not yet at the eye (OBSERVED). The raised camera and the large drawn eye come later, in the 4:47 to 4:50 seconds per the film critic's sheet. So at the edge itself the picture introduces the apparatus and the beginning of the gesture, and the "eye" payoff follows by a few seconds. I will count that as weak supporting content: a new shot starting on the layer's return, with a looking-theme subject, but not a frame-matched event. I would accept it as a second kind of evidence only if the same rule worked at other edges, and 1:53 (radiating lines, smile) and 4:11 (red coat, colour) are about as far apart in content as two images can be. So content does not reinforce a single code. It reinforces "something new starts here".

**Film critic Q2 (will I accept an independent-detector test, and what threshold and window?).** Yes, and I will set the terms now. Edges: onset-strength peaks from the full mix and from each stem, taken by a fixed rule (for example the top 10 novelty peaks, at least 20 s apart), listed before the cut list is consulted. Window: plus or minus 0.3 s, one window only. Null: circular shift of the cut list and shuffled shot durations, reporting both. Pass: p below 0.05 on both nulls. Fail on either, I withdraw "cuts follow the arrangement" and revert to "possible". I have not run it, because I have not got an independent edge list in this round; the one I used last round came from the reading's stem edges, which were assembled by people who had seen the dossier's coincidences.

**Insider Q2 ("the film looks back at old measuring-and-testing footage from a later vantage").** I accept the first half as observed: much of the footage is black-and-white and mid-century in look (the 4:46 woman with a folding camera and a 1930s to 1940s hairstyle is typical; OBSERVED as appearance, dating not checked beyond that), and the bookend beach and pier are in a different register. I do not accept "later vantage" as an observed structure unless the frames framing it are shown to be later by something other than image quality (a car model, a fashion, a date). The attitude stays open. What would count against it: more than a handful of seconds in the middle that look contemporary, or a bookend that looks as old as the rest. I cannot rule either out from 1 fps frames.

**Symbolist Q2 (what test would make a motif designed, not archival recurrence?).** Reuse of the very same shot (identical framing and action) at two points counts as deliberate reuse only if the two uses fall at musically matched places, since a small archive predicts reuse anywhere. A repeated desk-and-telephone at 2:23 and 3:25 would, by itself, prove an archive of limited size and an editor who went back to it. It becomes evidence of design if both fall on layer edges, or if the second is cut in the same position within a repeating 4-bar or 8-bar frame. Without that, I stay at "recurrence".

**Music critic's pushback (the boundary list matters more than the test).** Agreed, and it is the main weakness of my own round 2 result. Using a list that includes 3:44 (soft, flurry of cuts) and 2:07.5 (in the densest cutting patch) inflated the hits. I take the music critic's 5 of 10 at 0.3 s (p about 0.006 to 0.013) and my 5 of 9 (p about 0.004) as the same finding with the same caveat: indicative, probably real, size uncertain. My question to the music critic about a lag in the stem edges is still unanswered by anyone. Until it is answered I will not read "the editor anticipated the sound by 0.1 to 0.3 s" as intent: a systematic stem-edge lag would produce the identical pattern.

**Film critic's own question to me, as I understood it (is 2:01 to 2:09 a density artefact?).** No answer was given, so I treat it as unresolved. At 4:46 a similar issue does not arise: there are four cuts in 15 s there (4:46.2, 4:49.2, 4:51.0, 4:54.0), so a cut within 0.3 s of 4:46.5 is a rarer event than at 2:07. ## 2. Agreements and pushback

- **Agree** with the film critic and first_timer that "shouting" was my side's challenge and was conceded by all: open mouths are ambiguous. The residue worth keeping is first_timer's "alarmed, and nobody answers", but I mark it as the viewer's, not the film's.

I add that the "deadpan" reading has a simple cause too: an open fifth and a limited mix with no voice give no cue for any feeling, so whatever the viewer brings fills the space. That is not a design claim.
- **Push back** on symbolist's use of the picture fade reaching black before the sound as evidence of care. A fade is a fade; most editors do it. It raises the odds of authorship from about even to slightly above, and no more. Equally, the sound's cut to zero at about -70 dBFS is consistent with an automatic trim. Net: undecided, and I say it again without apology.
- **Push back** on cold_viewer and symbolist treating the 1:53 image as a "payoff". The image is graphic and arrives on the edge (OBSERVED, f_0114: smiling profile, converging white lines), so I lifted too; but the edge is the music's payoff, and a vivid picture on a payoff makes it feel bigger. Whether the editor chose the picture for the payoff, or only the cut point, I cannot tell.
- **Agree** with insider and music_critic that "hypnotic" is a listener's verdict and that the middle plateau (about 2:07 to 3:05 and 3:15 to 3:44) is carried by the picture.

## 3. The thread I develop: what the panel moved from, and what remains

The most interesting thing in this panel is how the debate shifted from "is there a message" to "is there craft", and how the sound supports craft more than meaning. Three observations stand on measurements: (1) the cuts do not follow the beat (33.3 % within 80 ms against 32.6 % chance; cut phase in bar p = 0.086; cut rate against loudness p = 0.61); (2) they probably follow arrangement changes (about 5 of 9 or 10 edges within 0.3 s, p about 0.004 to 0.013, tainted by edge choice); (3) the music's own events are subtractions and returns, about one every 30 s, which is exactly the grain at which a human editor listening in sections would cut. The three together produce a modest and boring-to-state result: someone listened to the track, cut at its big joins, and filled between them with archive material that suited a general mood. Everything beyond that (thesis, legibility, shouting, withheld voice, elegy) is projection I can name but not prove. The panel's real finding is the asymmetry: the arrangement is shared by music and picture, but the groove is not; where there is no edge, the two go their own way.

## 4. Settled reading (5 sentences)

1. The music is a stern, near-one-chord drive on an A centre with no demonstrated voice and no tune, whose events are layers leaving and returning (1:04.8, 1:53.4, 3:44, 4:11.5, 4:36.5, 4:46.5, 5:17), framed by 11.3 s of digital silence and 18.1 s of zero.
2. The picture is a collage of mostly black-and-white archive scenes of looking, testing and apparatus plus colour domestic shots, bookended by a beach and a pier, and I accept a recurring noun set (eyes, cameras, instruments) as a texture but not as a thesis.
3. The cuts follow the arrangement more often than chance would give (indicative, about 5 of 9 or 10 edges within 0.3 s) but not the beat, and the lead of 0.1 to 0.3 s may be a stem-edge lag, not intent.
4. The feeling it produces is estrangement, with the picture supplying the human moments (the infant at 3:06, the smile at 1:53, the colour at 4:11) over a music that does not take sides; the 18 s tail and the "elegy" are at most mildly authored, and cannot be shown to be.
5. Everything I would call meaning is a viewer's contribution; everything I would call craft is a cut or a layer change that lands on its neighbour.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 1:53.3 to 1:53.4.** A hard cut to a smiling profile among radiating lines (f_0114) arrives within 0.13 s of the measured lock-in of the music (bass on quavers, "other" back at full, drums busy), after the dropouts at 1:47 to 1:53 have held the release back. It is the one coincidence that every panellist accepts, it is the best case of picture and arrangement joining, and it happens in the one place where the music itself offers a payoff.
