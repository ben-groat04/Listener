# video1: the definitive reading (blind mode, moderator's synthesis)

Sources: `dossier.md` and all three rounds of the panel (Film critic, First-Timer, Insider, Music critic, Sceptic, Symbolist). Nothing here is new evidence; every claim is traceable to the dossier or a panel statement. No context file, research notes or web were used. Nobody on the panel can hear audio: sound claims are inferences from the checked music reading and measurements. Times are m:ss. Labels: SEEN (in a frame), DATA (measurement or transcript), INTERP (interpretation), SPECULATION. Where a later round corrected an earlier one, the later version is used and the correction is named.

---

## 1. The piece in one paragraph

A 3:08 black-and-white, archive-looking record of a huge, bitterly cold winter skating race in Friesland, whose date is spoken aloud as 18 January 1963 and whose name, Elfstedentocht, is printed repeatedly (newspapers, last banner) and heard clearly once in the closing refrain (2:44); earlier instances are garbled transcriptions read as the same word (INTERP). It is laid over a loud, dry, relentless 78 BPM drone-and-drum track with no tune and no cadence, and a flat, old-sounding Dutch voice (measurements only) that reads facts (date, hour, minus 16, 200 km, 575 racers and about 9,500 others, a roll-call of towns) and then leaves before the heaviest passages. Many shots are of backs, hoods, bibs, silhouettes and crowds, but there are also several face-led passages (0:58 microphone man, 1:58-2:14, 2:41-2:46); the film speaks through borrowed printed text (newspapers, signs, banners) more than through faces. What it does to you is mood rather than story: dread at the start, held breath, awe at tiny figures on a white void, a single stretch of real warmth (the tea, 1:55-2:13), a ceremony of town names, a release at the FINISH banner, and a hard stop that closes the record without resolving it. The panel's settled feeling is "respect with a bill attached" (Insider): collective endurance, with the cost (dropouts, injuries, an ambulance) left on the page, and neither a celebration nor a warning (see section 4).

## 2. Emotional journey

Each entry says what the viewer feels, then which sound and image choices produce it. SEEN and DATA are marked where the distinction matters; the feelings are INTERP and, for sound, rest on measurements only.

**0:00-0:09, dropped in, uneasy.** Hard hit at about 0:00.7 with no fade-in, drums alone, a wall of sound at about 0:01.3, a D held; the bass enters in gated hits from about 0:04 and is audible at about -28 dB by 0:07, and keeps growing to about 0:20 (roughly 18-22 dB in total) (DATA). Picture: a grey, streaked motion-blur with a dark band above it, a night street of silhouetted crowd with lit windows, then a tilted newspaper, "ELFSTEDENTOCHT OP VRIJDAG" (0:04), then a dark night scene with a lit white-lettered sign (POSTHUMA, last letter cropped) and tiny lights, a dark crowd to the right (0:07-0:08). The print gives the dread a reason; the double exposure suggests memory (INTERP).

**0:09-0:32, solemn, a little eerie, pressure beneath information.** The voice enters on the date (0:09.8) as the Friese Koerier page ("bovenmenselijk zwaar", "Chaos aan de finish") appears. The voice gives 18 januari 1963 (DATA, Whisper, both passes); no date is legible on any newspaper in the frames, so the page supports the subject (a very hard Elfstedentocht) but not the date. The strangest image follows at 0:12-0:13: two glowing discs with dark rims and a blown-out oval on black (f_0013). Nobody could identify it (section 8). The pre-dawn crowd and numbered backs accompany "half zes" (0:16.6), then a garbled sentence at 0:19-0:24 that Whisper renders as "200 visitors" (the word is uncertain); the spoken 200 km is at 0:54. At 0:22.5-0:27 the page "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar" holds still and tolls like a bell (Insider, First-Timer); the sub-headline beside it reads "Tal van uitvallers en vele gewonden", and a column "Cracks staken wedstrijd" (Insider, f_0025). The first live movement (0:27-0:31) is frost-covered figures running or walking along a snowy path at night, skates not visible; the first clear skating is the silhouette row at 0:38. The voice says what we read as the refrain, "Waar iedereen zich voor opmaakt, dat is de Elfstedentocht" (INTERP: a reading of garbled speech; Whisper renders the name as "Elfstedentuin" at 0:28). Underneath, the bass keeps growing (DATA).

**0:32-0:41, a held breath.** The voice stops at 0:32 (a hard cut at 0:31.88, just before it) as the picture goes quiet (a grey, murky scene: a dark mill-like tower with a lattice sail at 0:32, then a wooden platform on stilts beside it, a telegraph pole and a faint light at 0:34, f_0035) and the band widens into a groove (DATA). Short spoken bursts return at 0:41, 0:51, 0:54 and 1:03.5. Then a row of roughly eight or nine crouched skaters (count and hand position not clearly visible), reads as ritual. The calm is more unsettling than the noise (Insider). This is the first of the three big voice exits (0:32, 1:27, 2:48).

**0:44-0:56, cold as fact.** "Temperatuur: 16 graden beneden nul" and "een monsterrit van ruim 200 kilometer" land on wind-blown ice and a near-whiteout (0:49-0:56). The panel calls this the strongest word-picture match of the first minute (Film critic, Insider, Sceptic). A viewer-side caveat: the First-Timer drifted here and at 1:30-1:39, which the panel treats as a fact about attention, not a flaw proven in the film.

**0:57-1:13, the build and its self-awareness.** A large boxy camera with a lens-hood (no bellows clearly visible), a microphone man, a tripod operator, then a fast montage of bibs, bunched skaters, a lone skater and a crush (the mix peaks near 0:58; widest and brightest 1:06-1:14, DATA). The cutting speeds up with the rising weight.

**1:15-1:27, hollowing, counting.** The bass is cut at about 1:15.0-1:15.4 (the held A is heard from about 1:15.6), the mix falls 4-5 dB, drums continue, a sparse held tone moves from A to C (DATA; the exact bar alignment is disputed, section 8). The picture settles on a hut, a ringed hand writing in a notebook, bibs and papers. The voice reads "575 wedstrijdrijders, rond 9500 toerrijders..." (figures rest on Whisper digits; the surrounding word list differs between passes) and the band is thinner than its norm under most of the speech, clearest here where the bass is gone, though not the only place (2:44.5-2:47.5 is also thinned) and at 0:50-0:56 the voice sits over a nearly full band (Music critic: "the band thins when the voice speaks"; the stronger word "gate" was withdrawn). Felt as the most human-bureaucratic moment: individuals counted while the sound hollows. Sober rather than sad (Insider); chilling (Symbolist).

**1:27-1:40, small in the white.** The voice ends; the 0:58-1:14 sound returns without the bass and a little wider (about +20 dB at 2-5 kHz against the 1:16-1:26 trough, DATA; not brighter than the 1:06 wall) under a calm daylit street and then extreme-long shots of tiny skaters on a white void, reeds, a lone hunched figure beyond posts. Loud sound against empty pictures: awe with no one in it. The First-Timer and Insider both call this the most striking meeting of music and picture.

**1:40-1:55, the weight lands, then the rush.** The bass jumps from about -64 to -22 dB within about 0.2 s at 1:40.0, the largest step in the track (DATA). The picture at that moment is an empty, wind-scoured snow bank; the Film critic's frame check (f_0099 to f_0104) shows a cut into it about a second before the slam, with people only entering at about 1:41, and the Sceptic's reading of the detector log shows no cut near the slam (section 5). The effect is of weight arriving from outside the picture, "like weather one hears before one feels" (Insider). Then the loudest second (1:52, about -9.5 dBFS per per_second_levels.json; my own mono sum reads about 1 dB lower, the ranking holds) meets the fastest, most physical cutting: fur, ice-level legs, a muddy bank, a lunge (1:49-1:54). Exciting, but it blurs for first-time viewers.

**1:55-about 2:14.8, relief, fragility, cost.** (The tea sequence runs to about 2:14.8; frosted men's faces continue at 2:13-2:14.) The cut to a kettle, cups and what may be a loaf or cake (a dark patterned slab with a knife; about 1:55) arrives as the bass leaves; at about 1:58 the mix collapses to about -16 dBFS and the width metric narrows to 0.2-0.36, the narrowest in the track on that metric (DATA; the ranking depends on the metric). Young people drink from white cups and one looks almost into the lens (f_0120, 1:59); weathered, frost-covered men follow; an ambulance (a dark boxy vehicle with a white-circled red-cross-style cross) at 2:04 adds danger; a figure in white shorts stumbles (2:08-2:10); a man slumps at a trestle table (2:11). A near-sine sub at about 42.7-43 Hz sounds every 6.1-6.2 s (eight beats; six entries, 2:01 to 2:32, DATA); at 2:38.3 a pitched D2 bass takes over from it. The panel's strongest tenderness is here: small human faces after a long run of silhouettes. Both the First-Timer and the Insider name this as the most moving stretch, and it is the one place where picture and sound agree on a feeling. The Music critic and Sceptic add that the warmth is in the picture only; the music contributes absence.

**About 2:15-2:38, ceremony** (it begins visually with the CONTROLE booth at about 2:15). A two-bar loop with A on top (Music critic; I ran no whole-piece repetition test, so "the only repeating pitched figure" is unchecked). The voice recites towns while signs appear: CONTROLE, ULST (or IJLST; the first letter is worn), Stavoren inn (an inscription "De ...couwe van Stavor(en)" with a round hanging sign; "inn" is inferred), WELKOM IN WORKUM, BOLSWARD / BOALSERT, FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER, DOKKUM, the Leeuwarden signpost (2:15-2:36). A litany, "like ticking a map" (First-Timer), "place names as prayer" (Insider), and for some a timetable. Only Workum, Bolsward, Franeker, Dokkum and Leeuwarden have on-screen support; the earlier names are garbled speech recognition (section 8).

**2:38-2:47, waiting, crowd, refrain.** A pitched D2 bass (about 73 Hz) replaces the sub at 2:38.3, coinciding with a hard cut at 2:38.24 and the click thins for about five seconds (DATA; whether deliberate is open). A line of about a dozen bent figures on pale ice and a looped pale cloth in front of a crowd are ambiguous (section 5). Then the best run of faces in the finale: a trilby, an older woman with spectacles looking up at an officer, police, children (2:41-2:46). The voice says the refrain a second time (about 2:44.0-2:48.3; Whisper segment start 2:43.8, stem energy from 2:44.3). The First-Timer's and Symbolist's observation: the same sentence felt like pride at 0:28 and dread at 2:44, so the change comes only from what was heard in between.

**2:47.8-2:49, the hinge.** A violent sideways blur, drums falling about 40 dB for about 0.4 s with the last word ending on the dropout (DATA), a black frame, then the FINISH banner in front of a packed crowd, and a new wide, bright wall with centred bass begins. Every panellist names this as the best moment (section 7). The feeling is an intake of breath, a lifted hand before the downbeat (Music critic), "the held breath of everyone at once" (Insider).

**2:49-3:06, arrival as trance, not triumph.** From here weight, brightness and width are all present, as they already were at 0:58-1:14; what is new is a 20 s plateau, a new drum cell, the voice gone, and an arrival just after a 0.4 s hole (stem measurements; the Music critic had called it the first time weight and brightness coexist). The picture is dark, smeared and fast: officer's back, hands, bib "342", a white flash, skaters from behind, the "zwaar, zwaar, zw..." page again (2:57), cracked wet ice, crowds, a whip-pan. Relentless rather than joyful. The panel agrees the picture alone accelerates here while the level stays flat; opinions differ on whether that acceleration earns its place (section 5).

**3:06-3:08.7, recap and stop.** A 32nd-note drum roll begins at about 3:06.2, and at the same moment a four-sign recap runs at about 0.4 s per shot: ULST, DOKKUM / 50 km (the same shot as 2:34), BOLSWARD / BOALSERT, FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER (frame-accurate extraction from the mp4). A hand-lettered indoor banner follows at about 3:07.4, "...uitvoerige Reportage / ELFSTEDENTOCHT / op de parterre" (last line medium confidence), then black at about 3:08.1. The mix is cut at about 3:08.2 with no tail, in a pedal on D with no cadence. The First-Timer first felt "cheated for a second", then thought it fitted. The final reading is in section 4, disagreement 4.

## 3. Meaning and themes (consensus)

Ranked by how much of the panel stands behind them and how firm the evidence is.

**3.1 A collective endurance, recorded rather than dramatised. (Whole panel.)** The film tells no story and follows no hero. Evidence: the voice supplies tallies, not feelings; people often appear as backs, hoods, bibs and silhouettes (with face-led exceptions); no named person appears anywhere; the music has one drone, one tempo and no cadence. The Sceptic's limit stands: "stoic heroism" is a mood label from steady tempo and flat voice, and the footage itself shows "a cold, crowded, difficult day". The Music critic adds that any heroic or ordeal-like colour comes from the voice's content and the picture, not from the music.

**3.2 The film speaks in other people's words. (Film critic, Insider, Symbolist, Sceptic; strongest new structural finding.)** Almost every written statement is found text, never a caption: masthead pages (0:04, 0:09, 0:23, 2:57), shop and inn signs (0:07, 2:24), a control booth (2:15), town signs (2:18-2:36, 3:06), FINISH (2:48), a hall banner (3:07). The film alternates opaque shots (blur, white sky, crowd backs) with legible ones, and places the legible ones at structural points: FINISH on the hinge, the newspaper two-thirds through the finale, the sign recap on the roll, the banner on the stop (Film critic: "text is the film's anchor, the way a drone is the music's"). This explains why the film names so many places and so few people: it names what it can read. Insider's corollary: the cold, hard tone is partly the genre of these notices, and warmth appears only where nobody is writing anything (the tea, the faces at 1:59 and 2:01).

**3.3 The cost is on the record. (Whole panel; the Sceptic grants it as fact, not stance.)** On screen: "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar", "Tal van uitvallers en vele gewonden", "Cracks staken wedstrijd" (the favourites gave up), "Chaos aan de finish", an ambulance with a cross, a stumbling figure, slumped, frosted men. The event's own signs (WELKOM IN WORKUM, FINISH) are neutral to warm. The Film critic's count of negative text in the press against positive text on event signage is the strongest evidence anyone produced on the celebration question. The Sceptic's counter: any honest record would include its injuries.

**3.4 Display: hands that hold things up. (Symbolist; accepted by others only as a pattern, not a theory.)** Papers held tilted and close, the FINISH sheet, a slack cloth held by two men at 2:40, bibs worn on backs, signs on posts, a hand-lettered banner at 3:07 above a crowd seen from behind. In the Symbolist's phrase, everyone is either holding up a sign for someone else or being counted by one, and the film is the largest sign of all. The Sceptic's rating of a related motif, recording apparatus, applies: "the film notices cameras" is supported; "about witnessing" is a step too far.

**3.5 Time is arranged, not simply the race's time. (Symbolist, Film critic, Sceptic; settled after round 3.)** The film is "race-shaped, theme-assembled". Departures from race-day order the panel found: skaters in motion at 0:27-0:41 before registration at 1:18; a bibbed skater stepping down a wooden ramp onto the ice at 1:46-1:48 (setting off or adjusting; no lacing seen) after the white-void long shots; a standing line at 2:38 after the route list and the tea; the route signs replayed after FINISH as a four-sign recap (ULST, DOKKUM, BOLSWARD, FRANEKER at 3:06-3:07) against the earlier order ULST, WORKUM, BOLSWARD, FRANEKER, DOKKUM at 2:18-2:34, not a simple reversal (only DOKKUM before FRANEKER is reversed). Chronology still supplies the skeleton; the music supplies the placement. Intent is not shown, and economy (a short film reusing its best legible shots) is a sufficient explanation for the recycling.

**3.6 A sound built by subtraction. (Music critic, First-Timer, all.)** One thirdless D drone with a flat seventh, one tempo, no melody. The only compositional parameter is which layers are present: bass out 1:15, bassless wall 1:27, bass slam 1:40, hush with eight-beat sub 1:55, procession 2:13, then a wall that withholds nothing from 2:48. Weight and brightness do not coexist in the 1:15-1:40 and 1:55-2:38 passages, but they do before 1:15 and from 2:48; what makes 2:48 feel like arrival is the 20 s plateau, the new drum cell, the gone voice and the 0.4 s hole before it (DATA for the layers; the feeling is INTERP).

Withdrawn or demoted themes (so no one resurrects them): "elegy for anonymity" lowered to "a film about a collective, in which individuals appear as numbers and places as names" (Symbolist) and numbers read as the event counting itself (Insider); "death arc" and "birth, ordeal, death" light arc, dropped by the Symbolist and rejected by the Insider and First-Timer because the film ends in a lit interior; "heartbeat" for the sub (ten per minute is far too slow; "bell" or "tolling" is preferred, as metaphor only); "pulse is the race" (the same pulse runs under the tea and the town list, so it is a condition of the film); eyes or projector for the 0:12 discs; "press closes the film" (replaced by "bracketed by print").

## 4. Live disagreements

**1. Celebration, warning, or neither?**
- Celebration or civic pride (Insider's neutral reading, early): the event is large, public and named with pride, the crowd at FINISH is packed and many look at camera (whether it is cheering is not visible at this resolution), the welcome signs are warm.
- Warning (not held by anyone in final form): the headlines, ambulance and cold.
- Ambivalence (Film critic, First-Timer, Symbolist): the words on screen disagree ("zwaar" against "FINISH"), and neither the voice nor the drone resolves it. The Film critic's phrase: "respectful, with the cost on record". The Insider: "respect with a bill attached". The First-Timer: "awe with a bruise", and if the film is a screening notice (below), warnings function as stakes, as in a trailer, not as accusation.
- Sceptic: "quietly ambivalent" is the default of an honest record, not a position; the film does not argue either way. The panel does not settle this. The moderator's wording: neither celebration nor warning is the footage's thesis; the cost is on the page, the pride is on the signs, and the film keeps both without choosing.

**2. Do the picture and sound meet by design?**
- Meeting: the Film critic's best edits are where a legible image lands on a sound event: the hut and ledger over the cleared mix (1:15; a cut at 1:14.84), the whip-pan into FINISH (2:47.8), the sign recap beginning with the roll (DOKKUM's cut coincides with the roll start at about 3:06.2). The Music critic adds that for about two seconds in the roll the cut rate (about one per 0.75 s) equals the beat (0.77 s); medium-low, detector-dependent.
- Not meeting: the Sceptic's test: five of eight music edges lie within 1 s of a detected cut, which occurs 18 per cent of the time by chance; the coarse detector hides cuts at nearly every music edge: 0:31.88, 1:14.84, 1:27.16, 1:39.40, 1:55.04, 2:13.36, 2:38.24 and 2:48.16 are all real cuts (frame-difference check on the mp4). Cuts fall within 0.4 s of 6 of the 8 music edges; a rough random-position simulation gives about p = 0.06 for that, so suggestive, not proof. The sound-picture ties are more numerous than the Sceptic's original count of one (2:47.8 to 2:48.3), though statistically modest. (The 1:40 slam still lands in the snow-bank shot that began at 1:39.40, so the picture partner is a cut 0.6 s earlier, not a 21 s shot.) The Sceptic's reframing, which the Film critic accepted, was that a film in which the layers cross once and exactly is more interesting than one in which they cross everywhere; with the cut times corrected, the layers cross more often than that, but not at a rate proved beyond chance.
- Cut phase: the Sceptic and Music critic report n = 39, R = 0.365, p = 0.0055 against a null that ignores unknown tracker offset, mean phase about 0.32 beat (about 0.25 s late); a half-beat test is flat (p = 0.95); cuts do not cluster near voice onsets (mean 3.95 s against 2.90 s random). Possible readings: an editor lagging a heard click, or a fixed tracker offset. The panel leaves it as an unexplained tendency with no part in the settled reading. The dossier's headline (7.8 per cent within 80 ms against 19.7 per cent expected) is consistent with this and does not contradict it.

**3. How good is the finale's acceleration (3:00-3:08)?**
- Weak (Film critic): the 1:49 acceleration earns itself with varied, physical shots; the 3:00 one is "borrowed energy" (crowd backs, a bib in close-up, a blur, white sky: "pressure without events"). A stronger version would have used the faces held back for 2:41-2:46; those faces are in the wrong place for the finale to land on them.
- Defence (Symbolist): the crowd is the protagonist. The Film critic accepts this as an account of what the film attempts, not of how well it does it. The First-Timer felt the speed stood in for build, and could not hold on to anything.

**4. What is the ending: a door, a recap, a notice?**
- The sound: a hard cut at about 3:08.2, no tail, a pedal on D with no cadence. The Music critic reads the "amputated pedal" as stronger than a resolution ("this is the documentary's title card, and the race carries on"), with the caveat that the banner may belong to a different part of the film.
- The picture: the first panel round treated the 3:07 banner as a title card. The Insider found it is an indoor hall (strip-lights, a pillar, hat-backed heads, hanging paper cards), reading as a notice for a showing ("parterre" leans that way, medium confidence). Film critic, First-Timer and Symbolist all revised: "slammed door" now applies to the sound; the picture has already left the ice. First-Timer: the sound shuts "an announcement, not an unfinished race". Film critic: "closes the record like a ledger". Insider: "a record presenting itself for viewing".
- The Insider's own caveat, which the moderator keeps: the banner may simply be the next shot in the source reel; intent is not shown. The Sceptic's cheap hypothesis (it is what the footage supplied) remains alive.

**5. The 1:40 slam: counterpoint, accident, or neither?**
- Counterpoint or intended mismatch (round 1: Film critic, Music critic, Insider, Symbolist, Sceptic all called it "the cleverest thing in the piece" at first).
- Block-level choice only (Film critic, round 3): frame-level design cannot be shown.
- Accident of two separately built layers (Sceptic, Film critic as concession): one long weather shot laid under a section would give the same result.
- Settled description: weight arrives into an empty shot, and people appear a second later. Not shown to be designed; effective regardless (Film critic: "weight without a picture to meet it").

**6. Is the old-sounding voice period or recreated, and was the picture re-set to a modern track?**
Insider: a 1963 cinema report would not be cut to a click, which points to a period picture re-set to a modern track. The Music critic cannot tell archival from recreated from the data (the vocal stem is a separation output, gated to about -64 dB in gaps, and the band is limited to about 4 kHz, which fits either an old optical or tape track or a deliberate filter). The panel does not decide this; the Film critic finds the possibility interesting because the layers then meet only at a few chosen places.

## 5. Craft notes

**Editing.**
- Cutting follows words and music blocks rather than the pulse (DATA, with the caveats above). Whip-pans, blur and white-outs are used as punctuation (2:47, 2:49, 3:03, 3:04, 0:56).
- The strongest acceleration is 1:49-1:54, where energy is content-carrying (Film critic). The finale's acceleration is arguably borrowed.
- At 2:47.8 the picture is at its darkest at about 2:48.0 in the middle of the 0.4 s drum hole, and the FINISH banner arrives on the first returning beat (Sceptic's own frames cut at 2:47.6, 2:48.0, 2:48.4, 2:48.7 and 2:48.9). The Sceptic corrects the earlier "held banner": it is on screen for under about 0.7 s and then cut, a flash. The Music critic's account has the banner shot already running when the wall enters, with the next detected cut 0.8 s later; the Sceptic's account puts the arrival of the banner at about 2:48.1 to 2:48.3 and reads the detector's 2:48.8 as the cut away. Both agree it is the tightest picture-sound coincidence in the film; the moderator uses the Sceptic's frame-level timing.
- The 1:15 clearing and long takes (hut, notebook) are credited as a place where a legible image meets a sound event; shot length goes from about 1.5 s to about 11 s per the dossier, though the detector under-counts.
- The 1:49 acceleration earns itself; the 3:00 acceleration does not (Film critic).
- Shot-detector caution: `shots.json` badly under-detects cuts; frames and contact sheets are trusted over it.

**Music.**
- Structural, not melodic: layer presence, width, brightness and bass are the only variables. 78 BPM (possibly felt as 156), D drone with a flat seventh, no melody, no cadence.
- Best details (Music critic): the sub gridded to eight beats (six entries at about 2:01.0, 2:07.0, 2:13.2, 2:19.4, 2:25.6, 2:31.8, 6.1-6.2 s apart, onsets read at 0.5 s resolution; the earlier "seven, each with a +4 dB mix spike" list wrongly included the 2:38.3 pitched bass; at about 42.7-43 Hz between E1 and F1, so a felt weight and not a chord note); the 0.4 s dropout that takes the last word with it; the 32nd-note roll before a slammed door.
- Weaknesses admitted by the Music critic: narrow emotional vocabulary, an arrival made of recombined material (2:48 is addition: the 1:27 wall plus the 1:40 bass, plus a new drum cell), warmth that lives only in the picture. The First-Timer would find the full track tiring (by measurements only).
- Two climaxes of different kinds: 1:40-1:55 is pressure (loudest, bass without width); 2:48-3:08 is arrival (widest, brightest, centred bass, voice gone).
- Whether the 2:38.7-2:43.7 thinning of the click, the 2:39 darkening and the 2:48 gap are authored or partly separation artefacts remains open.

**Archive and picture.**
- Everything looks archival (grain, scratches, flicker, about 5:4 (600x480) framing close to the old 4:3 style, clothing, cars); whether any footage is newly made cannot be told. Newspaper close-ups may be filmed prop pages or real papers (undecidable).
- Recycled shots: DOKKUM (2:34 and 3:06, frames compared), "zwaar" page (0:23 and 2:57; the Film critic confirmed the same headline in f_0178), FRANEKER (2:32 and 3:06.96, not frame-compared). Whether the repeats are the editor's or the source's is open.
- Faces are spent sparingly and in the wrong place for the finale: the run at 2:41-2:46 comes before FINISH, then almost none after it (Film critic). The First-Timer's correction: a hooded person in dark glasses raises a hand at 2:47 (f_0168), the nearest thing to a face in the finale before the whip-pan takes it away.
- Light and exposure follow weather and time of day (Sceptic), alternating dark frames (0:02, 0:07, 0:18, 1:14, 2:50) with blown-white ones (0:56, 1:40, 2:08, 2:54, 3:04); the film begins in murk and the picture's last act is a lit interior, then black.

**Timing caution.** The 1fps frames and contact-sheet labels can be up to about 0.5 s out (f_0187 and the label 3:06.000 show DOKKUM, but the mp4 at 186.0 s shows ULST; DOKKUM starts at 186.20). Sub-second picture/sound claims here come from the video file, not from frames_1fps.

**Structure.** A hook of paper and dread, a voice-led statement (0:09-0:32), a mostly voice-free groove and build (0:32-1:15, with four short spoken bursts at 0:41, 0:51, 0:54 and 1:03.5), a collapse and count (1:15-1:27), a bright bassless wall (1:27-1:40), a slam and rush (1:40-1:55), the hush with tea (1:55-2:13), a procession of towns (2:13-2:38), a re-lock with the closing refrain (2:38-2:47), the breath, then a wall to a hard stop (2:48-3:08). ## 6. Context that changes the reading (inferred from clues only)

Everything below comes from what is on screen or in the transcript, not from outside knowledge.

- **Date and place.** The voice gives "18 januari 1963" (DATA, Whisper only; the Friese Koerier page at 0:09 coincides in time and subject, but no date is legible on it). Dutch headlines, Dutch and Frisian paired signs (FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER, BOLSWARD / BOALSERT), and a Leeuwarden signpost place it in Friesland. The Insider reads the Frisian spellings as a second language in public use, and observes that the film does not translate either line: a local viewer might get the route read in the home tongue (SPECULATION: the Whisper spellings of the towns are garbled, so nothing shows whether the roll-call uses Dutch or Frisian forms), a foreign viewer a litany of exotic names (INTERP, about audience, not design). The Frisian-in-public-use reading rests only on the BOLSWARD / BOALSERT and FRANEKER / FRJENTSJER signs.
- **The newspapers are the context that matters most.** They turn what could read as a pageant into a record of a hard day: "zwaarste uit historie" (partly legible, 0:04), "bovenmenselijk zwaar" and "Chaos aan de finish" (0:09), "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar", "Tal van uitvallers en vele gewonden", "Cracks staken wedstrijd" (0:23-0:26, f_0025). The Insider's reading: sober news with a tolling headline, not a boast, and a report that the event beat the favourites.
- **Numbers are administrative.** Bibs exist so that officials can check people in; the registration, CONTROLE booth and ledger scenes show exactly that (Insider, against the Symbolist's anonymity reading). The film's counting is the event counting itself, as well as a theme.
- **Where it was meant to be seen.** The indoor banner at 3:07 (a "Reportage" and a likely "parterre") suggests the whole piece sits in a report-for-screening genre (Insider, First-Timer: "who is this film for?"). If so, the warnings function like a trailer's stakes. SPECULATION; the cheaper reading is that this is simply the next shot of a source reel.
- **Sound and picture may come from different eras.** The cuts follow words and sections and do not cluster on the pulse, the voice is dry and narrow-band, and the bed is modern and clean. This suggests (but does not prove; nothing in the visuals can test it) a period picture set to a later track. The panel could not tell.
- **Weather as context.** Minus 16 (the voice gives "16 graden beneden nul" with no unit; Celsius is the natural but unstated reading), wind-blown ice, a whiteout, frosted hats and balaclavas, muddy bank and cracked wet ice (1:53, 2:58), which undercut a clean-white image.

## 7. The moments a human would remember

Ranked by how many panellists chose or independently praised them.

1. **2:47.8 to 2:48.3, the breath into FINISH. (Chosen by the Film critic, First-Timer, Music critic, Sceptic and Symbolist; the Insider chose the final banner instead.)** The refrain's last word, the blur, a black frame, a 0.4 s hole in the drums (about 40 dB), then a hand-lettered FINISH banner before a packed crowd, and the wide wall begins.
