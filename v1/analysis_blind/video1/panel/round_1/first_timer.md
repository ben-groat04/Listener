# First-Timer (panel, round 1): video1

Blind mode. I read the music reading and the dossier, and opened f_0013, f_0120 and f_0169 myself. I cannot hear audio, so everything about sound is from the measurements. Where I use a name or fact from the notes, it is the notes' claim, not mine. Times are m:ss.

## What it is about

It is a black-and-white archive film of a huge winter skating race across a snowy flat country, laid over a loud, steady, tune-less drone and drum track. A flat old voice reads facts: the date (18 January 1963), 16 below zero, 200 km, 575 racers and about 9,500 other skaters, and a list of towns. Twice it says the same line, "what everyone is getting ready for, that is the Elfstedentocht" (0:27.7 and 2:43.8). On screen, newspapers say "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar" (hard, hard, hard), "many dropouts and injured" and "chaos at the finish" (0:22-0:27). So for me it is about endurance and ordinary people in the cold, told as a record rather than a story. Nobody gets a name. People are numbers on bibs, backs, hoods and crowds. The notes' title is the event's name, which I take from the on-screen banner and the narration.

## How it made me feel, and where

- **0:00-0:09, hooked.** It starts with no fade-in, in the dark, with drums and a crowd of silhouettes. It is unsettling in a good way. The newspaper at 0:04 explains the dread.
- **0:12-0:13, puzzled.** Two glowing discs with dark rims and a blown-out oval above (f_0013). I could not tell what it is: eyes, lenses, lamps? It is the strangest image in the film and I liked not knowing. I would not claim more.
- **0:32-0:41, a held breath.** The voice stops and a grey, quiet landscape appears: a platform on stilts, then a row of crouched skaters. Here the track got me, the sudden release into groove (per the music notes) while the picture goes calm. Very effective.
- **1:15-1:27, the hollowing.** The bass drops out (measured) and the picture settles on a hut, a hand writing in a notebook, bibs and papers. A sense of being counted. Slightly dull on screen, but the sound makes it feel tense rather than boring.
- **1:27-1:40, odd and lovely.** The sound gets bigger and brighter while the picture goes white and empty: tiny skaters on a void. This is the most striking meeting of music and picture for me. Huge and lonely at once.
- **1:40, the slam.** The measured bass hit. I find it a bit of a letdown that the picture is a calm white blizzard at that moment. The sound lands and the picture does not.
- **1:49-1:54, the rush.** Fast cuts, mud, fur, lunges, over the loudest second (1:52). Exciting, but it blurs for me. I could not say what is happening.
- **1:55-2:12, the most moving stretch.** Kettle, cups, young people drinking, one looking at the lens, tired men in frosty hats (f_0120). The sound goes to a clenched hush with a slow thump. Small human faces after a long run of silhouettes. It works because it is the first warmth. The ambulance at 2:02 adds a chill.
- **2:15-2:36, the town list.** Words and signs together (Workum, Bolsward, Franeker, Dokkum). Satisfying, like ticking a map, though I had the sense of being read a timetable.
- **2:47.8-2:48.2 into FINISH (f_0169).** A tiny gap in the sound (0.4 s, measured) and then the banner in front of a packed crowd, and the wide loud wall begins. This is the best single edit in the film for me: it works as an intake of breath. A very good payoff for the earlier silence.
- **3:06-3:08.7, the ending.** A drum roll, a repeat of the Dokkum and Franeker signs, the "ELFSTEDENTOCHT" banner, then a hard cut to black with no tail. It is abrupt and unresolved. I felt cheated for a second, then thought it fits: the race just stops, as if the film runs out.

## What is clever

- The "subtract, then slam" design: sound changes by taking things away (bass at 1:15, hush at 1:55) so each return lands. That makes a track with no melody feel like it has a story.
- Newspapers and signs do the explaining, so there are no captions. The 2:57 return to the same headline page (per the dossier) closes a loop.
- The cup-and-kettle sequence arrives at the right time and makes the earlier crowd shots more human in hindsight.
- The voice leaves before the biggest passages (0:32, 1:27, 2:48), so the weight is carried by music and picture.

## What is weak or left me cold

- Long stretches are very similar: grainy figures in snow with a steady pulse. Around 0:44-0:56 and 1:30-1:39 I drifted. The sameness may be the point, but it asks patience.
- The picture is not on the beat (the dossier measures 7.8 per cent of cuts near a beat, below chance). I did not miss that at all, but it means the cuts follow words and sections rather than rhythm, and sometimes feel loose.
- The track itself is loud and relentless with almost no melody. I would find it tiring at full length, going by the measurements, though I cannot judge this by ear.
- The voice is flat and dry, so a good deal of the information (dates, numbers) is delivered with no feeling. I could not follow it without the notes.
- Hard to say who the film is for: someone who already knows the event may get far more than I do.

## What I would tell a friend

Watch it for the tea scene and the finish banner, and for the way it makes a crowd in the cold feel huge. Do not expect a story, a hero or a happy end. It is a mood piece about doing something hard together, and it stops mid-breath.

## Three questions for the other panellists

1. The track has no tune and no resolution, yet it builds and releases. Is the 1:40 bass slam placed over a calm white shot a deliberate mismatch (weight against emptiness), or a missed hit? What does each reading do to the film's meaning?
2. The newspaper headlines say "zwaar, zwaar, zwaar", "many dropouts and injured" and "chaos at the finish", yet the sound at 2:48 feels like arrival and triumph. Is the film celebrating the event, or quietly questioning it? Which moments back your view?
3. The voice speaks the same line at 0:27.7 and 2:43.8, and the film recycles the Dokkum sign, Franeker sign and the newspaper page. What are these repeats doing: a loop, a memorial, or economy? And does the film need the abrupt black ending, or would a tail have helped?
