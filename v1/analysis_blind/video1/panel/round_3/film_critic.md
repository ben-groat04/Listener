## 1. The 1:40 slam: I open the frames the Sceptic asked for, and the picture is more interesting than any of us said

The Sceptic asked whether the picture cuts at the slam. Their numbering (f_0040, f_0041) is a slip, since that is 0:39-0:40; the frames they mean are f_0099 to f_0102. SEEN:

- f_0099 (1:38): a grey flat field, evenly spaced posts, a small dark figure crouched beside a tuft of reed. This is the "lone figure beyond the posts" of the dossier.
- f_0100 (1:39) and f_0101 (1:40): a different shot, a snow bank with a ragged ridge and a wind-scoured foreground. No people. Both frames are the same shot (the ridge profile has shifted slightly, so the camera is travelling). f_0101 carries a vertical scratch on the left.
- f_0102 (1:41): the same bank, brighter, and a dark figure and a second smaller one enter at the left.
- f_0103 (1:42): the figure and a low dark shape beside it. I read the low shape as probably a dog (medium confidence; the dossier left it open).
- f_0104 (1:43): the aerial village.

So the cut into the ridge shot happens about one second before the slam, not at it, and the slam lands inside an empty travelling shot. The Sceptic's guess (blizzard cut at the slam, so a content mismatch only) is half right: there is a cut within about a second, but it is not on the slam. And the dossier's "two figures in the blizzard at 1:40" is wrong by a second or two; people only enter at 1:41. Finer than one second, 1 fps sampling cannot go. Detector DATA does not help: it logs no cut between 1:27 and 1:48.7.

What I now claim. The picture cut arrives early, sound lands late and into emptiness, then the first people appear a second after the weight does. That is a mild, real offset, and I credit it as an edit that lets the sound come first and the image fill in after it. I do not call it counterpoint (the First-Timer's "the picture does not flinch" is the better phrase for how it looks). Concession to the Sceptic and the Music critic: nothing here proves design at frame level, and the cut-then-slam order can be an accident of two separately built layers.

## 2. Answering the questions put to me

**Music critic: does the speed-up at 3:00-3:08 line up with the roll at 3:06.2?** I used the detector cut times plus the frames. Detected cuts from 2:59: 179.04, 181.4, 184.44, 185.04, 186.56, 187.36, 188.08. The gaps are 2.4, 3.0, 0.6, 1.5, 0.8 and 0.7 s. The shortest shot (0.6 s) is at 3:04.4-3:05.0, about 1.8 s before the roll. So the picture accelerates earlier, around 3:04, and the roll arrives on top of it. What does line up is the DOKKUM cut: it begins at 186.56 (3:06.56), about 0.35 s after the roll start at 3:06.2. Given the measurement error of both, I call that together within half a second. In the frames: f_0184 is a violent blur, f_0185 a white sky and a pole, f_0186 a back-of-crowd shot, then f_0187 the composed DOKKUM sign. So the editor cleared the screen with blur and white sky and then set one steady, legible image at the roll. That is the one place in the finale where I see a picture decision that sits with a sound decision. Answer: both; the speed-up is not tied to the roll, the arrival of DOKKUM is.

**First-Timer: is rising cut rate a recognised way to fake a climax, or a sign of ran-out-of-shots?** Both are true in different films. Rising cut rate is the most common way of making the picture feel intense when the sound has nowhere to go; it is a cheap lever and viewers read it as pressure. Whether it is faking depends on what the shots are. Here the shots at 1:49-1:54 are varied and physically energetic (fur, legs at ice level, mud, a lunge), so the acceleration carries content. At 3:00-3:08 the shots are crowd backs, a bib in extreme close-up, a blur, white sky: pressure without events. A stronger version would have kept the acceleration and cut it from a handful of strong images: a face at the line (the film had f_0165, the older woman looking up at an officer), a skater visibly breasting a tape, anything with a before and an after. My verdict: the 1:49 acceleration earns itself, the 3:00 one is borrowed energy. That is the weakness I named in round 1 and I keep it, with the Symbolist's defence (the crowd is the protagonist) accepted as an account of what the film attempts, not as a rescue of how it executes.

**Insider: is the 3:07 banner from the same reel as the ice footage, and does it change my sense of the cut?** I opened f_0188 and, from the strip, f_0187 and f_0186 next to it. SEEN: f_0188 is indoors, with ceiling strip-lights, a pillar, a hand-lettered banner reading "...uitvoerige Reportage / ELFSTEDENTOCHT / op de parterre" (I read the last line as the Insider and the Symbolist did, at best medium confidence) and small paper tags hanging below it; in the foreground are the backs of hats, a hood and a pale woolly cap. The grain and contrast are softer and lower-contrast than the DOKKUM sign (f_0187) and the dark skater shots; the frame has the look of a longer lens or a lower-light stock. I cannot tell whether that is another film stock or just a different room. I agree with the Insider that this is SEEN and not the "title card" that I and the Sceptic had treated it as. It still works as a closing title, because the dominant word in the frame is ELFSTEDENTOCHT in capitals. Whether it is an advertisement for a screening (the Insider) or a hall at the event, I cannot say; "parterre" leans to a showing. What changes in my reading: the last image is the event's name, but it is a name in a room, not on the ice. I think this makes the 3:08.2 stop feel more like a title shutting than a race stopping. The Insider's reading softens "unresolved" and I accept it as plausible, SPECULATION. It does not change my main claim that the picture's last act is a recap (DOKKUM, FRANEKER, banner) laid over a flat plateau.

**Symbolist and Sceptic: the cloth at 2:40 (finish tape, start barrier, or unresolved).** I opened f_0159 to f_0162. The two frames before the cloth (f_0159 at 2:38 and f_0160 at 2:39) show a long row of about a dozen dark figures, bent a little forward, all in profile or three-quarter view, standing on pale ice against a white sky, with scratched lines on the surface leading away. They look to me like people at a rope or line, but their pose does not separate a starting crouch from standing watchers; I count at least two figures not bent. f_0161 (2:40) is a crowd on a bank and two men holding a long, pale, slack, looped band that hangs low and touches the ground. It is not taut. A finish tape is normally stretched; a start rope also. This looks to me like a cloth, banner or soft rope being carried or held. I side with the Sceptic's second thought: unresolved. I withdraw my round 1 use of the cloth as "the editor withholds the finish by showing preparation". What I keep: 2:38-2:47 is a stretch of waiting (a line, a loop, a crowd, an officer's back, a woman looking up) that ends in the blur of 2:47 and the banner at 2:48, so the cut to FINISH is a release from a hold, whatever the cloth is.

**Sceptic and Symbolist on chronology: how does the structure depart from the race day?** I agree with the Sceptic's second thought and the Symbolist's list. The film is not a straight chronology: it shows skaters in motion at 0:27-0:41 before the registration ledger at 1:18, a bibbed skater lacing skates at 1:46 after the long-shot field at 1:30-1:38, a standing line at 2:38 after the route list, and a replay of two signs after FINISH. I withdraw the stronger claim I made in round 2 that the tea sequence's position is "almost forced by the event". It sits at the quietest point of the music and, in picture order, after the heaviest cutting; that is a placement. My revised account: the footage is arranged by theme in a race-shaped order, and the arrangement, not the race, accounts for the departures.

**Music critic and First-Timer: cut phase.** The Music critic's Q1 (is the quarter-beat offset a tracker artefact) I cannot settle from the picture side. What I can add is the First-Timer's question, put to the Sceptic: a cut lag of about 0.25 s after a beat is what you would expect from an editor cutting by ear to a heard click, and it is also what the tracker could produce. 39 cuts, R = 0.365: I would call that a tendency whose cause I cannot name, and leave it. It plays no part in my settled reading.

## 3. Pushing back

- **The Symbolist's "memory of a race kept by paper; the race sits inside the press like a body inside a coat."** The image is good and the frames give it some support: newspapers open (0:04, 0:09, 0:23) and close (2:57; f_0178 shows the same headline, "zwaar, zwaar, zw...", with a photograph below), and the final text is a banner. But the final long text is the press page at 2:57 and the last frame before black is a banner, so the press does not close the film by itself; it is followed by an event sign (DOKKUM, FRANEKER) and a hall banner. I accept "bracketed by print" and drop "inside a coat".
- **The First-Timer and Symbolist on "faces".** The strip f_0162 to f_0166 gives the best run of faces in the finale: a trilby, a woman with spectacles and a fur hat, a man in a peaked cap, children at the front. That is 2:41-2:46, before FINISH. After the banner there are almost no readable faces. So the film does give faces, and gives them before the arrival, then takes them away. That is not withholding by poverty of footage; it is the order in which they are spent. It sharpens my round 1 criticism: the faces are in the wrong place for the finale to land on them.
- **The Music critic's "arrival reads as arrival only because of the FINISH banner."** I agree and would put it more strongly from the picture side: the 0.4 s gap, the whip-pan and the banner are one gesture, and without the banner the gap would read as a dropout. The music's "recombination" and the picture's single held sign need each other.
- **The Insider's "a 1963 cinema report would not be cut to a click."** True as a prior, and it points the same way as the Music critic's earlier reading: a period picture re-set to a modern track. For the film critic this is where the interest of the piece lies: the cuts follow words and sections, the music was built later, and the two meet only at a few chosen places (2:47.8; 1:15; 3:06). I take that as DATA plus INTERP; I cannot demonstrate which layer came first.

## 4. The thread I most want to develop: the film is a ledger of signs, and it spends text where it cannot spend faces

Round 2 closed on press against event signage. With the frames above I can extend it. Count the shots in the finale that a viewer can read: the newspaper (2:57), the bib (3:00), DOKKUM (3:06), FRANEKER (3:06.5, not visible at 1 fps; from the dossier), the banner (3:07), plus FINISH (2:48). The unreadable shots (blur 2:49, 3:03; white sky 3:04; crowd backs) are the others. The film alternates the opaque and the legible and puts the legible at the bar lines it controls: FINISH on the 2:48 hinge, the newspaper at the two-thirds point of the finale, DOKKUM on the roll, the banner on the stop. Text is the film's anchor, the way a drone is the music's. That is a craft observation I can defend by frames, and it explains why the finale feels held together despite diffuse shooting: the viewer is given a word at each structural point. It also explains why the film has so few named people and so many named places: it names what it can read.

What this does and does not say about celebration or warning: the negative text (zwaar, uitvallers, chaos) is in the press, the event's own signs (WELKOM, FINISH) are neutral to warm, and the final text, the banner, is the event's name. I keep "respectful, with the cost on record", not "warning", and I note the Sceptic's point that any honest record would include its injuries.

## 6. Settled reading in five sentences

1. This is a themed assembly of archive footage, arranged in a race-shaped order, under a modern, tune-less drone that the picture follows by blocks and not by the beat (cut phase is a weak tendency, R = 0.37, cause unknown); the sound's structure and the footage's chronology are separate layers that meet at a few chosen places.
2. The best edits are the ones where a legible image lands on a sound event: the 1:15 hut and ledger over the cleared mix, the 2:47.8 whip-pan into FINISH, and DOKKUM set on the roll at 3:06.2.
3. The film speaks through found text (newspapers, signs, banners) and spends faces sparingly, giving the run of them at 2:41-2:46 and then withdrawing them, so the finale is carried by speed, blur and legible signs rather than by a person.
4. The 1:40 slam sits on an empty snow shot a second after the cut, and I read it as weight without a picture to meet it, which I find effective but cannot show to be intended.
5. The mood is quiet respect for a hard day, with its cost left in the headlines and the one cross-marked vehicle, and the stop at 3:08.2, after a hall banner naming the event, closes the record like a ledger.

## 7. The single moment

**2:47 to 2:48: the violent sideways blur and the 0.4 s dropout that opens on the held FINISH banner in front of a packed crowd (f_0168 to f_0169).** In one second the film spends its only silence, its only held frame after sixty seconds of jostling, and its only word that says what the day is for, and it does so on a hand-lettered sign, not a person.
