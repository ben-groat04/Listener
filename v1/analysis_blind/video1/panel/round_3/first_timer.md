## The thread I want to develop: who is this film for?

The Insider's f_0188 point makes me re-ask the celebration-or-warning question as a question about audience. If the last image really is a notice for a screening, the whole film is a trailer-like report: headline warnings first, a hush with tea in the middle, a crowd and a title at the end. Warnings in a trailer are there to raise the stakes, not to accuse anyone. That is why I felt "awe with a bruise" and never felt told off. The bruise is an ingredient, and the film does not make a case. I mark this as my interpretation, and the Sceptic's cheaper hypothesis (it is just what the footage supplied) is still alive.

## Settled reading (5 sentences)

1. This is a black-and-white film of a huge winter skating race in 1963, driven by a loud, even 78 BPM drone track, and what moved me was not the race but the tea scene (1:55-2:13) and the numbers and town names read out against faint, cold pictures.
2. The sound makes the drama by taking things away and slamming them back (bass out at 1:15, bass slam at 1:40, the 0.4 s gap at 2:47.8), while the picture mostly follows the day in order and the words; that is the best reading of how they relate, and it is partly checked (cut phase is significant at p = 0.0055, but only 39 cuts).
3. I came away impressed and slightly uneasy rather than told to cheer: the warnings (newspapers, ambulance, minus 16 degrees) sit early and the crowd and title arrive late.
4. The ending is a hard sound cut over a screening banner, which I now read as the sound shutting an announcement, not an unfinished race.
5. I would tell a friend: watch it for the weather and the crowds and the hush in the middle, expect the finale to be fast and hard to hold on to, and do not expect a winner or a face to follow.

## The single moment

**2:47.8 to 2:48 (the 0.4 s gap, then the FINISH banner before a packed crowd, f_0169).** The sound drops to almost nothing for about half a beat as the last words of the refrain end, and the picture lands on the one word the whole film has been heading towards. It is the only place where I am sure that sound, voice and image meant the same thing at once.
