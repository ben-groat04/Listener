Blind mode. All five other round 2 statements read, plus my own. Frames I re-opened this round: f_0188 (3:07) and f_0161 (2:40). Labels: SEEN, DATA, INTERP, SPECULATION. Times m:ss.

## 1. Answers and concessions

**The Insider's banner (f_0188) changes my ending, and I concede it.** SEEN: a hand-lettered white banner, "...uitvoerige Reportage / ELFSTEDENTOCHT / op de parterre" (last line small script, only roughly legible), with ceiling strip-lights above and the backs of hatted heads in the foreground. I read it as a lit interior, and I agree with the Insider that it is a notice for a showing. My round 2 claim that the cut at about 3:08.2 is "an archive shutting" survives only in part: the picture has already left the ice before the sound is cut. My "slammed door" is now a door closed on a room full of people, not on the race. I also withdraw "the title of the piece" from round 1: whether the banner is the film's title or a screening notice is undecidable (INTERP either way), but the screening reading is what the frame shows.

**This strengthens my "keeping" motif and weakens my "interrupted" language.** The film begins with print (newspapers, 0:04-0:27), passes through a bellows camera (0:57), a microphone (0:58) and a tripod (1:00), and ends with an advertisement for the report itself, in a room (3:07). That is a closed circle of record: the event is wrapped in the media that kept it. I keep this as INTERP, with the Insider's caution that the banner may simply be the next shot of the source reel.

**The cloth at 2:40 (my question to the Sceptic, now answered by two panellists).** I re-opened f_0161. SEEN: men at the front of a crowd on a bank holding a long, pale, soft, slack loop; a thin line on the ice at lower left. The Sceptic's "slack, bag-like, not a taut tape" is right. I therefore drop both my "start-line barrier" and the Film critic's "finish tape" as claims. What remains: a cloth held by people, in front of people, at a threshold. I stop trying to say which end of the race it marks. The film's own habit, which I noted in round 2, is the better evidence: it keeps showing people holding things up for others to see.

**The Sceptic's chronology point, which I accept and then use.** I conceded in round 2 that race-day order explains much. The Sceptic, in turn, concedes that registration (1:18), tea (1:55), route list (2:15) and a threshold at 2:38 are not strictly in race order. Neither of us can prove the departures are intended, and the Insider's point that the footage may have been assembled by any newsreel editor stands. My settled claim is smaller than in round 1: the film is race-shaped, theme-assembled, and I do not need intent.

**The Film critic on "life flashing" and the recycled signs.** Conceded. Economy (a short film reuses its best legible shots) is a sufficient explanation for DOKKUM at 2:34 and 3:06 and for "zwaar" at 0:23 and 2:57. What I keep is only the order: the replay at 3:06-3:07 runs Dokkum then Franeker, the reverse of 2:32-2:34 (dossier). That is mild evidence of a re-read list. I mark it "possible rhyme", not "memorial".

**The Music critic on "the pulse is the race itself".** Fair. A drone-and-drum bed plays identically under the tea and the town list. I withdraw it as a claim about the subject. What I keep is the weaker point, that the bed supplies endurance as a general mood for whatever sits on top of it, which is why the tea (1:55-2:13) reads as relief and not as interruption.

**The 42.6 Hz sub (Film critic's and Music critic's questions).** I cannot answer what pitch it is from the data and will not guess. I stay with the one observation: at one event per 6.1 s it is too slow to be a heart. "Bell" or "tide" is a metaphor, not a finding. I record it as the least verifiable image in my round 2.

**The First-Timer's observation that the refrain read as pride the first time and dread the second.** This is the best evidence in the discussion that the film's ambiguity is built by position, not by tone. The voice says the same sentence at 0:28 and 2:44 (DATA); what changes is the hundred seconds of headlines, cold figures, tea and an ambulance between them. A refrain whose meaning is altered only by what has been heard in between is the nearest thing in this film to a poetic device I can defend from the data.

## 2. Where I still disagree

- **With the Sceptic on "anonymity is just bibs".** The asymmetry stands: numbers in close-up (12 at 0:18, 170 at 1:05, 245 and 460 at 1:07-1:11, 157 at 1:21, 342 at 2:52; dossier) and places with sign-names, no caption naming a person, ever. I accept it is not proof of intent and that a 1963 audience may have seen itself in the crowd (the Insider). I lower the claim from "elegy for anonymity" to "a film about a collective, in which individuals appear as numbers and places as names".
- **With the Insider on "civic pride" as the neutral reading.** The printed words on screen are warnings (zwaar x3, "Tal van uitvallers en vele gewonden", "Chaos aan de finish"), and the Film critic's press-versus-signage count (negative text from newspapers, positive from event signs) is the strongest evidence anyone has produced. Pride and warning sit side by side; I do not think either wins. I side with the Film critic, the First-Timer and my own round 2: ambivalent.

## 3. The thread I most want to develop: hands that hold things up

The motif that survives every round is not eyes, not light and dark, and not a death arc. It is **display**. Look at what is held: newspapers, tilted and close (0:04, 0:09, 0:23, 2:57); the FINISH sheet in front of a crowd of faces (2:48); the slack cloth at 2:40 (f_0161); bibs worn on backs; town signs on posts; a hand-lettered banner at 3:07 (f_0188); a small card in a hand at 0:21 (dossier). Everyone in this film is either holding up a sign for someone else or being counted by one. And the film itself is the largest sign of all, held up before an audience who are shown at 3:07 with their backs to us, looking at the board. That is the poem: a community teaches itself what to feel about an ordeal by writing it on a sheet and raising it. The words on the sheets disagree ("zwaar" against "FINISH"), and the film does not settle them. The voice yields to the music where the argument would otherwise have to be resolved (0:32, 1:27, 2:48), and the music does not resolve it either, because it has no cadence (music reading, DATA).

## 4. Settled reading, in five sentences

1. The piece is about how a society keeps and shows its own endurance: a race, then the print that announced it, the camera and microphone that recorded it, the counting of its participants, and finally the banner that sells the record back to an audience.
2. It does not decide whether the ordeal is a triumph or a warning, because the words on screen disagree (headlines about dropouts, signs that say FINISH and welcome) and the music, a steady D drone with no cadence, offers weight but no verdict.
3. Individuals are shown as numbers and places as names, so the protagonist is the crowd, and the best human moments (the tea at 1:55-2:13, a face looking at the lens at 1:59) are the exceptions that make the rest feel like weather.
4. The picture follows the narration and the music's sections more than its beat (cuts weakly clustered about a quarter beat late, R = 0.37, p = 0.0055, with the tracker's phase unverified), so sound and image meet at the edges and drift apart in between, as when the bass slam at 1:40 lands on a calm white blizzard.
5. I hold the circle of record (print, lens, voice, ledger, banner) as interpretation, mark the death arc, the eyes, the heartbeat and the start-or-finish cloth as withdrawn or unresolved, and take the hard cut at 3:08.2 as the sound of the reel ending rather than of a runner stopping.

## 5. The one moment

**2:47.8-2:49: the 0.4 s hole in the music, then the FINISH sheet held in front of a crowd of faces (f_0169).** Voice, drums and the blurred run-up all drop out together, and the film's goal arrives as a plain hand-held word before people looking back at us. It is the one place where sound, picture, words and the whole film's habit of raising signs meet, and what it shows is not a winner but a crowd and a placard.
