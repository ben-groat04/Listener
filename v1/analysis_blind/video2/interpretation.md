## 1. The piece in one paragraph

A five-minute reel of grainy, scratched colour cine film (gauge, maker and purpose unknown: possibly amateur, possibly commissioned) [READING], opened by a dark board with white capitals reading "DE WINTER VAN / 1963 / IN FRIESLAND" (hand-lettered or letterboard; cannot tell) [SEEN 0:00], is carried by a single, machine-steady electronic loop in B minor at 120 BPM with no chorus, key change, tempo change or cadence [MUSIC]. The film moves from frost and empty snow, through a flat, dry spoken narration, probably Dutch (forced-Dutch transcription is coherent; language auto-detect failed), of a date, a distance, a temperature and entry numbers (0:42 to 2:36), to a crowd on the ice, and then, with the voice gone, to a pack seen from inside by backs and bibs (3:09 to 4:36); it ends on ducks, broken floes and a fade to black, with no finish line and no winner shown. The music tells its story by subtraction and slow brightening, not by tune or tempo: five low tolls, a limping groove, two rhythm-section dropouts, a drop, a voiceless stretch that blooms, a plateau, then a hard stop. The picture is not locked to the beat but is cut to the music's large form. What it does to you: hush and a cold awe, a trudge, a held breath and a relief at 1:28.6, a long hypnotic pressure that may read as trance or as sameness, a cold exhilaration in the pack, and an ending that stops without resolving and that the viewer must finish for themselves. The panel is close to unanimous that the feeling is made by the sound and the cutting, and that the footage mostly permits it rather than carries it [PANEL insider R3; MUSIC].

## 3. Meaning and themes (consensus)

**3.1 The piece is an edit built on a machine, and most of its meaning is the edit's.** Every panellist ends up here. The arc (frost, announcement, crowd, pack, thaw) is made in the cutting and by the music laid on it; the original footage presumably had no such arc [PANEL insider R1, R3; sceptic R2]. Strongest evidence: the music is a loop machine (kick inter-onset 499.99 ms, constant 120 BPM) and the picture follows its large form (see 3.2). The feeling of hush, then pressure, then thaw comes "largely from the music and the edit" [PANEL insider R3].

**3.2 The picture is cut to the music's form, not its pulse.** Agreed by all six after round 3, and the most firmly measured finding. On the 120 BPM kick grid, cuts are not significantly beat-locked (36% within 80 ms against 32% by chance; p about 0.09 on beat phase, p about 0.2 on bar phase). A possible early bar clustering in the first 42 s (n=10, p about 0.01, uncorrected) is too small a sample to rely on [DOSSIER, sync_kickgrid; fact-check]. (An earlier 37% against 43% figure tested the tracker's 161 BPM hi-hat grid, the wrong one.) Yet the hard cuts sit within about 0.1 s of the section edges at 0:28.1, 1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6 and 3:36.7 [DOSSIER]. From 2:36.6 to 4:00.6 eight of eight cuts fall within 0.16 s of a 12 s lattice (2:36.6, 2:48.6 ... 4:00.6); sliding the lattice through 120 phases, only one reaches 7 or more, and it is the music's own [PANEL sceptic R2]. The lattice does not exist in the first half (loop A's 28 s cell is hit once of four) [PANEL sceptic, music critic, insider R3]. The panel's best phrase: the edit is "organised at six bars and above, indifferent at one bar and below" [PANEL sceptic R3].

**3.3 Subtraction is the music's main expressive device.** The biggest events are removals: the drum stem thinning at 1:10, drums and bass out about 1:24.0 to 1:28.5, the narrator out at 2:36, the machine out at 5:00.86 [MUSIC; PANEL music critic R3]. Escalation is by brightness and automation (top band of the "other" stem about +22 dB over 3:00 to 3:50, only about +3 dB on the full mix), not by new tune or tempo [MUSIC]. The strongest evidence is the 4 s rhythm-section dropout before 1:28.6 (not silence: the narrator and pad continue).

**3.4 The film is about endurance in a crowd more than about any individual.** Narration counts (200 km, 16 below, 575 and about 9,500 under two labels, read as competition and tour riders); bibs count (201, 529, 157, 170, 48). Faces are scarce and surface roughly (2:36, 4:10, 4:35). If the two figures are disjoint participants, tourers are about 94 per cent (9,500 of 10,075); whether they all started is not stated, and the inference that the crowd was ordinary people is [SPEC] [PANEL insider R2, R3; sceptic R3]. This edit shows no winner and no finish line (the event itself presumably had both, but they are not in the footage).

**3.5 People are given in order, then removed.** No people before 0:48; two tiny distant walkers from 0:48, foreground figures from 0:53; a crowd arriving on the cut at 1:28.6; anonymous packs with bibs from 3:09; a few faces and a red hat at 4:10 to 4:35; one person's legs at 4:49 to 4:51, then nobody on screen from 4:52 [PANEL film critic R3; fact-check]. Warm red accents recur (lamps 2:44, hats 3:36, 4:06 to 4:30, flag 4:45 to 4:48) [DOSSIER].

**3.6 Sound and picture carry two layers of "number and particular".** The machine supplies framework (the 12 s cell, about one call per bar, the narrated digits); the stock and the handheld camera keep leaking particulars through it (a bobble hat at 4:32, a dark collar, bent-forward faces, burn-holed emulsion) [PANEL film critic R3]. This is a pattern a viewer registers without being able to name; it is a reading, not a finding.

**3.7 The voice changes role.** Measured: narration free of the bar (0:42 to 2:36); voice-like chops loosely bar-anchored (from 3:28.6); one principal voice-like hit per roughly 2.0 s bar (seven, 4:46.6 to 4:58.6), usually with a smaller echo or second hit about 0.3 s later [MUSIC; fact-check]. Consensus: whatever the source, a voice-shaped thing becomes regular, and the late voice is rhythmic first. Whether that is also a metaphor is rated medium-low [PANEL music critic R3] (see 4.3).

**3.8 The film's first and last motifs are ice.** Frost-laden branches open the film (0:02 to 0:24) and return at 2:37 to 2:44; ice floes close it (4:55 to 5:04). The surface is written on (title board, lorry lettering, bibs, skate tracks at 4:50) and then breaks up [DOSSIER; PANEL symbolist R2, R3]. Agreed as an observable frame of images; the "writing that does not last" gloss is the symbolist's reading.

**3.9 The ending refuses a finish line.** All panellists observe that the edit gives the event no ending of its own: about 12 s of nobody on screen (4:52 to 5:04: ducks, then floes), preceded by 3 s of one person's legs, nobody crossing anything [PANEL film critic R2; insider R3]. What it means is disputed (4.1).

## 4. Live disagreements

**4.1 Is the ending an elegy, a statement, or just the end of a reel?**
- *Elegy or meaningful (symbolist, early first-timer, early insider):* a hard sound stop over a soft picture, bare floes, "a door, then a dimmer", "a mechanism stopping is the only way a machine can grieve" [PANEL symbolist R1, R2].
- *Shrug or default (sceptic; music critic on vocabulary):* a reel must end; floes are what was filmed last; a loop machine's ordinary exit is a hard stop; the sound has no falling line, held chord or ritardando, so by the usual vocabulary it is not an elegy [PANEL sceptic R2, R3; music critic R3].
- *Where the panel now stands:* by round 3 everyone calls it a single, well-timed landing whose meaning is the viewer's. The symbolist keeps elegy "only as an effect"; the sceptic rates intent 50/50 (a real shift from round 1); the film critic and music critic read the landing as craft even if the stop is ordinary. Not settled by the data.

**4.2 Exactly how do the picture and the sound end?**
- The panel agrees the sound stops first (5:00.86), the three hits ring (to about 5:02.85) and the tail reaches digital zero at about 5:04.24 [MUSIC].
- *Film critic (frames opened) and fact-check luma re-measure:* mean luma is about 118 to 128 at 5:00 to 5:02, about 78 to 93 at 5:03.0, about 42 to 58 at 5:04.0, about 12 to 25 at 5:05.0, about 10 at 5:05.5. The fade begins at about 5:02.5; at the audio's zero (5:04.24) the picture is about a third of normal brightness, not black; it is near-black at 5:05 and not fully black until about 5:06 [SEEN, measured].
- *Sceptic and insider:* picture and sound reach black and zero together within about 0.2 s. The dossier's "fade from about 5:02" is closer to the truth; "black at 5:04" is too early.
- *Moderator's note (corrected after fact-check):* the picture is not undimmed at the slam (5:00.86 is still full brightness, but dimming starts at about 5:02.5, during the hits) and is neither black nor full-strength when the sound reaches zero. Neither "black with the sound at zero" nor "full strength at 5:03" holds.
- Whether the timing is planned or a timeline default is undecidable [PANEL film critic, insider, sceptic].

**4.3 Does the voice's arc (speech, then chops, then calls) mean something?**
- *Symbolist, first-timer:* speech to tick, face to number, number to ice; the film hands the telling from a person to a machine and then to the weather [PANEL first-timer R3; symbolist R1, R3].
- *Music critic, sceptic:* the late voice counts nothing audibly (no digits recovered); the calls answer no picture event (the symbolist conceded this: nothing at 4:46 to 4:58 is counted, named or timed to the calls); a producer simply retires a sample this way. Metaphor rated medium-low [PANEL music critic R3, symbolist R3].
- *Common ground:* the progression is measured; its meaning is the listener's.

**4.4 Why does the 12 s lattice drift after about 4:12, and does it?**
- *Round 2 view (sceptic, film critic, symbolist):* the grid loosens after 4:12 (0.76, 0.68, 0.60, 0.36 s off for 4:12.6 to 4:48.6); the editor "let go".
- *Sceptic's round 3 retraction:* the 4:12.6 miss comes from an 8.2 s gap between detected cuts (4:11.8 and 4:20.0) in which frames at 4:13 to 4:21 show clear unlisted cuts; the detector under-splits fast pack shots. So the drift is at least partly a detection artefact and "the data cannot say whether the lattice holds" after 4:00.6.
- *Film critic's round 3 view:* the mean shot length is about the same before and after 4:00 (2.7 s against about 3.0 s), so pace does not explain it; the change is in footage (from backs and bibs to faces, head-on packs, a red-hat close-up), so the drift, if real, is footage-led. The sceptic's retraction takes the air from this too (no drift to explain).
- *Music critic's round 3 measurement:* the drum accents every 12 s continue through 4:12, 4:24, 4:36 and 4:48, so the music keeps the cue even where the picture may have stopped obeying it. Fact-check: this drum accent is weak (pre-bar drum onset strength about 17% higher on lattice bars than on others; indistinguishable at 4:12.6 and 4:48.6; drum RMS flat), so it is not a clear cue.
- *Moderator's note:* the safest statement is that the lattice is proven for eight points (2:36.6 to 4:00.6) and unproven afterwards, in either direction.

**4.5 Where is the crest?**
- The picture's formal landmark is the 3:36.7 flash on the lattice; the music's loudest stretch is 4:12 to 4:24 (and the loudness margin across the plateau is only 2 to 3 dB). They are 36 s apart; the faces of 4:10 arrive just before the loudest music. The sceptic's point that "crest" is two different things in two channels was accepted by the symbolist and music critic [PANEL sceptic R2, R3]. The music's most defined events are the subtractions, not the loudest bars [PANEL music critic R3].

**4.6 Faces: withheld, ordered or accidental?**
- *Early view (film critic, symbolist, insider, first-timer):* faces were withheld for effect and paid off at the crest.
- *Sceptic:* faces occur throughout (1:50, 2:36, 2:57, 4:10, 4:35); the camera simply follows from behind in the pack scenes; the demographic is mostly anonymous tourers.
- *Resolution:* all conceded. The agreed wording is "faces are scarcer when the camera follows from behind, and the edit kept that for a long time" (first-timer) or "ordered, not withheld" (film critic). Whether the editor kept pack footage because it has few faces remains an open question [PANEL insider R3].

**4.7 Is colour and film damage designed or the stock's own?**
- *Sceptic:* picture warmth and brightness do not track the music (r about 0.2 and 0.03); flicker and flares are stock decay or telecine unless shown otherwise. Colour is not "an instrument".
- *Film critic (round 3, conceded in full on colour):* the damage belongs to the stock and is tolerated rather than designed; the editor kept shots with burn holes (4:52), the 3:37 burn flash and the 2:08 whiteout, which is what "respect for the material" looks like [SPEC].
- *Symbolist:* flares as "memory burning through the emulsion" is a metaphor, not a finding.
- *First-timer:* withdrew "flares appear at section changes, so they are craft" as by-eye pattern-hunting; keeps only that she liked them there. Two coincidences remain noted without weight (2:08 on a fill; 3:37 on the plateau's start).

**4.8 Is the middle (1:40 to 2:36) flat or hypnotic?**
- *Insider, first-timer:* a risk of numbness over similar skater shots with a voice reading facts over a static look; the first-timer felt it sag from 1:40 to 2:36 but not from 2:36 to 3:28 even though the music is flat in both.
- *Music critic, sceptic:* the sound alone cannot decide; what varies is the picture, so shot rate and camera proximity decide. The first-timer reports her own case: she drifted through 3:36 to 4:00 (backs and bibs) but not 4:05 to 4:24.
- *Common ground:* hypnotic or numb depends on attention; the panel's data point is that cutting method changes at 2:36.6 (shot length about 5.2 s over 1:28.6 to 2:36.6, 2.7 s from 2:36.6 to 4:00 and about 3.0 s after 4:00; detector-based) [PANEL film critic R3].

**4.9 Is the narration period or modern, archival or newly recorded?**
- Insider's early "possibly archival narrator" was dropped to low confidence. The music critic: band-limitation, dryness, abrupt edges fit an old source, a deliberately filtered modern one, and stem artefacts alike. The edge at 1:28.6 is a gate or edit, so the voice was cut to fit the band. No panellist claims a decade [PANEL music critic, insider R3].

**4.10 Single versus two sources for the late voice.** The 4:29 to 4:36 phrase wobbles (26 to 45 cents at 4 to 6 Hz, specialist figure, unverified), which favours a human-derived or modulated source; the calls (4:46.6 onwards) are tidier. The music critic's cheaper hypothesis is one source being moved onto the grid (medium-low); two sources is not excluded; "sampled crowd from the film" is speculation with no support [PANEL music critic R3].

**4.11 Is the edit felt or only organised?** The sceptic (round 2) called it "mechanically organised but not rhythmically felt", the sort of thing done against a zoomed-out waveform or a marker track. The film critic pushed back (round 3): holding a 12 s habit for 84 s while letting a lunge and a hat run free inside the cell is a decision about priorities: the grid governs the joints and the material the interior. The sceptic conceded that the edit is designed at the macro level and still denies any evidence of designed feel at beat, bar or colour level. Direction of causation (listened to the music versus used a loop-length marker) is unknowable [PANEL sceptic R2, R3; music critic R3].

## 5. Craft notes

**Editing.**
- Hard cuts keyed to the music's section edges to about 0.0 to 0.12 s: 0:28.08, 1:28.55, 1:40.56, 2:36.6, 3:28.5, 3:36.7 and 4:48.65 [DOSSIER; PANEL]. The 4:36 edge is less tight (about 0.1 to 0.6 s).
- No beat lock, no bar lock; between joints the picture follows its own handheld, irregular rhythm.
- A two-regime edit: before 2:36.6 the cuts meet section edges and the narration's content (the road goes empty as the narrator names the event, the crowd arrives as the voice is cut); from 2:36.6 to 4:00.6 cuts sit on the 12 s cell, with, at most, a mild drum-activity rise before cell starts (about 17% above other bars; a weak claim, see 4.4 note) [PANEL music critic R3; fact-check]. The insider calls these "two editing cultures": a documentary cut to events, and a machine cut to the loop.
- The edit follows the music's structure but not its stops: the 1:10 drum thinning and the 1:24.0 to 1:24.6 dropout have no picture event at all; only the arrival at 1:28.6 does [PANEL music critic R3].
- Detection caveat: shots.json misses the hard cut at 1:28.6, lists a cut at 69.2 s that is a cross-dissolve at about 1:13, over-splits colour-cycling shots and under-splits fast pack shots [DOSSIER; PANEL sceptic R3].

**Music.**
- 120 BPM (the tracker's 161 is probably the hi-hat cross-rhythm: its median interval is 0.372 s, close to 0.375 s but unproved); B minor Aeolian with a flat-sixth colour; two bass loops (loop A, 14 bars, E-G-[A]-D, E-G-[A]-D, B, with the tonic withheld until the loop's end; loop B, 6 bars, B-A-D-[E], with the tonic returning every 12 s) [MUSIC].
- Kick machine-exact (spread about 7 ms); at 1:28.6 it moves by one quaver from the off-beat onto every beat, the snare takes alternate beats, there is no tempo change [MUSIC].
- Production: a dry, centred kick and bass under a wide "wall" (instrument identity undecidable); narration band-limited, gated and dry; chops fall 20 dB within 0.14 to 0.59 s [MUSIC].
- The track touches 0 dBFS from about 1:20; the last hit is not its loudest sample. Fill positions are medium-low confidence [MUSIC].

**Archive.**
- Colour film (gauge unknown) with grain, scratches, rounded corners, drifting tints, light leaks, a recurring twig-like scratch at the top of frame, and burn-hole discs at 2:37 and 4:52 [DOSSIER; SEEN f_0158, f_0293].
- Text appears only inside the film's world (title board, lorry lettering, a "K" sign, bibs), never as an overlay [DOSSIER].
- Period clues in the frames: saloon cars, long coats, bobble hats, long-bladed skates, a tractor with a scraper on the ice, a motorbike with a sidecar [DOSSIER; PANEL insider R3].

**Structure.**
- Order: hush (0:00), the march begins (0:28), announcement (0:42), suspense (1:10), arrival (1:28.6), a long middle (1:40 to 2:36), voiceless bloom (2:36.6), pack (3:09 to 4:36), pull-back (4:36), thaw (4:49), hard stop (5:00.86), fade.
- Rhymes: frost in and out (0:02 to 0:24; 2:37 to 2:44); tiny figures on pale ground at both ends (1:14 is real; 0:36 specks unconfirmed as people; at 4:36 the lane is dark blue); roads to a vanishing point (1:02, 1:20, 2:37, 3:25 to 3:28, 4:36); a red accent that recurs [DOSSIER].
- No explicit rondo is claimed; the insider noted the frost return gives "a rondo shape".

## 6. Context that changes the reading (inferred from clues only)

- **Place, date, kind of event:** the board names Friesland and 1963 [SEEN]; "Friesland" is certain only from the board (it appears in the narration passes only as a low-reliability "Ffriesland" at 1:04.6). The narration (partly garbled; probably Dutch) gives a date ("18 januari 1963"), "schaatsmarathon" (moderately clear: variants "schaatsmarathon Zonderweg", "Schatmarathon"), about 200 km, a temperature ("16 graden beneden", Celsius inferred), 575 and 9500, and the pictures show mass skating on frozen waterways with snow-cleared lanes (a tractor with a blade, 2:54 to 2:56). It is a mass long-distance skating event, on what look like frozen waterways [READING] [PANEL; DOSSIER]. The race's name rests on garbled transcript strings and is not used as a fact.
- **Soundtrack of unknown date.** The score is a programmed loop-machine track and the cuts follow its form, so picture and music were put together deliberately; the date and origin of the score are unknown (nothing measured dates it). Whether any 1963-era edit survives under it cannot be told, and the author is unknowable [READING] [PANEL sceptic R3; MUSIC].
- **Who shot it.** Nothing in the data shows who shot it or why. A shaky held camera at skater height inside the pack and report-like narration fit an amateur, a club or a commissioned record alike [SPEC]. Whether the narration is archival is unknown (low confidence) [PANEL insider R1, R3].
- **An organised, communal leisure culture.** Numbered entrants, cleared lanes, a swept course (3:26), a few men in peaked caps (4:28; role unconfirmed) [SPEC], cars, spectators at railings. A viewer from the period would find the objects ordinary; a viewer now may see "a shared public activity that needed cold, trust and thousands of strangers" [SPEC, marked as the insider's gloss].
- **Arithmetic of the field:** if 575 and about 9,500 are disjoint participant groups, the field was mostly tourers (about 94 per cent); that the crowd was ordinary people is [SPEC]; it may explain the anonymity (not a statement about the editor's attitude).
- **Why the narration lands as understatement:** dry numbers leave the emotional work to music and picture. A "Dutch/northern habit" claim is outside the data and excluded.
- **Why the middle can feel flat:** the music is nearly flat in loudness from 1:40, and the facts are read over a static look; the viewer's attention decides [MUSIC; PANEL].

## 7. The moments a human would remember

1. **1:24.6 to 1:28.6: the hollow and the drop.** Four seconds with no drums or bass (narrator and pad play on) over an empty snow road with four walkers; then at 1:28.6 the kick lands on every beat, the voice is cut, and the picture jumps from empty road to a crowd at a bridge railing. All six panellists chose this as the single moment that best captures the piece (the film critic, sceptic and first-timer pick 1:28.6 itself; the insider, music critic and symbolist pick the 1:24.6 to 1:28.6 stretch). It carries the method in one gesture: subtraction, then arrival; voice against machine; emptiness against bodies; a cut that obeys the music's form but not its beat.
2. **0:00 to 0:28: the five tolls over frost.** Quiet and arresting; the first cuts meet the strikes at 0:08, 0:12 and 0:24.
3. **0:28: the limp begins.** Cut to shaky dark trunks with the bass; the sharpest early hinge.
4. **2:36.6: the narrator stops and a car drives away between frosted trees.** The symbolist's favourite and the insider's "loneliest moment"; the sceptic notes that a crowd-to-landscape cut is ordinary and that "settled key, unsettled life" is a pretty sentence with no handle for testing.
5. **3:36.7: the hard cut to an orange burn flare on the plateau's first bar** (no dark frame). The picture's formal climax.
6. **4:10: a young man in a dark collar (fur uncertain), lips parted, looking near the lens.** The second-choice moment of the first-timer ("the one face that looks back") and the panel's shared example of a person as a person; also 4:32 (a red bobble hat and a hairy jumper close to the lens).
7. **4:49 to 5:06: legs on scored ice, ducks with burn holes, floes, the hard stop, three off-beat hits, a fade to near-black.** The last unpeopled stretch (about 12 s from 4:52) and an ending that does not resolve.

## 9. Moderator's bottom line

A restrained, intelligently cut reel in which the sound makes the weather and the picture supplies the people. Its best writing is subtractive (the hollow before 1:28.6, the hard stop at 5:00.86); its best structural fact is that the picture obeys the music's form (to 0.1 s at the main joints, and to a 12 s lattice for 84 s) while ignoring its beat; and its emotional line, from hush through relief and pressure to an unpeopled thaw, is mostly made by the edit and the music rather than by the footage. The panel's firmest measurements are the edit's section locking, the 120 BPM groove, the drop at 1:28.6 and the dual ending; its softest claims are the meaning of the ending, the late voice, colour as design and anything about who made it or why.
