# First-timer (a first-time viewer, no special expertise): Round 1 reading of video2

Basis: the checked music reading, the dossier, and frames I opened myself (contact sheets 01, 10, 16; frame 1:29). Anything I did not see or find in the data is marked as a guess. Times are m:ss.

## What it is about

Home-movie footage of a hard winter, a title board reading "De Winter van 1963 in Friesland" [seen, 0:00], set to a steady electronic piece. It starts with frost and empty snow, moves through a graveyard, roads and walkers, to a long stretch of skaters. It ends on broken ice floes and black. Ice is the subject; people come and go. The narrator reads dry facts (a date, 200 km, 16 below, 575 entrants) in a flat voice. From the transcripts alone (hearsay, not verified) it is a long skating race; I never see a finish line, a winner or a celebration in the frames I opened. So for me it is about enduring cold and about scale (tiny figures on huge white). It is not a story with a hero.

## How it made me feel, and which moments did it

- **0:00 to 0:28, hushed and a little awed.** The title board, then blue frost-covered branches filling the frame [seen, sheet 01]. With a pad and no beat [music reading] it feels like holding your breath outdoors. It is lovely, and also slow; I would be patient for 28 s but not much more.
- **0:28, unease.** A cut to shaky dark trunks lands exactly as the bass and drum groove arrive (dossier: 0.06 s apart). This is the first moment I thought "someone planned that". The limping beat plus empty fields, a fence and a graveyard feels like a trudge; faintly funereal.
- **0:42 to 1:10, the voice.** A low flat voice reading a date over a graveyard gives it weight. I cannot follow the words (Dutch, per the data), so for me the voice is texture and tone, not information. That is a limitation of mine, and I suspect it would land harder for a Dutch speaker.
- **1:24.6 to 1:28.6, held breath, then the drop.** About 4 s of near silence over a whiteout road with a few walkers [seen, 1:29 frame], then at 1:28.6 the beat lands on every beat and the picture jumps to a crowd on a bridge. This is the best moment in the piece for me: relief, like stepping off a long walk into a lively street. Sound and picture agree.
- **2:36.6 to 2:48, the car and the frost.** The voice stops, and we cut to a small car on a frosty road and ice-laden branches, echoing the opening. The music keeps growing without people. A bit eerie, and I like the rhyme with the start.
- **3:09 to 4:36, inside the pack.** Handheld close-ups of backs and numbered bibs (440, 201, 504 [seen, sheet 10]), whip-blurs, a few strained faces (4:10, per the dossier). It is the most physical, immersive stretch and the music is at its fullest. I felt pressure and a sort of trance. But the music is also flat in loudness and repeats a loop; for several minutes I would be moved by the pictures rather than the sound. That is the stretch I could imagine drifting through.
- **4:36 to 5:06, the ending.** Pressure drops, a flag against cyan sky, ducks and gulls, floes in pink-brown water [seen, sheet 16], then the music slams to a stop at 5:00.9 with three off-beat hits, and the picture fades to black 1 to 2 s later. It does not feel like "the end of a race". It feels like a door closing, quiet and unresolved. I found it moving mainly because it refuses to cheer.

## What is clever

- Cuts are not on the beat (measured: random phase, 37% vs 43% by chance) but the big hard cuts sit on the music's section changes to about 0.1 s (0:28, 1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6, 3:36.7). As a viewer you do not count beats; you feel "the picture changed when the music changed". That works.
- Tension by subtraction before the drop: taking drums, then bass, away makes the drop feel earned.
- The frost opening returning at 2:37, and the ice opening out into thaw at the end, give the film a shape.
- The film shows mass and then individuals (a woman at 2:57, bibs, a red hat recurring), and the numbers in the voice match numbers on the bibs. That is a nice quiet echo, though I may be reading too much into it (interpretation).

## What is weak or bored me

- 1:40 to 2:36 and 3:36 to 4:36 are long, locked, loudness-flat stretches. The music does not change its tune at all (no tune, per the reading); only the top-end brightens. I felt the sameness.
- The colour flicker (1:43 to 1:50, 2:00 to 2:35) is distracting, and I cannot tell if it is film decay or an effect.
- No captions or names: I never learn who anyone is or who wins. If you wanted a story you will not get one.
- The second-half voice chops are just a texture to me; I could not tell if they are a voice at all.

## What I would tell a friend

"Watch the first minute and a half for atmosphere, wait for the drop at 1:28, then let the pictures of the pack carry you. The ending is the best bit because it just stops."

## Three questions for the other panellists

2. The film withholds faces and a result, and ends on empty water and black rather than a finish line. Is that a choice about endurance and anonymity, or simply what footage was available? What does the ending mean to you?
3. Is the long, flat middle (1:40 to 2:36 and 3:36 to 4:36) hypnotic or just repetitive? Which half of the room feels pull, and which feels numbness, and what in the sound or picture decides that?
