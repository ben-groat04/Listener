# Music critic and producer: Round 1 reading of video2

Blind mode. I cannot hear; I read the checked music reading (`music_reading.md`) and the dossier, and treat the viewers' outside facts as hearsay. Times are m:ss. Confidence in brackets where it matters. Section times and levels are measured; the feelings are my interpretation.

## 1. What this piece is about

As music, it is a piece about **endurance as mechanism**. A fixed 120 BPM machine (kick interval 499.99 ms, kicks correlating at 0.79) keeps going while a human voice reports facts over it, the voice then thins to a texture, and finally becomes a once-a-bar tick. The human narrative is laid over the loop and then absorbed into it. That matches the picture's arc (lone figures, then crowds, then a pack, then empty ice) without the music ever illustrating it. The words are flat, factual narration: a date, a distance, a temperature, a head count. I treat the Dutch-ness and the 1963 date as coming from the transcripts only. Nothing in the harmony tells you the subject. It tells you the register: serious, cold, unresolved.

It is a loop machine, not a song. There is no chorus, no key change, no tempo change and no cadence. B Aeolian throughout. Two bass loops: A (E-G-[A]-D, E-G-[A]-D, B; 14 bars) from 0:28, and B (B-A-D-[E]; 6 bars, 12 s) from 2:36.6. Form is delivered by subtraction, by brightness, and by when the voice is allowed in.

## 2. How it makes me feel, and exactly where

- **0:00-0:28, awe with a chill.** A wide, drumless, hazy pad at about -25 dBFS, with five long strikes (0:00, 0:08, 0:12, 0:20, 0:24) that behave like tolls. The chord blocks are slow (G+B+D, then F#/D/B, then E/B/G): a hopeful lift that sinks. No pulse, so time is marked, not felt. This is the best-judged minute of the whole piece: the withheld pulse makes the arrival of the groove land.
- **0:28, the limp.** The bass comes in (about 65 dB over the intro noise floor) with a programmed groove whose kick sits on the off-beat quaver. A trudge that leans. With loop A withholding the tonic, you get weight and distance, not drive.
- **0:42-1:10, the voice enters** low and flat (f0 about 118-142 Hz), dry, with no relationship to the bar. It is the "newsreel in front of a march" effect. The voice floats free of the clock, which is why it sounds like a document rather than a vocal.
- **1:10-1:20 and 1:24.6-1:28.6, suspense by subtraction.** First the drums go (the bass carries on), then drums and bass are both out for about 4 s. You count silently. This is the most effective writing in the first half.
- **1:28.6, the drop, my first emotional peak.** The kick moves to every beat (a shift of one quaver, 0.25 s, with the grid unchanged), the snare takes alternate beats, the mix rises about 5 dB, and the speech is cut at that instant. It is an arrival, not a surprise. Relief, floor under the feet. It works because of the preceding gap and the sudden removal of the voice.
- **1:40.6, the second landing.** The bass is up 10 dB, and the mix is narrower and drier. Grip and pressure.
- **2:36.6-3:28.6, human absence.** The narration ends exactly at the loop change; the tonic now returns every 12 s; the wall blooms 8 to 10 dB at about 2:47-2:50, then brightens (4-10 kHz on the other stem up about 22 dB across 3:00-3:50, finishing at 3:48.6, but only +3 to +8 dB on the full mix). This is "something inhuman and swelling". I find it moving, if it is at all, for its patience. A critic could also call it dull: harmony is frozen, loudness flat at about -12 dBFS.
- **3:28.6-3:36.6, voice-like bursts return;** 3:36.6-4:36.6, the plateau (mix -10 to -11 dBFS, loudest 4:12-4:24). Heat rising over a machine. Whether it moves you depends on attention; it is trance by design. The high phrase at 4:29-4:36 is the one human-sounding gesture (a 26-45 cent wobble at 4-6 Hz, a specialist figure not re-measured).
- **4:36.6-5:00.6, pressure leaves.** A 2 dB step-down, then one bass stab and one dry call per bar (4:46.6-4:58.6). The voice has become a clock tick. Quiet and eerie.
- **5:00.9, the hard stop,** then three off-beat hits (about 5:02.3-5:02.85) and digital zero at about 5:04.24. A slammed door with no cadence. This is the second emotional peak and the most considered decision in the piece: it refuses the lift-and-fade finale that the preceding 90 seconds set you up for.

## 3. What is clever

1. **Tension by subtraction.** The stops before 1:28.6 (and the 5:00.9 stop) do the work a conventional arrangement would give to a riser or fill.
2. **The voice as a structural element.** Narration is cut at section edges (1:28.6, 1:56.5, 2:36), is absent for 52 s, and then returns transformed (higher, gated, syllabic), finally becoming one call per bar at the bar-phase of the kick-only beat. Words give way to rhythm as brightness rises.
3. **The kick shift of one quaver at 1:28.6** inside an irregular 4 s gap: it re-barres the groove without a tempo change.
4. **Escalation without a tune.** The build is brightness, automation, and layering of the same harmony. Restraint is the signature.
5. **The 3+3+3 hi-hat cross-rhythm (0.375 s)** under a rigid 4/4 (medium confidence: a specialist figure I did not re-run) gives a sense of forward motion against the steady kick.

## 4. What is weak, or risky

- **Harmonic stasis.** Two loops, no real dominant, no new chord from 2:36 to 4:36. Without the picture, the 3:36-4:36 plateau risks sameness. The evidence that the brightening is what listeners feel is low-medium; it is only +3 dB on the mix.
- **Flat loudness from 1:40.** The drums stay near -19 dB from 1:40 to 4:50 and the mix near -12 dBFS. Dynamics are narrow, and the limiter touches 0 dBFS (374 samples above 0.999, first at 1:32.1). The result is pressure, but also fatigue.
- **The second-half voice is ambiguous.** If these are chopped syllables, they may read as texture or filler; "no intelligible words recovered" means the late voice does not carry the meaning the early voice did. I cannot tell whether the source is human or triggered sample.
- **The intro is stronger than the finale's build.** The crest at 4:12-4:24 is a broad top, not a peak. A producer might say the piece peaks in impact at 1:28.6 and is mostly plateau thereafter; the real surprise comes at 5:00.9.
- **Fill positions are medium-low.** I would not make a structural claim from the 12 s fill spacing.

## 5. Genre lineage (resemblance only, medium-low)

Loop-built, instrumental electro/dance-rock with a cinematic frame, and a second frame that borrows post-rock's long additive crescendo without the dynamic crash. Narration against a steady machine pulse is a documentary-sampling idea; no measurement gives a date or a named precedent, and the instrument identity of the "wall" (guitar, synth, organ, choir) is undecidable. I do not claim any.

## 6. Music against picture

The sync data says the cuts are not beat-locked (37% within 80 ms of a beat against 43% by chance; Rayleigh p about 0.8) but sit within about 0.1 s of the main section edges (0:28.1, 1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6, 3:36.7). As a producer I read this as an edit made to a macro map (phrase or waveform), not to the groove. The music is therefore not a soundtrack that chases the cut; it is a clock the film obeys at the big joints. That suits the material, because nothing here is meant to dance.

## 7. Three questions for the other panellists

1. **To the film critic and the viewers:** at 1:28.6 the picture jumps from empty snow to a crowd on a bridge at the same instant the kick lands and the voice is cut. Does it read as designed, and does the crowd, as opposed to the sound, give that moment its relief? What would the moment feel like with the cut moved half a bar either side?
2. **To the viewers:** from 3:36 to 4:36 the harmony is frozen and loudness is flat, while the picture goes into close, handheld, bib-numbered backs and a few strained faces (4:10, 4:35). Does the picture supply the escalation the music withholds, or does the sameness of the music flatten the pack race? Where, specifically, do you feel the crest: 3:37 (the burn flash), 4:12-4:24, or 4:29-4:36?
3. **To everyone:** the ending is a hard slam at 5:00.9 with three off-beat hits, and the picture lags by 1 to 2 s with a slow fade over ice floes. Do you hear it as a deliberate refusal of closure, a thaw and dissolution, or a technical edit? Does the narration's dry factual register (date, 16 below, 575 riders) make the final emptiness colder, or more human?
