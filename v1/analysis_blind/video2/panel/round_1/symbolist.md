# The Symbolist (poet and symbolist panellist): Round 1 reading of video2

**Sources and status.** Blind mode. I read the checked music reading first, then the dossier, and opened these frames myself: 0:00, 0:41 (f_0042), 1:28 (f_0089), 2:36 (f_0157) and 4:58 (f_0299). Everything else is taken from the dossier and marked as such. Interpretation is marked [READING]; observation is marked [SEEN] or [MUSIC]. Times are m:ss. The narration's race name is inference from garbled transcripts and is not used as fact.

## 1. What I think it is about

**Surface.** A home-movie record of a hard Frisian winter, "De Winter van 1963 in Friesland" [SEEN 0:00], set to a machine-steady loop piece in B minor at 120 BPM with no cadence [MUSIC]. Over the middle a flat voice reads dates and statistics: the date, a distance, "16 degrees below", "575" and "9500" [dossier, medium to high confidence on the numbers].

**Beneath it [READING].** The piece is about how a person becomes a number and a community becomes a landscape. It is a film about *endurance as something that dissolves the self*. It shows cold (the world stops), then people arriving as figures on white, then a crowd, then a pack, then bibs without faces, then nobody, then water. The narrator's statistics are the same movement in words: a date, then a distance, then a head count. The music says the same thing in its own terms: the voice (the one human, individual element) is switched off at 2:36 and then returns at 3:28 as chopped, gated syllables, and finally as one dry call per bar. Words become texture, then a clock tick. A named person becomes a unit in a rhythm. That is the arc I hear and see, and the whole piece rests on it.

**The deeper subtext [READING].** It is about *memory as thaw*. Frost is the film's first image-family (0:02 to 0:24) and ice floes are its last (4:55 to 5:04); the whole work is the time between "frozen" and "melting". Home movies exist because something is about to be lost. A film that opens with a hand-lettered title board and closes on drifting broken ice says: the year is fixed here, in this frame, and the ground it stood on is already going. I mark this as my reading, not a finding. The data shows only the order of images, not the maker's intent.

## 2. How it makes me feel, and exactly where

I feel, in order: awe and hush, then a trudging dread, then relief that is almost embarrassing, then trance, then a strangely cold exhilaration, then a small cruelty at the end.

- **0:00 to 0:28, hushed awe.** Five long-ringing low strikes (0:00, 0:08, 0:12, 0:20, 0:24 [MUSIC]) fall over frost-laden branches (dossier timeline, 0:02 to 0:24). They work like tolls, like a church bell tolled over a place without people. The cuts at 0:08, 0:12 and 0:24 meet strikes within about 0.2 s [dossier]. The pad's flat-sixth colour (G over B minor [MUSIC]) gives the "hopeful lift that sinks": the chord asks and the ground does not answer.
- **0:28, the limp begins.** A hard cut to dark, shaky trunks arrives with the bass (0:06 s apart [dossier]). The kick sits on the off-beat quaver [MUSIC]. It is a march with a limp, walking into weather. The best "sound and picture are one body" moment in the first half.
- **0:41 to 0:48, the date over the graveyard.** I opened the frame [SEEN f_0042]: stones with railings, drifts, a line of single footprints running up the middle of the picture. A low, flat voice says "18 januari 1963" [words, high confidence]. A date spoken over graves is the film's one explicit statement about time and loss, and the filmmaker did not have to underline it. I cannot know whether the pairing was intended; the footage may simply be what was shot. But the effect is real: the date reads as an inscription.
- **1:24.6 to 1:28.6, the held breath.** About 4 s with no drums and no bass [MUSIC], over a bleached road [SEEN f_0089]: warm cream haze, a clump of three or four walkers far off, a round sign on a pole. This is the film's emptiest image and the music's emptiest moment, with the narrator naming the event (inference from the transcript only). It is the "waiting room" of the piece.
- **1:28.6, the drop.** Hard cut (measured at 88.55 by the dossier) from empty road to a crowd at a railing, the kick on every beat, the mix up about 5 dB, the voice cut off in the same instant [MUSIC, dossier]. This is where I felt the relief: the world is full of people again. It is the strongest sound and picture hinge in the first half, and it is a cut on a *section edge*, not on a beat. I like that. Ritual, not rhythm.
- **2:36.6, the voice leaves and the car drives away.** I saw the man at the lens at 2:36 [SEEN f_0157]: a bystander at the right edge, blurred, looking toward us while skaters in pink light go past. The next hard cut (2:36.6) is a small saloon car driving away down a frosted road [dossier; I did not open it]. The voice stops at the same bar. For the next 52 s there are no words [MUSIC]. The film goes back to frost, the first motif, and a vehicle replaces people. It feels like the narrator leaving the room. This is my favourite moment in the piece, and it is the cleverest: the human is withdrawn while the music "blooms" (+8 to 10 dB on the wall at about 2:47 to 2:50 [MUSIC]). Something inhuman grows in the gap.
- **3:36.6 to 4:36.6, inside the pack.** Faces withheld, bibs instead (201, 529, 157, 145, 170 [dossier]); a flash-burn at 3:37 on the plateau's first bar; the music crests (mix about -10 to -11 dBFS) with the top end opening about 22 dB on the other stem by 3:48.6 [MUSIC]. I find this a trance state, not a climax. It is effort without destination. The red hat returns four times (3:36, 4:06 to 4:19, 4:30 [dossier]); I read it as the single human thread in the swarm. A readable face appears at 4:10 and 4:35 [SEEN per dossier]: mouths open, breathing. The wordless high phrase at 4:29 to 4:36 [MUSIC] gets the nearest thing the piece has to a cry.
- **4:36 to 5:04, the thaw and the closed door.** Pressure leaves, the lane is long and still, a banner against cyan sky (4:45 to 4:48), then ducks and gulls on open water, then red-brown floes [SEEN f_0299: extreme close-up of broken ice in warm plum-brown water, no figures]. The last voice calls come one per bar, dry, like a clock, until the hard stop at 5:00.86 and three off-beat hits [MUSIC]. The picture then fades 1 to 2 s later [dossier]. Sound slams, image sighs. A door, then a dimmer. That gap between a hard sound and a soft picture is where the grief sits.

## 3. Motifs and visual rhymes I would argue for

1. **Frost, then thaw.** First branches (0:02 to 0:24), again after the voice leaves (2:37 to 2:44), last floes (4:55 to 5:04). The film's frame is *ice in all its states* [dossier motif table]. Ice is both the stage and the clock.
2. **Tiny dark figures on white**, early (0:36, 1:14 to 1:19, 1:25) and the lone tiny skaters in the still lane at 4:36. The small solitary scale is the film's "ground state"; the crowds are an interruption. The opening and closing stills rhyme.
3. **Text only in the film's own world** (title board, the lorry, the "K" sign, bibs [dossier]). Writing exists only on things people made. I read bibs as the film's late, sad echo of the title board: a name on a board becomes a number on a back.
4. **Red as the sole warm signal**: lamps (2:44), hats (3:36, 4:06 to 4:30), banner (4:45). Red is the human pulse in a white and blue world.
5. **Roads to a vanishing point** (1:02, 1:20, 2:37, 3:24, 4:36): the promise of a destination the film never shows. There is no finish line in the film, only a pack and then water.
6. **Whiteouts and light leaks** (1:20, 2:08, 2:54 to 3:00, 3:37, 4:11 to 4:14): the film itself failing and overexposing. I read the flares as a different kind of thaw: memory burning through the emulsion. Caution: I cannot tell stock decay from deliberate grading [dossier open question 1].

**Sound motifs.** The five tolls at the start answer the three off-beat hits at the end: bells in, slams out. Loop A withholds the tonic (E-G-A-D, no B until the loop's end [MUSIC]) and loop B brings it home every 12 s from 2:36.6. In my reading "home" arrives exactly when the people leave. That is an irony the music does not state and the picture does: a settled key is not the same as a settled life.

## 4. What is clever

- **Structure by subtraction.** The drum stop (from 1:10), the drums-and-bass stop (about 1:24.6 to 1:28.6) and the hard stop at 5:00.86 are the piece's three biggest gestures, and all three are *absences* [MUSIC]. The feeling is built from what is taken away.
- **Cutting to the form rather than the beat.** Sync data: cuts are not beat-locked (37% within 80 ms of a beat against 43% by chance; p about 0.8), but the main hard cuts sit within about 0.1 s of section edges (1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6, 3:36.7 [dossier]). I would call that editing like a listener, not a dancer.
- **The voice's arc**: narrator, then absence, then chops, then metronomic calls. The one thing that should be a person ends as the tick of a mechanism, a quiet comment on what the film does to its people.
- **Escalation by brightness**: nothing new is written, the same wall merely opens. It matches a film in which nothing new happens, only more of it, closer.
- **No cadence, no key change, no tempo change** [MUSIC]: a piece about an event that is endured, not won.

## 5. What is weak, or what I am not sure is clever

- **The plateau's sameness (3:36 to 4:36).** Sixty seconds of one loop, flat drums (near -19 dB), crest as plateau. If attention lapses, trance becomes monotony [MUSIC says this directly]. The picture's own change (faces at 4:10, wide and close alternation) saves it, but only just.
- **The narration's role.** A report-voice stating numbers over images that do not show the numbers (e.g. "200 km" over a long road, which is fine; "16 below" over a blue-violet frame, which may be coincidence [dossier]). It anchors but at times explains; the film is stronger where it is silent.
- **The ending slightly muddles its own gesture.** The hard audio stop is brilliant; the picture's slow fade 1 to 2 s later softens it. A reading in favour is that it is deliberate (sound slams, image sighs). A reading against is that the edit simply did not cut to the stop. The sync data cannot tell me which.
- **I cannot judge** whether the film is one event, one roll, one operator, or a compilation. The dossier lists the evidence as ambiguous, and my reading of "one winter, one unnamed crowd" rests on that ambiguity being generous.
- **Sound's "feeling" claims** (hypnotic, yearning) are inference from measurements by a deaf pipeline. I trust the structure more than the adjectives.

## 6. Three questions for the other panellists

1. **The voice.** The narration ends exactly at the loop change (2:36) and the chops arrive 52 s later. Is the voice's gradual change from *speech* to *texture* to *clock-tick* a deliberate metaphor for people becoming numbers, or is it just how a producer treats a sample bank? What in the music (not the picture) would let you decide? (For the music critic.)
2. **The edit.** The hard cuts miss the beat and hit the section edges. A film critic should tell me: is that the mark of someone cutting to a waveform or cue list, or of one editor reacting to phrase and feeling? And does the *lack* of beat-lock make the footage feel archival (the world carrying on regardless) or merely careless? (For the film critic.)
3. **The ending.** After 4:36 the film leaves the race and ends on water and floes with no finish line, no winner, no faces. Does a first-time viewer feel that as grief, release or just an anticlimax? Which of the last frames (the banner at 4:45, the ducks at 4:52, the floes at 4:55 to 5:00) carries the weight, and does the picture's fade after the audio slam help or hurt? (For the first-timer.)
