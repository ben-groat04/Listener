## Answers to the questions put to me

**Symbolist, Q3: the ending, grief, release or anticlimax; which frame carries it; does the fade help?**
Honestly: neither grief nor anticlimax, more a held breath let out slowly. Walking through the last frames:
- 4:45, the flag [SEEN f_0286]: a red-and-white cloth on a pole against hard cyan sky, with rigging lines. It is the one jolt of saturated colour and it reads as "event, occasion". It is a cheerful frame, and what is odd is that it arrives after the pack and is not followed by any finish.
- 4:52, the ducks [SEEN f_0293]: water full of ducks and swans, with two round white burn holes in the emulsion at upper right. Here the film itself is damaged on screen. I did not expect that and it is oddly the most touching frame to me, because the world has gone back to birds while the film stock is visibly failing.
- 4:55, ducks standing on the ice [SEEN f_0296] and 5:00, floes on mauve water [SEEN f_0300]: the movement from "ice you can stand on" to "ice breaking up" is the thaw, and it is the frame that carries the weight. Pretty, empty, no scale.
So my answer is release, with sadness at its edge. I would pick 4:52 to 5:00 as a unit, not one frame. On the fade: the music stops first and the picture drifts on for 1 to 2 s [DATA]. As a viewer it helped me: the silence has a picture in it. I agree with the film critic that a fade is a stock way to end, but I did not notice it as a cliché; I noticed the quiet. I was wrong in round 1 to call it "a door closing"; a door shuts, and this does not. It is more like a tide going out.
I also have to concede to the sceptic that a loop-built track has to stop somehow (sceptic 4.3). My feeling is real, but I should not claim the track "refuses" anything.

**Insider, Q3 (and music critic Q2): 3:36 to 4:36, exhilaration or oppression? where does it tip, and does the picture supply the escalation the music withholds?**
Exhilaration first, oppression second. At 4:05 [SEEN f_0246] a skater in a stripy jacket and bobble hat is bent double, camera low and tilted over blue-lilac ice; that is pure motion and I liked it. At 4:10 [SEEN f_0251] the camera is in front of the pack and for the first time a bunch of faces comes straight at the lens, one mouth open, a yellow-and-black jacket in the group. That is where it tipped for me, towards pressure: the pack is close and coming. So the picture does supply the escalation: from backs and bibs (3:26 to 3:36) to a face-on rush (4:10). Where I feel the crest is 4:10 to 4:24, which matches the music critic's 4:12 to 4:24 loudness peak [DATA]; I did not feel the 3:37 flash as a crest, more as a door opening onto the plateau. The sameness of the music did not flatten it for me once the picture was this close, which revises my round 1 worry that I would drift through it. I would still drift in 3:36 to 4:00, where it is mostly backs.

**Music critic, Q1: does 1:28.6 read as designed, and what would half a bar either side feel like?**
It read as designed. I cannot test a half-bar shift, but my guess [GUESS] is that early would feel like a mistake (the cut arriving into silence) and late would feel like the beat landing on the old picture and the new one arriving as an afterthought. What I felt was the bridge crowd arriving with the kick and the narration gone. Crowd and sound both give the relief; I think the crowd matters more, since I would feel less relief if the same kick landed on more empty road.

**Sceptic, Q3: remove "funerary", "closed door", "heat rising", "unresolved". What is left?**
Fair test. Without the glosses, an ordinary viewer notices: (1) a hush, then a limping beat and shaky dark trees at 0:28, which is a noticeable change; (2) a pause where drums go at 1:24.6 to 1:28.6 and then a lively drop with people; (3) at 2:36 the voice stops and the picture goes quiet again; (4) a long loud sameness with close skaters; (5) a sudden stop and a pretty empty last image. That is already enough to feel something; the adjectives only name it. I concede "funerary" is mine too: I wrote "faintly funereal" in round 1 for 0:28 to 0:48. What I actually saw was snow, stones and a date, and the funerary feeling is partly because I know graveyards, not because the film says it.

## Agreements, pushbacks, concessions

- **Agree with the sceptic on the 12 s lattice (2:36.6 to 4:00, seven of seven within 0.16 s) being the strongest finding on the table.** I could not check it myself, but I can say what it would mean to a viewer: I do not feel a 12 s grid, yet the pack section never felt random to me in the way the bar-level cut data would suggest. That is consistent with the sceptic's reading that cuts follow phrases, not beats. Where I push back: the sceptic says the drift after 4:12 might just be the editor loosening. From the frames, 4:10 is exactly where the footage turns from backs to faces. I suspect the editor let the footage lead there [GUESS]. That makes "loosening" an editorial choice, not tiredness.
- **Push back on the sceptic's point 4.1 ("faces withheld is just camera position").** True as far as it goes, and I half-agree: the film critic's "withholding as the central idea" is stronger than the evidence. But a camera behind the pack for a whole minute and then a face-on shot at 4:10 is an editing choice about order, whatever the camera did. The order is what a viewer feels. So withholding is a fact about the cut, even if not about the shoot. I would say "ordered", not "withheld".
- **Agree with the film critic that the middle (1:40 to 3:28) is where the film sags.** I felt it too, but I disagree that the picture has nothing going for it: the frame at 2:36 [SEEN f_0157] has two skaters, a man at the right edge looking at the lens, and a church tower on the horizon. That glance at the camera is the first moment anyone acknowledges us. I now think the dossier and film critic undersell 2:36 to 3:28 as a place of small human contact, not only "music grows without people".
- **Concede to the sceptic (4.9)** that "ends exactly at the loop change" overstates 0.8 s. I quoted the 2:36.6 cut as "exactly" in my round 1 clever list too; the picture cut is the thing that is 0.00 s [DATA], and the voice is 0.8 s earlier.
- **Concede to the symbolist's point on numbers** (voice numbers, bib numbers) more than I should have: my round 1 "nice quiet echo" is a viewer's pattern-spotting. The bibs I saw (440, 201, 504) are not shown to match the spoken entry numbers. I withdraw it as evidence.
- **Agree with the symbolist** that the five bell-like strikes at the start and three off-beat hits at the end make a bracket. I did not notice it as a listener, but I notice it now, and the fact that it is findable is itself something.

## The thread I want to develop: the film is damaged at the end on purpose, or it is not, and it matters

The burn holes at 4:52 [SEEN f_0293] made me look again at the other flares. I counted the whiteouts the symbolist lists (1:20, 2:08, 2:54 to 3:00, 3:37, 4:11 to 4:14). They cluster in two places: around the section changes (3:37) and around where the film turns to faces (4:11). Early in the piece there are none that I saw in the opening frost. If the flares were random decay, I would expect them spread evenly. If an editor chose shots with flares to sit at transitions, that is craft. I cannot tell which from the data, and that is exactly the sceptic's caution about stock decay versus grading. But as a viewer I can say the flares at 3:37 and 4:11 help: they feel like the film overheating at the moment the music is loudest, and the final flare at 4:52 feels like the film wearing out as the music has ended. A viewer takes that as meaning whether or not anyone intended it. That is the gap between what is "earned by the data" and what is "supplied by us", and I think a decent human reading has to be allowed to sit in that gap as long as it is labelled as a reading.
