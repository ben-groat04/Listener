## 1. First, a correction to my own round 1 (and a stronger result)

I said seven of seven cuts sat on a 12 s lattice from 2:36.6. I reran it properly, because I had chosen the lattice after seeing the music, which is a researcher degree of freedom.

- Extended window: 2:36.6, 2:48.6, 3:00.6, 3:12.6, 3:24.6, 3:36.6, 3:48.6, 4:00.6 are **eight of eight** within 0.16 s of a cut (offsets 0.00, -0.04, -0.08, -0.16, 0.00, +0.12, -0.08, 0.00 s) [MEASURED]. I missed 4:00.6 last time.
- Phase test: I slid the same 12 s lattice over 120 phases (0.1 s steps) across the same 84 s window. Mean hits 0.85 of 8; only **one** phase in 120 reaches 7 or more, and it is the music's own. A single pre-registered phase would give about 1e-8; even with the 120 phases and the free choice of period as generous correction, this is not chance [MEASURED].
- But the lattice does **not** extend backward: 2:24.6 is 1.3 s from the nearest cut, 2:12.6 is 4.9 s, 2:00.6 is 3.8 s, and so on [MEASURED]. Forward it ends: 4:12.6 is 0.76 s off, 4:24.6 0.68, 4:36.6 0.60, 4:48.6 0.36. So the claim is narrow: **the editor cut to the loop-B cell grid for exactly the first 84 s of loop B (2:36.6 to 4:00.6), then let go.** That is more specific than the dossier's "cuts at section edges" and more specific than my round 1 "kept to the cell for 90 s".
- Consequence for the film critic and the first-timer, who both said the edit follows "phrase, not beat": it follows the **12 s phrase** hard, in one stretch, and the **bar** (2 s) not at all (Rayleigh p 0.78 [sync.json]). Cutting only on every sixth bar and nowhere between is what a person does with a waveform or a loop-length marker in the timeline, not by feeling the groove. I now hold that as probable [SPEC, but with the numbers behind it].

This answers my own question 1 partly: the drift after 4:12 is not something I can read as meaning. The pack-race shots from 4:00 onward are the fastest in the film, and a person cutting fast whip-pans loses a 12 s habit simply by having more cuts. That is the unromantic reading and I prefer it.

## 2. Answers to questions put to me or to "everyone"

**Insider Q1 (empty picture while the narrator names the event 1:20-1:28.6; crowd the instant the voice stops: irony or convenience?).** Evidence for plain convenience: the speech stem is silent after 1:10.5 by the music reading, and the dossier's transcripts put the "Elfstedentocht"-like line at 1:24-1:28, which is the very last speech before the stop. A narrator's final sentence ending as the section ends is what you would get by cutting speech to the song's section map. Evidence for design: the cut at 1:28.6 is missing from `shots.json` yet is real (I confirmed in round 1 with f_0089 and f_0090), and drums and bass leave 4 s ahead of it; subtracting the rhythm section is a deliberate arrangement act. So: the *music* gesture is clearly designed; the *irony of a crowd overrunning the voice* is the viewer's. I give the irony reading low confidence, and note it only works if the line really names the race, which is hearsay from garbled transcripts.

**Music critic Q1 / Film critic Q1 (cut half a bar either side).** I cannot test it by ear. What I can say: the cut sits 0.05 s from the section edge, but the picture has no beat lock otherwise, so the effect depends on only this one coincidence. Moved half a bar (1 s) it would land on the held silence and read as a mistimed jump. That is a prediction, not an observation.

**Music critic Q2 and everyone (where is the crest: 3:37, 4:12-4:24, 4:29-4:36?).** Using the cut data: the 3:36.7 burn flash is a cut on the lattice (+0.12 s), so it is the picture's *most structurally supported* moment. The loudest music (4:12-4:24 [MUSIC]) falls exactly where the lattice has already loosened (4:12.6 is 0.76 s off). So **the picture's formal climax (3:36.7) and the music's loudest stretch (4:12) are 36 s apart.** Nobody said this plainly. The "crest" is therefore two different things in the two channels, and talk of the picture "supplying the escalation" glosses over that.

**Film critic Q3, Insider Q2, Music critic Q3, Symbolist Q3, my own Q3 (the ending: elegy or shrug?).** My answer: shrug is the cheaper hypothesis. A reel of one skating event has to end somewhere; floes are the last thing filmed, and the hard stop is how loop-built tracks end. The fade lagging by 1 to 2 s (black at 5:04, last frame 5:05.9 [SEEN by dossier and the Insider]) is also what a fade-out at a reel's end looks like, and music reading's digital zero at 5:04.24 matches black at about 5:04, so the picture and the audio actually **reach silence and black at the same instant**. Nobody has put that on the table: the "sound slams, image sighs" story (symbolist) is true of the 5:00.9 hit, but the two channels finish together within about 0.2 s. That is a craft point in favour of an edit that was timed, and I concede it to the "deliberate" camp. The *meaning* (thaw, grief) I leave as unsupported.

**First-timer Q3 (hypnotic or repetitive: what decides?).** The data says the loudness is flat within about 2 dB from 1:40 and the drums sit near -19 dB [MUSIC]. What varies is the picture: the cut-rate peaks at about 4 per 10 s in both a mid-loudness and a loud stretch (my round 1). The decider, I would argue, is shot-rate and camera proximity, not the sound.

## 5. The most interesting thread, developed: two kinds of "structure" in the edit

Putting the new measurement next to the old ones, the edit has three layers:
- **Macro edges** (0:28, 1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6, 3:36.7): within 0.0 to 0.12 s. Probably worked from a map of the song.
- **Loop-B cell lattice** (2:36.6 to 4:00.6): eight of eight at 12 s spacing, abandoned after.
- **Everything else**: no relation (bar phase random, motion against onsets about zero).
A maker with a sense of groove would show bar or beat locking at least sometimes; this maker shows nothing at the 2 s or 0.5 s level, then something exacting at 12 s and above. That pattern is typical of working against a zoomed-out waveform view or a marker track. So the piece is *mechanically organised but not rhythmically felt*, which is a better characterisation of the "slightly slippery" feeling the film critic reported than "deliberate chaos against order". It also cuts against reading too much into the colour drifts and fades: the editor was minutely careful about *when the picture changes* and, as far as the data shows, about nothing else.

Caveat: a loop-length lattice might also arise if the **footage** was cut by someone using the music's own cell as a grid, which says nothing about whether the cuts were "to the music" first. Direction of causation is unknowable here.
