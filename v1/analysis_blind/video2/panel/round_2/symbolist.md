## 2. Pushing back

**The Sceptic's "a loop has to stop somehow" (round 1 §4.3).** True, and I accept it as the plain explanation. But the choice among ways to stop is still a choice. A loop can fade, or tail on a reverb, or resolve to the tonic, which loop B has offered every 12 s since 2:36.6 [MUSIC]. The piece instead stops on the digital cut with three off-beat hits and then, per the music reading, digital zero at about 5:04.24. The Sceptic says "ordinary", the music critic says "most considered decision in the piece". I side with the music critic: a hard stop is ordinary in loop-based tracks, but over this picture (a slow fade 1 to 2 s later) it creates a gap between ear and eye that the Sceptic's account does not address. I keep that gap as the ending's real subject, and I mark it [READING].

**The Sceptic's "colour is not scored" (r about 0.03 to 0.2 [sync.json]).** Agreed, and it strengthens something I only half-said: the film's colour is the footage's own drift (pink, lilac, cream). My round 1 claim that flares are "memory burning through the emulsion" was a metaphor and should be read as one. But f_0158 [SEEN] is useful here. At 2:37 the car shot has three bright, round, hole-like blotches on the right of the frame, which look like damage to the film itself (burn, punch, or a bad splice; I cannot say which). The first cut after the voice leaves is therefore the most *damaged-looking* picture I have opened. That is observation. That the picture is hurt at the moment the narrator goes is chance as far as the data can say; it is also exactly the kind of chance a poet is paid to notice.

## 3. Answering the Insider and the Film critic

**Insider: "Is the thaw a statement or the last footage on the reel?"** (round 1, question 2.) I cannot separate them from the data, and it is honest to say so. What can be said: the music's final 28 s (4:36 to 5:04) give the pack a long relaxation, and the picture follows with a still lane, a banner, then water, floes and, at 4:51 [SEEN], a pair of legs on scored ice. That last image is more important than I allowed. The ice at f_0291 is covered in crossing skate marks. Floes at 4:55 to 5:00 are *broken* ice. So the closing images are ice as a record of passage (the scoring) and then ice as something coming apart. The race leaves its trace and the trace is already going. [READING] I now think my "frost to thaw" frame is better stated as "ice as page": the surface is written on (title board, bibs, skate tracks) and the writing does not last.

**Film critic: "Is the lack of beat-lock archival (the world carrying on) or careless?"** (round 1, question to me.) I put the question; now I answer it from the Sceptic's measurements. Cuts are not beat-locked (37% within 80 ms of a beat against 43% by chance) but seven of seven fall within 0.16 s of a 12 s lattice from 2:36.6, then drift [MEASURED by the Sceptic]. That is neither careless nor beat-driven. It matches an editor who cut to the *phrase*. For a symbolist this is the most interesting fact on the table: the pictures obey the music's *breath* (12 s, six bars), and ignore its *pulse* (2 s bars, 0.5 s beats). The footage keeps its own, human, irregular rhythm underneath a long, patient musical breath. Pulse is the machine; breath is the body. [READING] That is why the film feels like labour rather than a dance.

## 4. The thread I want to develop: pulse versus breath, and who the "numbers" are

Round 1 gave me "people become numbers". The Sceptic and the First-timer sharpen it. The First-timer noticed that the voice's numbers match numbers on bibs (575, 9500; 145, 201, 529...). I opened f_0212 [SEEN] and the bib is a big, hand-painted, serifless "145", legible from behind. The narrator speaks 575 and 9500 (high-to-medium confidence [words]); the bibs show three-digit numbers. Those are not the same numbers and I must not claim the narration "names" the skaters; it counts them. What I can honestly say is that the picture shows a number as *an identity worn on the back*, and the narration gives numbers as *a population*. One number is a person; one number is a crowd. [READING] The film lets these two kinds of number sit side by side without comment, and the voice that could reconcile them is gone from 2:36.

Then the music. The voice's three states are: narrator (0:41 to 2:36), silence (2:36 to 3:28), chops then calls (3:28 to 5:00) [MUSIC]. The Sceptic warns that the chops may not be a voice at all (medium-low [MUSIC]) and that Whisper strings are hallucination. I accept that completely: my round 1 phrase "a named person becomes a unit in a rhythm" asserted a human origin the data does not support. Revised, the defensible claim is smaller and, I think, truer: after the speaking voice leaves, *something voice-shaped* occupies the same spectral role, and by the end it is regular as a clock (one call per bar from 4:46.6 [MUSIC]). Whether it is a human sample or a synth patch, the effect of a voice-like thing becoming periodic stands as a hearing, not as a fact about the source.

## 5. Where I think the panel is converging, and where it is not

- **Agreed by all six** (as I read them): the edit follows the music's large form, not its beat; the best single moment is the 1:24.6 to 1:28.6 subtraction then drop; the hard stop at 5:00.86 and the lagging fade are the ending's mechanism.
- **Not agreed**: whether the ending *means* anything. The Sceptic says a loop must stop; the music critic says the stop is deliberate; the Insider asks grief or machine switching off. My reply: both can be true. A mechanism stopping is the only way a machine can grieve, and a viewer who has heard 120 BPM unbroken for five minutes will feel the stop as an event whatever the author intended. I do not need intent to claim effect, but I should always mark which I am claiming.
- **Still open**: the Film critic's challenge to strip "funerary", "closed door", "heat rising" and "unresolved". I did it for my own list. Left standing: five tolls then a groove (0:28); a four-second hollow (1:24.6) then a drop; 52 s without a voice, then a voice-like texture; a hard stop with a soft picture. These are structural and I think a first-time viewer notices them without my adjectives. What goes is the afterglow of the words.
