## The thread I want to develop: the film changes who is speaking

Putting the panel's findings side by side gives something I did not see alone. In the first half a person speaks (flat, dry, reading out a date, a distance, a temperature) and the picture follows him, loosely, event by event. From 2:36 the narrator is gone for 52 s, and then something voice-like returns as chops, which become one call per bar by 4:46.6 [DATA, music reading]. The picture follows the same road: a man in a long coat and a woman at 1:43 to 3:00, then bibs, then a face at 4:10, then ducks and floes with no people at all (4:49 onward). Speech to tick, face to number, number to ice: the film hands the telling from a person to a machine, and then to the weather. I cannot prove the editor meant this, and the sceptic is right that each piece has a plainer explanation (a loop machine, a camera behind the pack, a thaw at the end of a roll). But the plain explanations all point the same way, and a viewer feels the direction whether or not anyone planned it. That is the point where, in my reading, the piece moves from "a record" to "a feeling".

## Settled reading, in five sentences

1. This is a five-minute reel of cold-winter archive footage, set to a steady, repeating electronic piece in a minor key, and it works best as a slow rise in pressure rather than a story. 2. The narrator gives the facts over a limping beat; the silence at 1:24.6 to 1:28.6 and the crowd landing with the kick at 1:28.6 are the first real payoff, and the edit follows the music's big sections and not its beat. 3. From 2:36 the voice leaves and the film gets quicker and more anonymous (bibs, backs), then at 4:10 to 4:24, the loudest stretch, faces turn towards us. 4. The ending is a hard sound stop at 5:00.9 and a quiet picture of ice floes, which I take as relief tinged with sadness, with the film's own damage visible at 4:52. 5. I would tell a friend: watch it for 1:24 to 1:40 and 4:05 to 4:30, put up with the middle, and notice that the music is a machine while the picture is the one that breathes.
