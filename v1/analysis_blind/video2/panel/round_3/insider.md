## 4. Developing the thread: two economies of attention

The most interesting thing I can add is what the world looks like depending on which half you watch.

1. **0:00 to 2:36 is a world seen from outside.** Wide shots, still camera, tiny figures on white, a spoken date and distance. Evidence of period and place: hand-lettered title board "DE WINTER VAN 1963 IN FRIESLAND" [SEEN 0:00], Dutch speech, a lorry with "...-SNEEK" lettering [SEEN f_0092, 1:31, tail only legible], saloon cars of a mid-century shape and bundled people with skates [SEEN 2:05; dossier], horse-less roads, farmhouses, a windmill [SEEN 1:40]. The inference "a Dutch-speaking, rural-to-small-town society organising a mass winter event, with cars present but still few" rests on those and is medium to high.
2. **2:36 to 4:36 is a world seen from inside.** Same culture, but now the camera is a participant: bibs (201, 529, 157), woollen hats, baggy trousers, hands clasped behind the back, a stick or pole carried (not identified), helpers in uniform coats at 4:28 [dossier]. Numbers are on bodies instead of in a voiceover. The cultural fact visible here is an *organised* leisure culture: numbered entrants, marshalled lanes, a swept course [SEEN 3:26], a tractor with a scraper on the ice (2:54, dossier).
3. **4:36 to 5:04 leaves the culture altogether**: a pole with a flag, then ducks, then floes. There is no finish, no winner, no crowd. I can state the observation and one inference: *the edit gives the event no ending of its own*. Whether that reads as thaw, closure or just the last roll is the Sceptic's open question, and I leave it open.

The point for a human reader: a viewer from the period would recognise every object here as ordinary; a viewer now recognises them as **evidence of a shared public activity that needed cold, trust and thousands of strangers**. Nothing in the frames says this was rare, so that last clause is my gloss and is marked as such [SPEC].

## 5. Settled reading (5 sentences)

1. This is a Dutch-language record of one very cold January day in 1963 in Friesland, shot on small-gauge colour film by someone who also lettered the title board, over which a modern loop-machine track in B minor at 120 BPM has been laid, with a placed, flat voice reading dates, distances, temperature and entry numbers. 2. The picture is not locked to the beat but is cut to the music's large sections to within about 0.1 s (1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6, 3:36.7), and for exactly 84 s (2:36.6 to 4:00.6) to its 12 s cell, so the editor listened to the form and let go of it afterwards. 3. The film moves from empty snow, to named facts, to a crowd, to a pack seen from inside by bibs and backs, to water and floes with no finish, which makes an event of ordinary participants the subject and leaves out any winner. 4. The feeling of hush, then pressure, then thaw comes largely from the music and the edit; the footage mostly permits it rather than carries it, and I mark "elegy" as our reading, not a property of the images. 5. The ending is a hard sound stop at 5:00.86 over an unchanged picture, followed by a fade that reaches black at about the same moment as digital zero (5:04), which I now treat as a single closing gesture of uncertain intent.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 1:24.6 to 1:28.6.** The drums and bass leave, the narrator is naming the event over an almost empty snow road with four walkers approaching, and at 1:28.6 the band lands, the speech is gated off (vocal stem -34 dB to -74 dB [MUSIC]), and the picture cuts to a crowd at a bridge rail [SEEN f_0092]. It holds everything the piece does: facts then bodies, subtraction then arrival, and a cut that obeys the music's structure rather than its beat.
