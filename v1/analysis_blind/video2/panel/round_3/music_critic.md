So the answer is yes: the fills are real in this file and they sit on the lattice. That gives the editor a visible, and audible, cue at every one of the eight lattice cuts. Two cautions. First, this stem file's absolute levels (about -47 dB mean over 2:30-3:50) do not match the reading's "drums near -19 dB", so the file is on a different scale or the demucs drum stem is mostly gated between accents; I trust the periodicity, not the levels. Second, per-second resolution cannot say whether the accent falls just before or just on the cell start. Now the second half of the Sceptic's question: does anything change at 4:08-4:12? Nothing I can find in the drum accents: the 12 s accents carry straight on through 4:12, 4:24, 4:36 and 4:48 [MEASURED], and the bass and "other" stems are within a few dB of their earlier values across 4:00-4:20. ## 3. The thread I want to develop: the form is subtractive, and the edit is the one that listens to the loop

1. The music's strongest decisions are removals: drums out 1:10, drums and bass out 1:24.6-1:28.6, narrator out 2:36, voice out on the drop, machine out at 5:00.86. Adding is by brightness and automation, not tune or new drums (the full mix moves +3 to +8 dB in the top band over 50 s).
2. The editor follows the *structure* (0:28, 1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6, 3:36.6 within about 0.1 s; then 8 of 8 on the 12 s cell with a drum accent at each), but does not follow the *event* of the stops (1:10, 1:24.6) and not the beat (37% vs 43% chance). One reading, consistent with everything: the picture was cut by someone who marked where cells begin and the groove arrives, not where it stops. The result is a film that arrives with the music and never hesitates with it.
3. The fills (drum accents every 12 s) and the cuts line up for 1:20 of the film, so the viewer who watches 2:36-4:00 receives a double signal: a new shot and a drum accent on the same second, every 12 s, without any change in tune. That is the only place where the track's pulse and the picture's pulse share a clock. As producer I would call that the film's real rhythm section, and it is at the very time the track is least eventful. It is a quiet, effective way to make repetition feel directed.

I treat (2) as measured and (3) as my interpretation. I cannot decide whether the editor heard these accents or whether the lattice came from a loop-length marker; the caveat about direction of causation stands.
