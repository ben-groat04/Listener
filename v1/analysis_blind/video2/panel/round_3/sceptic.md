## 1. Questions put to me, answered

**Insider Q1: does the editor's lattice exist in loop A (28 s cell from 0:28.14)?** No [MEASURED]. Grid points 0:28.1, 0:56.1, 1:24.1, 1:52.1, 2:20.1 are 0.06, 0.70, 2.02, 1.42 and 3.14 s from the nearest cut (1:24.1 and 2:20.1 are the clearest misses; 1:28.6 is 4.5 s from 1:24.1, as the insider said). Using a 14 s half-cell gives 0.98, 0.70, 0.94, 2.02, 0.46, 1.42, 1.58 and 3.14 s: nothing. Sliding a 12 s grid through 0:28 to 2:36 at 120 phases, the best any phase does is 3 hits of 10 and typically 0 or 1; the music's own phase gets 2. So the answer to the insider's second half is yes: **the lattice is a property of the second half's edit, not of the whole film.** The first half is cut to section edges and to the narration's content (see 1:28.6); the cell grid only appears when the voice leaves at 2:36.6. That fits the film critic's point that the method of cutting changes at 2:36.6, and I now accept that as the best structural finding of the panel.

**Music critic Q1: do the fills fall on the lattice cuts?** Partly [MEASURED]. Distances from fill to nearest cut: 2:48.6 0.04 s, 3:00.6 0.08 s (both are lattice points), 2:36.6 0.00 s, but 2:24 is 0.72 s off and 2:08 is 0.28 s off (before the lattice begins). The 2:08 coincidence is real but isolated; the music reading rates fill positions medium-low, so I put no weight on it. What does the lattice and fill coincidence mean? Fills in a 12 s cell sit at the cell turnover, which is where the lattice sits; they are the same landmark seen twice, not two pieces of evidence. I found nothing musical at about 4:08 to 4:12 (the reading lists no event there), which is where the music critic's own explanation applies: the loop turnover is least audible when the mix is densest.

**First-timer Q and Film critic: does the drift begin at the faces, and is it the editor following the material?** Here I have to correct my own round 2, and the correction cuts against all of us. The lattice point at 4:12.6 is 0.76 s from the nearest cut because the detector lists one cut at 4:11.8 and the next at 4:20.0, an **8.2 s gap**. I opened frames across it [SEEN]: 4:13 and 4:15 are a wide, bleached view of skaters approaching along a swept strip; 4:17 is a close, tilted shot of a back and a pale stick; 4:19 is a blurred close-up with a farmhouse behind; 4:21 is a blue-toned wide shot of skaters walking toward us. Cuts are clearly missing from the list between 4:15 and 4:19 (the dossier already says the detector under-splits fast pack shots). So **the drift after 4:12 is at least partly a detection artefact, not evidence that the editor loosened, got tired or followed faces.** I measured, I did not re-cut: the 4:24.6, 4:36.6 and 4:48.6 misses (0.68, 0.60, 0.36 s) are small enough that a missed or slightly mislocated cut explains them. The honest statement: **the lattice is solid for eight points, and after that the data cannot say whether it holds.** I withdraw "the editor let go" and ask the film critic and first-timer to drop "loosening deliberately" too. That also takes the air out of the film critic's footage-led explanation: no drift to explain.

**Film critic and insider Q: audio digital zero (5:04.24) and picture black (about 5:04).** I raised this myself; I do not think it decides intent either way. A default fade to black over a final shot would end at black whatever the sound did. What I will keep is the minimal fact that the two channels finish together within about 0.2 s, and that the sound's own last event (5:02.85) falls inside the picture's fade. That is compatible with a planned landing and with a tool default. I rate it 50/50, which is a real change from my round 1, where I treated the end as an accident of the reel.

**Symbolist Q1 (does the drift track music)?** See above: no, and probably not a drift at all.

## 2. Where I agree and where I push back

- **Agree with the music critic** that the sound alone does not choose between grief and shrug. A switch-off with a decorated tail (three off-beat hits, then a tail below -45 dBFS) has none of elegy's cues. The viewer supplies the rest, and I would add that the viewer supplies it from the picture, which at 5:00 shows no people.
- **Agree with the insider** that the narrator's 575 racers to about 9,500 tourers (numbers reliable in the transcripts) makes an anonymous mass close to the event's own demographic, and that this is a better explanation for the bibs and backs than "withheld faces". Camera position (f_0206-type following shots on a swept strip) does the rest. I continue to prefer "geometry" to "withholding", and I accept the first-timer's amendment that the film's *order* (a minute of backs, then faces) is a fact about the cut even if not about the shoot.
- **Push back on the insider's "an editor of the period would end on the finish".** This presumes the cut is a period edit. The data show a modern loop-machine track fitted to the reel with measured, grid-exact cuts; there is no sign of a 1963 edit at all. The ending is the end of the *new* piece, whose author we can say nothing about. I do not accept authorial intent about "what the event was for".
- **Push back on "the fade is the film's sigh"** (first-timer tide-going-out): a pleasant image, my mind accepts it as the viewer's feeling [SPEC], but not as a property of the file.
- **Concede to the film critic and music critic on design.** My round 1 stance that the picture is "competent compilation" with no sign of a planned relationship is wrong: eight of eight lattice hits, 0.0 to 0.12 s section edges, and picture and sound black/zero together are too many coincidences. The edit is designed against the **macro** structure of the music. What I still deny is any evidence of designed *feel* at beat, bar or colour level (Rayleigh p about 0.8; warmth r about 0.2; brightness r about 0.03).
- **Concede to the symbolist** that "ritual, not rhythm" is a fair description of the effect, provided it is labelled effect.
- **Remaining over-reading, in order of how often I saw it:** (1) "exactly" for the voice exit (0.8 s); (2) the brightening as a hard fact (+3 dB in the mix, medium-low); (3) colour as instrument; (4) the chops as crowd or ghost (no evidence, the music critic puts it at medium-low); (5) 1960s provenance of the narration (no data).

## 3. The thread I would develop: a film cut to a map, felt by an audience that has no map

The best-supported structure is a two-regime edit. Before 2:36.6, cuts meet section edges (0:28.1, 1:28.6, 1:40.6: 0.05 to 0.06 s) and content (empty road to crowd). From 2:36.6 to 4:00.6 cuts meet the 12 s cell on every boundary. Between these, and after, the data are silent or too coarse. A first-time viewer cannot count to 12, yet reports exactly what that structure would produce: a sense of the picture being "carried" in the middle, a noticeable change at 0:28, and a noticeable change at 1:28.6. So the viewers' feelings are not wrong; they are an outcome of a macro-level edit. My earlier slogan, "mechanically organised but not rhythmically felt", survives in a narrower form: **organised at 6 bars and above, indifferent at 1 bar and below**. Whether that is what a person working from a waveform does or what a loop-grid in a timeline does, the data cannot say.

## 4. Settled reading, in five sentences

1. This is a piece of five-odd minutes in which a steady 120 BPM loop machine carries a silent-looking archival reel of a Frisian winter skating event (title board, 1963), with a flat Dutch narration of date, distance, temperature and entry numbers (0:42 to 2:36) that is the only part of the sound which tells us what we see.
2. The edit is not locked to the beat, but is firmly locked to the music's large structure: section edges to about 0.1 s, and (2:36.6 to 4:00.6) every 12 s cell boundary, an effect a viewer feels as being carried even if they cannot count it.
3. The story is hush, announcement, crowd, mass, pack, thaw, with the pack race (3:36 to 4:36) as the loud, close, handheld centre and an emptiness at both ends; most of the elegy and dignity words are the viewers' and mine, not the file's.
4. Faces are scarce because of camera position, anonymity is almost the event's own demographic, and the colour drifts, flares and fades are best treated as stock or grading unless shown otherwise.
5. The end is a hard sound stop (5:00.86) with three late hits, then picture and sound reach black and digital zero together (about 5:04), which is the most carefully landed moment of the piece, and the reading of it as grief or as a mechanism switching off belongs to the viewer.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 1:28.6.** Four seconds of near-silence over an empty snow road, speech cut off, and then a hard cut to a crowd of coated people at a bridge on the instant the kick lands (picture 88.55 s, music 88.6 s [MEASURED, f_0089 and f_0090 SEEN]): emptiness to bodies, distance to proximity, and the one place where the music's design, the picture's design and the viewer's feeling are all demonstrably the same event.
