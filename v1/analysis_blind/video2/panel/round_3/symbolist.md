## 3. The thread I want to finish: pulse, breath, and ice as a page

The most interesting new fact on the table is the Sceptic's and Music critic's lattice: eight cuts out of eight on a 12 s grid from 2:36.6 to 4:00.6, only one phase in 120 matching, then gone [MEASURED]. Combined with the Film critic's shot lengths (2.6 s mean from 2:36.6, down from 5.2 s) the second half is a **picture that follows the music's breath and ignores its pulse**. Beat phase is random (37% against 43% chance [sync.json]). So the footage keeps a human, irregular, handheld rhythm, and the edit lays a patient six-bar breath over it. That is why it feels like labour rather than dance. I will not say the editor "meant" this. The Sceptic's plain account is that a person with a loop-length marker cut where the marker was. My claim is only about effect: a pulse made by a machine (kick 499.99 ms [MUSIC]) under bodies that do not move on it.

That joins to "ice as page". The picture is full of writing that does not last: the hand-lettered title board at 0:00, the lorry's lettering, the bib numbers, the scored skate tracks of f_0291, and finally floes, ice breaking up. The music is the same: loop, repeated, then stopped. Ice and loop are both surfaces that keep a trace and then cannot hold it. I hold that as a reading. The evidence that it is more than mine is that I can point to the frames: the title (0:00), the scored ice (4:51), the broken floes (f_0300, 4:59).

## 4. Where I still disagree

It is tested by whether a cold viewer, shown the frames without my words, would find the pairing apt. I cannot run that test here, and I say so. What I can do is keep each claim one step from an observation, and mark it.
- **With the Music critic's "medium-low" on the voice-as-metaphor.** I accept the rating, but the behaviour progression is real and measured: free of the bar (narration), loosely bar-anchored (chops), exactly one per 2.000 s (calls) [MUSIC]. Whatever the source, a voice-shaped thing becomes regular. That is a fact about the sound. Whether it is a metaphor is the listener's decision.

## 5. Settled reading (five sentences)

1. This is an old reel of a winter event, laid over a machine-steady loop in B minor at 120 BPM, and the interest is in what the two do not share: the pictures keep a human, irregular rhythm while the music keeps a fixed pulse and a patient 12 s breath.
2. The edit hits the music's structural edges (1:28.6, 1:40.6, 2:36.6, 3:28.6, 3:36.7 within about 0.1 s) and, for 84 s from 2:36.6, its 12 s cell, but never its beat; the one thing the editor clearly listened to is form, not pulse.
3. The film is mostly about people seen small, from behind or in numbers (the narrator's 575 and 9500, the bibs), with faces surfacing rarely and roughly (4:10, 4:35), so the emotional subject is endurance in a crowd more than any one person.
4. The surface everything happens on, ice, is also the piece's quiet metaphor for itself: written on by lettering, bibs and blades, then broken into floes, as the loop is written and then stopped; this is my reading, not a finding.
5. It ends with a hard sound-stop, three off-beat hits and a fade that reach black and digital zero together (about 5:04), which I now take as an unresolved but single landing rather than a gap, and as more likely the natural end of a reel than a statement.

**The single moment that best captures the piece: 1:24.6 to 1:28.6.** Four seconds with no drums and no bass [MUSIC], a flat voice naming the event over an empty snow road, then at 1:28.6 the kick lands on every beat, the voice is cut, and the picture jumps (46.8 frame difference at 88.6 s per the dossier) from empty white to a crowd of onlookers at a bridge. It holds every tension in the piece in one cut: a human voice against a machine, emptiness against people, a picture that ignores the beat but obeys the section edge.
